# Check: Lithuania | Scrap-yard purchase desk (claimed 4.5/10)

1. Duty: **confirmed, with corrections.** Law IX-565 (current consolidated text from 2023-03-01), Art. 3(1) says: "Supirkėjas privalo: 1) registruotis ...; 2) saugoti netauriųjų metalų laužą ir atliekas, tvarkyti jų apskaitą vadovaudamasis ... patvirtintomis taisyklėmis; 3) ... saugoti tik supirkimo vietose; 4) supirkti ... iš fizinių asmenų tik supirkimo vietose; 5) ne vėliau kaip prieš 3 darbo dienas ... pranešti policijos įstaigai" (export of non-ferrous scrap). Art. 3(2) bans buying items on the prohibited list. Point 7 (the statistics report) was repealed from 2023-03-01. Order 4-60 (2023-02-08, in force 2023-03-01) restated order 4-678. Rule 6 of the amended rules requires the "pirkimo–pardavimo faktą patvirtinančiame apskaitos dokumente" to show the details of the seller's proof of lawful acquisition for restricted items. The order also publishes the full prohibited list, and some items may be bought only from legal entities. The duty sits with the buyer (supirkėjas).
   Corrections:
   - Buyers now **register**; they no longer hold a licence (Art. 3(1)(1)).
   - "Netaurieji metalai" means base metals, ferrous **and** non-ferrous (Art. 2(2)), so the duty covers all scrap yards, not only non-ferrous ones.
   - The exact record fields in the consolidated rules were not read, so a specific duty to capture seller ID is unverified.

   Sources: https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/portal/legalAct/lt/TAD/TAIS.153237 ; https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/portal/legalAct/lt/TAD/f41531a2a7f111ed924fd817f8fa798e
2. Penalty: **confirmed: ANK Art. 143 applies; EUR 720-1,950 is the current range and EUR 550-1,500 is outdated.** The ANK text as of 2018-01-01 reads "nuo penkių šimtų penkiasdešimt iki vieno tūkstančio penkių šimtų eurų", plus optional confiscation. Infolex's consolidated ANK shows EUR 720-1,950 after a 2018-12-20 amendment. I saw the Infolex figure only as a search snippet because the page returned HTTP 403. Sources: https://commission.europa.eu/system/files/2018-05/lt_ank_ar_2018-01-01.pdf ; https://www.infolex.lt/teise/DocumentSinglePart.aspx?AktoId=336765&StrNr=143
3. Competition: **confirmed (weak evidence of absence).** Lithuanian and English searches found no Lithuanian scrap-yard purchase software. GPAIS tools (go-erp.eu, gpaisintegracijos.lt, gpais.com, imaslengviau.prg.lt) serve waste and packaging accounting, not the purchase counter. Mature Polish scrap-yard products do exist: Odpadomat (scale, BDO register and the FPOM metal-waste acceptance form) and ITOpen "Program do skupu złomu". These are possible entrants and a proven template, but neither shows Lithuanian localisation. Large chains (Metruna, Tolmets, Kuusakoski) probably run in-house systems (unverified). Sources: https://odpadomat.pl/oprogramowanie-dla-skupu-zlomu ; https://www.itopen.pl/program-do-skupu-zlomu/
4. Market count: **unverifiable.** Since licences were replaced by registration, I found no public register or statistic of buyers. The Waste Managers Register (ATVR) is the likely source but was not reached. The "low hundreds" estimate is still unsourced, though it is plausible now that ferrous yards count too. Source: https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/portal/legalAct/lt/TAD/TAIS.153237 (no count given)

Suggested score: 4/10. The duty and penalty are real and current, and there is no local tool. But the market is tiny and uncounted, the record fields are still unread, there is no new trigger, and Polish vendors could localise. This trims the score slightly, but it stays at the ranking threshold.
