# Check: Portugal | Legionella record-keeping and audit pack (old score 4.0/10)

Checked 2026-10-08.

- **Duty: confirmed, with one nuance.** Lei 52/2018 art. 2(1) covers (a) cooling towers, evaporative condensers and humidifiers, (b) public-access therapeutic or recreational water systems (spas) and (c) building water networks, "designadamente água quente sanitária". Art. 3(1) and art. 6 require a full "Plano", art. 8 requires audits "de três em três anos, por entidades acreditadas pelo IPAC" and art. 5 requires registration, but only for (a) and (b). For hot-water networks (hotels, care homes, gyms) art. 3(3) requires "um programa de manutenção e limpeza ... mantendo um registo atualizado das ações efetuadas". Portaria 25/2021 art. 2(4) adds "mantêm atualizados os registos ... incluindo o plano de controlo, os resultados analíticos e as respetivas medidas corretivas" for all art. 2 systems. Despacho 1547/2022 annex I sets the minimum frequency for hot-water networks: temperature 2x/week (daily at the tank and the return loop) and Legionella spp. quarterly (monthly in some cases). That is a heavy recurring logging duty. Art. 6(6) requires 5-year retention. Art. 16 names the inspectors: ASAE, ACT, ERS, IGAMAOT and IGAS (IGAS has published inspection guides, 2021 and 2023). Art. 19 (2018 text) set fines of EUR 500-4,000 for individuals and EUR 2,500-44,890 for companies. Later amendments: Lei 40/2019, then DL 9/2021, which turned these into "contraordenações económicas". An ERS decision (PCO 180/2022) classed a breach of art. 3(3) as "leve" and closed the case with an admonition, so current fine levels for small firms are probably lower than in 2018 and enforcement is mild. The claim's wording ("prevention plan" for every buyer) overstates this a little: small hot-water-only sites must keep a maintenance programme and records, not the full art. 6 Plan or the triennial audit. This does not change the score.
