## Opportunities

**Nothing viable.** No candidate reached 4/10 with all checks passed. One near-miss is recorded below, scored 3/10. It is not recommended for customer interviews unless a Caribbean-wide security-guard payroll track is wanted.

### Opportunity: Guard roster, payroll and contract-compliance pack for PSRA-registered security firms (near-miss)

**Industry:**
Private contract security (guard companies registered with the Private Security Regulation Authority, PSRA).

**Buyer:**
Owner or office manager of a small or mid-size guard company with roughly 20 to 500 guards that serves government and private clients.

**Trigger / Why now:**
A court ruled in September 2022 (Marksman and the National Housing Trust) that third-party guards are employees, not independent contractors, and ordered NHT contributions to start immediately (Jamaica Observer, 2022-10-03). Government contracts were amended from 1 April 2023 so a guard-related breach can end a contract (Gleaner 2023-01-20, headline and summary only). The minimum wage rose to J$17,000 per 40-hour week from 1 July 2026 (JIS and Observer reports, via search summary), with overtime at J$637.50 an hour. The PSRA Act (1992) is under review on the employee question, and no amendment was confirmed (JIS 2022-03-01).

**Current workflow:**
1. Build a monthly roster in Excel or on paper, with 12-hour shifts and frequent swaps.
2. Collect attendance from site supervisors, often by phone or WhatsApp (unverified).
3. Compute hours, overtime, holiday pay and a minimum-wage check by hand, then run payroll.
4. File the monthly S01 return to TAJ (PAYE, NIS, NHT, Education Tax) and pay HEART; track each guard's PSRA licence expiry.
5. Invoice clients and keep evidence of statutory compliance for government contracts.

**Pain:**
Industry body JSIS estimated a 50 per cent cost increase after the ruling and warned of lost contracts (Observer 2022-10-03). Reports say 88 guards lodged complaints with the Labour Ministry (Gleaner headline, 2023) and some guards say deductions were not remitted (Observer, 2024). No direct evidence of time spent on roster and payroll work was found.

**Existing solutions:**
- Jamaica payroll vendors: YaadBooks (attendance tracking and TAJ-format reports advertised), HeadOffice (S01 and S02), and an Odoo Jamaica payroll module with overtime, public-holiday pay and a flexible 40-hour week (all from search summaries; the YaadBooks page returned 403 to WebFetch and was not opened).
- Generic guard-scheduling products from outside Jamaica: Snap Schedule, WorkWave/TEAM Software, TheRMS, Everhour Shifts (none Jamaica-aware).
- The PSRA's own online renewal of individual licences (2020-22 reports; firm-side scope unclear).
- Accountants and payroll bureaus doing it as a service (not searched separately).

**The gap:**
A roster-to-payroll flow that applies Jamaican guard rules (12-hour shifts, overtime, minimum wage), allocates hours to clients for billing and produces a contract-compliance evidence pack. The statutory maths and S01 are already solved by existing payroll products, so the gap is only the roster and client-billing layer.

**Possible product:**
A roster and timesheet tool that outputs payroll-ready hours, client invoices and a per-guard compliance sheet (PSRA licence expiry, deductions remitted).

**MVP:**
Shift roster with swap approval, WhatsApp-style supervisor check-in form, overtime and minimum-wage check, CSV export to YaadBooks, HeadOffice or Odoo, and an expiry-reminder list for PSRA licences.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
Estimate: J$5,000 to 20,000 per month per firm, or US$1 to 2 per guard per month. At 331 firms the whole market is likely under US$100,000 a year (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**
PSRA's register of security organisations (public availability unverified), JSIS (28 member companies, per Observer 2022), the PSRA mobile registration unit events, and government tender award notices for security services.

**Risks:**
- About 331 firms in total, and big firms with their own systems.
- Payroll vendors can add rostering.
- The employee question may be settled by legislation, which would change the product.
- Margins are thin and firms may buy only a done-for-you payroll service.

**Kill condition:**
Interviews show the firms already run rostering and payroll in YaadBooks, HeadOffice, Odoo or Excel without pain, or fewer than about 100 firms have more than 50 guards.

**Score:** 3/10. This is below the 4.7 sub-score average on purpose: the market is tiny, the gap is thin against payroll vendors, and the statute text could not be opened (see Checks).

**Offline evidence:**
The employee status of about 25,000 guards was handled through contract renegotiation and court cases (Observer, Gleaner 2022-24). The PSRA digitised only individual renewals as of 2020-22 reports. I found no firm-side software, but I did not find direct evidence of Excel or paper rosters either (inferred).

**Offline channel:**
JSIS (28 member firms), the PSRA register and its mobile registration unit, and government tender notices. Not tested.

**Market count:**
331 security organisations registered or renewed in 2025 (up from 311 in 2024); 30,013 individuals; 4,211 armed and 25,802 unarmed (PIOJ Economic and Social Survey as reported by Jamaica Observer, 2026-07-23).

**Willingness to pay:**
Mostly a done-for-you payroll service or an add-on to existing payroll. Firms would pay for software only if it reduces contract risk or speeds client billing (estimate).

**Founder access:**
A non-local solo founder could sell remotely, but the sector runs on relationships and government contracts, so a local partner or reseller would help.

- Duty check: PSRA statement (JIS 2019-03-15): once registered, firms must make PAYE, NIS and NHT deductions and pay at or above the minimum wage; registration is renewed annually (PSRA, via search summaries). Court order: pay the 3 per cent NHT contribution immediately (Observer 2022-10-03). The duty falls on the guard company, which is the named buyer. Penalty under the PSRA Act or for late S01 not confirmed: the statute pages on laws.moj.gov.jm returned 503 on two attempts, and a vendor figure of 25 per cent surcharge plus 1.5 per cent monthly interest on late S01 is unverified. This is why the score stays below 4.

The text of the Act in force today was not read.

## Rejected

- **Produce receipt book digitisation (RADA/JAS, Agricultural Produce Act):** fines were raised by the 2023 amendment from J$250,000 to as much as J$3 million and custodial terms from 3 months to 3 years (JIS 2026-01-22). The system is paper, but the payers are farmers and the owner of any fix is RADA, JAS and the police; a 2013 SMS prototype existed and I found no follow-up. This is an attractive problem with poor distribution and a government-procurement buyer. The statute text was not opened (scanned PDF, then 503).
- **Microcredit lender compliance (BOJ):** only 10 licensed institutions on the BOJ list, and they are established finance companies.
- **Cannabis seed-to-sale (CLA):** about 200 licences ever issued, with StrainIQ claiming CLA-compliant software.
- **Early childhood institutions (ECC):** registration is every five years, and fee-paying capacity is low.
- **Funeral homes:** the RGD has an electronic burial order system since 2020.
- **Villa and guest-house room-tax compliance (GART):** one monthly line item, TAJ e-filing already mandatory since 2020-10-01, and a global compliance service exists. Operator count not found.
- **Scrap metal dealers:** no current Jamaican register held by police found; the regulations are from 2007 and 2013 and their status is unconfirmed.
- **Fisheries, well drillers, cambios, charities, JACRA dealers:** see the screening table; none showed a recurring filing, count and buyer together.
- **Extra idea (one line):** a Caribbean-wide guard payroll and roster tool would only make sense if Trinidad, Barbados or Guyana show the same employee-status shock; not researched here.
