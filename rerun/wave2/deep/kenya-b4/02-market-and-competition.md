# Kenya NDTCP pack: market size, buyers and competition (deep dive 02)

Date: 10 Oct 2026. Builds on [the B4 report](../reports/kenya-b4.md). Scope: market size, buyers, competition, channels and regional expansion. Law, product design and go-to-market detail are covered by the other agents.

Currency: KES 129 = USD 1 is assumed throughout, as in the B4 report (unverified). "My count" means I downloaded the register and counted it myself.

## Summary

- **The licensed base is now 281 lenders, and it is growing fast.** I counted 281 entries in CBK's Directory of Digital Credit Providers, updated 29 Sep 2026 ([CBK directory](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Directory-of-Digital-Credit-Providers-September-2026.pdf)).
  - There were 85 at the end of 2024 and 195 at the end of 2025 ([CBK Bank Supervision Annual Report 2025](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/banking_sector_annual_reports/1241268828_ANNUAL%20REPORT%202025.pdf)). 86 more were licensed between January and September 2026 (my count).
  - Under the draft transition rule these firms carry on "as if licensed" (final text unverified). They do not need the application kit, but they do need the new ongoing conduct work.
- **Most licensed lenders are small.** 111 of the 281 (40%) list a Gmail, Yahoo, Hotmail or Outlook address with CBK, and at least 219 give a Nairobi postal address (my count).
  - The loan book is large but concentrated. Licensed lenders had KES 110.1bn outstanding across 6.74m loan accounts at the end of 2025 ([CBK BSAR 2025](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/banking_sector_annual_reports/1241268828_ANNUAL%20REPORT%202025.pdf)). The list includes large names such as Tala, M-Kopa, Watu, Mogo Auto, Letshego, Platinum and Momentum.
  - The lenders' own association says many licences went to firms "not even doing business" ([Business Daily via Mwanaspoti, Aug 2025](https://www.mwanaspoti.co.tz/bd/economy/why-digital-lenders-seek-a-higher-sh50m-threshold-for-licensing-5162740)).
- **The pipeline is about 620 firms.** CBK has received more than 900 applications since March 2022 ([TechTrendsKE, 30 Sep 2026](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/09/30/cbk-licenses-29-more-digital-lenders/)). About 620 are not licensed: some pending, some withdrawn, some refused. CBK does not publish the split. CBK says the rest are "largely awaiting the submission of requisite documentation" ([CBK press release, Jul 2026](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/press_releases/632621862_Press%20Release%20-%20Licensing%20of%20Digital%20Credit%20Providers%20-%20July%202026.pdf)).
- **No one has counted the newly covered offline lenders.** This group includes logbook and asset lenders, credit-only MFIs, P2P platforms and incorporated moneylenders. I found no register, no statistic, and no count in CBK's impact statement ([CBK RIS](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Regulatory-Impact-Assessment-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf)).
  - The only proxies are an undated association figure of "about 400 providers" and an older press figure of "more than 500 unregulated microlenders".
  - My working estimate is 300-1,500 firms (low confidence).
- **Working base:** about **600-1,000 lenders** will need ongoing compliance support by 2028. About **450-950** may buy an application kit before 29 Mar 2027 (my estimates, unverified).
- **No product does the whole job.** No search in English or Swahili found a dedicated NDTCP product.
  - Loan-management systems cover the loan book, not the conduct duties: Loandisk at USD 59-346 a month ([Loandisk](https://loandisk.com/pricing.php)), Kovara from USD 249 a month, "CBK Ready" ([Kovara](https://kovara.io/kenya.html)), plus SuperLMS, Lendsqr and Tunza.
  - At least 8 small Nairobi law and consulting firms publish DCP licensing guides, and most offer application help. None publishes a price.
  - The nearest software is Trigarc, a generic Kenyan compliance tool with no NDTCP content and quote-only pricing ([FNJ](https://fnjassociates.co.ke/?p=2402)).
- **Willingness to pay is real but capped.** In year one a lender pays CBK KES 350k (registration) or KES 600k (licence) ([Tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/10/04/cbk-raises-licensed-lenders-annual-fee-to-kes-500000-from-kes-20000/)). A compliance specialist costs about KES 53k-142k a month ([Paylab](https://www.paylab.com/ke/salaryinfo/banking/compliance-specialist?lang=en)).
  - A tool at **KES 6k-13k a month (about USD 45-99)** sits below the price of the lender's own loan software and below one week of a compliance hire.
- **Regional upside is larger than Kenya:**
  - Tanzania: 2,938 licensed Tier 2 non-deposit lenders ([BoT BSAR 2025](https://www.bot.go.tz/Publications/Other/Banking%20Supervision%20Annual%20Reports/en/2026070216351588.pdf)).
  - Uganda: 1,302 licensed money lenders ([Eagle Online citing UMRA](https://eagle.co.ug/2024/10/03/money-lenders-association-pledge-to-clean-up-industry-after-musevenis-roar)).
  - Rwanda: about 209-250 non-deposit lenders (unverified, search summary) ([New Times](https://www.newtimes.co.rw/article/35128/news/finance/central-bank-tightens-oversight-revokes-seven-lenders)).
  - Nigeria: 521 registered digital lenders ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/01/07/loan-apps-521-companies-now-on-fccpcs-radar-as-january-deadline-lapses/)).
  - The same product engine with a new legal layer per country could reach about 5,000 lenders in East Africa (Kenya about 800, Tanzania 2,938, Uganda 1,302, Rwanda about 230; my arithmetic).

**Working base (my estimates, unverified):**
- **Application kit, now to 29 Mar 2027:**
  - 300-450 still-live applicants;
  - plus 150-500 offline lenders that choose to comply rather than stop lending;
  - total **about 450-950 firms**. Many will use a lawyer or do it themselves. Many will apply late.
- **Recurring compliance tool, 2028:**
  - the 281 licensed today;
  - plus 300-700 newly licensed or registered firms. CBK has been licensing about 110 a year (my count of 2025-26 batches), so its own speed caps how fast this group grows;

## Buyer profile and pain

**Size and shape.**
- The base is a long tail of small Nairobi firms behind a few large brands.
  - 40% list a webmail address, and at least 78% give a Nairobi address (my count of the [CBK directory](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Directory-of-Digital-Credit-Providers-September-2026.pdf)).
  - Large names on the list: Inventure (Tala), Jumo, M-Kopa Loan, Watu, Mogo Auto, Letshego, Platinum, Momentum and Zenka.
- Average loan size was about KES 16,000 in late 2025 (my reading of a poorly extracted Chart 16; unverified) in [CBK BSAR 2025](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/banking_sector_annual_reports/1241268828_ANNUAL%20REPORT%202025.pdf)).
- Many licence holders may be dormant. DFSAK chair Kevin Mutiso: "a lot of the licenses issued are to entities that are not even doing business." DFSAK asked for a KES 50m threshold instead of KES 20m to "separate players that are serious from those who are not" ([Business Daily via Mwanaspoti, 21 Aug 2025](https://www.mwanaspoti.co.tz/bd/economy/why-digital-lenders-seek-a-higher-sh50m-threshold-for-licensing-5162740)).
- 43% of DCPs had adopted AI in a CBK survey of March 2025 ([CBK BSAR 2025](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/banking_sector_annual_reports/1241268828_ANNUAL%20REPORT%202025.pdf)). That makes the new rule on automated decisions (Reg. 60, per [TechTrendsKE](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/10/05/cbk-non-deposit-taking-credit-providers-regulations/)) relevant to almost half the base.

**How they comply today.**
- **Applications:**
  - CBK's own "A-Z of licensing" guide ([CBK procedures, Oct 2024](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Procedures-for-licensing-Digital-Credit-Providers-Revised-October-2024.pdf));
  - blog guides from small firms ([Mwirigi](https://mwirigitaxconsultants.com/dcp-licensing-kenya-how-to-licence-a-digital-credit-provider-step-by-step/), [Bizbrokers](https://www.bizbrokerskenya.com/digital-credit-provider-license-kenya.html));
  - paid help from law firms.
  The Bizbrokers guide puts government costs under the old regime at KES 36,700 and "excludes legal, compliance, and operational setup costs".
- **Returns:** CBK's BSA portal, using templates downloaded and uploaded by the lender (B4 report, citing [CM Advocates](https://cmadvocates.com/blog/legal-alert-upcoming-bank-supervision-application-bsa-user-training-for-non-deposit-taking-credit-providers-stay-compliant/)).
- **AML:** CBK and the Financial Reporting Centre trained 95 DCPs on the national ML/TF risk assessment in June 2025 ([CBK BSAR 2025](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/banking_sector_annual_reports/1241268828_ANNUAL%20REPORT%202025.pdf)).
- **Data protection:** digital lenders must register with ODPC. The fee is KES 4,000, 16,000 or 40,000 by size ([ODPC compliance card](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Data-Controllers-360-Degrees-Compliance-Requirements.-Z-Card.pdf)).
- **Self-regulation:** the DFSAK code of conduct ([Business & Human Rights Centre](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/kenya-digital-lenders-associations-code-of-conduct)).
- **App stores:** since 2023, Google Play accepts Kenyan loan apps only from lenders listed in CBK's directory. Hundreds of apps were removed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/31/google-to-ban-unlicensed-loan-apps-in-kenya-as-new-rules-take-effect); [CM Advocates](https://cmadvocates.com/blog/google-set-to-ban-unlicensed-mobile-loan-apps-on-play-store-in-new-developer-program-policy/)). For app lenders, being licensed is a condition of distribution, not just of compliance.

**Software they already use.**
- Loan-management systems: Loandisk, Lendsqr, SuperLMS, Craft Silicon, Presta, Kovara and Tunza (see the competitor table).
- KYC and AML tools: YouVerify, from KES 8,000 a month ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/nigerian-startup-youverify-launches-in-kenya-4247756)), and Sumsub, which partners with a Kenyan fintech association ([Sumsub](https://sumsub.com/newsroom/sumsub-joins-forces-with-kenyas-premier-fintech-association-to-drive-digital-innovation/)).
- I found no lender using a compliance-calendar or conduct-register product (unverified).

**Pain (documented).**
- **The documents bottleneck.** CBK says applicants are "largely awaiting the submission of requisite documentation" ([CBK, Jul 2026](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/press_releases/632621862_Press%20Release%20-%20Licensing%20of%20Digital%20Credit%20Providers%20-%20July%202026.pdf)). In March 2024, 429 of 480 applications were pending documentation, and the association said members "lack clarity on what to submit" ([Business Daily, Mar 2024](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/digital-lenders-seek-cbk-help-to-unlock-429-licences-4551784), from the B4 report).
- **Fee shock.** The annual fee rose from KES 20,000 to KES 500,000 (licence) or KES 250,000 (registration). Paying late now costs KES 1m ([Tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/10/04/cbk-raises-licensed-lenders-annual-fee-to-kes-500000-from-kes-20000/); [TechTrendsKE](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/10/05/cbk-non-deposit-taking-credit-providers-regulations/)). Small lenders will be price-sensitive about anything else.
- **Too many regulators.** DFSAK complains of "the multiplicity of regulatory requirements" across CBK, ODPC and the Competition Authority ([The Standard, Aug 2025](https://thestandard.ke/sports/business/article/2001527611/www.digger.co.ke), from the B4 report). Industry also cites "regulatory overreach where the CBK can summon players alongside two other oversight bodies" ([Business Daily via Mwanaspoti](https://www.mwanaspoti.co.tz/bd/economy/why-digital-lenders-seek-a-higher-sh50m-threshold-for-licensing-5162740)).
- **Microfinance lenders feel mis-filed.** AMFI-K's CEO: "We don't think DCP regulations are a good fit for us" ([Business Daily via Mwanaspoti, Aug 2024](https://www.mwanaspoti.co.tz/bd/economy/why-credit-only-microfinanciers-are-seeking-digital-licences-4723408)). A scope-and-tier checker answers a real question.
- **Enforcement exposure.** Lending without a licence can bring a fine of up to KES 5m or three years in prison under the Act ([Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/news/publications/2022/Practice/Corporate/corporate-and-commercial-alert-2-february-the-central-bank-of-kenya-amendment-act-of-2021-regulation-of-digital-lenders-.html)). ODPC and the Competition Authority have fined lenders (see the B4 report).
- **Public forums.** I found no forum or Facebook group where lenders discuss compliance. The discussion happens in the press, in law-firm alerts and on LinkedIn (unverified).

**Reading.**
- Small lenders already pay USD 59-250 a month for loan software. A compliance add-on should cost less than their core system.
- **USD 45 a month (about KES 6k)** for a registered lender and **USD 99 a month (about KES 13k)** for a licensed lender fit under that anchor. Both are below one week of a compliance specialist's pay.
- The B4 report suggested KES 15k a month for everyone. That is right for licensed lenders, but too high for the registered tier, which has just been hit with a KES 250k fee.
- **Kit:** a one-off price of **KES 50k-90k (USD 390-690)** is below the CBK application fee of KES 100k and, almost certainly, below any lawyer's fee (unverified).
- **Advisers:** a firm handling 10-20 lenders could pay **USD 300-400 a month**. That is still cheaper per client than any single LMS seat.

## Competitor table and discussion

The duty list below is from the CBK draft and press coverage of LN 191 (see the law file):
- **D1:** scope and tier check (in or out of scope; licence or registration).
- **D2:** application pack: forms NDTCP 1-3, six policies, fit-and-proper document checklist.
- **D3:** CBK prior approval of product and price changes, with a 30-day customer notice log.
- **D4:** complaints register with the 7-day, 48-hour and 30-day clocks, and the report to CBK.
- **D5:** agent register and annual agent approvals.
- **D6:** BSA returns: preparing and checking them.
- **D7:** annual compliance return and the 31 Dec fee deadline calendar.
- **D8:** 30-day notice before a CRB listing.
- **D9:** AML/CFT and ODPC cross-duties.
- **D10:** governance of AI and automated decisions.

**Discussion.**
- **The gap is the ongoing conduct file, not the paperwork.** Nobody sells a tool for D3, D4, D5, D7 and D8 to small lenders. LMS vendors cover the loan book and at most D6. Lawyers cover D1-D2 once.
- **For the kit, the real competitors are small Nairobi law and consulting firms.** None shows a price, which suggests bespoke pricing (unverified).
  - A fixed-price kit beats them on price and speed.
  - A partnership with one or two of them handles the Advocates Act question and gives a sales force for the March 2027 rush.
- **Fastest-moving threats:**
  - Kovara and SuperLMS already market "CBK-ready" reporting.
  - Trigarc already has the calendar engine and could add an NDTCP pack.
  - The defence is speed, NDTCP-specific content, and pricing below their core systems.
- **No Swahili-language product or content was found.** All CBK material and all vendor sites are in English. English is enough for Kenya. Swahili matters for Tanzania.

## Implications for positioning and pricing

1. **Sell the running conduct file. Use the kit as the way in.**
   - The 281 licensed lenders need no kit. They do need to rework their policies and processes for LN 191: the code of conduct, rate-change approvals, complaints clocks, agents and AI rules.
   - Sell them a "LN 191 alignment pack" plus the recurring tool.
   - Sell the kit to applicants. Each kit should include 3 months of the tool, so the buyer converts to recurring use.
2. **Price below the lender's own loan software and tier by CBK class (my proposals, unverified):**
   - Registered tier: **USD 45 a month (about KES 6k)** or USD 450 a year.
   - Licensed tier: **USD 99 a month (about KES 13k)** or USD 990 a year.
   - Adviser plan, up to 20 lenders: **USD 349 a month**.
   - Kit: **USD 490 (about KES 63k)** one-off, or USD 890 with review by a partner advocate.
   - Card billing in USD through Stripe or Paddle fits firms that already pay Loandisk or Kovara in USD. Whether micro lenders can pay by card rather than M-Pesa is unverified (see the payments file).
3. **Timing is tight.** An MVP in 3 weeks and a sellable product in 6-8 weeks means a launch in late November to mid-December 2026. That leaves about 3-4 months of the rush.
   - Do the March 2027 wave through 2-3 small law or consulting firms, not direct sales.
   - Use direct outreach to the CBK list for the recurring product.
4. **Year-3 revenue (my estimates, unverified):**
   - **Low:** 600 lenders x 6% x KES 90k = KES 3.2m. Add 3 advisers x KES 400k = KES 1.2m and 25 kits x KES 60k = KES 1.5m. Total **about KES 6m (USD 46k)**.
   - **Base:** 800 x 12% x KES 120k = KES 11.5m. Add 5 advisers x KES 450k = KES 2.3m and 40 kits x KES 63k = KES 2.5m. Total **about KES 16m (USD 125k)**.
   - **Good:** 1,000 x 18% x KES 140k = KES 25.2m. Add 8 advisers x KES 450k = KES 3.6m and 60 kits = KES 3.8m. Total **about KES 33m (USD 253k)**.
   - The base case is in line with the B4 report (KES 15-27m). The two pools that move it most are the offline lenders and the share of dormant licences.
   - Uganda and Tanzania could double the base with the same engine and a new legal layer.
5. **Moats:**
   - NDTCP-specific content kept current with CBK circulars;
   - a partner-advocate review tier;
   - an adviser plan that recruits the small law firms instead of fighting them;
   - a low price that Kovara, SuperLMS and Trigarc would not match for small lenders.
6. **Watch:**
   - CBK publishing model policies or guidance notes, which DFSAK has asked for;
   - SuperLMS, Kovara or Trigarc adding an NDTCP module;
   - the registered tier shrinking if the KES 250k fee drives micro lenders out.

## Open questions

- How many of the ~620 unlicensed applicants are still live? CBK does not split pending, withdrawn and refused. A query to CBK or DFSAK may answer it.
- How many offline lenders (logbook, asset finance, credit-only MFIs, P2P) exist? No register exists until CBK publishes its first NDTCP list.
- What share of the 281 licensed lenders are dormant? Test by checking how many have a live website or app.
- The final scope carve-outs in LN 191 (BNPL, PAYG, hire purchase, incidental credit), and whether registered firms carry the same policy set. Kenya Law returned 403 ([LN 191 page](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2026/191/eng@2026-09-29)).
- What law firms and consultants charge for a DCP or NDTCP application. Ask 3-5 firms for a quote (mystery shopping).
- Do Craft Silicon, Presta or SuperLMS plan an NDTCP module?
- DFSAK, DCPAK and AMFI-K membership numbers and fees.
- Can micro lenders pay by card in USD, or do they need M-Pesa billing?
- Uganda: the current count of UMRA money lenders (2025-26) and their periodic returns. Tanzania: BoT Tier 2 reporting duties, and the TAMFI and TAMIU membership terms.
