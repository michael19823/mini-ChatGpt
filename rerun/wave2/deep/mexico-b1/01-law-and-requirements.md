# Mexico private-security register law, turned into product requirements

Status: complete as of 2026-10-10 (open questions at the end). The federal law and its Reglamento were read in full from the Chamber of Deputies PDFs. The DGSP website (dgsp.sspc.gob.mx) refused every connection, so DGSP forms and guides are known only from search snippets and are marked as such.

Short names used below:
- **LFSP** = Ley Federal de Seguridad Privada, DOF 6 Jul 2006, last reform DOF 17 Oct 2011 ([LFSP PDF](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LFSP.pdf)).
- **RLFSP** = Reglamento de la LFSP, DOF 18 Oct 2011, never reformed ([RLFSP PDF](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/regley/Reg_LFSP.pdf)).
- **LGSNSP** = Ley General del Sistema Nacional de Seguridad Pública, new law DOF 16 Jul 2025 ([LGSNSP PDF](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LGSNSP.pdf)).
- **LFD** = Ley Federal de Derechos (federal fees), compiled text with last reform DOF 7 Nov 2025 ([LFD PDF](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LFD.pdf)).
- **DGSP** = Dirección General de Seguridad Privada, the federal regulator inside the Secretaría de Seguridad y Protección Ciudadana (SSPC).
- **Registro Nacional** = Registro Nacional de Empresas, Personal y Equipo de Seguridad Privada (LFSP art. 8-13).
- **RNPSP** = Registro Nacional del Personal de Seguridad Pública. Private operational staff must be inscribed there too (LFSP art. 28-III). The form is the "cédula única de identificación personal" (RLFSP art. 2-II), and the person gets a CUIP key.
- **UMA** = Unidad de Medida y Actualización. The LFSP writes fines in "salario mínimo". The DGSP now applies them in UMA: MXN 117.31 per UMA in a Sep 2026 sanction ([SIDOF 5799650](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5799650)). So 1,000 UMA = MXN 117,310, 3,000 UMA = MXN 351,930 and 5,000 UMA = MXN 586,550 (my arithmetic).
- **Firm** = "prestador de servicios" (provider). **Client** = "prestatario".

## Summary

- **Two federal texts set the core duty, and both are old and stable.** The LFSP (last reform 2011) and the RLFSP (2011, never reformed) still apply. No Ley General de Seguridad Privada has been issued, although the Constitution has allowed one since May 2021 ([ASF audit 2020](https://www.asf.gob.mx/Trans/Informes/IR2020b/Documentos/Auditorias/2020_0095_a.pdf); [Proceso 2018](https://www.proceso.com.mx/nacional/2018/4/25/senado-da-luz-verde-ley-para-regular-empresas-privadas-de-seguridad-203866.html)).
- **Who.** Any firm or individual that provides private security in two or more states needs a DGSP authorisation, valid for one year (LFSP art. 1, 16, 17). Such a firm also needs a local authorisation in each state where it works (LGSNSP 2025 art. 122; the 2009 law's art. 150 had the same rule, per a search snippet of the [old text](https://www.ssc.cdmx.gob.mx/storage/app/media/Documentos/Seguridad%20Privada/LGSNSP_170616.pdf)). Single-state firms answer only to their state. There is no size exemption.
- **The core recurring duty.** "Within the first 10 calendar days of each month" the firm must report the status of, and changes to, every heading of the Registro Nacional (LFSP art. 13). The headings cover the authorisation, offices and branches, legal representatives, corporate changes, managers and admin staff, a full file per guard, and all arms, vehicles and equipment (LFSP art. 12). Annual exam results also go in this monthly report (RLFSP art. 48).
- **Event-driven duties sit on top.** Every guard needs a police-record check, RNPSP inscription and a DGSP ID card before working (LFSP art. 28, 32-XVI; RLFSP art. 30, 32). A leaver's ID card must go back to the DGSP within 3 business days (RLFSP art. 31). Theft or loss of documents and any suspension of activity must be reported within 3 business days (LFSP art. 32-XXI, XXIII). Each new uniform model, vehicle, arm and radio must be registered before use (LFSP art. 32-IV; RLFSP art. 34).
- **Annual duties.** Revalidation at least 30 business days before expiry (LFSP art. 19). Medical, psychological and toxicology exams for every guard at least once a year (LFSP art. 32-VI; RLFSP art. 47). Training in the modality and in human rights at least once a year, proven with STPS DC-3 certificates (RLFSP art. 42).
- **States add their own monthly report.** CDMX, Estado de México, Baja California and Tamaulipas want altas and bajas in the first 5 business days of each month (Guerrero, Guanajuato and Aguascalientes: the first 5 days). Nuevo León copies the federal 10-day rule. Jalisco wants a monthly written notice. Baja California wants an 8-file pack by email even when nothing changed ([BC guide](https://www.seguridadbc.gob.mx/Planeacion/padron/GUIA%20LLENADO%20CORRECTO%20DEL%20INFORME%20MENSUAL.pdf)). So a federal firm in three states can owe four monthly reports with two different deadlines and four formats.
- **Filing is paper, Excel and email, not a portal.** Federal filings go to the DGSP "ventanilla única" with a signed letter or form plus Excel and PDF annexes. DGSP staff key them into internal systems. Firms have no login (search snippet of the [DGSP equipment guide](https://dgsp.sspc.gob.mx/static/contenido/Guia_Solicitud_Alta_Baja_Equipo.pdf); [ASF audit 2020](https://www.asf.gob.mx/Trans/Informes/IR2020b/Documentos/Auditorias/2020_0095_a.pdf)). I could not find the federal monthly-report format itself (open question 1).
- **Enforcement is real but light.** In 2020 the DGSP did 67 verification visits for about 1,650 firms ([ASF](https://www.asf.gob.mx/Trans/Informes/IR2020b/Documentos/Auditorias/2020_0095_a.pdf)). It sanctioned "more than 20" firms in 2025, with fines up to MXN 325,710 ([Infobae](https://www.infobae.com/mexico/2025/12/16/sancionan-a-mas-de-20-empresas-de-seguridad-privada-en-2025-revelan-desafios-estructurales-en-la-regulacion-del-sector/)). The 2026 DOF notices show the same register gaps again and again: unreported altas, bajas, uniforms, branches, managers and training. A missed monthly report is formally only an amonestación (RLFSP art. 60-I). In practice the DGSP stacks findings and adds 1,000-1,500 UMA fines.
- **Change is coming in the channel, not the duty.** An SSPC Acuerdo signed 12 Feb 2026 merges nine DGSP procedures into one and gives the DGSP one year to adapt its registry systems ([SIDOF 5784083](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5784083)). A September 2026 bill keeps the 10-day monthly report and would publish amonestaciones in the Registro so clients can check a firm ([Gaceta, 8 Sep 2026](https://gaceta.diputados.gob.mx/Gaceta/66/2026/sep/20260908-II-1-2.html)).
- **Product.** 88 testable requirements follow. The core is a staff, equipment and office master with dated, reasoned changes; an onboarding gate; exam and training expiry tracking; a monthly federal report builder; state report modules (Baja California first); an event-notice clock; a revalidation pack; and an inspection pack. Sensitive data (health and toxicology results, criminal checks) must be protected under the 2025 data-protection law.

## Who is obliged

**Federal layer.**
- A "prestador de servicios" is any person or company that provides private security (LFSP art. 2-III). This includes individuals working alone, such as escorts or guards not employed by a firm. They carry the same duties as company staff (LFSP art. 2-IV, 34).
- A DGSP authorisation is needed when services are provided **in two or more states** (LFSP art. 1, 15, 16; RLFSP art. 3). Single-state services are governed only by state law (LFSP art. 1, second paragraph).
- The authorisation is personal, lasts **one year** and must be revalidated (LFSP art. 17, 19). It carries a registration number. Example format: "Registro Federal Permanente DGSP/208-19/3922" ([SIDOF 5799650](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5799650)).
- A multi-state firm needs **both** the local authorisation of each state concerned and the SSPC authorisation. Neither authority may demand more requirements than the sector rules list (LGSNSP 2025 art. 122, in force since 17 Jul 2025; the 2009 law had a similar rule in art. 150, per search snippet).
- The firm must be Mexican (LFSP art. 16, 25-I). A company's corporate purpose must match the modalities applied for (RLFSP art. 19-I).

**Modalities (LFSP art. 15; RLFSP art. 5).** The modality changes some duties, so the product must store it.

| Modality | What it covers | Extra duties |
|---|---|---|
| I. Persons | Protection of the client's life and body (escorts) | Arms opinion and SEDENA licence if armed |
| II. Property | Guarding movable and immovable property, including people inside | Most common guard-company modality |
| III. Transport of goods or valuables | Sub-modalities "especial" and "vigilancia" | Armoured vehicles, level IV or higher, for valuables (LFSP 32-XXIX; RLFSP 20-III) |
| IV. Alarms and electronic monitoring | Alarm installation and monitoring centres | Monitoring-centre standards, PROFECO contract, signage (LFSP 45-50) |
| V. Information security | Data, network and backup security | Technical staff qualifications (RLFSP 20-IV) |
| VI. Prevention and responsibility systems | Background checks, solvency, polygraph | One file per case with the client's written consent (RLFSP 26) |
| VII. Linked activities | Vehicle armouring; sale or installation of CCTV, GPS, access control, electric fences, ballistic vests | Buyer and user register filed every 6 months (LFSP 32-XXXII; RLFSP 35); armouring holograms (RLFSP 24-25) |

Note: the numbering of art. 15 changed in 2011. The LFSP now has seven modalities, but RLFSP art. 35 still refers to "fracción VII" for the buyer register, while LFSP art. 32-XXXII refers to "fracción VI". This is a drafting mismatch. The product should apply the buyer register to the linked-activities modality (my reading; unverified with the DGSP).

**Thresholds and exemptions.**
- No exemption by headcount, turnover or legal form. A one-guard firm has the same duties.
- The only scope rule is territorial: one state (state law only) versus two or more (federal plus each state).
- Armouring firms are regulated federally whatever their territory, until states regulate armouring (LFSP transitory art. 7).
- First-time applicants without staff or equipment get up to 30 business days after authorisation to hire and equip (RLFSP art. 20, last paragraph).
- In-house guard teams (a company guarding its own premises with its own staff) are not clearly covered by the LFSP definitions. Some state laws do cover them (unverified; open question).

**People inside the firm who must be registered.** Partners, legal representatives, directors and managers, administrative staff and operational staff (RLFSP art. 32). Managers and admin staff must meet LFSP art. 27 (no intentional-crime conviction, not dismissed from police or army for listed causes, not active police or military). Operational staff must meet LFSP art. 28 (no criminal record, adult, inscribed in the RNPSP, trained in the modality, not dismissed for listed causes, not active police or military).

**How many firms.** There is no current official count.
- The DGSP Registro held 863 federal firms with 126,383 staff in 2020. The SSPC reported 1,186 federal firms and 127,516 staff at 31 Jul 2020 ([ASF audit 2020](https://www.asf.gob.mx/Trans/Informes/IR2020b/Documentos/Auditorias/2020_0095_a.pdf)).
- Industry bodies speak of more than 5,000 or 6,000 authorised firms (federal and state together, unclear) and 500,000 workers ([Diario de Juárez / Reforma, 22 Feb 2026](https://diario.mx/nacional/2026/feb/22/apuntan-a-empresas-patito-de-seguridad-privada-1106610.html)). A 2026 bill cites AMESP's "more than 8,000 firms" and "over 600,000 workers" ([Gaceta, 8 Sep 2026](https://gaceta.diputados.gob.mx/Gaceta/66/2026/sep/20260908-II-1-2.html)).
- In 2020, CDMX (287), Jalisco (144) and Estado de México (143) held 6 of every 10 federal firms ([ASF](https://www.asf.gob.mx/Trans/Informes/IR2020b/Documentos/Auditorias/2020_0095_a.pdf)).

**Clients carry risk too (CDMX).** A client in Mexico City that hires a provider who does not meet the CDMX law is fined 3,500-5,000 Unidades de Cuenta (CDMX Ley de Seguridad Privada art. 37 Bis-II, [Congreso CDMX PDF](https://www.congresocdmx.gob.mx/archivo-5fd271a8a8d62a47b21b414c89e8b6390a77423a.pdf)). This makes "proof of compliance for clients" a real sales feature.

**How sanctions are published.** An amonestación is published on the SSPC website (LFSP 42-I). Every sanction is also published in the DOF and in a national newspaper at the firm's cost, naming the firm, the sanction, its authorisation number and address (LFSP 42, penultimate paragraph). Sanctions are entered in the Registro (RLFSP 59). Fines are paid in 15-30 business days (RLFSP 67-II).

**Record keeping and retention.**
- Registro-related evidence (service register, invoices, contracts) must be kept for the life of the authorisation or revalidation, at the head office (RLFSP 23-X, 53-IV). Since the authorisation is renewed every year, the law gives no clear end date (my reading).
- Labour records: contracts for the employment plus one year; payroll and attendance for the last year of employment plus one year (LFT art. 804, [mley.mx](https://mley.mx/LFT/articulo/804/)).
- Tax and social-security records: five years, extendable (CFF art. 30, summarised by [Cofide](https://www.cofide.mx/blog/cuales-son-los-plazos-para-conservar-nominas-y-contratos-de-trabajo), secondary).
- Product default: keep every Registro record and its evidence for at least 5 years after the person or item leaves, unless the client chooses longer (design choice, not a legal rule).

## Filing channels and formats

**Federal (DGSP).**
- **No firm-facing portal.** The DGSP keeps the Registro in internal systems: a "Sistema de Registro de Empresas de Seguridad Privada", a "Registro de Equipamiento para Empresas de Seguridad Privada", and the RNPSP on Plataforma México. DGSP staff enter what firms file. In 2020 the DGSP handled 5,966 Registro procedures this way ([ASF audit 2020](https://www.asf.gob.mx/Trans/Informes/IR2020b/Documentos/Auditorias/2020_0095_a.pdf)).
- **Channel: the DGSP "ventanilla única", in person.** The DGSP equipment guide says equipment registration "se realiza directamente en ventanilla única de la Dirección General", any business day, without waiting for the monthly report. Some documents are accepted only in electronic form (search snippet of the [DGSP equipment guide](https://dgsp.sspc.gob.mx/static/contenido/Guia_Solicitud_Alta_Baja_Equipo.pdf); the site refused connections, so not opened).
- **Formats seen (all from search snippets of dgsp.sspc.gob.mx, not opened):**
  - "Solicitud_Alta_Baja_Equipo_DGSP" in **Excel**, signed, with a letter from the legal representative. Uniform altas also need the purchase invoice and **four images per uniform** with size and resolution rules.
  - "Cedula_RNPSP.pdf", the CUIP form. Fields include the immediate boss's CUIP, baja type, reason for separation and monthly base salary.
  - "FORMATO_BAJA.pdf", a sworn baja form with start and end dates, reason and a copy of ID.
  - "Formato de Alta/Baja de Personal Directivo/Administrativo" with a letter on letterhead signed by the legal representative.
  - "Cedula_FIESP.pdf", an incidents form for private-security firms.
  - A personnel report delivered as a folder of complete CUIP cédulas scanned in colour to PDF, with file names matching a link (index) file.
- **Letters are being replaced by forms.** The May 2025 SSPC Acuerdo removed the free-form letter from the record check (SSPC-2019-268-003-A), ID card (-006-A), RNPSP inscription (-008-A) and arms or equipment registration (-007-A) procedures, and issued standard forms (Anexos Uno to Siete). It also removed the PDF email confirmations from some files and set a 60-business-day maximum response time ([SIDOF 5758085](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5758085)). The Feb 2026 Acuerdo did the same for certified copies and the single authorisation procedure SSPC-2019-268-005, with new Anexos I-III ([SIDOF 5784083](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5784083)).

- **Real-world timing.** A CDMX gestoría lists 8 steps and 45-90 business days to put one armed guard to work: record check 5-10 days, viability 7-15, RNPSP inscription and CUIP 10-20, credential 10-20, DGSP arms opinion 15-30, SEDENA military-service card check 5-10, SEDENA "Modelo 4" 10-15, arms assignment 5-10. So a product must track pending states, not just done or not done.
- **The monthly report format itself:** not found. The law only says it covers all Registro headings (LFSP art. 13). The 2025 and 2026 Acuerdos do not mention it. How the DGSP receives it (ventanilla, email, disk) is unconfirmed (open question 1).
- **A sign of under-reporting.** In 2020 the DGSP received 694 equipment altas but only 185 equipment bajas, 3 personnel bajas and 1 ID-card return, against about 25,000 new guard registrations ([ASF](https://www.asf.gob.mx/Trans/Informes/IR2020b/Documentos/Auditorias/2020_0095_a.pdf)). Guard turnover is high, so most bajas are either buried in monthly reports or never reported (my inference). The 2026 sanctions for unreported bajas fit this.

**States.** Baja California takes its monthly report by **email** with Excel and PDF files ([BC guide](https://www.seguridadbc.gob.mx/Planeacion/padron/GUIA%20LLENADO%20CORRECTO%20DEL%20INFORME%20MENSUAL.pdf)). I found no state with a firm-facing online register.

## Supervisors and enforcement evidence

**Who supervises.**
- Federal: the DGSP of the SSPC (LFSP art. 5; RLFSP art. 4). It authorises, keeps the Registro, inspects, sanctions, issues ID cards and gives the arms opinion.
- National registers: the Secretariado Ejecutivo del SNSP sets the rules, minimum data and update periods of the national registers, including the RNPSP (LGSNSP art. 105). Private firms must share staff, equipment and statistical data with it (LGSNSP art. 124). This data is classified as reserved (LGSNSP art. 101).
- Arms: SEDENA (Defensa) for the collective licence (LFSP art. 6, 24).
- States: each state security ministry for local authorisation and its own monthly report (see Regional differences).

**How inspections work.**
- The DGSP may inspect at any time, including before granting an authorisation (LFSP art. 36). A visit can be physical (property and equipment), on performance, on staff development, or on legality (LFSP art. 37). The Ley Federal de Procedimiento Administrativo governs the procedure (LFSP art. 38).
- The visited firm must identify each person and their function, and show invoices, contracts or similar proof of the services it provides. It must keep that evidence at the head office for the life of the authorisation. Inspectors may sketch, copy documents, take photos or video (RLFSP art. 53).
- Visits also follow complaints. Anyone can complain to the DGSP, which can then order a visit (RLFSP art. 51).
- I found no published DGSP inspection checklist or questionnaire. The checklist below is rebuilt from the law and from what 2025-2026 sanctions counted (my reading):
  1. Authorisation valid, modality and states match the contracts.
  2. Every guard on site: inscribed in the RNPSP, has a CUIP and wears the DGSP ID card.
  3. Exams within 12 months for every guard.
  4. Training proof (DC-3) for every guard; human-rights course in the last year.
  5. Staff list, managers, admin staff and branch heads match what the DGSP holds.
  6. Every uniform model, vehicle, radio and arm in use is registered; vehicle photos on file.
  7. Every branch opening, closure and address change reported; 1 m² sign at each office.
  8. Monthly reports filed on time, with training and exam results.
  9. Service register, invoices and contracts at the head office.

**Capacity and volume.**
- 2020: 1,651 firms were subject to verification. The DGSP planned 350 visits, cut the target to 76 after a 75% budget cut, and did 67 (47 in CDMX, 13 in Estado de México) ([ASF](https://www.asf.gob.mx/Trans/Informes/IR2020b/Documentos/Auditorias/2020_0095_a.pdf)).
- Aug 2019 to Jul 2020: 227 authorisations, 673 revalidations, 137 rejections and 86 firms sanctioned (SSPC report quoted by the [ASF](https://www.asf.gob.mx/Trans/Informes/IR2020b/Documentos/Auditorias/2020_0095_a.pdf)).
- 2025: "more than 20" firms sanctioned; top fine MXN 325,710; findings on staff, branch and equipment altas and bajas, uniforms, credentials, missing exams (nine guards in one case), missing DC-3 (22 guards), trust tests, signage, and unauthorised modalities ([Infobae, 16 Dec 2025](https://www.infobae.com/mexico/2025/12/16/sancionan-a-mas-de-20-empresas-de-seguridad-privada-en-2025-revelan-desafios-estructurales-en-la-regulacion-del-sector/)).
- Industry voices say the regulator is small for the number of firms, and the 2026 reform aims to supervise "the information" rather than each operation physically ([Diario de Juárez, 22 Feb 2026](https://diario.mx/nacional/2026/feb/22/apuntan-a-empresas-patito-de-seguridad-privada-1106610.html)).

What this means for the product:
- Two deadline families: **5 business days** (most states) and **10 calendar days** (federal, Nuevo León). A firm in CDMX and Estado de México with a federal authorisation has three reports in the first ten days of every month.
- Formats differ by state. Baja California is the best documented and the strictest, so it is the right first state module. CDMX, Estado de México and Jalisco hold most federal firms (ASF 2020) and are the next ones, but their formats must be collected from pilot firms.
- States add rules the federal law lacks: client fines (CDMX), state ID cards with a 3-day return (Jalisco), payroll reconciliation (Baja California), monthly activity reports listing clients and contracts (Baja California, Nuevo León, Guanajuato).
- The other 21 states were not checked (open question).

Each requirement is testable. "Basis" gives the legal source. Abbreviations as defined at the top; "BC guide" = Baja California monthly-report guide; "Acuerdo 2025" = [SIDOF 5758085](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5758085); "Acuerdo 2026" = [SIDOF 5784083](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5784083). Requirements marked (config) depend on a format not yet obtained; the software must hold that format as editable configuration.

**A. Scope, jurisdictions and set-up**

1. The firm profile must store: legal form (company or individual), RFC, DGSP registration number (free text, e.g. "DGSP/208-19/3922"), authorisation issue and expiry dates, authorised modalities and sub-modalities, and authorised states. Test: the modality picker offers exactly the seven LFSP modalities plus the RLFSP sub-modalities (transport "especial"/"vigilancia"; armouring; technical devices). Basis: LFSP 15, 17; RLFSP 5, 16.
2. For each state where the firm works, the system must hold a local authorisation record (number, authority, expiry). A firm that works in two or more states without a DGSP authorisation must get a blocking alert. Test: add a second state with no federal record → alert "federal authorisation required". Basis: LFSP 1, 16; LGSNSP 122.
3. The system must build the firm's obligation calendar from its profile: federal monthly report, each state's monthly report, revalidations, bond, exam and training expiries, buyer register, arms licence. Test: a federal firm working in CDMX and Estado de México shows three monthly reports with due dates "5th business day" (two states) and "10th calendar day" (federal). Basis: LFSP 13; CDMX law 35-V; Edomex law 30.
4. Deadlines must be computed in business days or calendar days as each rule says, using a maintained Mexican holiday calendar (federal plus per-authority closures as data). Test: a 3-business-day notice starting Friday before a Monday holiday falls due on Thursday. Basis: LFSP 19, 32-XXI, 32-XXIII, 32-XXVII; RLFSP 31.
5. Modality-specific duties must switch on only for the modalities held: armoured vehicles (transport of valuables), monitoring-centre duties (alarms), case files with client consent (prevention systems), buyer register and holograms (linked activities). Test: a property-guarding-only firm sees no monitoring or buyer-register tasks. Basis: LFSP 32-XXIX, 32-XXXI bis, 32-XXXII, 45-50; RLFSP 24-26, 35.
6. Recording a federal authorisation, revalidation or modification must create one notice task per state, due 30 calendar days after receipt. Test: revalidation received 1 Mar with three states → three tasks due 31 Mar. Basis: LFSP 32-XXVII.
7. Every rule (deadline, data field, format, fee) must be stored as data with its legal basis, effective date and source URL, so the vendor can apply legal changes without a code release. Test: a new deadline with effective date 1 May changes only periods from May onward. Basis: upcoming changes (Acuerdo 2026 art. Cuarto transitorio; bill of 8 Sep 2026).

**B. People master and personnel file**

8. Each person must have one record keyed by CURP. The system must validate CURP (18 characters, official structure and check digit) and RFC (13 characters for individuals). Test: a CURP with a wrong check digit is rejected. Basis: RLFSP 19-II, 33-I; LFSP 25-XII.
9. The record must hold every RLFSP 33 field: name, sex, RFC, CURP, birth date and state; education; address; altas, bajas, transfers, activity and rank changes with reasons; vehicle, arms and equipment assigned; training; work history; administrative or criminal sanctions; distinguishing marks; exam results; personal references. A completeness indicator must show missing fields. Test: a guard missing "personal references" shows 10/11 complete. Basis: RLFSP 33; LFSP 12-X.
10. The system must classify each person as partner, legal representative, director or manager, administrative, technical or operational, with role-specific required fields and reportable events. Test: changing a manager produces a Registro event under LFSP 12-IX, not 12-X. Basis: LFSP 12-VI, IX, X; RLFSP 32.
11. The system must run the eligibility checklist per role: managers and admin staff (LFSP 27-I to III), operational staff (LFSP 28-I to VI), including adult age from the CURP birth date, no criminal record, not an active police or military member, and not dismissed for the listed causes. A failing person cannot be set active. Test: a 17-year-old cannot be made an active guard. Basis: LFSP 27, 28, 32-XI; Jalisco Reglamento 42.
12. Every alta, baja, transfer, activity change and rank change must be a dated event with a reason code, a free-text reason, the user who entered it and optional evidence. Test: saving a baja without a reason fails. Basis: LFSP 12-X; RLFSP 32 (last paragraph), 33-IV.
13. Each guard must carry an RNPSP status (not started, record check requested, viability received, inscription filed, inscribed with CUIP, rejected) with dates and DGSP folio numbers. Test: a guard with any status other than "inscribed" cannot be assigned to a post (see 20). Basis: LFSP 28-III, 32-XVI; RLFSP 32.
14. The system must track each DGSP ID card: request date, issue date, number, validity, return date. It must alert when a card is not issued 20 calendar days after a complete file. Test: file complete on 1 Jun, no card by 21 Jun → alert. Basis: RLFSP 30; LFSP 5-VI, 32-XX.
15. The system must print the company credential with all RLFSP 19-VI fields: firm name, address and phone; person's name, CURP, CUIP if any, photo and signature; validity; signature of the firm or legal representative; arm and licence number if armed. Test: the PDF fails generation if any field is empty. Basis: RLFSP 19-VI.
16. The system must import payroll (CFDI nómina XML, Excel exports from common payroll tools, or the IMSS SUA file) and list people paid but not active in the register, and people active but not paid in the month. Test: a payroll name absent from the register appears in the discrepancy list. Basis: BC guide ("the cardex, the private-security system and the payroll must be exactly the same"); LFSP 13.
17. The system must record sanctions applied to a person and court proceedings that affect staff. Test: a court case entered on a guard appears in the Estado de México and Jalisco monthly outputs. Basis: LFSP 12-X; RLFSP 33-VIII; Edomex law 30; Jalisco Reglamento 41.

**C. Onboarding gate**

18. Onboarding must be a checklist with status and dates: record check (fee MXN 77.46), viability, documents (certified birth certificate, proof of address under 3 months old at filing, valid official ID, passing exam results), CUIP form, RNPSP inscription (MXN 260.11), ID card (MXN 73.51), and for armed guards the DGSP opinion and SEDENA licence steps. Test: a proof of address dated 100 days before the filing date is rejected. Basis: RLFSP 32-I to V; LFSP 32-XVI; LFD 195-X-III, V, VII.
19. The system must generate the record-check list (name, RFC, CURP per person) and a pre-filled CUIP form from the person record. Test: every CUIP-form field maps to a stored field or is flagged "manual". Basis: LFSP 25-XII; RLFSP 2-II, 32. (config)
20. A guard must not be assigned to a client post unless inscribed (or with inscription in process and a valid DGSP folio), with exams valid and training in the post's modality. An override requires a reason and is logged. Test: assigning a guard with expired exams is blocked. Basis: LFSP 28, 32-XXVI, 42-V-c; RLFSP 50.
21. The system must record a "double assignment" request and a "baja previa" requirement when the guard is active at another firm. Test: marking "active elsewhere" adds a task "obtain prior baja". Basis: SSPC-2019-268-008-A (Acuerdo 2025); [RACEK](https://www.racekcdmx.com/tramites-dgsp.html).
22. Every pending DGSP request must show its age in business days, with a warning at the 60-business-day legal maximum. Test: a record check filed 61 business days ago shows "overdue at DGSP". Basis: Acuerdo 2025 art. 2.

**D. Bajas**

23. A baja must capture date, reason code and text, a proof document (resignation, labour record or IMSS weeks report), produce the DGSP baja form, and open an ID-card return task due in 3 business days. Test: baja on Fri 5 Jun 2026 → return due Wed 10 Jun 2026. Basis: RLFSP 31, 32; DGSP FORMATO_BAJA (snippet). (config)
24. State modules must produce the state baja document (e.g. Baja California's "Cédula personal de baja", one per leaver) and state card returns (Jalisco: 3 calendar days). Test: three bajas in Baja California produce three cédulas. Basis: BC guide; Jalisco Reglamento 44.
25. If the ID card cannot be returned, the system must switch to the loss path: prosecutor's record, written DGSP report within 3 business days, replacement fee if reissued. Basis: RLFSP 30; LFSP 32-XXI.
26. On firm closure or loss of authorisation, the system must create a task to return all ID cards within 5 business days, with a full list. Basis: RLFSP 31.

**E. Exams and training**

27. Each exam record must hold type (medical, psychological, toxicological), institution, the institution's authorisation reference, date, result and certificate file. Test: an exam without a certificate file cannot be marked "pass". Basis: LFSP 32-VI; RLFSP 47, 49.
28. The next exam due date must be the last exam date plus 12 months, with alerts at 60, 30 and 7 days. On day 366 the guard is flagged "not fit to stay active". Test: exam on 1 Jan 2026 → flag on 2 Jan 2027. Basis: RLFSP 47, 50.
29. A failed exam must start a baja or reassignment-to-non-operational workflow. Test: a failed toxicology result removes the guard from all posts. Basis: RLFSP 50.
30. The monthly federal report must include all exams done in the month. The CDMX module must also produce exam results within 10 business days of each exam. Test: an exam on Tue 3 Mar 2026 appears in the March federal report and in a CDMX task due 18 Mar 2026 (16 Mar is a public holiday). Basis: RLFSP 48; CDMX law 35-XII.
31. Each training record must hold course, modality, topic tags (modality, human rights, use of force), trainer (internal with CV and certificates, or an external centre with DGSP authorisation and STPS registration), date, hours, participants and one DC-3 per participant. Basis: LFSP 29, 31; RLFSP 41, 42, 45; Acuerdo 2026 (DC-2, DC-3, DC-5).
32. Each operational guard must show at least one modality course and one human-rights course in the last 12 months, or be flagged. Test: a guard whose last human-rights course was 13 months ago is flagged. Basis: RLFSP 42; LFSP 32-III.
33. The system must store the training plan and programme, the STPS registration proof (DC-2) and the date a copy was given to the DGSP, and prompt an update at each revalidation. Basis: LFSP 19, 25-VIII, 31.

**F. Equipment**

34. Each uniform model must hold four colour full-body photos (front, back, both sides), proof of the sleeve and chest marks ("Seguridad Privada", logo, name), accessories, and its DGSP registration status. Test: a model with three photos cannot be exported. Basis: LFSP 25-XIV, 32-XIV; RLFSP 19-VII, 23-V; DGSP equipment guide (four images, snippet).
35. Uniform stock must be counted by model, with altas (purchase invoice) and bajas (cause), and show registered versus physical counts. Test: an invoice for 205 shirts creates a pending alta of 205 items. Basis: LFSP 12-XI; RLFSP 34-II; [SIDOF 5799650](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5799650); [SIDOF 5798715](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5798715).
36. Photos must be checked against DGSP size and resolution limits before export. Test: an image below the configured minimum resolution is rejected. Basis: DGSP equipment guide (snippet). (config)
37. Each vehicle must hold type, make, model, plates, VIN (17 characters, validated), armour level and armourer's certificate if armoured, photos of sides, front, back and roof, a livery check (name, logo, DGSP number, "Seguridad Privada") and its assignment. Test: an armoured vehicle without a certificate cannot be marked registered. Basis: LFSP 25-XVIII, XX, XXI, 32-XIII; RLFSP 19-IX, 20-III, 34-III.
38. Each firearm must hold serial number (matrícula), model, licence data, assigned guard and storage address. The system must check that every armed guard is named in the SEDENA licence and the DGSP opinion. Test: assigning an arm to a guard not in the licence is blocked. Basis: LFSP 24; RLFSP 34-I, 36, 38.
39. Each radio or electronic device must hold type, make, model and serial number, plus the frequency permit or carrier contract and its expiry. Basis: LFSP 25-XVII; RLFSP 34-IV.
40. Each dog must hold breed, age, colour, weight, size, name, training and health papers, and the trainer's certificate. Basis: LFSP 25-XVI, 32-XXX.
41. Equipment altas and bajas must export to the DGSP Excel layout "Solicitud_Alta_Baja_Equipo_DGSP" with the official request form. Test: one export file per filing, with one row per item. Basis: DGSP equipment guide (snippet); SSPC-2019-268-007-A. (config)
42. Equipment not registered with the DGSP must not be assignable to a guard or post without a logged override. Test: an unregistered radio cannot be issued. Basis: LFSP 32-IV, 42-V-h.

**G. Offices and corporate**

43. Each office (head office and branches) must hold address, proof of address, proof of legal possession if the proof is not in the firm's name, the person in charge (name, post), dated façade photos, and opening and closing dates. Basis: LFSP 12-IV, 25-V; RLFSP 19-III.
44. Any change of address, person in charge or façade must require new evidence and create a monthly-report line. Closing a branch must create a report line. Test: closing a branch on 12 Mar adds it to the March report. Basis: LFSP 32-V; [SIDOF 5797220](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5797220); [SIDOF 5799650](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5799650).
45. Each office must have a signage checklist with photo: sign of at least 1 m² with name, logo, authorisation number, address, opening hours and phones. Basis: RLFSP 23-VI.
46. The corporate record must hold partners and shareholdings, legal representatives and their powers, and statute changes. A change must open a "Cambios" filing (SSPC-2025-268-002-A, fee MXN 11,570.03) and a monthly-report line. Basis: LFSP 12-VI, VII, 32-XIX; RLFSP 32; Acuerdo 2025; LFD 195-X-VIII, IX.
47. The system must hold the training-centre and shooting-range addresses; a change opens a DGSP notice task. Basis: LFSP 32-XVIII.

**H. Federal monthly report (LFSP art. 13)**

48. The system must set the period (calendar month) and due date (10th calendar day of the next month) and show status: draft, signed, filed, receipt uploaded. Test: the March report is due 10 Apr. Basis: LFSP 13.
49. The report must list every event of the period under the twelve LFSP 12 headings, plus a status snapshot (active staff by role, equipment counts by type, offices). Test: one test event per heading lands in the matching section. Basis: LFSP 12, 13.
50. The report must include the month's exam results, training delivered, and the statement on criminal incidents. Test: a month with training and no incidents shows both sections, with "no incidents" stated. Basis: RLFSP 48, 23-VII, 23-XII; [SIDOF 5795399](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5795399); [ASF](https://www.asf.gob.mx/Trans/Informes/IR2020b/Documentos/Auditorias/2020_0095_a.pdf).
51. A report must be produced even with no movements, with a signed "no changes" statement. Test: an empty month still yields a signed PDF. Basis: LFSP 13 ("situación y actualizaciones").
52. Output must be a signed cover on letterhead, annexes in Excel and PDF, and an evidence folder of PDFs whose file names match an index file. Templates must be editable. Basis: RLFSP 33 (electronic or printed forms); DGSP CUIP-folder practice (snippet). (config)
53. A pre-export check must block or warn on: missing mandatory fields, altas without inscription, bajas without cause, equipment changes without registration, expired exams, and payroll mismatches. Test: a baja without cause blocks export. Basis: RLFSP 32, 33; LFSP 13; 2026 sanction findings.
54. Changes discovered after a period closed must be marked "late", keep their real event date and go into the next report. A dashboard must count late items. Test: a January baja entered on 15 Feb appears in February as "late, event 20 Jan". Basis: LFSP 13; [SIDOF 5799650](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5799650) (fixing after the visit did not avoid the sanction).
55. Each filed report must carry its stamped receipt (acuse) as a file. A month without an acuse must show "not proven filed". Test: removing the acuse turns the month red. Basis: LFSP 13; RLFSP 53 (evidence at head office).

**I. State modules**

56. The Baja California module must build the full pack: monthly altas report; one baja cédula per leaver; the month's last payroll (CFDI, lista de raya or SUA); the staff Cardex in Excel; the state fee receipt for each alta; the detailed activity report (clients with new and cancelled, modality, place, contract date and term, number and names of staff, arms, incidents); the arms summary in Excel if armed; and an optional clarification letter. It must draft an email to reportesdsp@seguridadbc.gob.mx with the firm name in the subject. Due on the 5th business day. Test: a month with no movements still produces every part. Basis: BC Reglamento 75-III, XI, XIV; BC guide. (config for the state Excel layouts)
57. The Cardex must equal the register and the payroll before Baja California export. The Cardex must never be exported as PDF. Test: one name spelled differently blocks export and shows the difference. Basis: BC guide.
58. The CDMX module must produce the monthly altas and bajas notice (5th business day), permit-file changes (15 business days), exam results (10 business days), and track the jefe de operaciones, the civil-liability insurance and revalidation (30 business days before expiry). Basis: CDMX law 21-22, 34, 35. (config)
59. The Estado de México module must produce the monthly report (5th business day) with altas, bajas with causes, court cases affecting staff, and changes to goods, services and equipment. Basis: Edomex law 30, 33. (config)
60. The Jalisco module must produce the monthly written notice of staff changes and incidents, return state cards within 3 calendar days, register vehicle and arms changes, schedule the annual vehicle review, and record the federal firm's registration with the Consejo Estatal. Basis: Jalisco Reglamento 41-44, 62-63, 69-70, 114. (config)
61. The Nuevo León module must produce the 10-calendar-day register report and the monthly activity report, and set revalidation 20 business days before expiry. Basis: NL law 15, 31-XXV. (config)
62. A single contracts and services register (client name and address, modality, site, contract date and term, staff assigned, arms, incidents) must feed the federal service register and every state activity report. Test: a new contract shows in the Baja California activity report and the federal service register. Basis: RLFSP 23-X, 53-IV; BC Reglamento 75-XI; NL law 31-XXV.

**J. Event notices and deadlines**

63. Theft, loss or destruction of company documents or staff IDs must open a written DGSP notice due in 3 business days, with proof attached. Basis: LFSP 32-XXI.
64. A suspension of activities must open a DGSP notice due in 3 business days with causes and expected restart (at most 6 months), a resumption notice, and a warning at 90 business days stopped. CDMX: notice in 3 business days, restart within 60 calendar days. Basis: LFSP 32-XXIII, 42-V-l; RLFSP 17; CDMX law 38.
65. A court or authority order freezing the firm's assets must open a notice due in 5 business days. Basis: LFSP 32-XXIV.
66. Alarm and monitoring firms must get notices for suspension, dissolution or court cases (5 business days) and for renewal of the PROFECO-filed contract. Basis: LFSP 48, 50.
67. An incident log must record events involving staff, the report to the authority, the notice to the DGSP, and feed the monthly incident statement. Basis: RLFSP 23-VII, 23-XII; LGSNSP 124.
68. Firms in the linked-activities modality must keep the buyer and user register (name, address, CURP or RFC, ID document data; supplier name and authorisation number) and file it every 6 months from the authorisation date. Test: authorisation dated Thu 15 Jan 2026 → the semester runs from Fri 16 Jan, so filings fall due about 16 Jul 2026 and 16 Jan 2027. Basis: LFSP 32-XXXII; RLFSP 35.

**K. Authorisation and revalidation**

69. Revalidation due date must be expiry minus 30 business days (federal), with state equivalents (CDMX 30 business days; Nuevo León 20 business days), and alerts from 90 days before. Test: expiry Tue 30 Jun 2026 → federal latest filing date Tue 19 May 2026 (30 business days, no holidays in between). Basis: LFSP 19; RLFSP 9, 15; CDMX law 22; NL law.
70. The revalidation pack must output the sworn "no change" statement or updated documents, and the Anexo III inventories: staff, uniforms, arms, vehicles (armoured or not), dogs, property, radio equipment, accessories, electronic devices; DC-2, DC-3, DC-5; and sworn statements on branches, staff, property, equipment, arms, vehicles and trainers. Basis: LFSP 19; Acuerdo 2026 art. 4, 6. (config)
71. The bond must be tracked: amount (5,000 times the reference value), insurer, policy number, required wording and validity, with an alert before expiry. Basis: LFSP 26-III.
72. After a first authorisation the system must track: fees and bond due within 10 business days of the approval notice; start of operations within 30 business days; staff and equipment proof within 30 business days if granted without them. Basis: LFSP 26; RLFSP 20, 23-XIII.

**L. Inspection readiness**

73. A one-click inspection pack for a chosen date must include: authorisation and state permits; the current staff list with CUIP, ID card, exam and training status; managers and branch heads; equipment inventory with registration proofs; the last 12 monthly reports with receipts; the service register and contracts; internal rules and manuals. Test: the pack builds for a 500-guard firm in under 60 seconds. Basis: LFSP 36-37; RLFSP 53.
74. A self-audit must score the firm on the nine-point checklist in this file and show each gap with its sanction level and UMA range. Test: an unregistered uniform model shows "amonestación up to revocation; observed practice 1,000 UMA". Basis: LFSP 42; RLFSP 60, 62.
75. A sanctions log must record each finding and sanction with its date and warn that any new breach within 6 months is reincidence. Test: a sanction on 1 Mar shows a reincidence window to 1 Sep. Basis: LFSP 40; RLFSP 62.

**M. Fees**

76. A fee table (LFD 195-X and 195-X-2, editable each year) must price every filing and link each fee receipt to its event. Test: inscribing 10 guards prices 10 × (77.46 + 260.11 + 73.51). Basis: LFD 195-X; LFSP 22.

**N. Audit trail and retention**

77. Every change must be logged (who, what, when) in an append-only log, with the event date stored apart from the entry date. Test: editing a baja date keeps the old value in history. Basis: RLFSP 23-IX; LFSP 42-V-b.
78. Each event must store its evidence files with a content hash; photos must be versioned, never overwritten. Basis: RLFSP 53.
79. Records must be kept for the life of the authorisation and at least 5 years after the person or item leaves (configurable), with a legal hold during any sanction procedure. Basis: RLFSP 23-X, 53-IV; LFT 804; CFF 30.

**O. Data protection and security**

80. The system must provide staff privacy-notice templates covering sensitive data (health, toxicology, psychology, criminal checks, physical marks) and record consent where the law requires it. Basis: LFPDPPP 2025 (detail of consent form unverified).
81. Exam results and criminal-check data must be visible only to authorised roles, and encrypted at field level. Test: a "site supervisor" role cannot open exam results. Basis: LFPDPPP 2025; LFSP 32-XXII; LGSNSP 101.
82. The vendor must act as data processor ("encargado") under a written agreement with each firm, list sub-processors and hosting locations, and keep each firm's data separate. Basis: LFPDPPP 2025 (unverified detail).
83. Baseline security: multi-factor login, encryption in transit and at rest, daily backups, a breach-response plan. Basis: LFPDPPP 2025 security duties (unverified detail); LFSP 32-XXII.

**P. Adjacent duties and client proof**

84. A REPSE tracker must hold the registration number and expiry, open the renewal window 3 months before, and remind ICSOE and SISUB filings in January, May and September. Basis: LFT art. 15 rules (secondary sources).
85. An arms-licence tracker must hold the SEDENA licence number, modality, named guards and expiry, with a revalidation request 60 days before expiry and a configurable 1- or 2-year cycle. Basis: LFSP 24; DEFENSA-02-026 (snippet); LFAFE reform 2025 (unverified).
86. The system must issue a one-page client certificate per site: authorisation valid, guards on the site inscribed, exams and training current. Test: a site with one expired exam cannot get a "compliant" certificate. Basis: CDMX law 37 Bis-II (client fines); bill of 8 Sep 2026 (public compliance history).

**Q. Language and format**

87. All screens and outputs must be in Mexican Spanish using the legal terms (alta, baja, cédula, prestador, prestatario, modalidad, acuse), dates as DD/MM/YYYY, amounts in MXN. Basis: official forms are Spanish only.
88. Every export must carry the firm's DGSP number, name, address and phone, as the law requires on all documentation. Basis: RLFSP 23-IV; LFSP 45.

## Open questions

1. **Federal monthly report: exact format and channel.** LFSP art. 13 sets the duty and the 10-day deadline, but I found no DGSP format or guide for it. Is it filed at the ventanilla única on paper with a CD or USB, by email, or as a set of separate alta and baja procedures? Fix: get a stamped acuse and the file set from 3 pilot firms; file a transparency request (PNT) to the SSPC asking for the format, the channel and the number of monthly reports received in 2025.
2. **DGSP forms.** The equipment Excel layout, the CUIP form, the baja form, the photo size and resolution limits, and the 2025 and 2026 Anexos were seen only in search snippets or as images. The DGSP site refused connections from here. Fix: download from a Mexican connection or get them from a gestoría.
3. **Will the DGSP launch an online channel by about April 2027?** The Feb 2026 Acuerdo gives it one year to adjust "registry systems". If a portal or API appears, the product becomes the data keeper and pre-filler. Watch the DOF and the Portal Ciudadano Único.
4. **New Secretariado Ejecutivo register guidelines** under LGSNSP art. 105 and 124 may change the RNPSP fields or add a direct data feed. Not yet found.
5. **State texts.** CDMX (copy read is 2014), Estado de México (snippets only), Jalisco (2004 Reglamento), Nuevo León (2026 reform not read), and 21 states not checked. Each state's current monthly format and channel must be collected before building its module.
6. **In-house guard teams.** Are companies that guard their own premises with their own staff covered by the federal or state registers? Not clear in the LFSP (unverified).
7. **2026 indexed fees.** The LFD amounts used here are from the compiled text (last reform Nov 2025). The 2026 Miscellaneous Tax Resolution may have updated them slightly (unverified).
8. **Bond amount basis.** LFSP art. 26-III says 5,000 times the CDMX minimum wage. Whether the DGSP now applies UMA or the minimum wage to the bond is unverified. The difference is large (UMA MXN 117.31 vs. the minimum wage), so the product should hold the amount as data.
9. **Arms-law reform (29 May 2025).** The 2-year collective-licence cycle comes from a blog only (unverified).
10. **Data-protection specifics.** The exact consent form for health data and the processor duties under the 2025 LFPDPPP and its regulations need a Mexican lawyer's check before launch.
11. **Retention end date.** The law ties retention to "the life of the authorisation", which renews every year. A lawyer should confirm the 5-years-after-exit default.
