Status: draft complete as of 2026-10-10. Open questions are listed at the end.

## Summary

- **The rule is new and already in force.**
  - Res. SBS 01015-2026 replaced the 2016 casino rule (Res. SBS 1695-2016).
  - It took effect on 9 Apr 2026, the day after publication. It gave **no adaptation period** (Art. Octavo; also [Santivañez](https://santivanez.com.pe/publicaciones/nueva-norma-sobre-prevencion-del-lavado-de-activos-y-del-financiamiento-del-terrorismo-para-casinos-y-tragamonedas/)).
  - Online operators have their own near-identical rule, Res. SBS 03622-2025. It took effect on 15 Oct 2025, with 120 days to implement, so it applied fully from 12 Feb 2026 (Arts. Cuarto and Noveno; [PRCP](https://prcp-r2-prd.postedin.com/o-apuestas-deportivas-a-distancia-bajo-supervisi%C3%B3n-del-Ministerio-De-Comercio-Exterior-Y-Turismo-3.pdf)).
- **Who must comply.** Every legal entity authorised by MINCETUR to run casino games or slot machines (Norma Art. 1.1). Also every Peruvian company or branch authorised to run remote games, remote sports betting or remote betting rooms (Online norma Art. 1).
  - In 2016, 333 firms owed the annual report ([MINCETUR 2017](https://consultasenlinea.mincetur.gob.pe/casinos/Agenda%20_Noticias/pdfs/2017/Presentacion_Charla_SPLAFT.pdf)).
  - In 2022 there were 714 slot rooms and 19 casinos with 72,823 machines ([Andina](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-casinos-y-tragamonedas-mincetur-realizo-mas-1300-visitas-fiscalizacion-908865.aspx)).
- **There is no small-firm exemption.** A few reliefs exist:
  - A full risk assessment is needed only by firms that run a casino room, run 500 or more slot machines, or run slot rooms in six named border regions (Norma Art. 4.1).
  - The general manager may be the part-time officer only if the firm is a MEPECO taxpayer, has 10 or fewer workers, is not in a group and does only gaming (Art. 19.4).
- **What must exist on paper or in software.**
  - Approved policies, a manual and a code of conduct (Art. 3.4, 16).
  - A registered officer (Art. 19-21).
  - Client, worker, director and supplier files on fixed data fields (Art. 10-12).
  - Training records, including a 30-day induction (Art. 6-7).
  - A daily RO "in IT systems", with a backup (Art. 14).
  - A register of unusual operations with a written analysis of each (Art. 15.2, 25(l)).
  - List checks (Art. 25(f)-(g)).
  - The IAOC and the IAI by 15 February each year (Art. 18, 27).
  - All of it kept for at least 5 years (Art. 28).
- **The RO grew.** It now covers every chip, ticket or other gaming-credit cash-out at the cage of **US$ 2,500 or more**. It also covers **every promotional prize winner, at any amount** (Art. 14.2-14.3). The officer sends the RO to UIF-Perú on an SBS template. The template sits behind Portal PLAFT, where only the registered officer can log in (Art. 14.6).
- **Suspicious reports got faster.** A ROS must reach UIF-Perú within **24 hours** of the operation being judged suspicious, through ROSEL (Art. 15.1, 15.3). The 2016 rule allowed 15 business days (Res. SBS 1695-2016 Art. 14.1, [PDF](http://consultasenlinea.mincetur.gob.pe/casinos/splaft/pdf/Nueva_Regulacion_SPLAFT_Resolucion_SBS1695_2016.pdf)).
  - Since 25 Apr 2026, officers must also report "alerts" through ROSEL for risk facts that are not suspicious ([El Peruano](https://elperuano.pe/noticia/294259-sbs-aprueba-guia-de-identificacion-y-reporte-de-alertas)).
- **Fines.** Each infraction is fined 1-3 UIT (minor), 4-7 UIT (serious) or 8 UIT (very serious). That is S/ 5,500 to S/ 44,000 per infraction. The very serious ones are a late ROS, failing to screen against UN lists, and failing to freeze or report frozen funds (RIS, as amended by 01015-2026).
- **Enforcement is real but light.**
  - Late filing is common. For 2016, 112 of 333 firms filed the IAOC late with UIF-Perú, and 52 did not file at all ([MINCETUR 2017](https://consultasenlinea.mincetur.gob.pe/casinos/Agenda%20_Noticias/pdfs/2017/Presentacion_Charla_SPLAFT.pdf)).
  - MINCETUR fined 10 firms in 2022 for not filing the IAOC ([Andina](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-casinos-y-tragamonedas-mincetur-realizo-mas-1300-visitas-fiscalizacion-908865.aspx)).
  - I found no public SPLAFT sanction data for 2023-2026 (unverified).
- **Three legal facts shape the business model.**
  1. **An officer can serve only one obliged firm at a time.** The only exception is a corporate officer for a group (Norma Art. 19.2; Online norma Art. 5.2). So a "one outsourced officer, many rooms" model is not lawful. The multi-client user is a consultant, trainer or adviser, not the officer.
  2. **Only the registered officer can open Portal PLAFT, ROSEL and SISDEL.** Every filing must use the officer's secret codes (Art. 14.6, 15.3, 26). So the product prepares files for upload. It cannot file for the customer.
  3. **MINCETUR and UIF-Perú use different IAOC formats.** Filing one does not excuse the other ([MINCETUR 2017](https://consultasenlinea.mincetur.gob.pe/casinos/Agenda%20_Noticias/pdfs/2017/Presentacion_Charla_SPLAFT.pdf)).
- **The portals leave most of the work undone.** They receive files. They do not keep the client files, the RO, training proof, supplier refresh cycles, the unusual-operation analyses or the IAOC statistics. The 86 requirements below cover that gap. Each one is traced to an article.

## Who is obliged

**Legal basis.**
- Ley 29038 Art. 3 lists the reporting entities. The SBS's 2026 list includes:
  - item 15: casinos, slot machines and remote games;
  - item 16: remote sports betting ([PRCP Lexmail](https://prcp-r2-prd.postedin.com/Lexmail-Aprueban-la-Gu%C3%ADa-de-identificaci%C3%B3n-y-reporte-de-alertas-aplicable-a-los-sujetos-obligados-a-informar-a-la-UIF-Per%C3%BA-2.pdf)).
- Ley 27693 Arts. 9-A and 10 give MINCETUR the power to supervise and sanction (RIS Art. 1, as amended by 01015-2026).
- Ley 31557 (2022) regulates remote gaming and lists AML prevention among its aims ([Ley 31557](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/DGP/DIDP/files/ley_31557.pdf), per search summary).

**Size and risk reliefs (the only ones):**
1. **Risk assessment** is required only if the land-based SO meets at least one of these (Norma Art. 4.1):
   - it runs a casino room;
   - it runs 500 or more slot machines across all its authorised rooms;
   - it runs slot rooms in Tacna, Puno, Ucayali, Loreto, Tumbes or Madre de Dios.
   - Online operators must always do it (Online norma Art. 28 has no such condition).
2. **The general manager as officer.** The GM may be a part-time officer only if all four of these hold (Art. 19.4):
   - the firm is MEPECO with SUNAT (medium and small taxpayers);
   - it has 10 or fewer workers;
   - it is not in a group;
   - it does no obliged activity other than casinos and slots.
   - If any condition is lost, the firm must report it within 5 business days and appoint a new officer.
3. **Internal audit.** A firm without an internal audit unit may have the IAI done by a manager other than the officer (Art. 18.1).
4. **External audit** of the SPLAFT applies only if the firm is legally required to have an external auditor (Art. 18.5; Reglamento UIF Art. 34.2). Which gaming firms meet that test is unverified.
5. **Case-by-case exemption.** UIF-Perú, after coordinating with MINCETUR, may exempt a firm from some requirements by letter (oficio). It considers risk, size, complexity and volume (Art. 3.6).
6. **Shared manual and code.** A firm that belongs to a registered trade association of obliged firms may use the association's manual and code. It stays responsible for approving and distributing them (Art. 16.8).

**Counts.**
- 333 SOs owed the 2016 IAOC ([MINCETUR 2017](https://consultasenlinea.mincetur.gob.pe/casinos/Agenda%20_Noticias/pdfs/2017/Presentacion_Charla_SPLAFT.pdf)).
- 714 slot rooms, 19 casino rooms and 72,823 machines in 2022 ([Andina](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-casinos-y-tragamonedas-mincetur-realizo-mas-1300-visitas-fiscalizacion-908865.aspx)).
- About 325 firms, per an undated MINCETUR slide deck for Congress. It is cited in the lead report, and I got a 404 when I tried to fetch it (unverified).

## Supervisors and enforcement evidence

**Who does what.**
- **MINCETUR's DGJCMT** is the AML supervisor and sanctioning body for land-based and online operators. Its powers cover Ley 27693, the Reglamento UIF and the Norma (Norma Art. 1.1, 17; RIS Art. 2).
  - Its Dirección de Control y Sanción investigates and proposes, and decides reconsideration appeals.
  - DGJCMT decides at first instance. The Vice-Ministry of Tourism decides at second and last instance (RIS Art. 11, 2026 text).
  - A case must rest on documents, inspection reports or information MINCETUR holds.
- **Supervision is risk-based.** MINCETUR may lean on the officer, internal audit and external auditors (Norma Art. 17.1).
- **UIF-Perú (part of SBS)** writes the rules. It receives the RO, the ROS, alerts and the IAOC. It assigns secret codes, may request information, may exempt firms (Art. 3.6), and may require a full-time officer (Art. 19.5).
- **MINCETUR authorises officers "in coordination with the UIF" and evaluates the IAOCs.** This is its stated role per a MINCETUR presentation to Congress ([Congreso PDF](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/comisiones2023/comercio/files/ppt_mincetur_congreso_-_ica_-_dgjcmt.pdf), per search summary; the file returned 404 to me).

**Enforcement evidence.**
- **2016 IAOC cycle** ([MINCETUR talk, 2017](https://consultasenlinea.mincetur.gob.pe/casinos/Agenda%20_Noticias/pdfs/2017/Presentacion_Charla_SPLAFT.pdf)):
  - 333 SOs.
  - MINCETUR received 314 on time, 6 late and 13 not filed.
  - UIF-Perú received 169 on time, 112 late and 52 not filed.
  - MINCETUR listed its main IAOC observations: wrong legal reference; missing SO address, phone or officer email; misreading the US$ 2,500 threshold as applying to ROS; detail on prize-register statistics; training topics not split by role; and missing due-diligence policies.
  - It warned that "the UIF and MINCETUR IAOC formats are different; presenting one does not exclude the other."
  - A chart shows **153 procedures started and 235 UIT in fines** under the SPLAFT rules. The period is not stated (unverified).
- **2022** ([Andina, 6 Sep 2022](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-casinos-y-tragamonedas-mincetur-realizo-mas-1300-visitas-fiscalizacion-908865.aspx)):
  - 1,346 inspection visits in Jan-Aug 2022.
  - 36 sanction procedures. 30 firms were fined 150 UIT (S/ 690,000), mostly for non-AML breaches.
  - Separately, **10 firms were sanctioned for not filing the IAOC**.
- **MINCETUR's own framing.** Its Oct 2021 deck lists "full implementation of the SPLAFT" among its sector goals. It says the RO and the prize-winner register "are sent to UIF-Perú and kept available to MINCETUR" ([MINCETUR 2021](https://consultasenlinea.mincetur.gob.pe/casinos/Agenda%20_Noticias/pdfs/2021/DGJCMT_Nov_2021.pdf)).
- **2023-2026.** I found no published SPLAFT sanction counts. MINCETUR's site has a "Control y Sanción" section and statistics pages, but I found no SPLAFT data there (unverified). Press in 2026 says the new rule means "less tolerance for compliance failures" ([El Peruano, Carlos Caro](https://elperuano.pe/noticia/293055-carlos-caro-nueva-norma-contra-lavado-de-activos-implica-menor-tolerancia-ante-fallas-de-cumplimiento)).

**What an inspector checks.** These are inferred from the IAI checklist in Art. 18.2 and the RIS items:
- the approved manual and code, with signed receipt statements;
- the officer's appointment and SISDEL proof;
- training certificates and induction records;
- client sworn statements matching RO rows;
- the RO itself, its backup and proof of sending;
- the unusual-operations register with written reasons;
- ROSEL receipts;
- dated list-screening logs;
- worker, director and supplier files with annual or 2-year refresh proof;
- the IAOC and IAI with both receipts;
- the risk report and methodology (if triggered).

## Regional differences

- **One national rule and one national supervisor.** The RIS applies "a nivel nacional" (RIS Arts. 1-2). DGJCMT supervises every region. I found no regional AML authority or regional variant.
- **Six border regions trigger the risk assessment.** Slot-room operators in **Tacna, Puno, Ucayali, Loreto, Tumbes or Madre de Dios** must do the full risk assessment even if small (Norma Art. 4.1.3). MINCETUR calls these "frontier departments" ([MINCETUR 2021](https://consultasenlinea.mincetur.gob.pe/casinos/Agenda%20_Noticias/pdfs/2021/DGJCMT_Nov_2021.pdf)). Elsewhere, a small slot-only operator with fewer than 500 machines does not need one.
- **Geography is a risk factor everywhere.**
  - The geographic-zone factor considers security, economic and socio-demographic traits and FATF statements (Art. 4.5(c)).
  - A report is needed before entering new zones (Art. 4.6).
  - The IAOC must list the location of every room (Art. 27.1.13).
- **Online operators** are national by nature. They must be Peruvian companies or registered branches (Online norma Art. 1).
- Municipal operating licences and gaming-law location rules are not AML duties. They are out of scope here.

## PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS

Each requirement is testable. "Basis" gives the legal source. Norma = Res. SBS 01015-2026, Art. Primero. ON = Online norma (Res. SBS 03622-2025). RIS = the sanctions annex. RUIF = DS 020-2017-JUS. Sources are linked in the sections above.

**A. Scope, firm profile and set-up**

1. The system must record the firm's segment (land-based, online, or both). It must apply the Norma or the ON rule set to match. Test: an online firm gets deposit, withdrawal and bet RO rules; a land firm gets cage and prize rules. Basis: Norma Art. 1; ON Art. 1, 22.
2. The system must record the number of slot machines, whether the firm runs a casino room, and the department of each room. It must set "risk assessment required" when any trigger is met: casino room, 500 or more machines in total, or a room in Tacna, Puno, Ucayali, Loreto, Tumbes or Madre de Dios. Test: a firm with 120 machines in Lima shows "not required"; adding one room in Tacna flips it to "required" and creates a task. Basis: Norma Art. 4.1.
3. For online firms, "risk assessment required" must always be on. Basis: ON Art. 28.
4. The system must check the GM-as-officer conditions (MEPECO status, 10 or fewer workers, no group, gaming only). It must warn when any changes, and start a 5-business-day countdown to notify DGJCMT and SISDEL. Test: raising headcount to 11 creates a red task due in 5 business days. Basis: Norma Art. 19.4.
5. The system must record UIF case-by-case exemptions (letter number, date, scope). It must switch off only the exempted duties, and show the exemption on every affected report. Basis: Norma Art. 3.6.
6. The system must hold a worker category list. It must exclude roles flagged as cleaning, security, gardening, courier or similar from training, manual-receipt and worker-file duties. Test: a "vigilante" gets no training task. Basis: Norma Art. 3.3.
7. The system must hold group data (companies, RUCs, ownership, corporate officer authorisation). It must run one SPLAFT workspace per obliged firm, with group-level shared documents. Basis: Norma Art. 5, 23, 27.4.
8. All user-facing documents and forms must be in Spanish. Article references must use the Norma's numbering. Basis: Norma (Spanish official text).
9. The calendar must use Peruvian business days (national holidays) for every "días hábiles" deadline, and calendar days where the Norma says "días" (Art. 2 defines "Días" as calendar days). Test: a 15-business-day appointment deadline skips weekends and 28-29 July; a 30-day induction does not. Basis: Norma Art. 2, 21.2.

**B. Risk assessment**

10. The risk module must segment clients on at least the three factors: clients (nationality, residence, occupation, age), products and services (channels and payment methods), and geographic zone. Test: the report cannot be finalised with a factor missing. Basis: Norma Art. 4.2.1, 4.5.
11. It must pull, per room and per month, the number and average amount of cage operations, the number of regular clients, and the currencies used, from the RO and cage imports. Basis: Norma Art. 4.2.2.
12. It must rate probability and impact, record controls (enhanced or simplified) and record monitoring results. It must store the **methodology document** with the report. Test: the export holds both. Basis: Norma Art. 4.2.3-4.2.5, 4.3.
13. It must cite the National Risk Assessment as an input field (version and date). Basis: Norma Art. 4.1.
14. It must set the next review at most 3 years after approval. It must re-open the assessment when a "new technology" or "service change" event is logged. Basis: Norma Art. 4.2.3, 4.4.
15. It must offer a "new geographic zone" report template, signed by the officer, before a room in a new department is activated. Basis: Norma Art. 4.6.

**C. Client due diligence (KYC) at the cage and for prize winners**

16. The client form must capture fields (a)-(l) of Art. 10.1 exactly, including spouse, purpose, PEP status, PEP-relative status and the operation beneficiary with origin of funds. Test: the form cannot be saved with a required field empty, and the "on behalf of a third party" branch requires that person's ID and PEP status. Basis: Norma Art. 10.1.
17. The client sworn statement must render in the **SBS official format** and allow on-screen or tablet signature with a timestamp. The SBS form version must be stored with each signature. Basis: Norma Art. 10.3.
18. The ID document type list must use SBS codes (at least DNI, carné de extranjería, passport, others). Foreign IDs must require nationality. Basis: Norma Art. 2, 10.1; 2016 Annex Table 1.
19. If the client refuses or data cannot be verified, the cashier flow must block the operation's completion record. It must open an "evaluate ROS" task for the officer. Test: a refused statement shows "operation not to be executed" and a task appears. Basis: Norma Art. 9.3.
20. The system must auto-route a client to **enhanced due diligence** if non-resident, PEP, PEP relative or spouse, an associate of a PEP-owned company, or flagged as investigated or linked. Basis: Norma Art. 10.2.1.
21. EDD must require: the PEP's relatives and companies (when PEP), a higher review frequency, extra enquiries, and the approval of the top manager or a delegate or committee, recorded with name and date. Test: an EDD client cannot be "accepted" without an approver who holds the senior-manager role. Basis: Norma Art. 10.2.2.
22. For a legal-person beneficiary, the form must require name, RUC or foreign equivalent, origin of funds and beneficial owner. Basis: Norma Art. 8.2, 10.1(l)(iii).
23. PEP look-back must be configurable. The default must be **5 years** (the stricter general SBS rule), with a note that the Norma says 2 years. Basis: Norma Art. 2; Res. SBS 4349-2016 as amended by 00199-2025.
24. The system must hold the SBS list of PEP functions and positions as a picklist, and log which list version was used. Basis: Res. SBS 4349-2016 and 00199-2025.
25. The client record must keep the full history of sworn statements and changes. It must show "last verified" dates for the monitoring stage. Basis: Norma Art. 9.2.3.
26. If due diligence would tip off a suspected client, the officer must be able to file a "ROS without due diligence" record with mandatory written grounds. Basis: Norma Art. 9.4.

**D. Operations register (RO)**

27. The system must create an RO row for every cage cash-out of chips, tickets or other gaming credit of **US$ 2,500 or more**, whatever the funds. Test: a S/ 9,000 cash-out is logged when the previous month's average rate makes it ≥ US$ 2,500, and is not logged otherwise. Basis: Norma Art. 14.2(1), 14.3(1).
28. The system must create an RO row for **every promotional prize winner**, with no minimum. Test: a S/ 50 raffle prize is logged. Basis: Norma Art. 14.2(2), 14.3(2).
29. Currency conversion must use the **average of SBS daily selling rates for the month before the operation**. The rate table must be stored with its source and date. Test: rates for March drive April thresholds. Basis: Norma Art. 14.3.
30. The firm must be able to set a lower internal threshold, with a stored justification. The RO must then include those rows. Basis: Norma Art. 14.3 (last para).
31. Cash-out rows must hold: names, ID type and number, age, nationality, address, amount, payment method, the client's own bank account number (if any), currency, FX rate, place (room), origin of funds. Prize rows must hold: promotion type, date, time, place, prize description, value and currency. Test: the export fails validation if a mandatory field is blank. Basis: Norma Art. 14.7.
32. RO rows must be entered on the operation day. Late entries must be flagged and logged with the entry time. The register must be strictly chronological and immutable: edits are corrections with an audit trail, never deletions. Basis: Norma Art. 14.4.
33. The system must make it impossible to exclude a client from the RO (no "regular client" exemption switch). Test: there is no setting to suppress rows by client. Basis: Norma Art. 14.8; Code infraction (viii) in Art. 16.4.
34. The system must keep an independent backup copy of the RO. It must be restorable and producible on demand for MINCETUR and UIF. Test: a restore drill produces an identical hash. Basis: Norma Art. 14.4; RIS grave 13.
35. The system must import cage and cash-desk exports and promotion results (CSV or Excel) and suggest RO rows. A human must confirm each suggested row. Basis: Norma Art. 14.4 (complete register).
36. The system must export the RO in the **SBS Portal PLAFT template structure**. The field mapping must be configurable, because the template may change by SBS resolution. Test: a mapping change does not need a code release. Basis: Norma Art. 14.6.
37. The system must track RO sending: period, date sent and the receipt file uploaded by the officer. It must alert before the due date once the frequency is configured. Basis: Norma Art. 14.6, 25(n); RIS grave 15.
38. For online firms, the system must log individual deposits, withdrawals and bets of US$ 2,500 or more, and wins with a prize of US$ 2,500 or more. Bet rows are dated at the bet date and win rows at the win date. The online fields must be captured. Basis: ON Art. 22.2-22.7.
39. A firm that also does another obliged activity must keep a separate RO per activity, except casino plus slots, which share one. Basis: Norma Art. 14.5.

**E. Unusual operations, ROS and alerts**

40. Any user must be able to escalate an "unusual operation" to the officer from a client or RO row. The escalation must be logged and visible to the officer only. Basis: Norma Art. 3.4.8, 16.4(x).
41. The system must ship the SBS gaming alert signals as a library. Examples: small notes into chips, winnings to cheques or transfers, repeated maximum bets, a winner linked to the operator, a request for a winnings certificate. The firm can add its own, each with a source tag (Portal PLAFT or own). Basis: Norma Art. 15.2.3, 16.2; 2016 Annex 4.
42. Rule-based detection must flag at least: repeated cash-outs just under the threshold; the same person winning many raffles; the same address or account used by unrelated clients; worker-client links. Thresholds must be configurable. Basis: Norma Art. 4.2.5, 25(c).
43. Each unusual-operation file must hold fields (i)-(xi) of 15.2.1, the person data of 15.2.2 including role (player, winner) and the alert data of 15.2.3. Test: closing the file without a reason for "not suspicious" is blocked. Basis: Norma Art. 15.2; RIS graves 17-18.
44. The system must keep a register of unusual operations not reported as suspicious, with reasons. Basis: Norma Art. 25(l).
45. When the officer marks an operation "suspicious", a **24-hour countdown** must start, with escalating reminders. Test: a timer shows hours left and alerts at 12 h and 2 h. Basis: Norma Art. 15.1; RIS muy grave 2.
46. The system must prepare a ROS draft in the **ROSEL template** structure, plus an attachment bundle. The draft must contain no name or identifier of the SO or the officer, only the secret-code placeholders, which the officer fills in. Test: an automated check blocks export if the firm's RUC or name appears in the narrative. Basis: Norma Art. 15.3.
47. The officer must be able to record the ROSEL receipt (number and date) against the case. The case then shows "filed" and the time it took. Basis: Norma Art. 15.2.1(x), 15.4.
48. The system must support "alert" drafts (adverse media, investigation, PEP list match) in the ROSEL alert structure, and link them to later ROS. It must block "alert" when the match is a UN Security Council list, and route that case to freezing instead. Basis: Res. SBS 01219-2026 (guide).

**F. List screening and freezing**

49. The system must screen clients, beneficiaries, workers, directors and suppliers against the UN Security Council consolidated list (1267, 1718, 1737, 2231 regimes) at onboarding and whenever the list changes. Every check must be stored with list version, time and result. Basis: Norma Art. 11.4, 12.2, 25(f); RIS muy grave 3.
50. It must also screen against OFAC SDN, the EU terrorist list and the other lists in Art. 25(g). It must tag clients from FATF high-risk or non-cooperative jurisdictions. Basis: Norma Art. 25(g)-(h).
51. It must re-screen the whole stored population (clients, workers, suppliers) and RO history when a list updates. Test: adding a test name to the list flags past RO rows within one day. Basis: Norma Art. 25(f) ("al inicio y durante").
52. A confirmed UN match must open a **freeze case**. It needs an immediate "notify UIF without delay" task, the freeze steps and the evidence. A reply of "no funds held" must also be logged and tracked. Basis: Norma Art. 25(f), 25(i)-(j); RIS muy graves 4-6.
53. When FATF updates its high-risk list, the system must send a staff notice and record who read it. Basis: Norma Art. 25(h).

**G. Workers, directors and induction**

54. The worker and director file must capture fields (a)-(j) of Art. 11.2, including an ID copy, police and criminal records, the 2-year work history and assets. Test: a file without an ID copy shows "incomplete". Basis: Norma Art. 11.2; RIS leve 6.
55. The system must screen each worker and director at hiring and on every list update. Basis: Norma Art. 11.4.1.
56. The system must create a yearly "update check" per person and record whether any change was found. Workers must be able to submit changes, with a 15-day duty reminder. Basis: Norma Art. 11.3, 11.4.2.
57. A new hire or director must trigger an **induction task due in 30 calendar days**, closed only by an uploaded or e-signed record. Test: day 31 without a record shows overdue. Basis: Norma Art. 6.4; RIS leves 1 and 5.
58. The system must hold a disciplinary-measures register for SPLAFT breaches by staff. Basis: Norma Art. 11.4.3, 27.1.11.

**H. Training**

59. The system must plan at least one AML training per person per calendar year, by role. It must show who is missing it before 31 December. Basis: Norma Art. 6.1; RIS leve 3.
60. Each session must store date, place, duration, modality, participant names and positions, syllabus and trainer. It must map the syllabus to the 11 minimum topics a)-k) of Art. 7 and warn if any is missing. Basis: Norma Art. 6.3, 7; RIS leve 4.
61. When the officer is the trainer, the system must produce the officer's sworn certificate. It must block the officer from certifying their own attendance. Basis: Norma Art. 6.3.
62. External trainer certificates must be uploadable and attached to each attendee's file. Basis: Norma Art. 6.2; RIS leve 2.
63. The system must compute the IAOC training statistics: worker count, number of sessions, number of people trained, number trained more than once, and topics by profile. Basis: Norma Art. 27.1.10.

**I. Suppliers**

64. The system must keep a supplier register limited to suppliers directly related to the gaming activity, with fields 1-11 of Art. 12.1 and the right sworn statement (natural or legal person). Basis: Norma Art. 12.1.
65. It must screen suppliers at selection and afterwards, and set a refresh due date **no later than 2 years**. A refresh with no change must record a "no change" note. Basis: Norma Art. 12.2.1-12.2.2.
66. It must record, per supplier, whether AML and confidentiality clauses were included or why not. It must hold supplier alert signals. Basis: Norma Art. 12.2.3-12.2.4.
67. When a third party does identification or verification, the system must require the third party's sworn statement and copies of the data. Basis: Norma Art. 13.

**J. Manual, code and acknowledgements**

68. The system must provide manual and code templates for gaming that cover every element of Art. 16.2 and 16.3. The code must contain the 11 minimum infractions. Versions must be numbered, with approval body, date and minutes. Basis: Norma Art. 16.1-16.4; RIS-2016 graves 22-28.
69. Approving a new manual or code version must trigger receipt statements to every in-scope person, **due in 30 days**. Each statement must hold the 10 fields of Art. 16.6. Test: the statement shows district, province, department, and day, month and year. Basis: Norma Art. 16.5-16.6.
70. Receipt statements must be kept at least 5 years and be exportable per person. Basis: Norma Art. 16.7.
71. The system must let a firm import an association-level manual and code and still record its own approval and distribution. Basis: Norma Art. 16.8.

**K. Compliance officer and portals**

72. The system must generate the officer appointment pack: a request with items a)-l) of Art. 21.3, the sworn statements on requirements a)-h) of Art. 20.1, and the appointing minutes. It must start a **15-business-day** deadline for SISDEL and DGJCMT notice. Basis: Norma Art. 20-21; RIS grave 29.
73. The system must track officer events: data changes, loss of a requirement, removal and vacancy (5 business days each); vacancy maximum 30 days; alternate activation at least 5 business days before the absence; absence maximum 4 months. Test: logging an absence 3 business days ahead shows "late, needs force-majeure grounds". Basis: Norma Art. 20.2, 21.4-21.6, 22.2.
74. The system must keep the officer's identity out of every document meant for third parties. It must restrict officer identity data to admin roles. It must **not** store the UIF secret codes unless the customer opts in, and then only encrypted, visible to the officer alone. Basis: Norma Art. 26; RIS grave 35.
75. The system must allow only one obliged firm per "officer" user role, except a corporate officer across a group. Consultant users must get a separate "adviser" role that cannot sign as officer. Basis: Norma Art. 19.2.

**L. IAOC, IAI and the annual calendar**

76. The system must pre-fill an IAOC draft with all 13 items of Art. 27.1. That includes **monthly** RO counts and amounts, monthly unusual-operation and ROS counts and amounts, shareholders and managers with ID and %, and the address of every room. It must output **two versions**: the UIF structure (for Portal PLAFT) and the MINCETUR format. Test: the monthly totals equal the RO and case registers for the year. Basis: Norma Art. 27.1, 27.3; MINCETUR 2017 note on separate formats; RIS graves 38-40.
77. The annual calendar must show these dates: 31 Dec year end; **30 Jan** for IAOC approval and IAI presentation to the board or GM (30 days after year end); **15 Feb** for sending the IAOC and IAI to both bodies. Approvals must be recorded (body, date, minutes) and both receipts uploaded. Test: on 1 Feb an unapproved IAOC is red. Basis: Norma Art. 18.3-18.4, 27.2-27.3.
78. The system must provide an IAI checklist with the 10 points of Art. 18.2. The IAI author cannot be the officer, which the system enforces by user. Basis: Norma Art. 18.1-18.2; RIS grave 9.
79. The system must hold a remediation log for findings from MINCETUR, UIF, internal audit and external audit, with owner, due date and status. It feeds IAOC item 12. A "voluntary correction" record must capture a written admission and the full fix before any charge notice. Basis: Norma Art. 27.1.12; RIS graves 3 and 11; RIS Art. 8-A(f).

**M. Records, requests, security and data protection**

80. All SPLAFT records must be kept **at least 5 years** from the operation or the end of the relationship, with deletion blocked before then. Retention must be configurable upward (for example to 10 years, the Reglamento UIF default). Basis: Norma Art. 28; RUIF Art. 24.2-24.3; RIS grave 37.
81. The system must have a register of information requests (requester, date, deadline, reply, attachments). Replies to UIF must be prepared without any SO or officer identifiers except code placeholders. Basis: Norma Art. 29.
82. The system must produce an "inspection pack" for a date range: manual and code with receipts, officer documents, training, client files linked to RO rows, the RO and its backup proof, the unusual and ROS registers (ROS content excluded for internal audit), screening logs, worker and supplier files, IAOC and IAI with receipts, and the risk report and methodology. Basis: Norma Art. 17.1, 18.2, 28.
83. Access must be role-based (cashier, room manager, officer, auditor, admin, adviser). ROS and alert content must be visible to the officer only; internal audit can see that a ROS was filed on time but not its content. Basis: Norma Art. 18.2.9, 15.3; Ley 27693 duty of reserve.
84. Every read and write of ROS, RO and client data must be logged in a tamper-evident audit log. Basis: Norma Art. 3.2 (indefinite duty of reserve), 14.1 (RO confidential).
85. Hosting and data handling must meet Peru's data-protection regulation. That means: a documented cross-border transfer basis if data are stored outside Peru; breach notice to the authority and to data subjects within 48 hours; a Peru representative if the vendor is foreign. Basis: Ley 29733; DS 016-2024-JUS (per [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/se-publica-el-nuevo-reglamento-de-protecci-n-de-datos-personales-en-per-)).
86. When a firm loses obliged status, the system must produce a full export of retained records for hand-over and log the 30-day deadlines. Basis: Norma Art. 1.2.

## Open questions

1. **RO sending frequency and file format.** The Norma leaves both to an SBS resolution and the Portal PLAFT template (Art. 14.6). I could not see the template or find the resolution. A registered officer must download it.
2. **MINCETUR's IAOC channel and format in 2026.** The Norma says "the means it determines". In 2017 the formats differed from UIF's. The current form and portal are unverified.
3. **PEP look-back.** The Norma says 2 years and the general SBS PEP rule (amended 2025) says 5 years. Which one MINCETUR applies is unverified.
4. **Supplier due diligence fines.** There is no specific land-based infraction. It would likely be "breach of the manual" (5 UIT), which is unverified.
5. **Risk update cycle.** RIS-2016 grave 7 still says "every two years", while Norma Art. 4.4 says 3 years. Which one MINCETUR enforces is unverified.
6. **External audit trigger.** Which gaming firms are "legally required" to have an external auditor is unverified.
7. **Enforcement 2023-2026.** No public SPLAFT sanction counts were found. The period of the 153 procedures and 235 UIT in fines in the 2017 deck is unclear.
8. **Number of obliged firms in 2026.** I have only 333 (2016) and about 325 (undated, unverified).
9. **Vendor role.** If the vendor runs list screening, does it become a "third party" under Art. 13 that must sign a sworn statement? This is unverified; the safe answer is yes.
10. **Data from SUCTR or casino-management systems.** Can cage cash-out data be exported from the systems small rooms use? This is unverified. It drives how much RO typing the product can save.
11. **Alert guide details.** The full guide is only on Portal PLAFT. There is no deadline and no fine for alerts in the sources I read.
12. **Data residency.** Peru's rules allow cross-border transfers subject to conditions. Whether gaming customers or MINCETUR expect data to stay in Peru is unverified.
