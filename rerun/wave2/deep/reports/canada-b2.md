# Canada B2: FINTRAC compliance-programme kit for factors, financing/leasing entities and cheque cashers

## Re-assessment (owner's criteria)

**Verdict: maybe. New score: 5/10 (old score: 4/10).**

**The case.** The duty is real and enforcement is now live. These businesses have had to run a full compliance programme since 1 April 2025 ([FINTRAC lease page](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/re-ed/lease-bail-eng)), and UCDA expects enforcement to "ramp up" from 1 April 2026 ([UCDA](https://www.ucda.org/fintrac/)). FINTRAC's filing system only takes reports. The work FINTRAC actually finds missing (risk ratings tied to controls, tested and tracked training, audit trails, documented reviews) is record-keeping work that software does well ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/fintrac-key-compliance-takeaways)). Nobody sells a tool that runs the whole programme for lessors, factors or in-house-financing dealers. Real estate shows the model sells: ReallyTrusted serves 900+ brokerages ([ReallyTrusted](https://reallytrusted.com)). The weak point is still market size. Only 865 businesses are officially counted, and the number of in-house-financing dealers is unknown. This makes it a solid small business at best, not a large one.

**Room for improvement over the portal or current practice.**
- **The portal covers filing only.** FINTRAC's lease page lists a compliance programme, client ID and due diligence, PEP checks, beneficial ownership, the 24-hour rule and record keeping alongside reporting ([FINTRAC](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/re-ed/lease-bail-eng)). The Web Reporting System handles only the reports ([FINTRAC system overview](https://fintrac-canafe.gc.ca/reporting-declaration/guide/sys-eng)). Everything else is left to the business.
- **FINTRAC names the gaps.** At its 29 June 2026 Industry Day it said that:
  - training is often "overly general, outdated, or insufficiently tailored", with weak tracking and testing
  - risk ratings are not clearly linked to controls
  - ongoing-monitoring records rely on templates and have weak audit trails
  - STR escalation is unclear and investigations are poor
  - source: [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/fintrac-key-compliance-takeaways)
  - A workflow tool fixes each of these.
- **Today's practice is Word templates and consultants.** UCDA gives dealers a policy template and a guidance document. One-on-one KPMG training "does cost some money" ([UCDA](https://www.ucda.org/fintrac/)). A template does not log training, link risks to controls, or keep KYC and STR decision records.
- **The workload is measurable.** The government assumes 48 hours a year to maintain the programme (range 24–96 hours) and 16 hours a year to prepare for a FINTRAC exam for a small firm ([Canada Gazette RIAS](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors67-eng.html)).
- **The portal is fragile.** After a March 2024 cyber incident, reporting systems were down for months, and FINTRAC set 31 March 2025 as the deadline for backlogged reports ([Better Dwelling](https://betterdwelling.com/canadian-anti-money-laundering-reporting-systems-still-down-post-hack/); [Canadian Jewellers Association](https://canadianjewellers.com/?p=22496)). Correcting batch reports has strict rules ([FINTRAC batch Q&A](https://canafe-fintrac.gc.ca/reporting-declaration/batch-lots/batch-qa-qr-eng)). These entities file few reports, so this is minor pain. A local log of what was filed, and why, still adds value.
- **Multi-client angle.** Dealer accountants and consultants (MNP, KRP, Doane Grant Thornton) sell programme builds and effectiveness reviews to many dealers ([KRP](https://www.krpgroup.com/article/how-can-your-dealership-comply-with-fintracs-anti-money-laundering-requirements); [Doane GT](https://www.doanegrantthornton.ca/insights/is-your-dealership-prepared-for-fintracs-new-anti-money-laundering-requirements/)). A per-consultant, multi-client licence could suit them (unverified demand).
- **Language.** Quebec dealers and lessors need French ([MNP, French](https://www.mnp.ca/fr/points-de-vue/bibliotheque/que-doit-faire-concessionnaire-conformer-regles-canafe-contre-blanchiment-dargent)). No French sector tool was found.

## Summary

**Verdict: maybe. Score: 4/10.**

The duty is real, in force and getting harder. Factors, cheque cashers and financing or leasing entities have been FINTRAC reporting entities since 1 April 2025 ([Canada Gazette, SOR/2025-68](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors68-eng.html)). The soft "outreach" year ended around 1 April 2026, and Bill C-12 raised penalty ceilings about 40-fold ([Torys](https://www.torys.com/en/our-latest-thinking/torys-quarterly/q1-2026/fintac-amps); [McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/canada-s-aml-reform-advances-bill-c-12-brings-hefty-penalties-and-higher-compliance-expectations-into-force)). The problem is size. The government's own impact statement counts only 865 newly covered businesses: 65 factors, 600 cheque cashers and 200 financing and leasing companies. It estimates their compliance cost at about $2.8k a year per small business ([Canada Gazette RIAS, SOR/2025-67 page](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors67-eng.html)). The only way to a bigger market is car dealers who finance or lease in their own name, but how many there are is unknown. Industry associations (UCDA, CFLA), dealer platforms (Dealertrack, AutoCorp, Paays) and consultants (KPMG, MNP, Substance Law) already cover parts of the job. A cheap "programme in a box" for small lessors and in-house-financing dealers is a plausible niche, but probably a side business rather than a company.

## Duty

**Legal basis.**
- The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), paragraph 5(j), as extended by SOR/2025-68. SOR/2025-68 was registered on 4 March 2025 and published in Canada Gazette Part II on 26 March 2025 ([Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors68-eng.html)).
- The general coming-into-force date is 1 April 2025. Discrepancy reporting and some related items started on 1 October 2025 ([Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors68-eng.html)). The start date had originally been planned for 1 October 2025 and was brought forward ([Canada Gazette RIAS](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors67-eng.html)).
- **Date conflict resolved.** FINTRAC's "Who must report" page marks these sectors "new as of July 10, 2025" ([FINTRAC](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/reporting-declaration/info/re-ed-eng)). FINTRAC's own sector page gives 1 April 2025 as the effective date and was last modified on 10 July 2025 ([FINTRAC lease page](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/re-ed/lease-bail-eng)). So 10 July 2025 is the date the guidance was published. The obligations themselves started on 1 April 2025 (my inference from these two pages).

**Who is covered.**
- **Financing or leasing entity:** "a person or entity that is engaged in the business of financing or leasing". It is covered when it finances or leases (a) non-real property for business purposes, (b) passenger vehicles in Canada, or (c) non-real property worth $100,000 or more ([Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors68-eng.html); [FINTRAC](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/re-ed/lease-bail-eng)).
- CFLA says the rule covers financing and leasing done "either directly or indirectly", and that the $100,000 limit applies only to consumer goods ([CFLA notice PDF](https://cfla-acfl.ca/files/Industry-Intelligence/AML%20Changes%20to%20Leasing%20&%20Finance%20Industry%202025%20FINAL.pdf)).
- **Cheque cashers** are now regulated as money services businesses (MSBs). They must register with FINTRAC and renew every two years ([FINTRAC, French "Qui doit présenter des déclarations"](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/reporting-declaration/info/re-ed-fra); [Canada Gazette RIAS](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors67-eng.html)).
- **Factors** carry the full set of obligations, with carve-outs for amounts received from financial entities or public bodies ([Canada Gazette](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors68-eng.html)).

**Car dealers: the scope is unclear.**
- Car dealers asked for a $250,000 threshold, for vehicle financing to be exempted, and for an exemption from penalties. The government rejected all three because it rates vehicle financing as higher risk ([Canada Gazette RIAS](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors67-eng.html)).
- UCDA (Ontario used-car dealers) says dealers that finance or lease "in their own name" are likely reporting entities. It says dealers that only arrange third-party financing "may not be subject in the same way" ([UCDA FINTRAC hub](https://www.ucda.org/fintrac/)).
- Another UCDA newsletter says both in-house and third-party arrangements are captured ([UCDA Frontline](https://frontline.ucda.org/?p=1236)).
- Cassels reports that FINTRAC advised that dealerships partnered with a financial institution count as independent reporting entities ([Cassels](https://cassels.com/insights/aml-your-independent-finance-company-may-no-longer-be-exempt/)). FINTRAC itself has not published this (unverified).

**What must be done** ([FINTRAC lease page](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/re-ed/lease-bail-eng); [FINTRAC compliance program guide](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/guidance-directives/compliance-conformite/guide4/4-eng)):
- **Compliance programme:**
  - a compliance officer
  - written policies and procedures
  - a documented risk assessment
  - a written, ongoing training programme
  - an effectiveness review at least every two years
- **Know your client:**
  - identity verification
  - business-relationship records and ongoing monitoring
  - beneficial-ownership information
  - third-party determination
  - politically exposed person (PEP) determination
- **Reports:**
  - suspicious transaction reports, with no threshold
  - listed person or entity property reports
  - large cash transaction reports for $10,000 or more, under the 24-hour rule
  - large virtual currency transaction reports
  - beneficial-ownership discrepancy reports, from 1 October 2025 ([FINTRAC changes page, French](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/businesses-entreprises/changes-changements-fra))
- **Record keeping:** transaction and client-identification records.

**Penalties and enforcement.**
- **Criminal penalties**, as summarised by UCDA ([UCDA Frontline](https://frontline.ucda.org/?p=1236)):
  - Failing to file a suspicious transaction report: up to $2M and/or five years in prison.
  - Record-keeping failures: up to $500,000 and/or five years.
  - Large cash or EFT reporting failures: up to $500,000 for a first offence and up to $1M after that.
- **Administrative monetary penalties (AMPs):** Bill C-12 received Royal Assent on 26 March 2026. It raised maximums about 40-fold: minor violations up to $40,000, serious up to $4M, very serious up to $20M ([Torys](https://www.torys.com/en/our-latest-thinking/torys-quarterly/q1-2026/fintac-amps); [McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/canada-s-aml-reform-advances-bill-c-12-brings-hefty-penalties-and-higher-compliance-expectations-into-force); [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/canada-s-new-anti-money-laundering-legislation-significantly-raises-penalties-and-adds-new-violation/)). C-12 also requires programmes to be "reasonably designed, risk-based and effective", and it created mandatory compliance agreements ([McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/canada-s-aml-reform-advances-bill-c-12-brings-hefty-penalties-and-higher-compliance-expectations-into-force)).
- **Enforcement is active.** FINTRAC issued 30 AMPs in 2025, a record ([Torys](https://www.torys.com/en/our-latest-thinking/torys-quarterly/q1-2026/fintac-amps)). Small MSBs have been fined for missing written policies and risk assessments: Juba Express ($67,150) and Commerciale I.C.–Pacific ($224,235) ([FINTRAC notice, 5 Feb 2026](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/new-neuf/nr/2026-02-05-eng)). Juba Express is from search-result snippets (unverified detail).
- **No penalty against a lessor, factor or dealer found yet.** I found no public AMP against one (unverified; the penalty list was not filtered by sector).
- **The grace period has ended.** FINTRAC promised a first year of "engagement, outreach and guidance" ([Canada Gazette RIAS](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors67-eng.html)). UCDA says enforcement is "expected to ramp up" from 1 April 2026 ([UCDA](https://www.ucda.org/fintrac/)).
- **FINTRAC is engaging dealers directly.** It ran a dealer Industry Day on 29 June 2026 ([UCDA event page](https://www.ucda.org/fintrac-webinar-for-ontario-auto-dealers-common-compliance-deficiencies/)). The deficiencies it listed included generic training with no testing or completion tracking, risk ratings not tied to controls, and effectiveness reviews that only check that controls exist ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/fintrac-key-compliance-takeaways)).

**Coming change.** C-12 adds universal enrolment for all reporting entities, with an application, renewal and change-of-information cycle. Failing to enrol is an offence. The start date will be set by Order in Council and is not yet fixed ([BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/04/preparing-for-universal-enrolment-higher-penalties-and-fintrac-enforcement); search summary, unverified detail).

**Car dealers that finance or lease in their own name** are the swing segment.
- Canada has 3,783 franchised dealerships ([CADA 2024 Data Report](https://cada.ca/common/Uploaded%20files/EconomicReports/Data%20Report/2024CADADataReport-EN.pdf)).
- It has about 7,560 used-car dealer businesses ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/canada/number-of-businesses/used-car-dealers/1004/), commercial estimate).
- Ontario alone has over 8,000 registered dealers ([OMVIC](https://omvic.ca/)).
- The share that finance or lease in their own name ("buy-here-pay-here", in-house leasing) is unknown (unverified). It may be in the low thousands.
- The RIAS's 200 financing and leasing companies clearly does not include the whole dealer universe. Most franchised dealers place loans with captive or bank lenders. Under UCDA's reading, those dealers may fall outside.

**How they comply today.**
- **Lessors:** CFLA member guidance ([CFLA](https://cfla-acfl.ca/industry-intelligence/anti-money-laundering/overview.html)), plus law firms and accountants.
- **Dealers:** the UCDA policy template and programme guidance document, a recorded UCDA/KPMG webinar, and paid one-on-one KPMG training ([UCDA](https://www.ucda.org/fintrac/)). For KYC, dealers use their lending platform's ID and screening add-ons, such as Dealertrack and AutoCorp.
- **Cheque cashers:** MSB-focused consultants and platforms.
- **Effort assumed by the RIAS:**
  - one-time: 20 hours to build the programme, 4 hours to update intake forms, and 16–32 hours of IT setup
  - ongoing: 48 hours a year to maintain the programme and 16–32 hours to prepare for an exam
  - source: [Canada Gazette RIAS](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors67-eng.html)
- This is mostly paper and Word work, which suits a template-plus-workflow tool.

**Bottom line.**
- Filing is free through FINTRAC.
- KYC and screening are covered by dealer platforms.
- Programme documents are covered by association templates and consultants.
- No tool runs the whole programme for small lessors, factors or in-house-financing dealers: risk assessment, training with tests, KYC records, the 24-hour cash rule and the two-year review. Real estate has such tools (ReallyTrusted), which shows the model works when a sector is large.

## Willingness to pay

- **The government's own cost estimate:**
  - about $2.8k a year per small factor, cheque casher or lessor
  - $21,611 present value, or $3,077 a year, per small business across the whole package
  - source: [Canada Gazette RIAS](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors67-eng.html)
  - A tool that removes most of that labour could charge $600–1,500 a year.
- **Consultants:** none publish prices. All quote on request ([Substance Law](https://substancelaw.ca/compliance-program/)). A one-off programme build or effectiveness review from a Big-4 or mid-tier firm likely costs $5k–20k (unverified).
- **Penalties:**
  - Before C-12, MNP cited $1,000 for minor violations up to $500,000 for the most serious ([MNP, French](https://www.mnp.ca/fr/points-de-vue/bibliotheque/que-doit-faire-concessionnaire-conformer-regles-canafe-contre-blanchiment-dargent)).
  - Since C-12, minor violations can reach $40,000 ([Torys](https://www.torys.com/en/our-latest-thinking/torys-quarterly/q1-2026/fintac-amps)).
  - Fines of $25k–225k against small MSBs for paperwork failures show the risk is real ([FINTRAC](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/new-neuf/nr/2026-02-05-eng)).
- **Ceiling on the market:**
  - 761 small entities × about $1,000 a year ≈ $0.76M at 100% take-up.
  - At a realistic 10–15% share, that is about $75–115k a year in revenue.
  - Dealers could add several times that if in-house financers number in the thousands (unverified).

## Channels

- **UCDA (Ontario used-car dealers).** It already distributes FINTRAC templates and KPMG training. It is a partner or reseller target, and possibly a competitor ([UCDA](https://www.ucda.org/fintrac/)).
- **CFLA.** The trade body for asset-backed finance, vehicle and equipment leasing. It runs AML webinars and guidance ([CFLA](https://cfla-acfl.ca/industry-intelligence/anti-money-laundering.html)).
- **CADA and provincial dealer associations**, for franchised dealers ([CADA](https://cada.ca/)). Quebec dealers would need French materials.
- **Regulator registers:**
  - OMVIC (Ontario, 8,000+ dealers, [OMVIC](https://omvic.ca/)), AMVIC and other provincial dealer registers, to find buy-here-pay-here dealers.
  - FINTRAC's public MSB registry, to list cheque cashers (not counted this session).
- **FINTRAC Industry Days and sector webinars.** Visible dealer and lessor audience ([UCDA event](https://www.ucda.org/fintrac-webinar-for-ontario-auto-dealers-common-compliance-deficiencies/)).
- **Partners:**
  - Dealer accountants and dealer-focused CPA firms (MNP, KRP, Doane GT) as referral partners for effectiveness reviews.
  - Dealer platforms (Dealertrack, AutoCorp, Paays) as integration partners, since they only do KYC and screening.
- **Other associations:** factoring associations and payday or cheque-cashing associations (unverified; not researched).

## Risks

- **Market too small (main risk).** The officially counted segment is 865 businesses, and only 600 of them are cheque cashers ([Canada Gazette RIAS](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2025/2025-03-26/html/sor-dors67-eng.html)). The dealer upside rests on an unresolved scope question.
- **Scope may narrow.** If FINTRAC confirms that dealers who only arrange third-party financing are out, as UCDA reads it ([UCDA](https://www.ucda.org/fintrac/)), the dealer segment shrinks to in-house financers.
- **Associations bundle it free.** UCDA templates and CFLA guidance set a price anchor near zero for the document part.
- **Platform bundling.** Dealertrack, Paays or AutoCorp could add programme modules to their existing KYC flows, and they already own the dealer workflow.
- **The cheque-casher segment is MSB-crowded.** Generic MSB AML platforms and consultants already serve it.
- **Liability.** Vendors in this space all disclaim responsibility ([Dealertrack](https://dealertrackcanada.com/aml/)). A newcomer would need clear "not legal advice" terms and probably E&O insurance. Writing someone's policies edges toward legal advice; a lawyer or CPA partner reduces the risk.
- **Language.** Quebec has 946 franchised dealerships ([CADA 2024](https://cada.ca/common/Uploaded%20files/EconomicReports/Data%20Report/2024CADADataReport-EN.pdf)). A French version is needed there.
- **Regulator tool.** FINTRAC offers free filing and guidance, but no programme builder (no evidence found). The risk of the regulator building one looks low.
- **Repeal.** Very unlikely. The 2026 trend is tougher rules (C-12, universal enrolment).

## First product

**Who it is for.** A "FINTRAC programme in a box" for small financing and leasing entities and dealers that finance or lease in their own name. Factors come second. Cheque cashers only if they want it, because MSB vendors already serve them.

**What version one should do.**
1. **Scoping wizard.** Answers "am I a reporting entity?" for in-house, indirect and $100k-plus deals. Cites FINTRAC's lease page.
2. **Risk-assessment builder.** Sector-specific risk factors (vehicle, equipment, cash, client type, geography), with ratings tied to named controls. FINTRAC flagged ratings that are not tied to controls.
3. **Policy generator.** Fills an editable policy and procedures document from the wizard answers. Bilingual English and French.
4. **Training.** Short sector-specific modules with a quiz and a completion log per employee, because FINTRAC flagged generic, untested training.
5. **KYC record forms.** ID method, beneficial ownership, PEP and third-party determinations. Can link to Dealertrack or Paays screening results rather than replace them.
6. **Large-cash 24-hour-rule tracker and STR decision log.** Records why each report was or was not filed, with a link to FINTRAC's free reporting system.
7. **Two-year effectiveness-review checklist** with sample testing and remediation tracking, plus a one-click "exam binder" PDF export.

**Price.** About $79–129 a month, or $900–1,400 a year. Add an optional partner-delivered effectiveness review.

**First 30 days.**
- **Week 1:** Map FINTRAC's lease, factoring and compliance-program guidance into a requirements checklist. Read the UCDA template if it can be obtained.
- **Week 2:** Build the wizard, the risk-assessment builder and the policy generator. Plain web app with PDF and DOCX export.
- **Week 3:** Build one training module with a quiz and a log, and the KYC record form.
- **Week 4:**
  - Run 10 interviews with buy-here-pay-here dealers (from OMVIC lists) and CFLA small lessors.
  - Pitch UCDA and CFLA on a member discount.
  - Line up one CPA firm for the effectiveness-review add-on.
