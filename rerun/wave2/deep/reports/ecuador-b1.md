# Ecuador B1: compliance file and expiry tracker for private security companies

## Summary

**Verdict: maybe. Score: 4/10.**

The duty is real, and enforcement got much tougher in 2026. Under the Ley Orgánica de Vigilancia y Seguridad Privada (LOVSP), letting the liability or life/accident policy lapse, or running on an expired operating permit, is a "very serious" infraction. The penalty is permanent cancellation of the permit ([LOVSP arts. 85-86, RO 496](https://asobanca.org.ec/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Publicacion-Registro-Oficial-Ley-de-Vigilancia-y-Seguridad-Privada.pdf)). Each firm also has to keep the state system SICOSEP up to date with its guards, arms, posts, vehicles and partners, and retrain every guard every two years. That gives a firm dozens or hundreds of expiry dates to track. The armed forces inspected 663 of at least 1,130 registered firms in early 2026, and several firms were closed ([Primicias, Apr 2026](https://www.primicias.ec/seguridad/control-armas-fuerzas-armadas-empresas-seguridad-inspecciones-guardias-privados-riesgo-crimen-organizado-120326/)). The case is still weak. The market is small: roughly 700 to 1,130 firms, with thin margins. The state already runs the filing system (SICOSEP), an arms-check app (SincoAr), and a quarterly feed of policy status from the insurance supervisor to the ministry. The software would only add reminders, a document vault and inspection readiness, and a spreadsheet can do much of that. It could work as a small niche product, perhaps bundled with guard rostering, but it is not a strong standalone business.

## Duty

**Legal basis.** Ley Orgánica de Vigilancia y Seguridad Privada, Registro Oficial Suplemento 496, 9 Feb 2024. It repealed the 2003 law ([RO 496 text](https://asobanca.org.ec/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Publicacion-Registro-Oficial-Ley-de-Vigilancia-y-Seguridad-Privada.pdf)). The Interior ministry's own consolidated copy, posted Aug 2025, lists the law as in force. Its only change is a fe de erratas of 28 Feb 2024 (RO Supl. 507) ([Ministerio del Interior, LOVSP](https://www.ministeriodelinterior.gob.ec/wp-content/uploads/downloads/2025/08/LOVSP.pdf)).

**What firms must do (recurring parts):**

- **Operating permit.** It is valid two years, and firms remain subject to random checks (art. 38) ([LOVSP](https://www.ministeriodelinterior.gob.ec/wp-content/uploads/downloads/2025/08/LOVSP.pdf)). The renewal goes online through SICOSEP at least 90 days before expiry. Firms upload a sworn list of contracts, operating staff and arms with valid permits, both insurance policies (each valid at least two years), the RUC and a two-year lease. An on-site inspection follows. The fee is USD 50, plus USD 200 for each year the permit went unrenewed ([gob.ec MDI-61, updated 9 Jun 2025](https://www.gob.ec/sites/default/files/sign/procedure-MDI-61-20260909171717-20332781-signed.pdf)).
- **Insurance (art. 39).** Firms must carry civil liability insurance, plus life and accident cover for staff around the clock from day one of employment. Cash-in-transit firms also need transport cover. The policy amounts are to be set by the general reglamento ([LOVSP](https://www.ministeriodelinterior.gob.ec/wp-content/uploads/downloads/2025/08/LOVSP.pdf)).
- **Keep records current (art. 41.7 and Disposición General Quinta).** Firms must keep their data current in the state information system. That covers hires and exits of operating and admin staff, the list of arms and ammunition, the policy register with validity and cover, partners and administrators, contracts and georeferenced service posts, and vehicles. Any change must also be recorded ([RO 496, pp. 34-35](https://asobanca.org.ec/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Publicacion-Registro-Oficial-Ley-de-Vigilancia-y-Seguridad-Privada.pdf); [LOVSP DG Quinta](https://www.ministeriodelinterior.gob.ec/wp-content/uploads/downloads/2025/08/LOVSP.pdf)). Firms must also register hires and exits with the labour ministry (art. 41.8), pay wages only into a bank salary account (arts. 41.3 and 44), and include each guard in the life policy from day one (art. 41.5) ([RO 496](https://asobanca.org.ec/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Publicacion-Registro-Oficial-Ley-de-Vigilancia-y-Seguridad-Privada.pdf)).
- **Guard retraining every two years (art. 52).** Level I and Level II guards must pass a retraining programme every two years. The employer pays for it ([RO 496, pp. 40-41](https://asobanca.org.ec/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Publicacion-Registro-Oficial-Ley-de-Vigilancia-y-Seguridad-Privada.pdf)). Level II (armed) requires physical and psychological fitness, trust and toxicology tests (art. 51) (same source).
- **ID cards.** Firms had 180 days to reissue guard ID cards to the new format (Transitoria Cuarta). Not supplying an updated card is a light infraction (art. 81.7) ([RO 496, pp. 55, 69](https://asobanca.org.ec/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Publicacion-Registro-Oficial-Ley-de-Vigilancia-y-Seguridad-Privada.pdf)).
- **Arms.** Arms permits come from the armed forces' arms control. Third-party reporting says permits for legal persons last five years ([Expreso](https://www.expreso.ec/actualidad/guardias-seguridad-sera-policia-armas-155954.html)) (unverified against the ministerial agreement). Defence Acuerdo Ministerial 249 (RO Supl. 76, 8 Jul 2025) issued a new reglamento on registering arms and munitions. Whether it adds specific duties for security firms is (unverified).

**Penalties (arts. 80-86).** The SBU in 2026 is USD 482 ([El Diario](https://www.eldiario.ec/centro/salarios-minimos-sectoriales-en-ecuador-2026-estas-son-las-proyecciones-tras-el-nuevo-sueldo-basico-de-482-22012026/)). All articles below are from [RO 496, pp. 54-63](https://asobanca.org.ec/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Publicacion-Registro-Oficial-Ley-de-Vigilancia-y-Seguridad-Privada.pdf).

- **Light** (art. 81). Examples: not keeping SICOSEP data updated, missing ID cards, guards working with expired accreditation. Fine: 1 SBU (USD 482), then 2 SBU for a repeat within a year (art. 82).
- **Serious** (art. 83). Examples: blocking an inspection, missing uniforms, late pay or late IESS contributions, hiring staff who do not meet the requirements, filing false information. The first offence costs 6 SBU (USD 2,892). A second within 365 days brings a 15-30 day suspension, and a third brings cancellation (art. 84).
- **Very serious** (art. 85). Examples: operating with an expired permit, not holding or keeping current the liability or life/accident policy, not adding a guard to the life policy from day one, not enrolling workers in IESS within 15 days. Penalty: permanent cancellation of the permit. A firm that keeps operating afterwards is fined 15-20 SBU, and the Superintendencia and SRI are told to start dissolution (art. 86).

**Enforcement is real and rising.**

- **Dec 2025.** SERCOP-led inspections found irregularities at five firms, and the ministry closed one ([Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/seguridad/sercop-irregularidades-empresas-privadas-vigilancia-ecuador-110724/)).
- **Feb 2026, Quito and Cayambe.** Two firms were temporarily closed for having no permit. Inspectors also found problems with staff lists, uniform permits and training certificates ([Vistazo](https://www.vistazo.com/politica/nacional/2026-02-07-sercop-inspecciona-empresas-seguridad-privada-ecuador-clausura-falta-de-permisos-FA10671765)).
- **19-20 Feb 2026, Guayaquil.** Two firms were suspended and closed, and 319 weapons were seized ([Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/economia/bajos-montos-contratos-seguridad-vigilancia-privada-sercop-irregularidades-inspecciones-suspension-116508/); [Expreso](https://www.expreso.ec/actualidad/dos-empresas-de-seguridad-son-suspendidas-tras-inspecciones-del-sercop-en-guayaquil-275310.html)).
- **Apr 2026.** The armed forces had inspected 663 of at least 1,130 registered firms, with random checks to continue. Expired arms permits lead to seizure, and a missing operating permit can lead to closure ([Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/seguridad/control-armas-fuerzas-armadas-empresas-seguridad-inspecciones-guardias-privados-riesgo-crimen-organizado-120326/)).
- **13 May 2026, Quito.** A multi-agency operation (Interior, Police, Armed Forces, Labour ministry, IESS, Fire Department) closed two firms and one training centre ([Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/sucesos/clausura-empresas-seguridad-irregularidades-formacion-guardias-allanamientos-policia-122810/)).

**Open points on the duty.**

- **Reglamento.** The general reglamento was due within 90 days (Transitoria Primera) ([LOVSP](https://www.ministeriodelinterior.gob.ec/wp-content/uploads/downloads/2025/08/LOVSP.pdf)). Industry said it was still missing ([Expreso](https://www.expreso.ec/actualidad/uniformados-refuerzo-empresas-esperan-reglamento-214314.html)). I found no decree issuing it as of Oct 2026 (unverified). Without it, the policy amounts are not fixed.
- **Policy status reporting.** The law makes the Superintendencia de Compañías report each firm's policy status to the ministry every quarter (Disposición General Décima) ([LOVSP](https://www.ministeriodelinterior.gob.ec/wp-content/uploads/downloads/2025/08/LOVSP.pdf)). So the state can see lapsed policies without the firm reporting them.
- **Firm-filed periodic reports.** I found no monthly or annual report that firms must file. The old police "semiannual reports" passed to the ministry under Transitoria Novena (b) ([RO 496, p. 70](https://asobanca.org.ec/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Publicacion-Registro-Oficial-Ley-de-Vigilancia-y-Seguridad-Privada.pdf)). Whether they still exist is (unverified).

## Buyers

- **Firms with permits.** In 2022, 541 firms held an operating permit (83 new, 458 renewed), at a time when renewal was annual. That year the SRI had 2,984 taxpayers with private security activity on their RUC, according to Interior and SRI data cited by [El Universo (Apr 2023)](https://www.eluniverso.com/noticias/informes/porte-armas-guardia-privada-decreto-707-medidas-urgentes-seguridad-guillermo-lasso-nota/).
- **Police count.** An undated police count gave 556 firms with valid permits, 135 in process and 203 expired ([El Comercio](https://www.elcomercio.com/actualidad/ecuador-guardiasdeseguridad-policia-informalidad-capacitacion/)).
- **2026 count.** At least 1,130 registered firms, and more than 90,000 guards ([Primicias, Apr 2026](https://www.primicias.ec/seguridad/control-armas-fuerzas-armadas-empresas-seguridad-inspecciones-guardias-privados-riesgo-crimen-organizado-120326/)).
- **Renewal volume as a cross-check.** The gob.ec renewal page shows 364 "atenciones" from Sep 2025 to Aug 2026, and 513 in calendar 2025 ([MDI-61](https://www.gob.ec/sites/default/files/sign/procedure-MDI-61-20260909171717-20332781-signed.pdf)). On a two-year cycle, that suggests roughly 700-1,000 firms with live permits. Reading "atenciones" as renewals is my inference (unverified).
- **Growth.** New firms rose from 134 in 2022 to 194 in 2023 ([InSight Crime](https://insightcrime.org/es/noticias/criminales-ecuador-aprovechando-empresas-seguridad-privada/)).
- **Segments.** About 178 firms are on SERCOP's public-sector security catalogue, valid two years from Sep 2025 ([Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/economia/sercop-catalogo-servicio-seguridad-vigilancia-privada-104670/); [SERCOP](https://portal.compraspublicas.gob.ec/sercop/servicio-de-seguridad-y-vigilancia-privada/)). These face the most inspections and are the best first segment. The rest serve private clients: banks, residential complexes, shrimp farms and retail.
- **Size.** Every firm is a limited-liability company with minimum paid-in capital of 75 SBU, about USD 36,150 in 2026 (art. 34) ([RO 496, p. 30](https://asobanca.org.ec/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Publicacion-Registro-Oficial-Ley-de-Vigilancia-y-Seguridad-Privada.pdf); [NMS Law](https://nmslaw.com.ec/blog/2025/07/28/requisitos-capital-minimo-lovsp-ecuador/)). With roughly 65,000-90,000 guards across about 1,000 firms, the average firm has about 60-90 guards. A few large firms (G4S and others) hold a big share (unverified).
- **How firms comply today.** Most likely an in-house admin or HR person, the firm's lawyer for the renewal file, and spreadsheets (unverified). An industry body said only about half of the roughly 120,000 accredited guards were enrolled in IESS ([Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/noticias/sociedad/seguridad-privada-rol-policia-ecuador)). That points to widespread informality.

## Competition

- **SICOSEP (state, free).** The ministry system for issuing and renewing permits. It has modules for companies, training centres and public look-ups, plus a job board added in v2.0 ([SICOSEP user manual, 2018](https://www.ministeriodegobierno.gob.ec/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/MANUAL-DE-USUARIO-COMPAÑÍAS-DE-SEGURIDAD-PRIVADA-V2.0.pdf)). Anyone can look up a guard's accreditation levels and retraining by ID number, and a firm's permit by RUC ([elyex](https://elyex.com/sicosep-ecuador-consultar-guardias-seguridad-privada/)). It is the system of record that firms must keep updated. The manual covers only filing up to the permit being issued, with no expiry dashboards or reminders. Whether the current version sends alerts is (unverified).
- **SincoAr (state).** The armed forces' arms-control system, with an app that checks possession and carry permits on site by QR code or serial number ([Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/seguridad/control-armas-fuerzas-armadas-empresas-seguridad-inspecciones-guardias-privados-riesgo-crimen-organizado-120326/)).
- **Superintendencia policy feed (state).** Quarterly policy status sent to the ministry (DG Décima, [LOVSP](https://www.ministeriodelinterior.gob.ec/wp-content/uploads/downloads/2025/08/LOVSP.pdf)). This is for the regulator, not for firms.
- **Secusoft.** Dutch parent "with roots in Ecuador", on a .ec domain. It covers shift planning, hours, NFC guard tours, logbook, reports, staff management and invoicing, with a free 14-day demo. No prices are published, and it lists no permit, policy, arms or accreditation expiry features ([secusoft.ec](https://www.secusoft.ec/)). This is the closest local-facing vendor, and it could add a compliance module.
- **International guard-tour and workforce tools.** C-Guard Pro (Mexico; rounds, GPS attendance, payroll) ([Comparasoftware](https://www.comparasoftware.com/c-guard-pro)), ShiftFlow ([shiftflow.app](https://www.shiftflow.app/es/industries/security)), OfficerReports (USD 20/month per Appvizer) ([Appvizer](https://www.appvizer.es/organizacion-planificacion/agencias-seguridad/officerreports)), Trackforce, SOVA and Guardhouse. None handles Ecuadorian permit, policy or accreditation compliance.
- **Lawyers and consultants.** They prepare constitution and renewal files ad hoc. I found no published packages or prices (unverified).
- **Local ERP and payroll vendors.** Not checked in depth. Some may track IESS and payroll for security firms (unverified).

No free state tool tracks a firm's own expiries across permit, policies, guard accreditations, ID cards and arms permits in one place. But the state holds most of the underlying data.

## Willingness to pay

- **Cost of a lapse.** A light infraction costs USD 482-964. A serious one costs USD 2,892, then suspension. A lapsed policy or permit means permanent cancellation, which ends the business ([RO 496, arts. 82-86](https://asobanca.org.ec/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Publicacion-Registro-Oficial-Ley-de-Vigilancia-y-Seguridad-Privada.pdf)). A late renewal adds USD 200 a year in fees ([MDI-61](https://www.gob.ec/sites/default/files/sign/procedure-MDI-61-20260909171717-20332781-signed.pdf)). Firms on the SERCOP catalogue also risk losing public contracts.
- **Ability to pay.** The sector is low-margin. Public contract values are described as low and linked to precarious wages ([Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/economia/bajos-montos-contratos-seguridad-vigilancia-privada-sercop-irregularidades-inspecciones-suspension-116508/)). A guard costs roughly the SBU (USD 482) plus benefits.
- **Price anchors.** International guard-tour tools start around USD 20/month ([Appvizer](https://www.appvizer.es/organizacion-planificacion/agencias-seguridad/officerreports)). No local compliance tool prices were found.
- **Plausible price.** USD 40-150/month per firm, tiered by guard count. That gives a ceiling of about 1,000 firms × USD 80 × 12 ≈ USD 1m/year in theory. With 5-10% take-up, the realistic figure is USD 50-100k/year (my estimate, unverified). Willingness to pay was not confirmed with any firm.

## Channels

- **Industry bodies.** AEESPRIV (Unión Asociativa Ecuatoriana de Empresas de Seguridad Privada), recognised by Interior Acuerdo MDI-DMI-2025-0145, RO 20 Oct 2025, based in Quito ([Registro Oficial](https://esacc.corteconstitucional.gob.ec/storage/api/v1/10_DWL_FL/eyJjYXJwZXRhIjoicm8iLCJ1dWlkIjoiY2YzNWFlZTItZTQ1ZC00Y2VjLTgxZWMtYjliMzdhYzcxOWI3LnBkZiJ9)). Also CASEPEG, the Guayas chamber ([Asamblea Nacional](https://www.asambleanacional.gob.ec/es/noticia/casepeg-presento-aportes-al-proyecto-de-reformas-ley-de)), and ANESI, quoted in the press ([Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/noticias/sociedad/seguridad-privada-rol-policia-ecuador)). Their current membership and activity are (unverified).
- **Lead lists.** SERCOP catalogue suppliers (178) and SICOSEP public look-ups by RUC can build a target list ([SERCOP](https://portal.compraspublicas.gob.ec/sercop/servicio-de-seguridad-y-vigilancia-privada/); [elyex](https://elyex.com/sicosep-ecuador-consultar-guardias-seguridad-privada/)).
- **Partners who touch every firm.** Insurance brokers selling the mandatory policies, accredited training centres doing the two-year retraining, and lawyers who file renewals. All could resell or refer (unverified).
- **Timing hooks.** Inspection waves and SERCOP catalogue renewal (next due around Sep 2027) create urgency.

## Risks

- **State tool risk (high).** SICOSEP already holds the firm's guards, arms, posts and policies. The law tells the ministry to make all control systems interoperate (Transitoria Octava) ([RO 496, p. 70](https://asobanca.org.ec/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Publicacion-Registro-Oficial-Ley-de-Vigilancia-y-Seguridad-Privada.pdf)). Adding expiry alerts would be a small step for the ministry.
- **Small, low-margin market.** Roughly 700-1,130 firms, many informal. Firms pushed to the passive register or closed shrink the base further (DG Décimo Cuarta) ([LOVSP](https://www.ministeriodelinterior.gob.ec/wp-content/uploads/downloads/2025/08/LOVSP.pdf)).
- **Licensing barrier.** Art. 43 says firms providing "connected services" such as software design must be accredited by the ministry. Selling products for security services without accreditation is an infraction (arts. 81.11 and 83.30) ([RO 496, pp. 35, 55, 59](https://asobanca.org.ec/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Publicacion-Registro-Oficial-Ley-de-Vigilancia-y-Seguridad-Privada.pdf)). A compliance SaaS may need this accreditation. Scope (unverified).
- **Regulatory flux.** The reglamento is still missing, policy amounts are undefined, and renewal requirements could change when it arrives.
- **Low frequency.** The big filing comes every two years. Value depends on continuous record-keeping (guard churn, retraining, arms), which firms may keep in Excel.
- **Liability and trust.** A missed alert could cost a firm its permit. The sector also has links to organised crime, so data handling and customer vetting matter ([InSight Crime](https://insightcrime.org/es/noticias/criminales-ecuador-aprovechando-empresas-seguridad-privada/)).
- **Competition from rostering vendors.** Secusoft or C-Guard could add an expiry module.

## First product

**Version 1: "Expediente LOVSP".** A Spanish-language web app for one firm:

1. A company file tracking: permit expiry with a T-90 renewal countdown, both policies (validity, insurer, beneficiary list), lease, capital, partners' sworn statements, and the SERCOP catalogue and RUP status.
2. A guard register with a row per guard: ID number, accreditation level, last retraining date with a two-year expiry, ID card issue date, IESS enrolment date, inclusion in the life policy, and post. Imported from Excel.
3. An arms register: serial, type, permit expiry, assigned post or guard.
4. Email and WhatsApp alerts at 90, 60, 30 and 7 days. A "changes to update in SICOSEP" list each time a guard is hired or leaves.
5. A one-click inspection pack (PDF): permit, policies, guard list with valid accreditation, arms list. Plus a draft of the sworn list of contracts, staff and arms for renewal.

**First 30 days.**

- Week 1: interview 10 firms from the SERCOP catalogue list and 2 brokers. Confirm the pain, the price, and whether art. 43 accreditation applies.
- Week 2: build the data model and Excel import.
- Week 3: build alerts and the inspection PDF.
- Week 4: pilot with 3 firms for free. Price at USD 49/99/149 per month by guard count.
- Pursue a partnership with one training centre or broker.

- What exactly do "atenciones" on MDI-61 count? How many firms hold live permits today?
- Do firms still file semiannual reports to the ministry?
- What do lawyers charge for a permit renewal? What would a firm pay per month?
