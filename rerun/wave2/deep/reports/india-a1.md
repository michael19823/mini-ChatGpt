# India A1: Lift licence, inspection and AMC compliance tracker

## Summary

**Verdict: no-go. Score: 3/10.**

State lift acts do make owners register or license each lift, renew it, and file AMC or safety papers. Enforcement is real in Uttar Pradesh. But the duty is getting lighter and more automated, not heavier. Tamil Nadu's 2025 amendment replaced its old yearly renewal with a 5-year licence that the state portal auto-generates online (https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_states/tamil-nadu/2025/Bill22of2025TN.pdf). UP registration runs 5 years and Haryana's periodic fee covers 3 years. Penalties are small: Rs 10,000 at most in TN and UP, and Karnataka's late-renewal fee is capped at Rs 2,500 per lift. The work is already done by lift AMC firms, consultants (IncorpX from Rs 14,999), society ERPs (ADDA) and free state portals. A standalone tracker would remind owners of a few dates every 3-5 years, and nobody has a budget line for that.

**Named killer:** The duty comes too rarely and is too cheap to miss, and AMC firms and free state portals already absorb it. A tracker that only reminds owners of dates has no buyer who will pay.

## Duty

**Tamil Nadu (in force 3 Feb 2026).**
- Law: the Tamil Nadu Lifts and Escalators Act 1997, as amended by the TN Lifts and Escalators (Amendment) Act 2025 (Act 35 of 2025). It came into force on 3 Feb 2026 under G.O.Ms.No.13, Energy Department (https://www.stationeryprinting.tn.gov.in/extraordinary/2026/45_Ex_II_2_2026.pdf ; https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/52526/tamil-nadu-govt-notified-the-tamil-nadu-lifts-and-escalators-amendment-act-2025/).
- New s.5(4): the working licence is "auto-generated and granted online" and is "valid for a period of five years". Section 6 of the 1997 Act is omitted (https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_states/tamil-nadu/2025/Bill22of2025TN.pdf). Before this, the licence was renewed every year, after inspection, on Form G filed three months before expiry (https://www.india.gov.in/application-renewal-licence-working-lift). The lead file assumed that older yearly cycle.
- The owner must authorise a "competent person" to apply through the online portal. The owner must also upload a completion report within one month of erection, and report accidents online and to the police (new ss.4, 5, 13, same bill).
- Penalties (new s.15): up to Rs 10,000 for a first contravention and Rs 20,000 for a repeat, plus up to Rs 500 a day while it continues. Penalties are set by the Senior Electrical Inspector and can be compounded (ss.15-A to 15-D, same bill).
- The bill's own Statement of Objects says its aim is to "decriminalise", auto-generate permissions and "digitalise all processes" under the SimpleGov programme (same bill).
- Permissions carry a QR code in the Inspector's name (https://simpliance.in/India/EHS/govt_notification/tamil-nadu/notification-under-the-tamil-nadu-lifts-and-escalators-amendment-act-2025-8614).

**Uttar Pradesh (in force).**
- The UP Lifts and Escalators Act 2024 got assent on 26 Feb 2024. The Rules took effect on 25 Sep 2024, and the portal went live on 20 Oct 2024 (https://www.therealtytoday.com/news/trending/uttar-pradesh-cabinet-approves-new-lift-and-escalator-safety-rules/ ; https://elevatorworld.com/news/daily-news/indian-state-rolls-out-lift-registration-portal/).
- Owner duties: register each lift, and keep a comprehensive AMC with the OEM or an authorised agency. Except on private premises, a copy of the AMC is submitted every year. Monthly maintenance needs a fitness certificate. Public premises must keep a logbook, run two mock drills a year and fit CCTV. Accidents must be reported within 24 hours (https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/32469/uttar-pradesh-lifts-and-escalators-act-2024).
- Existing lifts had six months to register from 29 Oct 2024 (https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MTI4NDk=/noida-confusion-delays-lift-registration-despite-rising-safety-concerns).
- Registration is renewable every 5 years at Rs 5,000 per lift. This comes from the first check and is (unverified) against the Act text, because India Code returned 403 (https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19956/1/act_lift_eng.pdf).
- Penalties reported in Ghaziabad for late registration: Rs 200 a day for up to 7 days, Rs 500 a day for 7-15 days and Rs 1,000 a day for 15-30 days. After 30 days the lift is stopped and a Rs 10,000 fine is imposed (https://www.etvbharat.com/en/state/repeated-lift-accidents-expose-safety-gaps-as-over-1000-lifts-remain-unregistered-in-ups-ghaziabad-enn25121302477).

**Karnataka (in force).**
- Law: the Karnataka Lifts, Escalators and Passenger Conveyors Act 2012 (Act 9 of 2013) (https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/7182/1/9_of_2013.pdf).
- The Amendment Rules of 27 Jan 2025 set fees per lift by number of floors (https://www.legitquest.com/act/karnataka-lifts-escalators-and-passenger-conveyors-amendment-rules-2025/107F6):
  - Licence grant or renewal: Rs 2,500-6,000 for residential lifts and Rs 3,500-7,000 for non-residential ones.
  - Periodic inspection: Rs 1,000-3,500 residential and Rs 1,500-5,000 non-residential.
  - Late renewal: Rs 500 per month, capped at Rs 2,500 per lift.
- The renewal interval is not confirmed. An old proposal was 10 years after installation, then every 5 years (https://www.deccanherald.com/content/402394/lift-lurch.html) (unverified as current).

**Maharashtra (in force).**
- Law: the Maharashtra Lifts, Escalators and Moving Walks Act 2017 plus the Bombay Lift Rules 1958, administered by the Chief Electrical Inspector (https://www.ceimah.in/help).
- MyGate says (https://mygate.com/blog/mumbai-repository/lift-safety-regulations-every-rwa-in-mumbai-must-follow/):
  - the licence is valid for 20 years but is renewed every 5 years;
  - inspections happen twice a year;
  - penalties go up to Rs 50,000 plus Rs 1,000 a day.
  None of this is cited to a statute (unverified).
- ADDA's society compliance calendar lists lift inspection and fee payment as half-yearly, citing Lift Rule 8(1) (https://blog.ind.adda.io/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Society-Compliance-Calendar-.pdf).
- The 2026 amendment bill passed the Assembly on 5 Mar 2026. It changes definitions and officer powers, not owner duties (https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_states/maharashtra/2026/Bill7of2026MH.pdf).

**Haryana (in force).**
- Under the Haryana Lifts and Escalators Act 2008, owners must submit their AMC copy and an annual safety certificate every year (https://ceiharyana.com/UserManual/Haryana-Lifts-and-Escalators-Act-2008.pdf).
- Fees: Rs 3,500 for a new lift (Rs 1,000 registration plus Rs 2,500 inspection). The periodic fee is Rs 1,000 "for 3 years" (https://ceiharyana.com/Procedure_For_Registration_Lift_Exclator.aspx).

**Kerala.** The Kerala Lifts and Escalators Act 2013 applies (https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/21687/1/act_18_of_2013.finaleng.pdf). A fee order sets a yearly periodic inspection (https://jameskutty.info/dept/feeGO_09_01.pdf). Renewal is on Form H, Rule 5 (https://jameskutty.info/dept/lift_rule_12.pdf) (unverified detail).

**Assam.** Licences are issued for three years. The renewal fee is Rs 500 (https://cei.assam.gov.in/sites/default/files/Schedule%20of%20fees%20for%20Lift%20and%20Escalators_0.pdf ; https://sentinelassam.com/topheadlines/assam-do-lifts-in-malls-and-apartments-have-safety-norms).

**Andhra Pradesh.** The Assembly passed the bill on 11 Mar 2025. Existing lifts must be licensed within 12 months of commencement (https://elevatorworld.com/news/daily-news/india-passes-andhra-pradesh-lifts-and-escalators-bill/). No commencement notice or rules were found (unverified).

**Telangana.** Still a draft. The CM cleared it on 25 June after pressure from the High Court. It proposes yearly inspection by government inspectors and maintenance only by recognised agencies. It still needs the Law Department, the Cabinet and the Assembly (https://www.siasat.com/telangana-cm-clears-draft-law-on-lift-safety-after-hc-push-3511228/ ; https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/telangana-govt-to-table-lifts-and-escalators-bill-in-cabinet-1972992). I found no report that it has passed.

**Direction of change.** States are moving to auto-generated online licences, longer validity and decriminalised penalties, with TN the clearest case. That cuts the owner's admin burden, which is exactly the pain a tracker would sell against.

## Buyers

- **Installed lifts.** No official national count was found. One market site puts the base at over 600,000 units, with 50,000-60,000 added a year. An industry article claims 150,000+ new units a year (https://www.indexbox.io/store/india-elevator-controller-market-analysis-forecast-size-trends-and-insights/ ; https://themachinemaker.com/news/global-vertical-mobility-industry-to-gather-in-bengaluru-at-smart-lift-mobility-world-2026/) (unverified, sources conflict).
- **UP registrations.** About 12,600 registrations covering about 16,000 lifts since October 2024, over 80% of them in Noida (https://therealtytoday.com/news/trending/lift-and-escalator-act-2024-noida-dominates-registration-drive-across-uttar-pradesh).
- **Ghaziabad.** About 4,000 operating lifts, of which 2,991 were registered at 12 Dec 2025 (https://www.etvbharat.com/en/state/repeated-lift-accidents-expose-safety-gaps-as-over-1000-lifts-remain-unregistered-in-ups-ghaziabad-enn25121302477).
- **Noida.** One December 2024 report claims "roughly 80,000 lifts across 350 high-rise buildings" (https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MTI4NDk=/noida-confusion-delays-lift-registration-despite-rising-safety-concerns). That is about 230 lifts per building, which looks wrong (unverified).
- **Segments.** Buyers are housing societies (AOAs and RWAs), commercial buildings, hospitals, schools, malls and hotels. Ghaziabad sent notices to all of these (ETV Bharat, above). In terms of who pays, most owners have one to twenty lifts. Large portfolios sit with builders, facility managers and the AMC firms themselves.
- **How they comply today.**
  - The lift OEM or AMC firm usually files and keeps the papers. UP's rules tie maintenance to the OEM or an authorised agency (https://propnewstime.com/latestnewsstories/MTI4NDk=/noida-confusion-delays-lift-registration-despite-rising-safety-concerns).
  - In TN, an authorised "competent person" files on the owner's behalf (TN bill, above).
  - Societies also use consultants or society ERPs.
  - No survey of how they comply was found (unverified).

## Competition

- **Free state portals.** These are the main competitor:
  - TN: online auto-generated licence and permissions with QR codes (TN bill; simpliance notification, above).
  - UP: e-portal, where approval is automatic once fees are paid (https://elevatorworld.com/news/daily-news/indian-state-rolls-out-lift-registration-portal/).
  - Haryana: CEI online services (https://ceiharyana.com/OurOnlineServices.aspx).
  - Maharashtra: CEI online services (https://www.ceimah.in/online-services).
  - Assam: Sewa Setu (https://sewasetu.assam.gov.in/site/service-apply/license-for-operating-a-lift-installed-before-the-commencement-of-the-act).
  - Whether these portals send expiry reminders was not confirmed (unverified).
- **Lift AMC firms and OEMs.** They hold the maintenance records and often handle the filings. This is the default way owners comply today (inferred from the UP and TN rules above).
- **ElevatorPlus (Accucia, Pune).** Service software for lift companies. It flags AMC renewals 60 days before expiry and handles tickets and inventory (https://www.capterra.ca/software/1075289/ElevatorPlus). Price not retrieved (403). Whether it tracks statutory licences is (unverified).
- **Service CRM (IndiaMART).** A lift-maintenance software package at Rs 20,000 a year (https://m.indiamart.com/service-crm).
- **Society ERPs.**
  - ADDA has an "AMC & Compliance Tracker" (https://blog.ind.adda.io/?p=21800 ; https://ind.adda.io/association-erp) and publishes a society compliance calendar that includes lift inspection (ADDA calendar, above).
  - MyGate and NoBrokerHood mostly publish blog content on lift rules. Blogs quote their prices at about Rs 5-15 per flat per month (https://codingclave.com/blog/best-society-management-app-india-2026) (unverified).
- **Consultants.**
  - IncorpX: lift licence assistance in many cities "starting @ Rs 14,999", including an "annual renewal service" (https://www.incorpx.io/lift-installation-license-in-pimpri-chinchwad).
  - IndiaMART lift-licence consultants such as Fisco Rating Agency, Ghaziabad, with no price shown (https://m.indiamart.com/fiscoratingagencyghaziabad).
- **Gap.** I found no tool built just for statutory lift licences. The gap is narrow, because free portals, AMC firms and society ERPs each cover part of the job.

## Willingness to pay

- **Government fees per lift are low.**
  - Karnataka renewal: Rs 2,500-7,000 (legitquest, above).
  - Haryana: Rs 1,000 for 3 years (CEI Haryana, above).
  - Assam renewal: Rs 500 (Assam fee schedule, above).
  - UP: Rs 5,000 for 5 years (unverified).
- **Penalties are low.**
  - Karnataka late fee: capped at Rs 2,500 (legitquest, above).
  - TN: up to Rs 10,000 for a first contravention (TN bill, above).
  - UP: up to Rs 10,000 plus day rates (ETV Bharat, above).
  - Maharashtra: up to Rs 50,000 is claimed (unverified).
  - The real cost of non-compliance is having the lift stopped or sealed, plus liability after an accident.
- **AMC spend is the bigger budget.** It runs about Rs 10,000-50,000 per lift per year for residential lifts, and comprehensive contracts cost 60-70% more (https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/lift-amc-evaluation-guide-india). Licence handling is usually bundled into the AMC.
- **Consultants charge Rs 14,999+** for a full licence job (IncorpX, above). That is a one-off service fee, not a software budget.
- **Implied software price.** A pure reminder tool is worth perhaps Rs 0-2,000 per society per year, and society ERPs already sit at Rs 5-15 per flat per month (unverified). Willingness to pay for a standalone tracker looks very low (inference).

## Channels

- **Lift AMC firms and independent lift companies.** These are the best channel and the most likely payer, since they manage many owners' lifts. A good place to reach them is the Smart Lift & Mobility World expo (Bengaluru, 5-7 Feb 2026, 200+ exhibitor brands) (https://elevatorworld.com/news/daily-news/slm-world-2026-to-showcase-latest-vt-technologies/).
- **Housing federations.** For example, the Maharashtra State Cooperative Housing Federation and the Mumbai Jilha Co-operative Housing Federation (https://www.punekarnews.in/?p=238749 ; https://www.indiancooperative.com/co-op-news-snippets/fadnavis-unveils-key-co-op-housing-initiatives-in-mumbai/amp).
- **District enforcement drives.** UP districts run lift-safety committees and send notices to societies, which creates moments when owners are looking for help. Examples are the Gautam Buddh Nagar five-member committee (https://therealtytoday.com/news/news/five-member-safety-committee-formed-to-ensure-lift-and-escalator-safety-in-gautam-budh-nagar) and the Ghaziabad notices (ETV Bharat, above).
- **Society ERP marketplaces.** ADDA, MyGate and NoBrokerHood could offer it as an add-on. These platforms are more likely to build the feature than to buy it (inference).

## Risks

- **Rule changes reduce the pain.** TN moved from yearly renewal to a 5-year auto-generated licence and decriminalised penalties (TN bill, above). Other states may copy the SimpleGov model.
- **The regulator's own tool.** State portals already handle filing and auto-approval. Adding expiry SMS or email reminders would be trivial for them.
- **Fragmentation.** More than ten state regimes have different intervals (yearly, half-yearly, 3-year, 5-year), forms and portals, so the rules engine is expensive to keep current.
- **Delays.** AP has no commencement notice yet and Telangana is still a draft (sources above).
- **Market too small.** The UP base is about 16,000 registered lifts (therealtytoday, above). Even across eight states, the number of paying owners is thin at a low price.
- **Buyer ambiguity.** Societies pay little. AMC firms already track their own contracts and may see a third party as a threat.
- **Liability.** A missed reminder could be blamed for a sealed lift or for an accident (inference).

## First product

- If pursued at all, build it for lift AMC firms, not for societies.
- **Scope:** a multi-site register of lifts. For each lift, record the state, the licence or registration number, its expiry, the next inspection date, the AMC expiry and the insurance expiry. Send WhatsApp and email reminders to the AMC firm and the society secretary. Generate a one-page compliance status sheet per building to share with AOAs.
- **First 30 days:**
  1. Encode the rules for UP, TN, Karnataka, Haryana and Maharashtra (intervals and forms).
  2. Import lifts from a spreadsheet.
  3. Build the reminder engine and add WhatsApp.
  4. Interview ten independent AMC firms in Noida and Ghaziabad.
- The better business is probably to sell this as a feature to ElevatorPlus or a society ERP, or to run it as a consultant-led filing service. A SaaS of its own looks weak.
