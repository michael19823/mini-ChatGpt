# Kenya: licensing and compliance pack for non-deposit-taking credit providers (CBK NDTCP Regulations)

## Re-assessment (owner's criteria)

**Verdict: maybe. New score: 6/10 (old score: 5/10).**

**The case.** Under the new rules, about 281 licensed digital lenders now carry stricter ongoing duties. About 548 more applicants are stuck in the CBK queue. More offline lenders must apply by about 29 March 2027 ([Tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/10/04/cbk-raises-licensed-lenders-annual-fee-to-kes-500000-from-kes-20000/), [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/complaints-about-digital-lenders-jump-five-times--5528224)). The CBK portal and the BSA returns system only take submissions. They do not draft the policy set, run the complaints clock, log rate-change approvals or track the 31 December fee and return. No local product covers that job: law firms sell hourly advice, loan-management systems focus on the loan book, and the one local compliance-calendar tool is generic. These buyers already pay KES 250k–500k a year in CBK fees alone, so a KES 15k a month tool is a small line item. The case is a small, niche business with year-3 revenue of roughly KES 15–27m (about USD 115k–210k). It is not a big SaaS business, and the offline-lender pool is still unmeasured.

**Room for improvement over the portal or current practice.**
- **Applications stall on documents, not on the portal.** In March 2024, 429 of 480 applications were "pending documentation". The lenders' association said members "lack clarity on what to submit" and asked CBK for guidance notes ([Business Daily, Mar 2024](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/digital-lenders-seek-cbk-help-to-unlock-429-licences-4551784)). In July 2026, about 548 of 800+ applications were still under review ([Business Daily, Jul 2026](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/complaints-about-digital-lenders-jump-five-times--5528224)). CBK itself says most are "largely awaiting the submission of requisite documentation" ([CBK press release](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/press_releases/1035898107_Press%20Release%20-%20Licensing%20of%20Digital%20Credit%20Providers%20-%20July%202026.pdf)). A guided document pack with a checklist for each director and officer goes straight at this bottleneck.
- **Ongoing duties need records that the portal does not keep.** These are listed in the final rules or the draft:
  - CBK written approval before any interest-rate change, plus 30 days' notice to customers (regs 26 and 55) ([Tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/10/04/cbk-raises-licensed-lenders-annual-fee-to-kes-500000-from-kes-20000/)).
  - Complaints handled on a 7-day, 48-hour and 30-day clock, and reported to CBK.
  - Annual agent approvals and an agent register.
  - 30 days' notice to a borrower before CRB listing.
  - An annual compliance return by 31 December ([CBK draft](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Draft-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf)).
  
  These need a product-change log, a complaints register with timers, an agent register and a calendar.
- **Missing a deadline is now costly.** Paying the annual fee in the three months after 31 December costs double: KES 1m for a licence, KES 500k for registration. After that, CBK may revoke the licence ([Tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/10/04/cbk-raises-licensed-lenders-annual-fee-to-kes-500000-from-kes-20000/)). Deadline tracking alone has clear value.
- **Complaints are a growing exposure.**
  - Complaints about digital lenders to the Competition Authority rose from 67 to 355 in a year ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/complaints-about-digital-lenders-jump-five-times--5528224)).
  - The data regulator (ODPC) has logged 5,284 complaints since 2021 and issued eight penalty notices ([Money254 via search summary](https://money254.co.ke/post/40-digital-lenders-under-investigation-over-data-breaches)) (unverified, from a search summary only).
  
  A complaints register that keeps an audit trail helps with inspections.
- **Lenders face several regulators at once.** The digital lenders' association (DFSAK) complains of "operational challenges posed by the multiplicity of regulatory requirements" across CBK, ODPC and CAK ([The Standard, Aug 2025](https://thestandard.ke/sports/business/article/2001527611/www.digger.co.ke)). One tool that maps CBK, ODPC and CAK duties is a real usability gain.
- **Returns are spreadsheet-based.** BSA training covered downloading the "Outstanding Digital Credit Return" template, completing it and uploading it ([CM Advocates, via search summary](https://cmadvocates.com/blog/legal-alert-upcoming-bank-supervision-application-bsa-user-training-for-non-deposit-taking-credit-providers-stay-compliant/)). Checking the data before upload is a possible add-on. The full return list and frequencies are still (unverified).
- **Multi-client work.** Law firms and accountants handle many lenders each (Bowmans, CDH, Oraro and CM Advocates all advertise the work). A multi-entity dashboard for advisers is a second sales line.

**Competitor reality check.**
- **CBK portal and BSA:** free, but they only accept submissions and returns. They draft nothing and track nothing ([CBK procedures](https://centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Procedures-for-licensing-Digital-Credit-Providers-Revised-October-2024.pdf)). Not a killer.
- **Law firms** (Bowmans, CDH, Oraro, CM Advocates): they advise on applications and liaise with CBK ([search results summary; Bowmans DCP note](https://www.bowmanslaw.com/insights/banking-and-financial-services-regulatory/kenya-central-bank-of-kenya-digital-credit-providers-regulations-2021/)). None publishes a price. Their fees are likely far above a template pack (unverified). They are a channel more than a rival.
- **Loan-management systems:**
  - SuperLMS says it serves 40 MFIs and digital lenders and covers "the reports your regulator expects". It names no CBK return, complaints register, policy set or calendar, and publishes no price ([Super Systems](https://supersystems.co.ke/)).
  - Lendsqr charges USD 0, 200 or 500 a month. It lists no regulatory reporting features, and its pricing page shows no Kenya pricing ([Lendsqr pricing](https://lendsqr.com/pricing)).
  
  These cover the loan book and maybe returns, not the conduct duties. They are partial at best.
- **Trigarc Compliance (FNJ & Associates, Nairobi):** a generic compliance-calendar platform with self-assessments, corrective actions, licence tracking and an audit trail. Its target clients include digital lenders. It shows no NDTCP content, no policy library and no complaints register, and its price is "request a quote" ([FNJ](https://fnjassociates.co.ke/?p=2402)). This is the closest rival: generic and of unknown price. It is an opening, not a killer.
- **YouVerify and Zigram:** KYC and AML screening only ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/nigerian-startup-youverify-launches-in-kenya-4247756), [Zigram](https://www.zigram.tech/?p=37915)).
- **Conclusion:** I found no well-priced local product that does the whole NDTCP job.

## Summary

**Verdict: maybe. Score: 5/10.**

The duty is real, and a deadline is close. CBK's new Non-Deposit Taking Credit Providers (NDTCP) Regulations, 2026 replace the 2022 Digital Credit Providers rules. Every credit-only lender not regulated under another law must apply for a licence or registration by about 29 March 2027 ([Bowmans](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/kenya-non-deposit-taking-credit-provider-regulations-are-here-what-lenders-need-to-know/), [TechTrendsKE](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/10/05/cbk-non-deposit-taking-credit-providers-regulations/)). The application is heavy on documents. The draft lists at least six written policies, a Data Protection registration certificate, fit-and-proper forms and pricing parameters ([CBK draft](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Draft-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf)). CBK itself says most of the 800+ applications it has received since 2022 are stuck "largely awaiting the submission of requisite documentation" ([CBK press release, July 2026](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/press_releases/1035898107_Press%20Release%20-%20Licensing%20of%20Digital%20Credit%20Providers%20-%20July%202026.pdf)). That is direct evidence of the pain a document pack would solve. Against this: the buyer pool is probably only a few hundred to about a thousand firms. The rush ends in March 2027. Annual fees of KES 250k to 500k may push the smallest lenders out. Law firms and loan-management vendors already cover parts of the job. A good fit for a small "application pack + compliance calendar" service sold through or alongside a law firm, but not a large standalone SaaS business.

## Duty

**Legal basis.**
- Central Bank of Kenya Act (Cap 491), s.57(1), (3) and (4), as amended by the Business Laws (Amendment) Act, 2024. The amendment renamed "digital credit providers" as "non-deposit taking credit providers" and widened the scope to all non-deposit credit business not regulated under another law ([CBK Regulatory Impact Statement, May 2026](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Regulatory-Impact-Assessment-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf); [Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2025/Practice/Corporate-Commercial/corporate-commercial-alert-kenya-22-January-banking-and-finance-amendments-introduced-by-the-business-laws-amendment-act-20-of-2024)).
- CBK (Non-Deposit Taking Credit Providers) Regulations, 2026. The press reports them as Legal Notice 191 ([TechTrendsKE](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/10/05/cbk-non-deposit-taking-credit-providers-regulations/)). They revoke the DCP Regulations 2022 (LN 46). I could not open the gazetted text. Kenya Law and Bowmans both returned 403, and CBK's legislation page still lists only the 2025 draft ([CBK legislation page](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/policy-procedures/legislation-and-guidelines/)). The LN number and the publication date of about 29 Sep 2026 are therefore **(unverified against the primary text)**.
- The rules have 11 parts: licensing, registration, operations, consumer protection, AML/CFT, reporting and oversight, an enforceable Code of Conduct, enforcement, review and transition ([CBK RIS](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Regulatory-Impact-Assessment-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf)).

**Who is covered.**
- The rules cover all non-deposit-taking credit business not regulated under another written law. The draft excludes banks, microfinance institutions licensed under the Microfinance Act, licensed SACCOs and KPOSB. It also excludes credit that is "merely incidental" to selling goods or services, and hire-purchase agreements governed by the Hire-Purchase Act, which are carved out of the BNPL definition ([CBK draft, Part I and the permissible activities list](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Draft-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf)). Whether these carve-outs survive in the final text is **(unverified)**. They matter because they may take most hire-purchase sellers and merchant-financed PAYG out of scope.
- Press coverage of the draft named BNPL firms, hire-purchase operators, credit guarantors, P2P platforms and PAYG lenders as affected ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/cbk-sets-threshold-for-micro-lender-licences-5146584), [Daba Finance](https://dabafinance.com/en/news/kenya-credit-market-regulation-overhaul)).
- The applicant must be a company incorporated under the Companies Act ([CBK draft](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Draft-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf)). Sole-trader moneylenders ("shylocks") must incorporate first.

**Licence or registration.**
- Initial capital above KES 20m means a licence. Below KES 20m means registration. A registered firm must convert to a licence once its capital, borrowings or loan book exceeds KES 20m ([CBK draft, Parts II–III](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Draft-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf)). Whether the threshold is the same in the final text is **(unverified)**.
- Transition: anyone already lending must apply within six months of publication and may keep trading while CBK decides. DCPs licensed under the 2022 rules continue "as if licensed" under the new Regulations. Pending applications are processed under the new Parts II/III ([CBK draft, Transition](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Draft-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf)). The final deadline is reported as 29 March 2027 ([Bowmans](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/kenya-non-deposit-taking-credit-provider-regulations-are-here-what-lenders-need-to-know/)).

**What must be filed at application** ([CBK draft, Part II](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Draft-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf)):
- Form CBK NDTCP 1, plus fit-and-proper forms NDTCP 2 and 3 for directors, the CEO, senior officers and significant shareholders.
- A Data Protection Act s.19 registration certificate (from ODPC) and a statement on compliance with Part VII of the Consumer Protection Act.
- A credit policy, a code of conduct, a consumer protection policy, an AML/CFT policy, a data protection policy and a corporate governance policy.
- Pricing parameters, product terms, an ICT system description, delivery channels, and a complaints redress mechanism.
- A sworn source-of-funds declaration, plus a good conduct certificate, tax compliance certificate and CRB report for each director, officer and significant shareholder.
- For a licence: three years of audited accounts.

**Ongoing duties** ([CBK draft, Parts IV, V, VII](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Draft-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf)):
- Pay the annual fee by 31 December.
- Submit an annual return by 31 December certifying compliance with the Act and Regulations.
- Submit periodic returns "as the Bank may specify". These cover complaints, number of loans, audited financial statements, shareholders and directors, borrowings, bank and paybill accounts, number of agents, outsourced services and NPLs.
- Get CBK's prior written approval for any new product or change to a product, including interest rates, and give customers 30 days' notice ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/thugge-raises-compliance-fees-non-deposit-taking-credit-firms-5617420)).
- Notify CBK 30 days before a new delivery channel.
- Get CBK approval for agents and renew it annually, keep an agent register, and notify CBK of outsourcing.
- Keep a complaints register. Acknowledge complaints within 7 days, confirm unresolved oral complaints in writing after 48 hours, resolve within 30 days, and report complaints to CBK.
- Share credit information with CRBs and give borrowers 30 days' notice before listing them.
- Meet AML/CFT duties under POCAMLA, and meet new rules on AI and automated lending decisions (Reg. 60) ([TechTrendsKE](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/10/05/cbk-non-deposit-taking-credit-providers-regulations/)).
- Returns go through CBK's Bank Supervision Application (BSA) portal. A 2025 law-firm alert lists outstanding credit returns, quarterly financial statements, annual audited accounts, consumer protection reports and AML/CFT reports ([CM Advocates, via search summary](https://cmadvocates.com/blog/legal-alert-upcoming-bank-supervision-application-bsa-user-training-for-non-deposit-taking-credit-providers-stay-compliant/)). Exact frequencies and deadlines are **(unverified)**; I could not open the page (503).

**Fees** ([CBK draft, Second Schedule](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Draft-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf); confirmed for the final rules by [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/thugge-raises-compliance-fees-non-deposit-taking-credit-firms-5617420)):
- Application fee: KES 100,000 (was KES 5,000).
- Licence: KES 500,000 a year (was KES 20,000).
- Registration: KES 250,000 a year.
- New in the final rules: a KES 1m penalty for missing the 31 December fee deadline ([TechTrendsKE](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/10/05/cbk-non-deposit-taking-credit-providers-regulations/)).

**Penalties.**
- Under the draft, CBK can impose up to KES 2m, or three times the gain made or loss avoided, plus up to KES 10,000 a day while a breach continues. It can also remove officers, disqualify directors and suspend or revoke licences ([CBK draft, Part IX](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Draft-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf)).
- A licence can be revoked for failing to resolve complaints conclusively or for unjustifiable charges ([Spencer West](https://www.spencer-west.com/?p=7535)).
- Lending without a licence or registration is prohibited.

**Enforcement evidence.**
- CBK has run a three-stage licensing process since 2022 and asks the public to report unregulated lenders to dcps@centralbank.go.ke ([CBK press release](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/press_releases/1035898107_Press%20Release%20-%20Licensing%20of%20Digital%20Credit%20Providers%20-%20July%202026.pdf)).
- The data regulator (ODPC) actively fines lenders:
  - Whitepath: KES 250k in 2025, after KES 5m in 2023 ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/03/13/whitepath-fined-2000-for-unlawful-data-use/)).
  - Mulla Pride: KES 2.9m ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/?p=120609)).
  - Rocketpesa: KES 700k for abusive debt collection in 2026 ([Spencer West](https://www.spencer-west.com/?p=7535)).
  - ODPC has also asked CBK to revoke two lenders' permits ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/dpo-asks-cbk-to-revoke-permits-for-two-digital-lenders-5162924)).
- The Competition Authority fined Mogo Kenya about USD 84k in 2024 ([search summary of TechCabal/Daba](https://dabafinance.com/en/news/kenya-fines-fintech-elevings-local-unit-in-digital-lending-probe)).
- I found no CBK monetary penalty published against a DCP **(unverified)**.

**Parallel regime.** Non-deposit-taking microfinance businesses (NDTMBs) were pulled in under the Microfinance Act. Their six-month window to apply closed on 27 June 2025 ([Spencer West](https://www.spencer-west.com/?p=7535)). A Microfinance Bill 2026 is also in Parliament ([Bowmans](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/kenya-microfinance-bill-2026-key-implications-for-microfinance-banks/)). The boundary between NDTMB and NDTCP is a source of confusion for lenders, and a guidance product could resolve it.

## Buyers

- **Licensed DCPs (already in):** 252 as of July 2026 ([CBK press release](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/press_releases/1035898107_Press%20Release%20-%20Licensing%20of%20Digital%20Credit%20Providers%20-%20July%202026.pdf)) and 281 by September 2026 ([TechTrendsKE](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/10/05/cbk-non-deposit-taking-credit-providers-regulations/)). They do not need to reapply. They now face the higher annual fee and the stricter ongoing duties, so they are buyers for recurring compliance tooling, not for the application pack.
- **Pending applicants:** CBK has received more than 800 applications since March 2022 ([CBK press release](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/press_releases/1035898107_Press%20Release%20-%20Licensing%20of%20Digital%20Credit%20Providers%20-%20July%202026.pdf)). With about 281 licensed, roughly 500 are pending, withdrawn or rejected; the split is **(unverified)**. CBK says the remaining applicants are "largely awaiting the submission of requisite documentation". This is the warmest buyer group.
- **Newly in-scope offline lenders:** logbook lenders, asset financiers, BNPL, P2P, credit-only lenders and moneylenders. I found no official count. One commercial site says about 250 providers were on a CBK non-deposit credit register as of April 2026 ([paybillke](https://paybillke.com/guides/logbook-loan-scams-and-red-flags-kenya-2026)) **(unverified)**. FinAccess 2024 shows informal credit use at 5.2% of adults ([FSD Kenya](https://www.fsdkenya.org/blogs-publications/2024-finaccess-household-survey-key-insights-into-kenyas-financial-landscape/)), but it gives no lender count. Logbook lending is growing fast ([Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/logbook-loans-gain-traction-as-alternative-financing-option-n376784)).
- **Realistic total:** a few hundred to about 1,000 firms need the application pack before March 2027. About 300 to 1,000 then need ongoing compliance support. All of this is my estimate **(unverified)**.
- **How they comply today:** Larger firms use law firms (Bowmans, CDH, CM Advocates, Oraro, Spencer West) and their own compliance staff. Smaller firms rely on an accountant or self-file through CBK's portal and BSA system. CBK runs BSA training ([CM Advocates](https://cmadvocates.com/blog/legal-alert-upcoming-bank-supervision-application-bsa-user-training-for-non-deposit-taking-credit-providers-stay-compliant/)). The CBK backlog suggests that many smaller applicants cannot produce the documents unaided.

## Competition

- **Free state tools:** the CBK online licensing process and the BSA returns portal. They take submissions but do not draft policies, track deadlines or run a complaints register. CBK publishes a DCP licensing procedures note ([CBK procedures, Oct 2024](https://centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Procedures-for-licensing-Digital-Credit-Providers-Revised-October-2024.pdf)). No free state tool covers the full job.
- **Law firms:** Bowmans, Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr, CM Advocates, Oraro and Spencer West all publish NDTCP and DCP alerts and sell licensing work ([Oraro](https://www.oraro.co.ke/operationalisation-of-the-registration-of-digital-credit-providers/), [CM Advocates](https://cmadvocates.com/blog/how-to-start-up-a-fintech-company-in-kenya/)). I found no published prices **(unverified)**.
- **Loan-management systems** claim to handle regulatory reporting: SuperLMS ([Super Systems](https://supersystems.co.ke/)), Lendsqr, Presta and Craft Silicon. I found no published Kenyan SaaS prices. One article put a custom lending module at USD 27k–53k as a build cost ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/microfinance-loan-management-software-cost-nairobi-2026)). These vendors could add a CBK compliance module.
- **KYC/AML tools:** YouVerify (from KES 8,000 a month) ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/nigerian-startup-youverify-launches-in-kenya-4247756)) and Zigram ([Zigram](https://www.zigram.tech/?p=37915)). Both cover AML screening only.
- **Dedicated NDTCP compliance or application-pack product:** none found, in English or in Swahili searches. A 2026 Chambers guide says Kenya has "no established practices on regtech" ([Chambers Fintech 2026](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/fintech-2026/477/)).

## Willingness to pay

- Buyers already face KES 100k to apply and KES 250k–500k a year in CBK fees ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/thugge-raises-compliance-fees-non-deposit-taking-credit-firms-5617420)). A KES 50k–150k application pack is small next to that.
- The cost of getting it wrong is high. Breaches can cost up to KES 2m plus KES 10k a day ([CBK draft](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Draft-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf)). A late fee costs KES 1m. ODPC fines run from KES 250k to 5m. Lending without a licence or registration is prohibited.
- Law-firm licensing mandates likely cost several hundred thousand KES or more **(unverified; no published prices found)**.
- **Plausible pricing:**
  - KES 60k–150k (about USD 450–1,150) for a one-off application pack with the policy set, forms checklist and fit-and-proper workbook.
  - KES 5k–15k a month for an ongoing compliance calendar, complaints register, product-change request templates and annual certification workpapers.
  - A premium tier with lawyer review, sold through a partner firm.
- The smallest lenders may refuse any spend, because KES 350k in year-one CBK fees already strains them.

## Channels

- **Associations:** the Digital Lenders Association of Kenya (DLAK) ([People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/business/dlak-mulls-code-of-ethics-over-licensing)) and the Association of Microfinance Institutions of Kenya (AMFI-K), which litigated the DCP rules in 2022 ([Bowmans](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/kenya-dcp-regulations-to-apply-to-non-deposit-taking-microfinance-institutions/)). An asset-finance or logbook lenders association was not found **(unverified)**.
- **Public lists:** CBK publishes the names of licensed DCPs with each licensing round ([CBK press release](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/press_releases/1035898107_Press%20Release%20-%20Licensing%20of%20Digital%20Credit%20Providers%20-%20July%202026.pdf)). It must publish an annual list of licensed and registered NDTCPs by 31 March ([CBK draft, Part VII](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Draft-Central-Bank-of-Kenya-Non-Deposit-Taking-Credit-Providers-Regulations-2025.pdf)). These lists are a ready target list for recurring tools.
- **Partners:**
  - Law firms that write NDTCP alerts could resell a pack or take referrals.
  - Accountancy and audit firms (ICPAK network) serve small lenders.
  - LMS vendors (SuperLMS, Lendsqr, Presta) could bundle the pack.
  - ODPC data-protection consultants already sell the s.19 registration step.
- **Content:** a plain-language "NDTCP 29 March 2027 checklist" in English and Swahili, aimed at logbook and asset-finance lenders through Business Daily and TechTrendsKE coverage, LinkedIn and Nairobi fintech events.

## Risks

- **Short window.** The application rush ends about 29 March 2027, under six months away. After that the market shrinks to annual certification, complaints and returns.
- **Small, shrinking pool.** The big fee rise (from KES 20k to 500k a year) may push micro lenders out or underground. Licensed DCPs do not need the application pack.
- **Scope carve-outs.** Hire purchase under the Hire-Purchase Act and credit incidental to a sale are excluded in the draft. That removes much of the PAYG and hire-purchase segment **(final text unverified)**.
- **Text not verified.** I could not read LN 191. The threshold, return frequencies and deadline may differ from the draft.
- **Legal-practice limits.** Selling drafted legal policies for a fee may run into Advocates Act restrictions on unqualified persons preparing legal instruments **(unverified)**. Template packs with a partner-advocate review tier reduce this risk.
- **Liability.** A pack that fails CBK review harms the customer, and CBK has rejected or stalled hundreds of applications.
- **Competitors adding the feature.** LMS vendors could add CBK return templates cheaply. CBK could also publish model policies.
- **Language.** English works for this audience; regulatory documents and filings are in English.

## First product

**Version 1, an "NDTCP Application Kit" (web app plus document generator):**
1. A scope and tier checker. It asks whether the firm is in scope (versus NDTMB, hire purchase or incidental credit) and whether it needs a licence or registration (the KES 20m test), then shows fees and the deadline countdown.
2. A guided questionnaire that generates the required policy set from the firm's answers: credit policy, code of conduct, consumer protection, complaints redress, AML/CFT, data protection and corporate governance.
3. A document checklist tracker with status for each person: certificate of good conduct, KRA tax compliance certificate, CRB report, fit-and-proper Forms NDTCP 2/3, ODPC registration certificate, and pricing parameters sheet.
4. A complaints register that applies the 7-day, 48-hour and 30-day rules and exports a CBK complaints report.
5. A compliance calendar: annual fee and certification by 31 Dec, agent renewals, the product-change approval log and 30-day customer notices.

**First 30 days:**
- Week 1: get LN 191 from the Kenya Gazette or Kenya Law and diff it against the 2025 draft. Map every "shall" to a checklist item.
- Week 2: write the policy templates, with a partner advocate reviewing them for a fee or revenue share. Build the scope checker and checklist in a simple web app.
- Week 3: build the complaints register and calendar, and a Swahili explainer page.
- Week 4: pilot with 5–10 pending DCP applicants or logbook lenders found through LinkedIn, DLAK and law-firm referrals. Price the pack at about KES 75k with a money-back guarantee if CBK raises a documentation query that the pack should have covered.
