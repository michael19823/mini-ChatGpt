# Paraguay A1: SIRO compliance kit for pawnshops, jewellers and gold buyers

## Re-assessment (owner's criteria)

**Verdict: maybe. New score: 5/10 (old score: 3/10).**

**The case.** Jewellers and pawnshops alone are too few (about 131 paying SIRO users) and face no enforcement, so they cannot carry a business on their own. The same duties apply, almost word for word, to vehicle dealers (Res. 196/20, which the lead's buyer list already includes) and to real-estate firms (Res. 201/20). Together that is about 2,400 paying firms, and SEPRELAD does enforce on them: in 2024 it warned 1,692 of the 2,043 firms it checked through SIRO ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)). SIRO only receives filings. It does not keep the manual, the risk assessment, the KYC file, the calendar or the inspection evidence, and small firms key their operations reports one by one. I found no local product for this. Willingness to pay is the open question, because the state fee buyers know is only about US$30-45 a year.

### Room for improvement over the portal or current practice

- **Most of the duty sits outside SIRO.** Res. 196/20 and Res. 201/20 require a risk assessment every 2 years, a manual, a code of ethics, a compliance officer, an annual internal review within 90 days, an external audit within 180 days, and KYC with thresholds ([Ferrere on Res. 196/20](https://ferrere.com/es/novedades/nuevo-reglamento-de-prevencion-de-lavado-de-activos-y-financiamiento-del-terrorismo-para-personas-fisicas-o-juridicas-involucrad/); [Ferrere on Res. 201/20](https://ferrere.com/es/novedades/nuevo-reglamento-de-prevencion-de-lavado-de-activos-y-financiamiento-del-terrorismo-para-empresas-y-personas-involucradas-en-la/)). SIRO is a reporting channel, not a record-keeping tool ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)).
- **Missed deadlines are common.** SEPRELAD's 2024 remote check found failures at 1,692 of 2,043 firms, about 83%. The failures were missing negative reports, operations reports and annual forms, and 1,238 real-estate firms and 454 vehicle dealers got warning notes ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)). A deadline calendar with reminders targets this directly.
- **Annual-form filing is low.** Only 357 vehicle dealers and 526 real-estate firms filed the annual form in 2024 ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)). Compare 845 and 1,451 firms that paid the SIRO fee in 2025 ([SIRO statistics](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/siro/estadisticaExterna/estadistica.xhtml), per the first pass). These figures come from different years, so the gap is indicative only.
- **Registration in SIRO fails often.** Of 2,042 registration requests in 2024, 532 were annulled for not meeting SIRO's requirements, about 26% ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)).
- **Users ask for help.** SEPRELAD's help tool, E-porandu, answered 855 questions between 14 Aug and Dec 2024. The top topics were SIRO access, registration requirements, and how to send an operations report or a suspicious-operations report. The listed users include consultants, advisers, lawyers, accountants and external auditors ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)), which suggests intermediaries already do this work for clients.
- **No bulk upload for small firms.** SIRO takes operations reports as a JSON file, but only from firms with "hundreds or thousands" of operations a month and their own system. Small firms type each operation into a form ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)). A tool that prepares, validates and exports that JSON, or a clean list for keying, is a real data-preparation gap. Whether SEPRELAD opens the JSON route to third-party tools is unverified.
- **Fee arrears are common.** SEPRELAD had to run a fee amnesty (Res. 226/2024) for unpaid SIRO fees from earlier years ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)).
- **The rules keep changing.** In 2025 a new real-estate operations-report form was approved (Res. 03/25), and SEPRELAD began rewriting the pawnshop resolution under the GAFILAT action plan ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)). Each change means template and form updates, which a subscription can deliver.

### Competitor reality check

- **SIRO** is free apart from a yearly fee of about Gs 217,000-334,000. It handles registration, filing and the fee. It does not hold the manual, risk matrix, KYC files, training records or a calendar ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)). It complements a product rather than replacing it.
- **No local software found.** Five searches in Spanish found no Paraguayan AML compliance software for small obligated subjects. Searches for SIRO integration tools also found nothing. Foreign tools such as CONLAFT target Argentina ([CONLAFT brochure](https://www.cancilleria.gob.ar/userfiles/ut/conlaft_espanol.pdf)). Shufti Pro and TheKYB sell identity checks only ([Shufti](https://shuftipro.com/supported-countries/paraguay/); [TheKYB](https://thekyb.com/our-data/paraguay/)).
- **The real competitors are people.** SEPRELAD's register had 64 newly admitted specialist external auditors in 2024 ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)). Accountants and law firms (Ferrere, Amaral) also work in this space. Their prices are not published (unverified). They are better treated as a sales channel than as a rival.
- **SEPRELAD itself** gives free training (27 events and 533 participants in 2024) and is building a supervision module ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)). That raises pressure to comply but does not do the firm's own record-keeping.

### Price per customer

- **Anchors.**
  - The SIRO fee is about Gs 217,000-334,000 a year (US$30-45), which is low ([SIRO statistics](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/siro/estadisticaExterna/estadistica.xhtml), per the first pass).
  - Fines under Ley 1015/97 reach up to 500 minimum wages for people and 5,000 for companies ([Ferrere](https://ferrere.com/es/novedades/inspecciones-aleatorias-de-seprelad-bajo-resolucion-36-21/)). With a minimum wage of Gs 2,899,048 from July 2025, 500 minimum wages is about Gs 1.45bn ([Decreto 4122/2025](https://impuestospy.com/impuestos/decreto-n-4122-2025/)). In practice SEPRELAD has issued warnings, with only one fine in 2025.
  - The other anchor is the owner's or bookkeeper's time to write a manual and a biennial risk assessment, and the cost of an outside consultant to do it. Consultant prices were not found (unverified).
- **Suggested prices** (all unverified until tested):
  - **Single firm:** about Gs 150,000-250,000 a month (about US$20-33). This covers the calendar, templates, KYC register and an evidence folder.
  - **Accountant, auditor or consultant plan:** about Gs 1,000,000 a month (about US$130) for up to 25 client entities.
  - **One-off setup pack:** about Gs 1-2m (about US$130-260) for a manual, code of ethics and risk matrix.
- **Blended estimate:** about US$300 per firm per year, and about US$1,500 per multi-client professional per year.

### Ease of implementation and sale

- **Build: low to medium difficulty.**
  - The core is plain software: a calendar per resolution, document templates, a KYC register with minimum-wage thresholds, and an evidence log.
  - The JSON export for operations reports depends on a SIRO schema I could not see (unverified).
  - The three resolutions (196/20, 201/20, 222/20) share one structure, so one template engine covers all of them ([Ferrere on Res. 196/20](https://ferrere.com/es/novedades/nuevo-reglamento-de-prevencion-de-lavado-de-activos-y-financiamiento-del-terrorismo-para-personas-fisicas-o-juridicas-involucrad/); [Ferrere on Res. 201/20](https://ferrere.com/es/novedades/nuevo-reglamento-de-prevencion-de-lavado-de-activos-y-financiamiento-del-terrorismo-para-empresas-y-personas-involucradas-en-la/)).
- **Onboarding: easy.** It is a questionnaire, then generated documents, then reminders. The product needs no integration with a client's own systems.
- **Sale: medium difficulty.**
  - SEPRELAD's public register of obligated subjects gives a lead list ([Ferrere](https://ferrere.com/es/novedades/la-serpelad-crea-un-modulo-de-consultas-para-verificar-el-catastro-de-sujetos-obligados-registrados-en-el-siro/)).
  - The 1,692 warned firms are a warm segment.
  - Registered auditors and accountants are a natural channel.
  - Buyers are price-sensitive and used to a US$45 state fee (unverified willingness to pay).

### Remaining risks

- **Low willingness to pay.** The only enforcement so far is warning notes, plus one fine in 2025 ([SIRO statistics](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/siro/estadisticaExterna/estadistica.xhtml), per the first pass). Firms may tolerate warnings rather than pay.
- **SEPRELAD could extend SIRO** with a supervision module, and might add calendar or reminder features ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)).
- **Liability.** SEPRELAD could reject a generated manual or risk matrix. Selling "compliant" documents carries professional-liability risk.
- **Informal firms.** Many jewellers and small car lots may never register.
- **Thin pricing data.** No consultant or auditor fees were found. Every price above needs 10-15 customer interviews to confirm.
- **Upside risk.** The GAFILAT action plan is rewriting the pawnshop rules ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)). A new evaluation round could raise enforcement on all non-financial obligated subjects (unverified).

## Summary

**Verdict: no-go. Score: 3/10.**

The duty is real. Jewellers have Res. SEPRELAD 222/20, and pawnshops and vehicle dealers have the 2023 SIRO resolutions. Both bring a compliance officer, a manual, KYC, quarterly negative reports and an annual internal review.

The idea fails on market size. SEPRELAD's own SIRO statistics show that only **108 jewellers** and **23 pawnshops** paid the annual SIRO fee in 2025. Jewellers filed only 166 negative reports that year, which is about 40 firms filing every quarter. SEPRELAD recorded **zero sanctions** against jewellers or pawnshops from 2022 to 2026.

The state SIRO portal is free to use beyond a fee of about Gs 330,000 (about US$45) a year. Filing a negative report there takes minutes.

The wider group of unsupervised obligated subjects is bigger, and SEPRELAD does enforce against it: 845 vehicle dealers and 1,451 real-estate firms paid the fee in 2025, and 1,692 of them got warning notes in 2024. That wider group is a different idea, and only a "maybe".

## Duty

- **Legal basis.** Ley 1015/97 makes pawnshops and dealers in jewellery, stones and precious metals obligated subjects. It is also the source of the sanctions ([Ferrere, Res. 36/21](https://ferrere.com/es/novedades/inspecciones-aleatorias-de-seprelad-bajo-resolucion-36-21/)). Pawnshops also have their own sector law, Ley 2283/2003 ([Justia](https://paraguay.justia.com/nacionales/leyes/ley-2283-nov-10-2003/gdoc)).
- **Jewellers: Res. SEPRELAD 222/20.** Ferrere's note on Res. 222/20 lists these duties ([Ferrere, 27 Jan 2021](https://www.ferrere.com/es/novedades/nuevo-reglamento-de-prevencion-de-lavado-de-activos-y-financiamiento-del-terrorismo-para-comerciantes-de-joyas-piedras-y-metales)):
  - A risk assessment at least every 2 years, with the method verified every 4 years.
  - A senior compliance officer. In a sole proprietorship, the owner can hold the role.
  - A manual and a code of ethics.
  - An annual internal-review report to SEPRELAD within 90 days of year-end.
  - An external audit only if turnover is above about Gs 9.2bn (about US$1.3m), due within 180 days.
  - Full KYC from 30 minimum wages (Gs 65.8m, about US$9,470). Simplified KYC up to 50 minimum wages a year.
  - ROS filings, plus a negative report after any 3 months with no ROS.
- **Jewellers: registration.** Jewellers had to register or re-register in SIRO from 19 Sep 2022. Older certificates became void on 1 Jan 2023 ([Ferrere](https://ferrere.com/es/novedades/nuevo-procedimiento-para-registro-como-sujetos-obligados-del-sector-comercio-de-joyas-piedras-y-metales-preciosos/)).
- **Pawnshops, vehicle dealers and others.** SEPRELAD resolutions of Feb-Mar 2023 require pawnshops, vehicle importers and sellers, notaries, remitters and political organisations to get SIRO access and to file ROS and negative reports there ([Ferrere, 24 Apr 2023](https://ferrere.com/es/novedades/seprelad-amplia-la-implementacion-del-sistema-de-reporte-de-operaciones-sospechosas-para-varias-categorias-de-sujetos-obligados/)). The resolution numbers and the negative-report cadence for pawnshops are not given (unverified). I found no pawnshop-specific AML rule like Res. 222/20 (unverified).
- **Vehicle dealers: Res. 196/2020.** This rule has a compliance officer, operations reports (RO, art. 31) and negative reports (art. 37). Res. 246/2022 adds an annual form due 31 May ([SEPRELAD Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)).
- **Fees.** Obligated subjects with an active SIRO user pay a yearly fee (canon), set each year. The 2024 fee was set by Res. 30 of 25 Jan 2024, and pawnshops are named in it. Registration fees are set by Res. 007/2018, 235/2020 and 29/2023 ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)).
- **Since 2024, SIRO is the only channel.** SEPRELAD says SIRO has been the only channel for these reports since 2024 ([SIRO statistics module](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/siro/estadisticaExterna/estadistica.xhtml)).
- **Penalties.** Ferrere summarises the Ley 1015/97 sanctions ([Ferrere](https://ferrere.com/es/novedades/inspecciones-aleatorias-de-seprelad-bajo-resolucion-36-21/)):
  - Natural persons: a warning, a public reprimand, a fine of up to 500 minimum wages, or 1-10% of the operation.
  - Legal persons: a fine of up to 5,000 minimum wages, or up to 50% of the operation, and suspension or revocation.
- **Enforcement.**
  - Random inspections began in late 2021 under Res. 36/21. They check for a manual, a named compliance officer and staff training records ([Ferrere](https://ferrere.com/es/novedades/inspecciones-aleatorias-de-seprelad-bajo-resolucion-36-21/)).
  - In 2024, SEPRELAD remotely checked 2,043 obligated subjects through SIRO. It sent warning notes to 1,692 of them: 1,238 real-estate firms and 454 vehicle dealers. The warnings were for missing negative reports, operations reports and annual forms ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)).
  - The SIRO sanctions table shows 0 sanctions in 2022 and 2023, and 1,694 warning notes in 2024, all against vehicle dealers and real-estate firms. In 2025 there was 1 fine, on a vehicle dealer, now under appeal. 2026 shows 0. **Jewellers and pawnshops: none in any year** ([SIRO statistics](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/siro/estadisticaExterna/estadistica.xhtml), queried 9 Oct 2026).
- **Changes.** Paraguay passed its GAFILAT mutual evaluation in July 2022 and is under enhanced follow-up. Its recommendations were due by July 2025 ([SEPRELAD IEM page](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/?page_id=2039); [PJ](https://www.pj.gov.py/notas/22516-paraguay-aprobo-el-examen-del-gafilat)). A new evaluation round could tighten DNFBP supervision, but I found no specific new rule for jewellers or pawnshops (unverified).

## Competition

- **SIRO (free, state-run).** It handles registration, ROS, negative reports, the annual form, external-audit uploads and fee payment. SEPRELAD keeps adding modules, including a supervision module and a public lookup of registered subjects ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)). **It already covers the filing job itself.**
- **SEPRELAD free training.** 27 events in 2024, plus a Q&A tool called "E-porandu" ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)).
- **Registered specialist external auditors.** 64 applicants were admitted in 2024 under Res. 218/2020 ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)). These firms sell audits and, very likely, manuals and risk matrices (unverified).
- **Law and consulting firms.** Ferrere publishes guidance notes on these rules. Amaral offers AML training to real-estate firms, nonprofits and car dealers ([InfoNegocios / Amaral](https://infonegocios.com.py/amp/default/capacitacion-en-prevencion-de-lavado-obligacion-legal-para-algunos-ventaja-estrategica-para-todos)). I found no prices.
- **Compliance-officer certification.** The BNF/FOTRIEM programme runs 120 hours, approved under Res. 889/21. Its price is not published ([FOTRIEM](https://www.fotriem.edu.py/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Ref.-17-BNF-Programa-Certificacion-en-Prevencion-de-Lavado-de-Activos-en-Paraguay-Formacion-de-Oficial-de-Cumplimiento.pdf)).
- **Generic KYC vendors.** Shufti Pro and TheKYB claim Paraguay coverage but sell identity checks, not a compliance kit ([Shufti](https://shuftipro.com/supported-countries/paraguay/); [TheKYB](https://thekyb.com/our-data/paraguay/)).
- **What is missing.** Four searches found no Paraguayan SaaS or template pack for small DNFBPs.

## Willingness to pay

- **SIRO costs little.** Firms pay only about Gs 217,000-334,000 a year, roughly US$30-45 ([SIRO statistics](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/siro/estadisticaExterna/estadistica.xhtml)). That fee is the price anchor buyers already know.
- **Fines are large on paper but absent in practice.** The legal maximum reaches 500 or 5,000 minimum wages. Yet jewellers and pawnshops have had zero sanctions, and the 2024 sweep against other sectors produced only warning notes ([SIRO statistics](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/siro/estadisticaExterna/estadistica.xhtml)).
- **Consultant prices were not found** (unverified). A plausible guess is a one-off manual and risk matrix for Gs 1.5-5m (US$200-650), plus an optional yearly retainer (unverified).
- **The ceiling for this niche is tiny.** 130 firms at US$15/month gives about US$23,000 a year, if every one of them bought.

## Channels

- **SEPRELAD.** Its training tour and its public register of obligated subjects in SIRO can be used to build a lead list ([Ferrere](https://ferrere.com/es/novedades/la-serpelad-crea-un-modulo-de-consultas-para-verificar-el-catastro-de-sujetos-obligados-registrados-en-el-siro/)).
- **Registered specialist external auditors** (64 admitted in 2024) and accountants could resell the product or refer clients.
- **CADAM**, the dealers' chamber, for vehicle dealers. Its stated training focus is sales and service, not AML ([ABC](https://www.abc.com.py/edicion-impresa/suplementos/comercial/60-aniversario-cadam-2025/2025/06/24/fortalece-la-cadena-de-valor-automotriz-en-paraguay/)).
- **Real-estate associations** (not researched).
- **Jewellers' associations.** I found none (unverified).

## Risks

- **Market too small (killer).** About 130 active jewellers and pawnshops in total.
- **No enforcement pressure.** These two sectors have zero sanctions. SEPRELAD's sweeps target real estate and vehicles.
- **Free state tool.** SIRO already covers filing, and SEPRELAD is extending it, for example with a supervision module and automated operations reports ([Memoria 2024](https://www.seprelad.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024.pdf)).
- **Liability.** Selling a "compliant" manual or risk matrix carries professional liability if SEPRELAD rejects it.
- **The auditors are in the way.** Registered auditors already sit between the regulator and the firms that would buy.
- **Informality.** Many jewellers are artisanal and may never register.

## First product

The case is a no-go for this niche. If you pursue anything, widen it to all SEPRELAD-direct subjects: real estate, vehicles, jewellers, pawnshops and possibly nonprofits. That is about 2,400 paying commercial firms. A first version would:

1. Run a compliance calendar for each sector: quarterly negative reports, the annual form by 31 May, the internal review within 90 days, the risk assessment every 2 years, and yearly training. It would send WhatsApp and email reminders.
2. Generate a manual, a code of ethics and a risk matrix from a questionnaire, per sector resolution (196/20, 201/20, 222/20).
3. Keep a KYC register with threshold alerts at 30 and 50 minimum wages, and keep records for 5 years.
4. Provide a short training module with a certificate as inspection evidence.

For the first 30 days:

- Interview 10 real-estate firms and car dealers that received the 2024 warning notes.
- Build the calendar and reminder service.
- Draft the vehicle-dealer manual template.
- Pilot it with 2 registered auditors.
