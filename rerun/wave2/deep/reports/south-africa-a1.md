# South Africa: an annual FICA compliance pack for high-value goods dealers in jewellery and precious metals (south-africa-a1)

## Re-assessment (owner's criteria)

**Verdict: maybe (leaning go if a pilot sells). New score: 6/10. Old score: 4/10.**

Re-assessed 9 October 2026. Budget used: 12 web searches and 7 page fetches. None was blocked.

**The case.** The buyer base is now counted, and it is bigger than the first pass feared. At 31 March 2025 the FIC had 5,184 registered item 20 dealers: 4,072 motor, 386 precious metals (including Krugerrands), 220 precious stones and 506 other goods ([FIC Annual Report 2024/25, pp. 26 and 30](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf)). Their compliance is poor: only 54% to 83% filed the risk and compliance return, and only 1,451 of them sent in an RMCP when the FIC asked in March 2025 ([same, pp. 30-31](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf)). Inspection pressure on jewellers is high: 90 of the FIC's 556 inspections in 2024/25 were of precious metal or stone dealers, out of about 606 registered ([same, p. 35](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf)). No product found does the whole yearly job (RMCP, deadline calendar, transaction and report register, inspection file). A simple annual pack at about R9,000 a year could reach roughly R3m to R4m a year by year 3 across all item 20 dealers.

### Room for improvement over the portal or current practice

- **goAML is only a filing channel.** It takes the RMCP upload and the reports. It does not draft the RMCP, keep a CDD file, track deadlines or build an inspection file. A successful upload does not even mean the FIC accepts the RMCP ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/?p=74912)).
- **Low filing rates show real pain.** Risk and compliance returns received by 31 March 2025: motor dealers 2,183 of 4,072 (54%), precious metals 231 of 386 (60%), other goods 285 of 506 (56%), precious stones 182 of 220 (83%) ([FIC AR 2024/25, p. 30](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf)).
- **Few RMCPs were filed.** When the FIC asked for RMCPs by 12 March 2025, only 9,281 of all 55,262 registered institutions sent one. High-value goods dealers sent 1,451, about 28% of 5,184 ([FIC AR 2024/25, p. 31](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf)). Directive 12 now makes this a yearly filing, due 31 October for item 20 ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61635)).
- **Portal access is a known problem.** Many institutions filed on the last day, so the FIC accepted late RMCPs but called them non-compliant. The FIC said it "continues to receive numerous enquiries on how to access goAML", mostly password and login resets ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-accepting-rmcps-after-the-12-march-deadline/)).
- **Thin RMCPs.** Many RMCPs are only three or four pages and fail the FIC's test ([DealerFloor](https://dealerfloor.co.za/industry-news/seven-key-points-to-become-fully-fica-compliant); [FAnews](https://www.fanews.co.za/article/compliance-regulatory/2/general/1082/fica-reality-check-2026-brings-reckoning-for-sa-s-high-value-goods-dealers/43138)).
- **Real reporting volume to manage.** Item 20 dealers filed 10,037 cash threshold reports and 28,028 suspicious and unusual transaction reports in 2024/25 ([FIC AR 2024/25, p. 28](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf)). A register that flags R50k+ cash and keeps the CDD copies has a clear job.
- **More filings keep arriving.** Directive 10 (every office location, due about 29 October 2026), Directive 12 (yearly RMCP) and draft PCC 126 (one registration per SADPMR permit) all add work in 2026 ([Acts Online](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2026/08/fic-directive-10-geographic-location-reporting); [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61913)).
- **Multi-entity work.** Dealer groups and jewellers with several permits or branches need one registration per permit under PCC 126 and must list every branch under Directive 10. That suits per-entity and per-consultant pricing.

### Competitor reality check

- **nCino KYC (formerly DocFox).** Sells paperless client verification, watchlist screening, risk rating and a compliance helpdesk to motor dealers. Its dealer page does not mention RMCP drafting, goAML filing, cash report tracking or deadline tracking. No price or customer count is published ([nCino KYC](https://blog.kycafrica.ncino.com/the-best-motor-dealerships-across-south-africa-are-already-using-ncino-kyc-africa-heres-why-yours-should-too)). It covers the CDD part well, not the yearly filing part. A new product should complement it, not fight it.
- **Moonstone FICA Toolkit.** R4,995 excl. VAT for a generic RMCP template, risk register and legal library. It dates from June 2023. Help to build the RMCP is billed hourly, five hours minimum ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/new-do-it-yourself-fica-compliance-solution-for-accountable-institutions/)). Moonstone has no bullion-dealer template ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=57175)). It has no calendar, register or yearly update. Partial.
- **ClearComply.** From R99 a month. Tracks deadlines and FICA registration status, but shows no RMCP drafting or goAML features ([ClearComply](https://www.clearcomply.co.za/blog/fica-compliance-south-africa)). Partial.
- **Law firms and consultancies (mjkinc, GoLegal, acpas).** Custom RMCPs and advice. mjkinc lists fee pages, but no rand figures are shown for this work ([mjkinc](https://mjkinc.co.za/rmcp)). Prices unverified. Bespoke and not recurring software.
- **Conclusion.** Every incumbent does one slice. None is a well-priced product that does the whole yearly job for a dealer. This is an opening, not a killer.

### Price per customer

- **What they face today.**
  - FIC fines: 118 fines of R10,000 each were paid in 2024/25 under the FIC's "admission of non-compliance" process. The 25 formal sanctions on non-financial businesses totalled R782,000 ([FIC AR 2024/25, p. 37](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf)). Listed sanctions on other sectors for not filing the risk and compliance return ran at R10,000 to R50,000 each ([same, p. 38](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf)). The legal maximum is R50m for a company ([same](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf)).
  - DIY: R4,995 template plus at least five consultant hours ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/new-do-it-yourself-fica-compliance-solution-for-accountable-institutions/)). Law-firm RMCP work is probably R10,000 to R30,000 (unverified).
- **Suggested pricing.**
  - Single dealer: R750 a month or R8,000 to R9,000 a year. This covers the RMCP builder and yearly update, the deadline calendar, the transaction and report register and the inspection file.
  - Each extra branch or permit: R250 a month.
  - Consultants and accountants: about R2,500 a month for up to 10 dealer clients, then R200 per client (unverified willingness).
  - Optional: a one-off RMCP review by a partner attorney at R3,000 to R5,000 (unverified).
- **Why this works.** R9,000 a year is below one R10,000 FIC fine and close to the cost of a template plus five consultant hours.

### Ease of implementation and sale

- **Build: easy to medium.** A guided RMCP questionnaire, a deadline calendar, a simple register and a PDF inspection file. No goAML integration is needed at first; the dealer uploads the files. Work is in English.

- **Sale: medium.** Deadlines create clear buying moments (31 October RMCP, RCR window, Directive 10). ## Summary

**Verdict: maybe. Score: 4/10.**

The duty is real and it now recurs every year. Since 19 December 2022, a dealer who sells any single item for R100,000 or more counts as an accountable institution under FICA Schedule 1 item 20. That dealer must register on goAML, keep an RMCP and file several kinds of report ([Webber Wentzel](https://webberwentzel.com/News/Pages/worth-their-weight-in-gold-high-value-goods-dealers-fall-within-fica-ambit.aspx)). Two new 2026 directives add yearly paperwork. Final Directive 12 makes these dealers resubmit their RMCP through goAML by 31 October every year ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61635)). Directive 10 makes them report every office location by about 29 October 2026 ([Acts Online](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2026/08/fic-directive-10-geographic-location-reporting)). Draft PCC 126 (30 September 2026) brings in the whole precious-metal and stone chain and asks for one FIC registration per SADPMR permit ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61913)).

The weak point is the market. The Jewellery Association covers about 1,000 retailers and 140 wholesalers ([LBMA](https://www.lbma.org.uk/publications/spotlight-on-the-south-african-market/chapter-5-jewellery-and-retail-investment)). Only some of them sell single items of R100k or more. Cheap partial substitutes already exist: a R4,995 RMCP template and generic KYC software. So this looks like a small niche service, or a vertical add-on, rather than a stand-alone SaaS business. It gets stronger if it widens to all item 20 dealers (motor, art, antiques, coins).

## Duty

- **Legal basis.** FICA (Act 38 of 2001), Schedule 1 item 20. This covers any business that receives payment of R100,000 or more for an item of that value, whether in one payment or in linked payments. It has been in force since 19 December 2022 ([Webber Wentzel](https://webberwentzel.com/News/Pages/worth-their-weight-in-gold-high-value-goods-dealers-fall-within-fica-ambit.aspx); [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/new-accountable-institutions-have-until-19-march-to-register-with-the-fic)).
  - The test is per item. A jeweller whose stock is mostly cheaper still qualifies if it sells one ring for R100k.
  - Labour charges do not count towards the threshold.
  - Underpricing goods to stay below the threshold may itself breach FICA ([Moonstone, FIC HVGD guidance](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-issues-guidance-on-what-is-a-high-value-goods-dealer/)).
  - Krugerrand dealers used to be Schedule 2 reporting institutions. They moved into item 20, with transition rules in PCC 58 ([SAIT](https://thesait.org.za/tax-chronicles-issue-67-february-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2)).
- **What must be done.**
  - Register on goAML within 90 days of starting business ([accountingacademy](https://www.accountingacademy.co.za/news/read/fic-accountable-institutions-must-register-under-correct-item-numbers)).
  - Keep an RMCP and appoint a compliance function (s42, s42A).
  - Do customer due diligence and identify beneficial owners.
  - Screen customers against targeted financial sanctions lists before accepting them, and again whenever the lists change.
  - Report cash of R50,000 or more (s28), suspicious transactions (s29) and sanctioned property (s28A) ([Moonstone, PCC 126](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61913); [FAnews](https://www.fanews.co.za/article/compliance-regulatory/2/general/1082/fica-reality-check-2026-brings-reckoning-for-sa-s-high-value-goods-dealers/43138)).
  - Train staff.
- **Recurring returns.**
  - **Risk and compliance return (RCR).** Directive 7 applies it to item 20 dealers ([farmingportal](https://farmingportal.co.za/index.php/farming-news/viewpoint/12066-viewpoint-how-are-high-value-goods-dealers-hvgds-and-high-value-goods-hvgs-dealt-with-in-terms-of-the-fica-act)). The 2026 round (Directive 11) closed on 31 July 2026 ([Engineering News](https://engineeringnews.co.za/article/have-you-started-your-directive-11-rcr-submission-yet-2026-07-16)).
  - **Annual RMCP submission (Directive 12).**
    - Gazette 55337, published 4 September 2026, in force from 7 September.
    - It covers items 1, 2, 3, 9, 11, 14, 20, 21 and 22.
    - For item 20 the deadline is 31 October 2026, then 31 October every year.
    - The RMCP must be approved by the board or the most senior person and uploaded on goAML.
    - An amended RMCP is due within 10 days of approval ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61635); [Accounting Weekly](https://www.accountingweekly.com/financial-intelligence-centre/fic-issues-directive-12-your-rmcp-must-now-be-sent-to-the-fic-every-year)).
    - **This settles the "9 vs 31 Oct" question from the lead.** 9 October applies to items 1, 2, 9 and 11. Item 20 has 31 October.
    - A successful upload does not mean the FIC has approved the RMCP ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/?p=74912)).
  - **Geographic footprint (Directive 10).**
    - Gazetted and in force on 31 July 2026.
    - Existing registrants have 90 days to report the head office, all branches, subsidiaries and their branches. For each location they give the name, licence number, address and compliance contact.
    - Acts Online and Accounting Weekly give 29 October 2026 as the deadline. nCino KYC gives 31 October ([Acts Online](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2026/08/fic-directive-10-geographic-location-reporting); [Accounting Weekly](https://www.accountingweekly.com/financial-intelligence-centre/directive-10-the-fic-now-wants-every-office-address); [nCino KYC](https://blog.kycafrica.ncino.com/fic-updates-directive-10-draft-directive-12-guidance-note-7b)). 29 October matches the 90-day count.
    - Any change must be reported within 90 days (per Accounting Weekly).
    - Whether item 20 is expressly listed is reported by nCino KYC, and is otherwise unverified against the gazette.
- **Upcoming change: draft PCC 126.** Issued 30 September 2026, with comments closing 16 October 2026 ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61913)).
  - **Who is covered.** Miners, refiners, manufacturers, wholesalers, retailers, importers and exporters, brokers, cutters and polishers, jewellery manufacturers, and "secondary market" buyers and sellers.
  - **Metals.** Gold, silver and platinum-group metals, in forms that include bullion and Krugerrands.
  - **Stones.** Diamonds and coloured stones.
  - **Threshold.** R100k per metal, stone or bundle sold as one unit. Separate goods are not added together.
  - **Registration.** One FIC registration for each SADPMR authorisation where item 20 applies, such as a jeweller's permit, an import permit or a diamond dealer licence.
  - **Extra due diligence.** Check the client's permits, the source of the goods and the source of funds.
  - **Gaps.** It does not expressly name scrap or second-hand ("cash for gold") buyers (unverified whether "secondary markets" catches them).
  - An earlier draft, PCC 119, also mentioned some second-hand dealers ([SAIT](https://thesait.org.za/tax-chronicles-issue-67-february-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2-2)) (unverified; status unclear).
- **Penalties and enforcement.**
  - **Legal limits.** Administrative sanctions under s45C run up to R10m for a natural person and R50m for a legal person per breach (DocFox figure from about 2023, via [FAnews search summary](https://www.fanews.co.za/article/compliance-regulatory/2/general/1082/fica-reality-check-2026-brings-reckoning-for-sa-s-high-value-goods-dealers/43138)) (unverified against the Act).
  - **In practice.** Fines start at R10,000 for failing to complete an online form. "Dozens" of motor dealers have been sanctioned, and about 90% of those penalties were R100k or more (per [FAnews](https://www.fanews.co.za/article/compliance-regulatory/2/general/1082/fica-reality-check-2026-brings-reckoning-for-sa-s-high-value-goods-dealers/43138), Jan 2026).
  - **Inspections.** Dealers that missed RCRs face targeted inspections ([farmingportal](https://farmingportal.co.za/index.php/farming-news/viewpoint/12066-viewpoint-how-are-high-value-goods-dealers-hvgds-and-high-value-goods-hvgs-dealt-with-in-terms-of-the-fica-act)).
  - **No jeweller cases found.** I found no published sanction against a jeweller or gold dealer. The FIC sanctions page URL I tried returned 404.
  - **Pressure stays on.** Scrutiny continues after South Africa left the FATF grey list, ahead of the 2026 FATF review ([Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/do-not-wait-for-the-fic-to-come-knocking-2026-03-05)).

Their number is unknown, and most single buy-ins are under R100k.
- **Estimate (unverified).**
  - Jewellery, bullion and precious-metal businesses that meet the per-item test: perhaps 300 to 1,000.
  - Adding motor dealers, art, antiques and boats, the whole item 20 group is likely several thousand. Motor dealers are the largest group ([nCino KYC](https://blog.kycafrica.ncino.com/fica-compliance-no-longer-optional-for-south-africas-automotive-industry)).
- **How they comply today.** Mostly through law firms and consultancies (mjkinc, GoLegal, acpas), DIY templates, generic KYC software, or not at all. The FAnews piece describes common failures: RMCPs of only a few pages, missing beneficial-owner checks and unreported cash over R49,999.99 ([FAnews](https://www.fanews.co.za/article/compliance-regulatory/2/general/1082/fica-reality-check-2026-brings-reckoning-for-sa-s-high-value-goods-dealers/43138)).

It has a code of ethics and runs mediation, so it is a natural partner for a member compliance pack ([Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-12-11-know-your-carats-dodge-crooks-cash-in-on-gold-safely.md); [LBMA](https://www.lbma.org.uk/publications/spotlight-on-the-south-african-market/chapter-5-jewellery-and-retail-investment)).
- **Trade media.** *The Jeweller* (thejeweller.co.za) publishes the JCSA annual reports ([The Jeweller](https://thejeweller.co.za/jewellery-council-of-south-africa-jcsa-publishes-2023-annual-report/)).
- **SADPMR permit holders.** The draft PCC 126 links FIC registration to SADPMR permits, so SADPMR mailers or outreach could reach them (unverified whether SADPMR shares lists).
- **Refiners and bullion houses** such as Metcon, which publish their own compliance policies ([Metcon](https://www.metcon.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/RAC-POL-001-Responsible-Jewellery-Council-Compliance-Policy-Iss-1.1.pdf)). They could refer their small trade customers.
- **Accountants and bookkeepers** who serve jewellers.
- **Content timed to deadlines.** Content around the 31 October RMCP deadline and the PCC 126 consultation. Moonstone, GoLegal and Engineering News all run FICA news that ranks well.
- **Wider item 20 groups.** Motor dealers through dealer bodies (unverified names) and DealerFloor ([DealerFloor](https://dealerfloor.co.za/industry-news/vehicle-dealers-from-reporting-to-accountable-institutions)).

## Risks

- **Small market.** The per-item R100k test excludes many small jewellers. No official count exists.
- **Strong incumbent next door.** nCino KYC already sells to item 20 dealers and could bundle an RMCP. Moonstone could add a jeweller template.
- **Draft guidance.** PCC 126 may change after 16 October 2026. Its reach into scrap and second-hand buyers is unclear.
- **Liability.** A template RMCP that fails an FIC inspection could expose the seller. The FIC warns that an RMCP must reflect the business's own risks ([nCino KYC](https://blog.kycafrica.ncino.com/breaking-the-myths-around-fica-outsourcing)). Disclaimers and a review step by a qualified person are needed.
- **Regulator tooling.** goAML could add RMCP or footprint wizards (unverified; none announced). It already accepts RMCP uploads.
- **Compliance fatigue and avoidance.** Some dealers may underprice or split sales to stay below R100k, although the FIC treats this as non-compliance ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-issues-guidance-on-what-is-a-high-value-goods-dealer/)).
- **Language and licensing.** These are low. Work is in English. No licence is needed to sell compliance software, but legal advice may touch on the attorneys' reserved work (unverified).

## First product

**Version 1: an annual compliance pack for item 20 dealers, sold per year.**

1. **Applicability check.** A short quiz covering the per-item R100k test, the SADPMR permits held, and one registration per permit under PCC 126. It tells the dealer whether they are in scope and under which item and sub-category, including Krugerrands.
2. **RMCP builder.** A guided questionnaire that produces a jeweller, bullion or motor-specific RMCP. It covers the risk assessment, CDD, sanctions screening, record-keeping, s28, s29 and s28A reporting, and training. The output is a PDF ready for board sign-off, named to the goAML upload rules.
3. **Deadline calendar.** RMCP on 31 October each year. Directive 10 footprint within 90 days of any change. RCR window. Amended RMCP within 10 days. Email and SMS reminders.
4. **Transaction register.** A simple log of R100k+ sales and R50k+ cash. It flags when a cash report is due and keeps CDD copies, giving the dealer an inspection-ready file.
5. **Step-by-step goAML guides** for each filing.

**First 30 days.**
- Week 1: submit comments on PCC 126 by 16 October to learn the issues. Interview 10 jewellers and bullion dealers through JCSA contacts.
- Weeks 2 and 3: build the applicability quiz and the RMCP questionnaire. Have a FICA attorney review the RMCP template.
- Week 4: launch a "31 October RMCP" landing page with a fixed-price pack, about R3,500 to R5,000 including review. Do the first sales by hand.
