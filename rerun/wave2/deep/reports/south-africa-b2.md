# South Africa B2: compliance pack for small estate agencies (PPRA trust-account audit, FIC registration, RMCP and RCR)

## Re-assessment (owner's criteria)

**Verdict: maybe. New score: 5/10 (old score: 5/10).**

**The case.** About 9,086 estate-agent registrations sit on the FIC's goAML system, and only 6% of them sent in their RMCP when the FIC asked in March 2025 ([FIC web notice, 10 Mar 2025](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2025.3-WN-Update-on-Compliance_Submission-of-RMCP-.pdf)). That gap is now a yearly duty: Directive 12 makes every estate agent upload an approved RMCP to goAML by 31 October ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-revises-annual-rmcp-deadlines-in-final-directive-12/)), on top of the trust audit, the RCR and FFC renewal. The free portals only take filings, so there is real room for a records, evidence and deadline tool. But the field is no longer empty. Realty Comply (R999 a month plus R499 per seat, IEASA partner), eFICA (RMCP Builder R3,500 a year) and Entegral Vault (pay per check) already cover parts of the job ([Realty Comply](https://www.realtycomply.com/); [eFICA](https://efica.co.za/); [Entegral](https://www.entegral.net/blog/product-updates/introducing-vault-simplify-your-fica-compliance-and-onboarding/)). The opening is a cheap, deadline-led tool for one- and two-person agencies and for the accountants and auditors who serve many of them, which would make a small but real business.

### Room for improvement over the portal or current practice

- **goAML is a filing channel only.** Agents must submit the RMCP on goAML and check its message board daily ([FIC notice](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2025.3-WN-Update-on-Compliance_Submission-of-RMCP-.pdf)). It does not draft the RMCP, keep training or screening logs, or remind anyone of deadlines ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-revises-annual-rmcp-deadlines-in-final-directive-12/)).
- **Evidence of portal and process pain:**
  - Only 568 of 9,086 estate-agent registrations (6%) had sent their RMCP by 10 Mar 2025, against a 12 Mar deadline. The figure for all sectors was 6% ([FIC notice](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2025.3-WN-Update-on-Compliance_Submission-of-RMCP-.pdf)).
  - About 70% of estate agents had filed the earlier RCR by April 2025, so about 30% had not ([STBB](https://stbb.co.za/?p=40089)).
  - goAML registrations get rejected for missing ID copies and missing business fields. Password resets, login changes and adding users fill the FIC's call lines ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-registration-turmoil/); [Accounting Academy](https://accountingacademy.co.za/news/read/fic-goaml-login-information)). These reports are older (2016-2019).
  - An RCR cannot be edited or withdrawn once filed, so mistakes go through a formal FIC query ([Nanogram](https://www.nanogram.co.za/blog1.html)). This makes checking the data before filing worth paying for.
  - IEASA sells paid webinars on how to submit the RMCP under Directive 12 ([Quicket](https://www.quicket.co.za/events/395674-ieasa-webinar-how-to-submit-rmcp-the-step-by-step-guidance-on-the-new-directive/)). Consultants such as MJK Inc sell FIC help to estate agents ([MJK](https://mjkinc.co.za/fica/estate-agents)).
- **Inspection readiness.** Agencies are now expected to produce their RMCP, risk assessments and due-diligence files at once during inspections ([BusinessTech, May 2026](https://businesstech.co.za/news/finance/861026/government-cracking-down-on-estate-agents-in-south-africa/)). A fine of R266,000 was upheld because compliance existed "only on paper" (same source).
- **Many dates, many bodies.** A new principal must handle CIPC, PPRA firm and principal registration, FFCs, tax clearance, B-BBEE, a trust account, an IRBA auditor, FIC registration, an RMCP, CDD, CPD and FFC renewal between 1 Jul and 31 Oct ([ProCompare](https://www.procompare.co.za/articles/accountants/ppra-compliance-roadmap-new-principal)). No single free tool tracks these.
- **RCR date fixed.** Two sources give 31 July 2026 for estate agents under Directive 11 ([STBB](https://stbb.co.za/newsflash-the-fic-issues-directive-11-on-2026-risk-and-compliance-return-submissions/); [Nanogram](https://www.nanogram.co.za/blog1.html)). This replaces the "unverified" date in the first pass.

### Ease of implementation and sale

- **Build: medium.** A deadline calendar, questionnaire-driven RMCP builder, evidence logs and checklists are plain web software. All in English. No licence is needed. The hard part is legal content that keeps up with the FIC: Directive 12 dates moved between draft and final ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-revises-annual-rmcp-deadlines-in-final-directive-12/)), and the FIC punishes paper-only programmes ([BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/finance/861026/government-cracking-down-on-estate-agents-in-south-africa/)). A paid compliance reviewer is needed.
- **Onboarding: easy.** One questionnaire, then a calendar and checklists. No integration with goAML is needed or possible; the user still uploads by hand.
- **Sale: medium.** The 31 Oct RMCP and 31 Aug audit deadlines create yearly buying moments. But IEASA already partners with Realty Comply ([Realty Comply](https://www.realtycomply.com/)), so the easiest trade-body channel is taken. REBOSA, small audit firms and accountants are the open routes (unverified).

## Summary

**Verdict: maybe. Score: 5/10.**

South African estate agencies face a stack of recurring duties. There is a yearly trust-account audit under the Property Practitioners Act. Under the FIC Act they must register with the FIC, keep an RMCP and file a Risk and Compliance Return (RCR). From 2026 a new FIC Directive 12 adds a yearly RMCP upload to goAML, due 31 October. The penalties are real: the FIC upheld a R266,000 fine on a small agency, and the PPRA can block a Fidelity Fund Certificate (FFC), without which an agent cannot trade. No product I found runs this whole workflow for a small agency. The rivals are template kits (Moonstone, R4,995), a free REBOSA template, KYC tools, trust-accounting software and consultants. The case is held back by three things. The buyer pool is small: about 9,000 estate agents are counted in FIC material, and about 65% of agencies are small. Small agencies spend little. And the trust audit itself must be done and filed by an IRBA auditor, so software can only help prepare for it. A low-priced compliance calendar plus RMCP and evidence tool, sold through IEASA and auditors, is plausible as a small side business. It is unlikely to grow large.

## Duty

**1. PPRA trust-account audit (Property Practitioners Act 22 of 2019)**
- Section 54 requires business practitioners to keep separate trust accounts. The trust and business accounts must be audited every year ([acts.co.za s54](https://www.acts.co.za/property-practitioners-act-2019/54__trust_account); [IEASA/Quicket](https://quicket.co.za/events/301709-ieasa-webinar-how-to-comply-with-audits-and-trust-accounts-18-march-2025)).
- The report is due within 6 months of financial year-end. The old law allowed 4 months ([accountingacademy](https://accountingacademy.co.za/news/read/2022-trust-account-audits-property-practitioners)). For a 28 Feb 2025 year-end the cut-off was 31 Aug 2025, and the PPRA said it would grant no extensions ([acts.co.za blog](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2025/06/ppra-2025-audit-report-submissions); [accountingacademy 2024](https://accountingacademy.co.za/news/read/property-practitioners-regulatory-authority-ppra-2024-trust-account-audit-reports-submission-reminder)).
- Only IRBA-registered assurance auditors may audit and file. They file on the MyPPRA Auditors Portal (theppra.org.za/myaudit) using IRBA illustrative report templates from March 2023 ([accountingacademy guidelines](https://accountingacademy.co.za/news/read/property-practitioners-guidelines-on-audit-and-trust-accounts); [IRBA illustrative reports](https://www.irba.co.za/upload/25_%20PPRA%20illustrative%20reports.pdf); [myproperty FAQ](https://myproperty.co.za/faq/industry/audit-report-trust-account)).
- The PPRA's guideline on audit, accounting records and trust accounts dates from May 2022 ([accountingacademy](https://accountingacademy.co.za/news/read/property-practitioners-guidelines-on-audit-and-trust-accounts)).
- **Penalties.** Section 54(6) allows a late filing on payment of a prescribed penalty ([acts.co.za s54](https://www.acts.co.za/property-practitioners-act-2019/54__trust_account)). A PPRA article from Dec 2023 gives R20 per day for the first three months, plus a further R25,000 fine if the report is still missing three months after the deadline ([accountingacademy reprint](https://accountingacademy.co.za/news/read/ppra-the-property-practioner-s-landscape-reminder-of-late-submission-penalties)). One secondary source cites Regulation 38 as the basis (unverified, since I did not read the gazetted regulation text).
- **FFC block.** Practitioners with missing audit reports get "disqualification blocks" that can stop FFC renewal. In May 2026 the PPRA issued a notice removing certain 2026 audit-report blocks once reports are filed and penalties are paid ([Accounting Weekly, 26 May 2026](https://www.accountingweekly.com/trending-news/ppra-removes-certain-2026-audit-report-disqualifications)).
- **Exemption.** Regulation 2(1) of the 2022 Regulations lets a practitioner who holds no trust money, or who uses an accredited payment processing agent, apply by affidavit for an exemption. Exempt firms need only an independent review by an accountant, not a full audit ([acts.co.za reg 2](https://www.acts.co.za/property-practitioners-act-2019/proc47_2__exemption_from_trust); [PayProp](https://www.payprop.com/za/blog/why-getting-trust-account-exemption-is-the-smartest-business-decision-you-can-make-this-year)). The number of exempt firms is not published (unverified).

**2. FIC Act 38 of 2001 duties (estate agents are Schedule 1, item 3)**
- **Registration** on goAML within 90 days of starting business ([globallawexperts](https://globallawexperts.com/fic-compliance-south-africa/); [Moonstone on item numbers](https://www.moonstone.co.za/accountable-institutions-must-ensure-they-are-correctly-registered/)).
- **A compliance function** under s42A. One adviser argues the compliance officer role cannot be fully outsourced ([nCino KYC Africa](https://blog.kycafrica.ncino.com/breaking-the-myths-around-fica-outsourcing)).
- **An RMCP** under s42, written to Guidance Note 7A, which has now been replaced by GN 7B ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/?p=74912); [Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/best-agent-ceo-warns-estate-agencies-face-serious-fica-compliance-risks-398468a)).
- **Directive 12** (Gazette 55337, 4 Sep 2026, in force 7 Sep 2026): an approved RMCP must be uploaded to goAML every year. Estate agents are due **31 October**; the draft had said 30 Sep. Each Schedule 1 registration needs its own submission. Amended RMCPs must be uploaded within 10 days of approval. A successful upload does not mean the FIC has approved the RMCP. The FIC refused a transition period ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-revises-annual-rmcp-deadlines-in-final-directive-12/)).
- **Directive 10**: geographic particulars must be updated on goAML by 31 Oct 2026 ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/?p=74912); [nCino KYC Africa](https://blog.kycafrica.ncino.com/fic-updates-directive-10-draft-directive-12-guidance-note-7b)).
- **RCR, Directive 11 of 2026**: covers the period 1 Apr 2023 to 31 Mar 2026. The window opened 4 May 2026, and STBB gives a due date of 31 Jul 2026 ([STBB](https://stbb.co.za/newsflash-the-fic-issues-directive-11-on-2026-risk-and-compliance-return-submissions/)). Other sources give 30 Jun 2026 for some categories ([Accounting Weekly](https://www.accountingweekly.com/financial-intelligence-centre/fic-2026-rcr-deadline-is-coming-are-you-ready)), so the estate-agent date is unverified. The RCR has been issued about every 2-3 years, under Directives 6 and 7 in 2023 and Directive 11 in 2026. It is not yet yearly (unverified).
- **FIC sanctions**: up to R10m for a natural person and R50m for a legal person ([globallawexperts](https://globallawexperts.com/fic-compliance-south-africa/)). Capital Point Properties was fined R266,000: R108,000 for having no RMCP, R108,000 for no TFS screening and R50,000 for breaching Directive 6. The Appeal Board upheld it in Aug 2024 ([Accounting Weekly](https://www.accountingweekly.com/trending-news/fic-upholds-penalties-on-estate-agency-for-compliance-failures); [BusinessTech, 23 May 2026](https://businesstech.co.za/news/finance/861026/government-cracking-down-on-estate-agents-in-south-africa/)). One source says more than half of published FIC sanctions in a year were for RCR non-submission, mostly by lawyers and estate agents (unverified; [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/accountable-institutions-face-being-fined-for-not-submitting-their-risk-and-compliance-returns/)).
- **FFC link**: practitioners with weak RMCPs or outdated reporting risk FFC delays or refusals, and inspectors expect timestamped records, training registers and screening evidence ([Property Professional, 26 May 2026](https://propertyprofessional.co.za/2026/05/26/warning-to-estate-agents-fic-enforcement-is-no-longer-optional/)). This comes from a law-firm commentator, not a PPRA rule (partly unverified).

**3. Other duties in the same pack.** These are FFC renewal, POPIA and PAIA, and CPD ([IEASA A-Z compliance webinar](https://www.quicket.co.za/events/358861-ieasa-webinar-a-to-z-of-compliance-in-an-estate-agency-17-february-2026)). FFCs ran on a 3-year cycle that was renewed in Jul to Nov 2025 ([Property Professional](https://propertyprofessional.co.za/2025/01/27/ppra-2025-a-year-of-challenges-and-opportunities/); [Accounting Weekly](https://www.accountingweekly.com/trending-news/property-practitioners-ffc-renewal-extended-to-30-november-2025)). Employee FFCs cannot be issued until the principal is compliant (same source). Since Jan 2026 each practitioner gets one FFC covering all their firms ([Accounting Weekly](https://www.accountingweekly.com/trending-news/ppra-news-one-fidelity-fund-certificate-now-covers-all-your-firms)).

  - A late audit costs R20 a day for 3 months, about R1,800, then a further R25,000 ([PPRA via accountingacademy](https://accountingacademy.co.za/news/read/ppra-the-property-practioner-s-landscape-reminder-of-late-submission-penalties)).
  - An FIC fine can be R266,000, as in the Capital Point case ([Accounting Weekly](https://www.accountingweekly.com/trending-news/fic-upholds-penalties-on-estate-agency-for-compliance-failures)).
  - A blocked FFC stops trading ([Property Professional](https://propertyprofessional.co.za/2026/05/26/warning-to-estate-agents-fic-enforcement-is-no-longer-optional/)).
- **Current spend:** R4,995 for a once-off template kit ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/new-do-it-yourself-fica-compliance-solution-for-accountable-institutions/)). Webinars cost R300-400. Trust audit fees were not found (unverified).
- **Plausible price:** R200-450 per month, or R2,500-4,500 a year, for a one-office agency. Add-on hours could be sold for the RMCP review. This is a judgement, not a tested price (unverified). At about 1,000 paying firms it would bring in roughly R3-4.5m a year.

## Channels

- **IEASA:** runs paid compliance webinars on audits, RMCP and Directive 12 ([Quicket Directive 12](https://www.quicket.co.za/events/395674-ieasa-webinar-how-to-submit-rmcp-the-step-by-step-guidance-on-the-new-directive/); [Quicket audits](https://quicket.co.za/events/301709-ieasa-webinar-how-to-comply-with-audits-and-trust-accounts-18-march-2025)). Sponsoring or co-presenting is a direct route.
- **REBOSA:** already publishes the free RMCP template, so it is a natural partner ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/new-do-it-yourself-fica-compliance-solution-for-accountable-institutions/)).
- **Small IRBA audit firms and SAAA/SAICA accountants** who audit agencies. Accounting Weekly and Accounting Academy cover each PPRA deadline ([Accounting Weekly tag](https://www.accountingweekly.com/trending-news/tag/PPRA)). Auditors could resell the tool or refer clients to it.
- **Trade media:** Property Professional, MyProperty and PropData Standpoint.
- **Franchise groups:** head offices could license the tool for their small franchisees (unverified).
- **Timing:** sell before the 31 Oct Directive 12 deadline and before the 31 Aug audit deadline for February year-ends.

## Risks

- **Small, thrifty market.** There are about 9,000 FIC-registered estate agents in total ([STBB](https://stbb.co.za/?p=40089)). Many one-person agencies will settle for the free REBOSA template.
- **Audit is the auditor's job.** The filing is done by IRBA auditors ([accountingacademy](https://accountingacademy.co.za/news/read/property-practitioners-guidelines-on-audit-and-trust-accounts)), and the trust-exemption route reduces the number of audits ([PayProp](https://www.payprop.com/za/blog/why-getting-trust-account-exemption-is-the-smartest-business-decision-you-can-make-this-year)).
- **The FIC dislikes generic RMCPs.** Compliance "on paper only" was itself sanctioned ([BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/finance/861026/government-cracking-down-on-estate-agents-in-south-africa/)). A tool that churns out templates could expose users to liability and expose the vendor to reputational harm.
- **Incumbents adding modules.** KYC vendors (nCino, TVA) or trust software (PropWorx, PayProp) could add an RMCP module.
- **Rule churn.** Directive deadlines changed between draft and final ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-revises-annual-rmcp-deadlines-in-final-directive-12/)), and the RCR is periodic, not yearly. This is a content-upkeep cost, but it also means buyers need help.
- **Regulator-built tools.** The PPRA is improving its portal ([Accounting Weekly](https://www.accountingweekly.com/trending-news/tag/PPRA)) but has a poor IT record and suffered a ransomware attack in 2025 (same source). A full state compliance tool looks unlikely soon (unverified).
- **Low barriers otherwise.** Everything is in English and no licence is needed to sell compliance software. Legal advice must be framed carefully.

## First product

**Version 1: "Agency Compliance Calendar and RMCP Pack" (web app)**

1. Onboarding questionnaire covering firm size, sales or rentals, trust account or exemption, financial year-end and FIC Org ID. It produces a personal calendar of PPRA audit, Directive 12, Directive 10, RCR, FFC and CPD dates.
2. A guided RMCP builder following GN 7B: a business risk assessment questionnaire produces a tailored RMCP with a version history and an approval record signed by the principal. It exports a PDF named and formatted for the goAML upload, plus a step-by-step upload checklist.
3. An evidence vault with timestamped logs: staff training register, TFS screening log (links to the free FIC TFS list), CDD file checklist and internal STR decision log.
4. Audit-prep pack: a checklist of documents the auditor needs (bank statements, monthly trust reconciliations, interest on trust account, deposit register), an auditor contact and handoff, and a "report filed?" confirmation before the 6-month deadline.
5. An RCR prep worksheet that gathers likely answers ahead of the next directive.

**First 30 days:**
- Week 1: read Directive 12, GN 7B, Directive 11, the PPRA audit guideline and the IRBA templates. Interview 10 agency principals and 3 auditors.
- Week 2: build the questionnaire, the calendar with email and WhatsApp reminders, and the RMCP generator.
- Week 3: build the evidence logs and the audit checklist. Have one compliance practitioner review the content.
- Week 4: run a pilot with 10-20 agencies before 31 October. Pitch an IEASA or REBOSA webinar slot and two audit firms as resellers.
