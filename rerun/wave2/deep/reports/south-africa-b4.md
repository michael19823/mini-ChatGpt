# South Africa b4: annual RMCP filing kit for small law firms and other Schedule 1 institutions

## Summary

**Verdict: maybe. Score: 5/10.**

The duty is real, final and yearly. FIC Directive 12 took effect on 7 September 2026. Small law firms, non-bank credit providers, trust and company service providers and gambling businesses must upload a board-approved RMCP through goAML by 9 October each year. High-value goods dealers, estate agents and a few others must upload by 31 October ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-revises-annual-rmcp-deadlines-in-final-directive-12/)). On top of that, Guidance Note 7B (3 August 2026) replaced the old RMCP guidance, so most existing RMCPs now need revising ([nCino KYC](https://blog.kycafrica.ncino.com/fic-updates-directive-10-draft-directive-12-guidance-note-7b)). About 9,300 law firms and roughly 7,000 credit providers are the core buyers. Nearly all are tiny and price-sensitive. The case is weakened by four things. Free tools cover a lot of the job: the FIC's own template for small businesses (PCC 53), the LSSA's free 55-page guide and the goAML upload itself. nCino KYC (formerly DocFox) is entrenched with attorneys through an LSSA partnership. The FIC and LSSA both say a generic template is not compliance. And typical fines on small firms are low (about R10,000 per admission-of-non-compliance matter in 2025/26). A cheap, guided "tailor, approve, upload, remind" tool could sell. It would be a small, seasonal business, not a large one.

## Duty

- **Legal basis.**
  - Section 42 of the FIC Act (38 of 2001) requires every accountable institution to "develop, document, maintain and implement" an RMCP. The board or highest authority must approve it (s42(2A)), and it must be reviewed at regular intervals (s42(2C)) ([LSSA guide, p.2 and p.5](https://www.lssa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Final-Draft-RMCP-Guidelines-6-5-25-Final.pdf)).
  - The FIC issues directives under s43A, after consulting on a draft under s42B ([Gazette 55097, GN 7749, 31 Jul 2026](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202608/55097gon7749.pdf)).
  - Sanctions fall under s45C ([acts.co.za](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2026/09/fic-directive-risk-management-compliance-programme-submissions)).
- **Directive 12, final version.**
  - Published in Government Gazette 55337 on 4 September 2026 and effective 7 September 2026 ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-revises-annual-rmcp-deadlines-in-final-directive-12/)).
  - Items 1 (legal practitioners), 2 (trust and company service providers), 9 (gambling) and 11 (credit providers other than bank-group credit providers) file by **9 October each year**.
  - Items 3 (estate agents), 14 (Postbank), 20 (high-value goods dealers), 21 (SA Mint) and 22 (crypto asset service providers) file by **31 October each year**.
  - New institutions file within 90 days of starting business. A revised RMCP must be filed within 10 days of approval. The FIC refused to limit this to "material" changes.
  - Each Schedule 1 item registration needs its own submission. There is no small-firm exemption and no transition period.
  - The FIC will publish a goAML user guide on file naming and format. It had not appeared when Moonstone wrote its article. A successful upload only proves the file format; it does not mean the FIC approves the RMCP (all from [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-revises-annual-rmcp-deadlines-in-final-directive-12/)).
  - I could not read Gazette 55337 itself; the final details come from secondary sources. The primary draft I did read proposed 30 September and 31 October ([GG 55097](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202608/55097gon7749.pdf)).
- **Related duties a kit could bundle.**
  - *Directive 10:* existing institutions in the same items must add head-office and branch locations on goAML. The source gives the deadline as 31 October 2026 ([nCino KYC](https://blog.kycafrica.ncino.com/fic-updates-directive-10-draft-directive-12-guidance-note-7b)).
  - *Directive 11 RCR:* a separate self-assessment filed via goAML. It covers three years, and items 1, 3, 9 (non-casinos) and 20 had to file it by 31 July 2026 ([acts.co.za](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2026/04/fic-directive-11-2026-rcr-submission); [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-flags-filing-errors-as-rcr-deadline-closes/)). It is not a yearly return.
  - *Registration:* changes must be notified within 90 days (s43B). The LPC asks for the FIC Org ID when a practice is registered and in the annual Fidelity Fund certificate statements ([LSSA guide, p.5](https://www.lssa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Final-Draft-RMCP-Guidelines-6-5-25-Final.pdf)).
- **Content change.** Guidance Note 7B (3 August 2026) replaced GN 7, 7A and Revised 7A with immediate effect. It adds proliferation financing and a risk assessment for new products, and it says low-income clients are not automatically low risk ([nCino KYC](https://blog.kycafrica.ncino.com/fic-updates-directive-10-draft-directive-12-guidance-note-7b)).
- **Enforcement.**
  - In 2025/26 the FIC issued 549 inspection reports, 169 of them on legal practitioners and 76 on credit providers.
  - It issued 361 admission-of-non-compliance notices. 149 were finalised for R1.49m in total, about R10,000 each by my calculation, and 212 went to the Adjudication Panel.
  - It imposed 28 enforcement sanctions on non-financial institutions; the 19 finalised totalled R635,000.
  - The two most common deficiencies were having no RMCP and having an RMCP that does not meet FICA ([Moonstone, FIC 2025/26 annual report](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61901)).
  - The largest sanction on an attorney firm was R7.7m against Kunene Ramapala Inc, upheld by the Appeal Board, partly for having no RMCP ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/r7-7m-fine-stands-as-fic-appeal-board-rules-against-law-firm/)).
  - In March 2026 the High Court barred the FIC from backdating penalties to before 19 December 2022, in a case where Len Dekker Attorneys faced about R235,000 ([Accounting Weekly](https://www.accountingweekly.com/trending-news/high-court-clips-fic-power-to-backdate-penalties); summary via search, page not opened).
  - Statutory maximums are R10m for natural persons and R50m for legal persons ([ClearComply](https://www.clearcomply.co.za/blog/fica-compliance-south-africa)).
- **Upcoming.** The FIC says further directives may extend yearly RMCP submission to all other accountable institutions, including financial institutions ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-revises-annual-rmcp-deadlines-in-final-directive-12/)).

## Buyers

- **Total registered.** 60,115 reporting entities were registered at 31 March 2026, up from 55,262. The FIC supervises more than 80% of them. 11,147 RMCPs were submitted to the FIC in 2025/26, before Directive 12 made filing yearly ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61901)).
- **Law firms (item 1).** For 2026 the LSSA lists 35,541 attorneys and 9,307 firms: 6,969 sole practitioners, 2,241 firms of 2-9 attorneys, 66 of 10-19, 19 of 20-49 and 12 of 50+ ([LSSA statistics](https://www.lssa.org.za/about-us/about-the-attorneys-profession/statistics-for-the-attorneys-profession/)). An earlier 2025 version reportedly showed about 18,600 firms, including 14,242 sole practitioners (unverified; the sources conflict). Either way, more than 98% of firms have fewer than 10 attorneys. Legal practitioners filed 76% of their risk and compliance returns ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61901)).
- **Credit providers (item 11).** The NCR had about 6,974 registered credit providers in 2023/24 and nearly 7,600 by June 2024 (unverified; from search summaries of [PMG](https://pmg.org.za/committee-meeting/38516/) and [The Outlier](https://theoutlier.co.za/business/2024-06-25/87899/top-number-25-june-24-credit-providers)). About 4,000 had let their registrations lapse ([BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/finance/792115/credit-warning-to-south-african-consumers/), unverified). How many are also registered with the FIC is unknown.
- **High-value goods dealers (item 20).** I found no count. This group had the lowest return-filing rate, 49% ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61901)).
- **How they comply today.** Small firms draft their own RMCP from the free LSSA guide or the FIC's PCC 53 template ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-publishes-risk-management-and-compliance-programme-guide-for-smaller-non-financial-entities/)), buy a template kit (Moonstone), use nCino KYC's onboarding, or hire consultants. Some firms have no RMCP at all, which is the FIC's top finding. I found no survey showing the split (unverified).

## Competition

- **Free state and professional-body tools.**
  - FIC PCC 53 gives smaller non-financial businesses an RMCP template, but it warns that a too-simple template may not be enough ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-publishes-risk-management-and-compliance-programme-guide-for-smaller-non-financial-entities/)).
  - The LSSA publishes a free 55-page guidance document for attorneys, current to May 2025. It states that "the adoption of a 'template/sample' RMCP is not envisaged under FICA" ([LSSA guide, p.2](https://www.lssa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Final-Draft-RMCP-Guidelines-6-5-25-Final.pdf)).
  - goAML handles filing free of charge but does not help draft the RMCP.
  - The free tools cover the content, but not the tailoring, tracking or reminders.
- **nCino KYC (formerly DocFox).**
  - A KYC/CDD platform with a customisable RMCP template and a paid service in which its experts help compile a tailored RMCP ([nCino KYC](https://blog.kycafrica.ncino.com/fic-updates-directive-10-draft-directive-12-guidance-note-7b)).
  - LSSA partnership: free onboarding (valued at R7,500-R45,000) and free yearly training ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/docfox-lssa-fica/)).
  - Integrations with AJS, LegalSuite and Lexpro ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/?p=57648)).
  - Runs free FICA and RMCP webinars ([GoLegal events](https://www.golegal.co.za/event/webinar-rmcp-fica/)).
  - Subscription price not found (unverified). This is the strongest alternative for law firms.
- **Moonstone Compliance FICA Toolkit.** Costs R4,995 excluding VAT, one-off. It includes an RMCP template, instructions, a risk register and a training voucher. Optional implementation help is billed hourly with a five-hour minimum; the rate is not shown ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/new-do-it-yourself-fica-compliance-solution-for-accountable-institutions/)).
- **Other vendors.** AML GO (bought by UPAY) does screening against a client's RMCP. VOCA by SearchWorks360 does CDD and risk profiling ([search summary of IT-Online](https://it-online.co.za/?p=296896), unverified). ClearComply is a CIPC/SARS deadline dashboard from R99/month that does not build RMCPs ([ClearComply](https://www.clearcomply.co.za/blog/fica-compliance-south-africa)).
- **Consultants and law firms.** Firms such as MJK Inc offer FICA services to legal practitioners ([MJK](https://mjkinc.co.za/fica/legal-practitioners)). Prices are quote-based and not published (unverified).
- **Gap.** I found no self-serve tool that interviews a small firm, writes a firm-specific RMCP and business risk assessment, records approval, and tracks the yearly 9 October / 31 October filing plus the 10-day amendment rule.

## Willingness to pay

- **Price anchors.** Moonstone charges R4,995 one-off plus hourly help ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/new-do-it-yourself-fica-compliance-solution-for-accountable-institutions/)). nCino values its onboarding at R7,500-R45,000 ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/docfox-lssa-fica/)). ClearComply shows that small SA firms pay about R99/month for deadline tracking ([ClearComply](https://www.clearcomply.co.za/blog/fica-compliance-south-africa)). Market training is often free or cheap: nCino webinars are free, and IEASA runs paid "how to submit RMCP" webinars for estate agents ([Quicket](https://www.quicket.co.za/events/395674-ieasa-webinar-how-to-submit-rmcp-the-step-by-step-guidance-on-the-new-directive/); price not seen).
- **Fines compared with the price.** The typical small-firm penalty in 2025/26 was about R10,000 per finalised non-compliance matter ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61901), my calculation). Rare large cases reach R7.7m ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/r7-7m-fine-stands-as-fic-appeal-board-rules-against-law-firm/)). Inspections are frequent for attorneys (169 in one year), so the fear is credible.
- **Plausible price.** About R1,500-R3,500 a year for a sole practitioner or small credit provider, with a once-off "first RMCP" fee of about R2,500-R5,000 (my estimate, unverified). If 1,000 paying firms pay R2,500 a year, revenue is about R2.5m (about US$140k) a year.

## Channels

- **Law firms.** The LSSA and its LEAD training arm, the LPC, and the Fidelity Fund certificate cycle, which already requires the FIC Org ID ([LSSA guide, p.5](https://www.lssa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Final-Draft-RMCP-Guidelines-6-5-25-Final.pdf)). The BLA and NADEL associations (unverified). Practice-management vendors such as AJS, LegalSuite and Lexpro, which already integrate FICA tools ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/?p=57648)). The GoLegal news site and its events listings.
- **Credit providers.** MicroFinance South Africa, which tracks NCR registrations ([BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/finance/792115/credit-warning-to-south-african-consumers/), unverified), and NCR compliance consultants.
- **Dealers.** The Jewellery Council of SA and motor-dealer associations (unverified).
- **Cross-sector.** Compliance newsletters (Moonstone, Accounting Weekly, acts.co.za). Bookkeepers and accountants who already file CIPC and SARS returns for these firms; ClearComply has an accountants page. Webinars timed to the September and October deadlines, as IEASA runs for estate agents ([Quicket](https://www.quicket.co.za/events/395674-ieasa-webinar-how-to-submit-rmcp-the-step-by-step-guidance-on-the-new-directive/)).

## Risks

- **Template distrust (main risk).** The FIC says firms should drop generic "one-size-fits-all" templates. It also says "a one-person firm does not need a 200-page document" ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-urges-businesses-to-simplify-compliance-focus-on-risks-not-paperwork/)), and the LSSA says templates are "not envisaged" ([LSSA](https://www.lssa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Final-Draft-RMCP-Guidelines-6-5-25-Final.pdf)). The product has to produce a tailored, short document from real answers. It also carries liability risk if a client is sanctioned.
- **Entrenched incumbent.** nCino KYC has the LSSA endorsement, free onboarding and practice-software integrations ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/docfox-lssa-fica/)).
- **Free substitutes.** PCC 53, the LSSA guide and free webinars.
- **Seasonality.** Filing happens once a year. The first deadline for items 1, 2, 9 and 11 is today (9 October 2026), so the next big sales window is September-October 2027, apart from the 31 October 2026 group, new registrants and GN 7B revisions.
- **Low fines.** Typical sanctions are small, which caps urgency ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61901)).
- **Regulator tools.** The FIC already publishes templates and could add a builder inside goAML (no sign of this found; unverified).
- **Overlap.** The estate-agent candidate (item 3) is the same duty family. It is better to treat the two as one product with sector modules.
- **Licensing.** No licence is needed to sell software or templates (unverified). Giving tailored legal advice to firms may need care. Everything is in English, so there is no language barrier.
- **Upside.** The FIC may extend yearly submission to all accountable institutions ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-revises-annual-rmcp-deadlines-in-final-directive-12/)).

## First product

**Version 1: "RMCP in an afternoon" for sole and small firms (items 1 and 11 first, then item 20).**

1. A guided interview about services, clients, geography, delivery channels, cash use and staff. It produces a short, firm-specific RMCP and business-level risk assessment, mapped to the s42(2) elements and GN 7B, including proliferation financing and new-product risk.
2. A client risk-rating matrix and CDD checklists that match the RMCP, since implementation is what inspectors test.
3. An approval pack: a highest-authority resolution and a version log. Each change starts a 10-day amendment countdown.
4. A goAML filing checklist that follows the FIC's naming and format guide once it is published, plus a filing-proof record.
5. A deadline calendar: 9 October or 31 October RMCP, Directive 10 locations, RCR cycles, 90-day registration changes, staff training records.

**First 30 days:**
- Week 1: get Gazette 55337, GN 7B, PCC 53 and the goAML user guide, and map every RMCP element to an interview question.
- Week 2: build the attorney module and a web form that writes a Word/PDF document.
- Week 3: test with 5-10 sole practitioners through LSSA provincial contacts or GoLegal readers. Have a FICA attorney review the output.
- Week 4: add reminders and the amendment tracker. Launch a "GN 7B update" offer for the 31 October group and new registrants. Test a price around R2,500 a year.

## Open questions

- The exact text of Gazette 55337, and whether the FIC's goAML user guide prescribes a structure for RMCP files.
- How many FIC registrations fall under items 1, 11 and 20, and why the 2025 and 2026 LSSA firm counts differ so much.
- What nCino KYC's subscription costs, and how many LSSA firms use it.
- Typical consultant fees for drafting an RMCP for a sole practitioner.
- How the FIC will review the yearly uploads: automated checks or only at inspection.
- Whether a vendor that generates an RMCP carries liability or needs legal-practice cover.
