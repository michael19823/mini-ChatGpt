# Firearm Business Register and Inspection Pack (South Africa)

## Re-assessment (owner's criteria)

**Verdict: maybe. New score: 5/10 (old score: 4/10).**

**The case.** No state portal or local software exists for these registers. Firms keep bound paper books in ink, and police and PSIRA inspect them often, without warning ([Reg 22](https://www.acts.co.za/firearms/r345_22__registers_in_respect_of_a_holder_of_a_licence_for_business_purposes); [PMG NW2477](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/37997/); [SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/79677)). Private security firms alone hold about 126,500 licensed firearms, so a few thousand firms carry a heavy daily issue-and-return workload ([Daily Maverick, Jan 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-01-16-private-militias-warning-after-cape-town-taxi-shootout)). A simple tool for issue/return, daily reconciliation, expiry alerts and an inspection pack has a clear job and no real local rival. The ceiling is modest: about R3.6 million (about US$200,000) a year by year 3 on the numbers below. It is a fair small business, but the sale is slow, the buyers are cost-squeezed, and the rules lean toward paper.

**Room for improvement over current practice**

- There is no portal to improve on. SAPS never built the dealer link ordered by the court, and FCMS covers licence applications only ([Daily Maverick, 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-07-13-sas-gun-controls-dire-dealers-association-speaks-out); [ProtectionWeb](https://www.protectionweb.co.za/civil-security/saps-detail-progress-and-partnerships-in-new-firearms-management-system/)). Even SAPS writes its own firearm issues by hand since its biometric system collapsed ([IOL, Jul 2026](https://iol.co.za/news/politics/2026-07-30-cachalia-defends-sapss-strict-firearm-control-amid-manual-recording-concerns/)).
- Current practice is error-prone. In the Durban case, police found firearms missing from the premises, an improperly completed register and pencil entries ([Pretoria News](https://pretorianews.co.za/news/2026-03-20-calvin-mathibelis-security-firm-under-scrutiny-for-incomplete-firearms-records/)). Security firms reported 847 firearms lost or stolen in half a year (2024/25) ([Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-01-16-private-militias-warning-after-cape-town-taxi-shootout)).
- Inspections are frequent and carry high stakes. PSIRA ran 2,411 firearm inspections in 2025/26. These check licences, firearm registers, ammunition records and competencies. PSIRA suspended or withdrew 2,427 businesses that year (all causes) ([PMG NW2477](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/37997/)). Operation Buyisa also checks safes and daily issuing registers ([Business Report](https://businessreport.co.za/news/2026-03-20-calvin-mathibelis-security-firm-under-scrutiny-for-incomplete-firearms-records/)).
- Paper books do not reconcile stock, do not flag licence or competency expiry, and do not track the monthly Reg 21 refresher training that large firms run. That training appears in armourer job ads ([Fidelity armourer ad](https://www.myjobmag.co.za/job/armourer-fidelity-services-group-1)). They also do not combine records across sites or produce an inspection bundle.
- The legal form is workable for software. Reg 22 says entries must be "written or printed in permanent ink" on numbered pages, and alterations must be crossed out and signed ([Reg 22](https://www.acts.co.za/firearms/r345_22__registers_in_respect_of_a_holder_of_a_licence_for_business_purposes)). "Printed" suggests that numbered, locked printouts could qualify. Whether inspectors accept them is (unverified). Dealers may already keep registers as monthly computer printouts under Reg 37 ([Reg 37](https://www.acts.co.za/firearms/r345_37__registers_in_respect_of_a_dealer)).

**Competitor reality check**

- I found no South African product that does this job. Searches in English and Afrikaans turned up only the regulations and news ([Afrikaans regulations, NATSHOOT](https://natshoot.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Regulasies-2004-Afrikaans.pdf)).
- CybateSoft, the "South African" lead from the first pass, looks like a general software house with no firearm product. Its profile lists web, mobile, ERP and gaming work ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/virtualdoxx.com/competitors)). That it is Indian rather than South African is (unverified). It is not a real competitor.
- The foreign armoury systems (NextGen IoT, FN SAM, ArmorerLink) are built for military and police. They are quote-priced, and FN SAM is tied to FN hardware ([Capterra ZA](https://www.capterra.co.za/software/1080884/NextGen-IoT-Armory-Management); [FN](https://fnherstal.com/en/security/small-arms-management/fn-sam-software/)). An unnamed biometric issue/return system for the SADC region is listed on G2, but its vendor and its local use are (unverified) ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/chips-firearms/competitors/alternatives)). None of these products prints Reg 22 formats.
- Guarding software sold in South Africa covers rostering, the officer app, occurrence books, billing and payroll. It has no armoury module ([Kolonell guide](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/facility-management-security-guarding-software-cost-johannesburg-2026)). The real incumbents are paper books, Excel and in-house armourers. That is an opening, not a killer.

**Price per customer**

- Proposed prices (my estimates):
  - Small armed security firm (up to about 30 firearms, one armoury): R500 a month.
  - Mid-size firm (several sites): R1,500 a month.
  - Dealer (Reg 37 registers plus weekly returns): R1,000 a month.
  - Game farm, hunting outfitter or range: R400 a month.
  - Optional inspection-readiness audit: R3,000 to R8,000 per visit.
- Blended price for security firms: about R800 a month (R9,600 a year). On average a firm holds about 38 firearms (126,529 firearms ÷ 3,340 firms). **Ease of implementation and sale**

- **Build: easy to medium.** The product is a register app with signature capture, numbered PDF printouts, an append-only audit log, expiry alerts and an offline mode. It needs no state API, because none exists.
- **Onboarding: medium.** The firearm list can be imported from licence records. Each armoury needs a tablet, and maybe a barcode scanner, plus armourer training.
- **Sale: medium to hard.** Buyers are many small firms that are wary of putting firearm data in the cloud. They also need to know that inspectors will accept the output. Channels exist through SASA, training providers, PSIRA agents and trade media (see Channels below). Inspection news creates urgency ([DA call for KZN audit](https://www.da.org.za/2026/03/da-calls-for-province-wide-audit-of-private-security-companies-in-kzn-following-saps-raid)).

**Remaining risks**

- Inspectors may insist on bound, hand-written books. Software then becomes a shadow system plus a printing tool, though it still helps with reconciliation and inspection readiness. This is the key open question ([Reg 22](https://www.acts.co.za/firearms/r345_22__registers_in_respect_of_a_holder_of_a_licence_for_business_purposes)).
- Many small guarding firms run on thin margins. PSIRA suspends or withdraws many firms each year ([PMG NW2477](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/37997/)), so churn and bad debt will be high (my judgement).
- Data sensitivity: a list of firearms and storage sites is a theft target, so an on-premises or encrypted offline option is needed.
- A guard-management vendor could add an armoury tab. FCMS could add business-holder e-services, but nothing has been announced ([ProtectionWeb](https://www.protectionweb.co.za/civil-security/saps-detail-progress-and-partnerships-in-new-firearms-management-system/)).
- Policy could shrink the market (the amendment bill) or grow demand (the draft PSIRA tracking-device rules). Both are still pending (unverified status).

**Killer under the new criteria: none.** No local product does the job. The buyers can pay modest fees. The duty is in force and enforced.

## Summary

**Verdict: maybe. Score: 4/10.**

The duty is real, detailed and enforced. Dealers must keep four registers, take computer printouts to the police every month for stamping, and file weekly returns to the Central Firearms Register. Security firms and other business licence holders must keep issue/return, discharge and training registers. SAPS and PSIRA both inspect these registers often: SAPS reported 3,728 inspections in February 2026, and PSIRA runs about 2,400 firearm inspections a year. No South African software for these registers turned up. The tools that did turn up are US FFL point-of-sale systems and foreign armoury products. The problem is the market and the buyer. There are probably only about 300 dealer companies and about 3,300 security businesses with firearm licences (both old figures). Many keep paper books because the rules are written around bound, ink-signed pages. Firearm stock data is very sensitive. SAPS is also under a court order to build an electronic link for dealers, and it has been late for years. A narrow service (paper-compatible register software plus inspection readiness for small armed security firms) could earn a modest side income. It is unlikely to become a strong standalone business.

## Duty

**Dealers (Firearms Control Act 60 of 2000, s39, and Firearms Control Regulations, Regs 37-40)**

- Regulation 37 requires four registers: a Firearms Stock Register, an Ammunition Stock Register, a Firearms Safe Custody Register, and a register of the people who trade on the dealer's behalf. Registers can be books or computer printouts. With books, every page is numbered and signed and date-stamped by the Designated Firearms Officer (DFO) before use. Printouts must be handed to the DFO within seven days after the end of each month for signing and stamping. Entries must be in ink, corrections must be initialled, no page may be removed, and records are kept for 10 years at the licensed premises. The Registrar can also demand ad hoc ammunition returns by written notice. ([acts.co.za Reg 37](https://www.acts.co.za/firearms/r345_37__registers_in_respect_of_a_dealer))
- Section 39(6) requires a dealer to keep a workstation that links its registers to the central dealers' database. The Registrar may exempt a dealer, and an exempted dealer must file weekly returns to the Central Firearms Register. ([acts.co.za s39](https://api.acts.co.za/firearms-control-act-2000/39__duties_of_dealer.php))
- Regulation 39: an exempted dealer files a return within seven days after the end of every week, on the Registrar's form or as a computer printout. The return covers all firearms and ammunition acquired or transferred, and is due even when nothing moved. ([acts.co.za Reg 39](https://www.acts.co.za/firearms/r345_39__weekly_returns))
- Regulation 38: the workstation must link the Reg 37 registers to the central dealers' database. It uses "software and an electronic network connectivity" compatible with SAPS standards, and sends the previous business day's data every day. ([acts.co.za Reg 38](https://www.acts.co.za/firearms/r345_38__manner_in_which_a_workstation_must_be_established_maintained_and_linked_to_the_central_dealers_database))
- That central link was never built. The dealers' association SAAADA won a 2019 North Gauteng High Court structural order requiring SAPS to build electronic connectivity between dealers and the Central Dealers Database by 31 July 2022 ([SAFLII 2021 judgment](https://www.saflii.org/za/cases/ZAGPPHC/2021/452.html); [Western Cape Government, 2019](https://www.westerncape.gov.za/news/electronic-database-firearms-mec-fritz-welcomes-gauteng-high-court%E2%80%99s-ruling)). SAPS missed the deadline. In 2023 Parliament heard SAPS could be in contempt ([Daily Maverick, 13 Jul 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-07-13-sas-gun-controls-dire-dealers-association-speaks-out)). So in practice dealers most likely run on paper or printout registers plus weekly returns. That they do so under formal exemptions is (unverified).
- Gunsmiths have parallel register and weekly-return rules (Regs 57 and 59). I did not read their text ([acts.co.za Reg 59](https://www.acts.co.za/firearms/r345_59__weekly_returns)). Manufacturers have Reg 47 registers.
- The earlier checks named an "SAP 350" stock return form. I could not confirm that form number (unverified).

**Security firms and other business-purpose licence holders (Reg 22)**

- Every business licence holder keeps a general register of its licensed firearms. A business that provides firearms to staff keeps an issue/return register with date, time and signatures at handover and return, plus the PSIRA number for security officers. Security providers also keep a discharge register (with police reference numbers) and a training register. The rules are 10-year retention, numbered pages, permanent ink and no erasures. The text describes a bound paper-style register and does not mention electronic records or DFO stamping. ([acts.co.za Reg 22](https://www.acts.co.za/firearms/r345_22__registers_in_respect_of_a_holder_of_a_licence_for_business_purposes))
- Whether a computer-printed issue/return register meets Reg 22 is unclear (unverified). This matters a great deal for any software product.

**Penalties and enforcement**

- Section 120(1) makes any breach of the Act or of licence conditions an offence ([SAnews, Feb 2026](https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/79677)).
- SAPS reported 3,728 compliance inspections at gun dealers and security companies. They led to the arrest of a Middelburg dealer and the seizure of 537 firearms ([SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/79677)).
- In March 2026, police inspected a Durban North security firm under s109. They reported register discrepancies (815 firearms recorded against more than 850 expected) and entries made in pencil ([Pretoria News, 20 Mar 2026](https://pretorianews.co.za/news/2026-03-20-calvin-mathibelis-security-firm-under-scrutiny-for-incomplete-firearms-records/); [Business Report](https://businessreport.co.za/news/crime-and-courts/2026-03-19-where-are-the-missing-firearms-from-calvin-mathibelis-security-company/)).
- PSIRA ran 2,252, 2,490 and 2,411 firearm inspections in 2023/24, 2024/25 and 2025/26. These inspections check firearm licences, firearm registers, ammunition records and competencies. PSIRA says it keeps no separate statistics on firearm non-compliance ([PMG NW2477, June 2026](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/37997/)).

**Upcoming changes**

- Draft amendments to the Private Security Industry Firearms Regulations were gazetted on 28 March 2025. They include tracking devices on every firearm, prior applications to use certain weapons, and annual psychological evaluations. Industry bodies and Sakeliga opposed them ([ProtectionWeb](https://www.protectionweb.co.za/civil-security/psira-rejects-concerns-over-firearms-regulation-amendments/); [Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/private-security-industry-bodies-challenge-proposed-firearms-regulations-2025-04-16)). Whether they have been finalised is (unverified).
- A Firearms Control Amendment Bill is under debate in 2026. It would remove self-defence as a licence reason and restrict ammunition. The biggest importer and retailer has asked for it to be paused ([EWN, Jun 2026](https://ewn.co.za/2026/06/25/firearm-industry-wants-gun-law-changes-paused-pending-new-research); [EWN, Sep 2026](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/09/30/firearms-control-amendment-bill-targets-wrong-group-says-gun-owners-body)). I found no dealer register clauses in it (unverified). If passed, it would shrink civilian sales and so the dealer base.
- SAPS's new Firearms Control Management System (FCMS) is late. Release 02 slipped to the end of January 2026 ([PMG NW1447](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/29103/)). A pilot is planned at three Gauteng stations, and no go-live date has been published. A Technical Work Team that includes dealer, manufacturer and gunsmith associations was set up in September 2023 ([ProtectionWeb, Dec 2025](https://www.protectionweb.co.za/civil-security/saps-detail-progress-and-partnerships-in-new-firearms-management-system/)). I found no published dealer e-services in FCMS. SAPS's own internal firearm control is still manual since its biometric system collapsed ([IOL, 30 Jul 2026](https://iol.co.za/news/politics/2026-07-30-cachalia-defends-sapss-strict-firearm-control-amid-manual-recording-concerns/)).

## Buyers

- **Dealers.** In an older parliamentary submission, SAAADA spoke of about 300 licensed dealer companies, about half of them members, handling over 95% of licensed imports and sales ([SAAADA submission](https://pmg.org.za/files/1/firearmsbill_SAAADA.htm)). An earlier check cited about 600 licences held by those roughly 300 companies, and a later claim that only about 50 dealers remained by 2006 ([gunpolicy.org](https://www.gunpolicy.org/firearms/citation/quotes/5263)). I found no current count (unverified). The market is concentrated: Outdoor Investment Holdings describes itself as the largest importer, distributor and retailer ([EWN](https://ewn.co.za/2026/06/25/firearm-industry-wants-gun-law-changes-paused-pending-new-research)).
- **Security businesses with firearms.** A PSIRA audit of the Central Firearms Register found 8,345 institutions licensed for firearms. 3,340 of them were security businesses, and the private security sector held 101,612 of their 122,788 firearms (about 83%) ([PMG NW2280, Oct 2017](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/6974/)). PSIRA now lists 16,704 registered security companies (7,141 in Gauteng, 3,045 in KZN) ([PMG NW2477](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/37997/)). The armed share today is (unverified). It is probably 3,000 to 4,500 businesses (my estimate).
- **Other business licence holders.** The other roughly 5,000 institutions in the 2017 audit include hunting outfitters, training providers, ranges, game farms and other businesses ([PMG NW2280](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/6974/)). They all keep Reg 22 registers. Their mix is (unverified).
- **Ranges and gunsmiths.** SAPS range accreditation is required in practice: state tenders ask for a valid SAPS range certificate ([SANParks RFQ 058/26](https://www.sanparks.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/ERRATUM-TO-RFQ-058-26-FIELD-GUIDE-TRAINING-1.pdf)). I found no national count of ranges or gunsmiths (unverified).
- **How they comply today.** Security firms appear to use paper registers. The Durban case involved pencil entries, and police stressed dispatcher and recipient signatures ([Pretoria News](https://pretorianews.co.za/news/2026-03-20-calvin-mathibelis-security-firm-under-scrutiny-for-incomplete-firearms-records/)). Large guarding groups employ dedicated armoury staff ([Fidelity job ad via earlier check](https://www.myjobmag.co.za/job/armoury-personnel-fidelity-services-group-1)). How dealers produce printouts (POS export, Excel or paper books) is (unverified).

**Rough total:** about 300 dealers plus about 3,300 armed security firms plus some thousands of other business holders. That is about 4,000 to 8,000 obliged entities (my estimate). Most are small, and the highest-pain segment is small and mid-sized armed security firms.

## Willingness to pay

- I found no published price for a South African firearm register or armoury module (search returned none).
- Comparable SaaS: international guarding tools charge €4 to €9 per officer per month, and a bespoke system for a 900-officer South African firm is estimated at US$20,000 to 46,000 ([Kolonell guide](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/facility-management-security-guarding-software-cost-johannesburg-2026)). PSIRA registration agents charge about R2,990 per facilitation ([Company Partners](https://companypartners.co.za/psira-registrations/)), which shows small firms pay for compliance help.
- Cost of failure is high but uneven. A failed inspection can lead to criminal charges under s120(1), seizure of stock (537 firearms in Middelburg), PSIRA improper-conduct enquiries, suspension or withdrawal (2,427 companies suspended or withdrawn in 2025/26, for all causes) ([SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/79677); [PMG NW2477](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/37997/)). This is a story about losing the licence, not about a fixed fine.
- Plausible price: R350 to R900 per site per month (about US$20 to 50) for small armed security firms and single-store dealers. An inspection-readiness audit or stock reconciliation could sell for R3,000 to R10,000 per visit. These are my estimates (unverified). Even at 10% uptake among about 3,300 armed security firms at R500 per month, revenue is about R2 million (about US$110,000) a year.

- **Compliance intermediaries:** PSIRA registration agents and accredited firearm training providers, who already sell to small security firms ([Company Partners](https://companypartners.co.za/psira-registrations/)).
- **Inspection news hooks:** police operations such as Operation Buyisa create urgency in specific provinces ([Business Report](https://businessreport.co.za/news/2026-03-20-calvin-mathibelis-security-firm-under-scrutiny-for-incomplete-firearms-records/)).

## Risks

- **Small market.** There are only about 300 dealer companies, and the large ones may already run ERP/POS exports. The armed security segment is a few thousand firms, many of them cash-strapped (estimates above).
- **The rules favour paper.** Reg 22 describes numbered, ink-written pages and does not mention electronic records ([Reg 22](https://www.acts.co.za/firearms/r345_22__registers_in_respect_of_a_holder_of_a_licence_for_business_purposes)). If inspectors insist on the bound book, software becomes a shadow system plus a printing tool, which weakens its value.
- **State build.** Reg 38 and the 2019 court order require SAPS to build a dealer link. If it lands, it could make dealer-side return filing redundant. It could also create demand for SAPS-compatible dealer software, so it cuts both ways ([Reg 38](https://www.acts.co.za/firearms/r345_38__manner_in_which_a_workstation_must_be_established_maintained_and_linked_to_the_central_dealers_database); [SAFLII](https://www.saflii.org/za/cases/ZAGPPHC/2021/452.html)). SAPS's poor record (missed deadlines, the R644 million FDA dispute) makes this unlikely in the near term ([IOL](https://iol.co.za/news/politics/2026-07-30-cachalia-defends-sapss-strict-firearm-control-amid-manual-recording-concerns/)).
- **Data sensitivity and liability.** A cloud list of firearm serial numbers and storage sites is a theft target. A breach or a reconciliation error could expose the vendor to claims and reputational damage. Buyers are likely to resist cloud hosting (my judgement).
- **Policy shrinkage.** The Firearms Control Amendment Bill and the PSIRA draft regulations could cut civilian sales or armed guarding ([EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/09/30/firearms-control-amendment-bill-targets-wrong-group-says-gun-owners-body); [ProtectionWeb](https://www.protectionweb.co.za/featured/sakeliga-calls-for-withdrawal-of-draft-private-security-regulations/)). They could also add tracking and reporting duties that raise demand.
- **Guard-SaaS bolt-on.** Existing guard-management vendors could add an armoury tab quickly.
- **Language:** low barrier, since English is the business language and Afrikaans is helpful for rural dealers and farms. **Licensing:** no software licence is needed. Selling hardware such as safes or RFID tags may bring in other rules (unverified).

## First product

**Focus:** small and mid-sized armed security firms, with dealers second.

**Version 1:**
1. Firearm inventory: make, type, calibre, serial number, licence number and expiry, and storage site, with licence-expiry alerts.
2. Issue/return register matching Reg 22(2) fields: PSIRA number, date and time out and in, and signature capture on tablet. It prints numbered, locked pages in a format that can be stamped and bound, and edits are append-only with no erasures.
3. Discharge register (Reg 22(3)) and training register (Reg 22(3A)), plus tracking of officer competency certificates.
4. A daily "every firearm accounted for" reconciliation and an annual stocktake report.
5. Inspection pack: a one-click PDF bundle of registers, licence list and competencies for a SAPS s109 or PSIRA visit.
6. Dealer mode: Reg 37 stock, ammunition and safe-custody registers, a monthly printout for DFO stamping, and a weekly Reg 39 return printout.
7. Offline-first or on-premises option with encrypted local storage, to answer the data-sensitivity objection.

**First 30 days:**
- Week 1: get the actual DFO-stamped register books, a Reg 39 return form and PSIRA inspection checklists from 3 to 5 firms. Confirm whether printed issue registers are accepted.
- Week 2: build the inventory and issue/return flows with tablet signatures and numbered-page PDF output.
- Week 3: build the inspection pack and the expiry and competency alerts. Pilot with 2 small security firms in Gauteng or KZN.
- Week 4: interview SAAADA and SASA about pricing, the cloud versus local question and FCMS overlap, then decide whether to add dealer mode.
