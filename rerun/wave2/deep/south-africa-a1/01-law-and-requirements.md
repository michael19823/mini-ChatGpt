# South Africa: FICA duties of high-value goods dealers (jewellers, gold, bullion, coin and stone dealers), turned into product requirements

Status: complete as of 10 Oct 2026 (open questions at the end). Almost every fact below comes from primary documents on fic.gov.za: the Act, the Regulations, gazetted directives, PCCs, guidance notes, the FIC annual report, published sanction notices and one High Court judgment. I downloaded and read them myself.

Short names used below:
- **Act** = Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001, consolidated text published by the FIC ([Act PDF](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Financial-Intelligence-Centre-Act-2001-Act-38-of-2001.pdf)). The cover page lists amendments only up to 2017, but the text already contains the 2022 changes (proliferation financing, s42(2A)-(2C), s42(2)(qA), s61B), so I treat it as current.
- **Regs** = Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Control Regulations, GN R.1595 of 2002, as amended up to GN R2943 ([Regs PDF](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Money-Laundering-and-Terrorist-Financing-Control-Regulations.pdf)).
- **FIC** = Financial Intelligence Centre. **goAML** = the FIC's registration and reporting platform ([goAML login](https://goweb.fic.gov.za/goAMLWEb_PRD/Home)).
- **HVGD** = high-value goods dealer (Schedule 1 item 20). **DPMS** = dealers in precious metals and stones. **KRD** = Krugerrand dealer.
- **RMCP** = risk management and compliance programme (s42). **RCR** = risk and compliance return. **CTR** = cash threshold report (s28). **STR/SAR** = suspicious or unusual transaction or activity report (s29). **TPR** = terrorist property report (s28A). **TFS** = targeted financial sanctions. **CDD** = customer due diligence. **PEP/PIP** = politically exposed person / prominent influential person.
- **PCC** = public compliance communication. **GN** = guidance note. The FIC says its guidance is "authoritative" and that a firm that does not follow it must show it complied "in an equivalent manner" ([PCC 58, preface](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024.03-PCC-HVGD-guidance.pdf)).
- "Days" for the report deadlines in Reg 24 means business days: Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays do not count ([Regs, reg 1 definition of "days"](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Money-Laundering-and-Terrorist-Financing-Control-Regulations.pdf)).

## Summary

- **The duty is national, statutory and now recurs every year.** Item 20 of Schedule 1 has made high-value goods dealers "accountable institutions" since 19 December 2022 ([Act, Schedule 1 item 20](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Financial-Intelligence-Centre-Act-2001-Act-38-of-2001.pdf); in-force date per [FIC sanction notice, para 2](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Administrative-sanction-%E2%80%93-Miller-Gold-House-Pty-Ltd.pdf)). The FIC itself supervises them. Schedule 2 names no other supervisory body for item 20 ([Act, Schedule 2](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Financial-Intelligence-Centre-Act-2001-Act-38-of-2001.pdf)).
- **Who.** Anyone who, as a regular part of business, buys or sells a physical item (or a bundle sold as one unit) for R100,000 or more, paid in any form, in one payment or linked payments. This catches jewellers who sell even one R100,000 ring, Krugerrand and coin dealers, bullion dealers, diamond and coloured-stone dealers, and second-hand or scrap gold buyers who buy a R100,000 lot ([PCC 58, paras 1.4-1.5, 3.1, examples 2-5](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024.03-PCC-HVGD-guidance.pdf)). There is no small-firm exemption. At 31 March 2026, 5,581 item 20 dealers were registered ([FIC Annual Report 2025/26, p. 29](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026-1.pdf)). Of these, 661 were jewellery, bullion, coin or stone registrations: 176 precious metals, 244 precious stones, 241 Krugerrand ([FIC media release, 17 July 2026](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Media-release-RCR-closing-dates.pdf)).
- **What must exist on paper.** A written entity-wide risk assessment. An RMCP covering the 19 elements of s42(2)(a) to (s), with reasons for any element marked "not applicable", approved by the board or the owner ([Act s42](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Financial-Intelligence-Centre-Act-2001-Act-38-of-2001.pdf); [GN 7B, paras 180-190CC](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Guidance-Note-7B-%E2%80%93-Implementation-of-various-aspects-of-the-FIC-Act.pdf)). A compliance function or compliance officer (s42A). Ongoing staff training (s43). Employee screening records ([Directive 8](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2023-DIR-Directive-8-of-2023-Screening-employees.pdf)). CDD files and transaction records kept for 5 years (s22, 22A, 23).
- **What must be filed, and when.**
  - Register on goAML within 90 days of opening. Report changes within 90 days ([Regs 27A(3)](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Money-Laundering-and-Terrorist-Financing-Control-Regulations.pdf); [Act s43B(4)](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Financial-Intelligence-Centre-Act-2001-Act-38-of-2001.pdf)).
  - **RMCP upload on goAML by 31 October every year.** The first one is due 31 October 2026. New dealers upload within 90 days of opening. An updated RMCP goes up within 10 days of its approval ([Directive 12, Gazette 55337, 4 Sep 2026](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Directive-12-On-the-submission-of-risk-management-and-compliance-programmes.pdf)). Format: one PDF named YYYYMMDD_RMCP.pdf, where the date is the approval date ([FIC "How to submit an RMCP"](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/HTSRMCP.png)).
  - **Geographic locations** of the head office, branches and subsidiaries, for existing registrants with more than one location: within 90 days of 31 July 2026, so by 29 October 2026. After that, within 90 days of any change ([Directive 10](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Directive-10-On-information-pertaining-to-geographic-locations.pdf)).
  - **Risk and compliance return** whenever the FIC issues a directive. The 2026 round (Directive 11) ran from 4 May to 31 July 2026 for item 20. It asked for data for three periods back to 1 July 2023 ([Directive 11, Gazette 54439](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Directive-11-%E2%80%93-Risk-and-compliance-return.pdf)).
  - **Event reports.** Cash above R49,999.99: within 3 business days. Suspicious or unusual transactions: within 15 business days. Property of a sanctioned person: within 5 business days ([Regs 22B, 24](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Money-Laundering-and-Terrorist-Financing-Control-Regulations.pdf)).
- **The FIC enforces it, and jewellers are inspected heavily.** In 2025/26 the FIC issued 549 inspection reports. 61 went to precious metal and stone dealers (17 metals, 12 metals and stones, 32 stones; 43 of the 61 in Gauteng). 37 went to motor dealers. It issued 361 "admission of non-compliance" fines (R10,000 each when settled) for missing RCRs or registration. One item 20 dealer was fined R210,000 (R105,000 payable, R105,000 suspended) for an inadequate RMCP, no sanctions screening, weak governance and a registration failure; it has appealed ([FIC Annual Report 2025/26, pp. 40-47](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026-1.pdf)). A Krugerrand dealer that repeatedly failed to file cash reports lost its High Court appeal against a R1.71m penalty in March 2026 ([Scoin Trading v FIC, paras 4-5, 13, 25](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/SCOIN-TRADING-PROPRIETARY-LIMITED-v-THE-FINANCIAL-INTELLIGENCE-CENTRE-APPEAL-FINAL_.pdf)).
- **Dealers are struggling.** By 15 July 2026, two weeks before the deadline, only 14% of precious metal dealers, 20% of stone dealers and 11% of Krugerrand dealers had filed the 2026 RCR ([FIC media release, 17 July 2026](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Media-release-RCR-closing-dates.pdf)). The FIC also saw dealers upload RMCPs when an RCR was due, because they mixed up the two ([FIC notice, 30 July 2026](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026.7-GN-Notice-2026-RCR-submission-30-July-2026-update.pdf)). Only 1,670 RMCPs from item 20 dealers had reached the FIC by 31 March 2026, against 5,581 registrations ([FIC AR 2025/26, pp. 29, 36](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026-1.pdf)).
- **What goAML leaves undone.** goAML only receives filings. It does not draft or version the RMCP, record board approval, keep the CDD file, compute RCR answers for three periods, track the 3/5/15-day report clocks, keep a training register or build the evidence pack inspectors ask for. The most common inspection findings are exactly these gaps: no RMCP or an inadequate one, failure to send the RMCP when asked, weak CDD on legal entities and PEPs, late or stale registration, and no evidence of sanctions screening ([FIC AR 2025/26, p. 41](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026-1.pdf)).
- **Upcoming.** Draft PCC 126 (30 Sep 2026, comments close 16 Oct 2026) would require one goAML registration per SADPMR permit or licence. It adds checks on the client's permits and on the source of the goods, and it confirms that silver and laboratory-grown stones are in scope ([draft PCC 126](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2026.9-PCC-DPMS.pdf)). The next FATF mutual evaluation runs from mid-2026 to October 2027 ([National Treasury statement, Jan 2026](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/National-Treasury-media-statement-%E2%80%93-General-Laws-Amendment-Bill-2025.pdf)).
- **Three legal constraints on the product itself.**
  - FIC guidance (PCC 53's RMCP template, GN 7B, PCC 58) is copyright. It may be reproduced "in an unaltered form only for personal and non-commercial use" ([PCC 53, copyright notice](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2022.08-PCC-PCC-53-RMCP.pdf)). The product's RMCP text must be original and only cite the guidance.
  - A dealer that lets a third party keep its FICA records must tell the FIC who that third party is ([Act s24(3)](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Financial-Intelligence-Centre-Act-2001-Act-38-of-2001.pdf); [Regs 20, 29(6)](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Money-Laundering-and-Terrorist-Financing-Control-Regulations.pdf)). A SaaS that stores CDD files is such a third party.
  - goAML login credentials "may only be used by the person who originally registered them" ([FIC statement on Directive 2](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2014.4-DIR-Directive-2-on-use-of-login-credentials-following-registration-with-the-FIC.pdf); [PCC 5C, para 2](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/PCC-5C-Accountable-and-reporting-institutions-registration.pdf)). The product must prepare filings for the dealer to upload, not log in for the dealer.

## Who is obliged

**The legal test.** Item 20 covers "a person who carries on the business of dealing in high-value goods in respect of any transaction where such a business receives payment in any form to the value of R100 000 or more, whether the payment is made in a single operation or in more than one operation that appears to be linked", where high-value goods means "any item that is valued in that business at R100 000 or more" ([Act, Schedule 1 item 20](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Financial-Intelligence-Centre-Act-2001-Act-38-of-2001.pdf)).

**How the FIC reads it** ([PCC 58, 28 March 2024](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024.03-PCC-HVGD-guidance.pdf)):
- **Per item, not per basket.** One item or one unit must be worth R100,000 or more. Several separate items that add up to R100,000 do not count. Example: 50 Krugerrands sold separately for R1.42m total do not qualify, but one coin or a bundle sold as one unit at R100,000 or more does (paras 1.5.6, example 2).
- **A bundle is one item.** A unit of scrap metal bought for R120,000 is one high-value good, so the second-hand dealer is an HVGD (example 2).
- **Any payment form.** Cash, cheque, EFT, card and crypto all count. Linked payments count, for example a R20,000 deposit and an R80,000 balance (paras 1.5.10.2, 1.5.11.1).
- **Buying counts as well as selling.** "Dealing" means to trade, sell or buy, retail or wholesale. Intermediary buyers and brokers are included. A service provider paid only a fee, who never receives or pays the price, is not (paras 1.5.4-1.5.5).
- **Physical goods only.** New, used and second-hand goods count. Shares and commodity investments do not (paras 1.5.8-1.5.9).
- **One qualifying item is enough.** A jeweller whose stock is mostly R20,000 watches, but who sells one R100,000 diamond ring, is an HVGD (example 3).
- **Register before the first qualifying sale.** A business that reasonably expects to deal in such goods must register and set up controls before it does (para 2.2).
- **Structuring is a breach.** Splitting a deal to stay under R100,000 may itself be non-compliance (para 1.10).

**Duties are ring-fenced to qualifying transactions, with two exceptions** ([PCC 58, paras 1.6-1.9, 2.11-2.18](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024.03-PCC-HVGD-guidance.pdf)):
- The firm-level duties always apply once the firm is an HVGD: registration, RMCP, compliance officer, training and records (example 5).
- CDD, CTR and TPR duties apply only to transactions in an item of R100,000 or more. Cash of R50,000 or more inside such a transaction must be reported even if the rest is paid by EFT (example 7).
- **Exception 1:** STRs under s29 apply to every transaction of every business, whatever the value (para 1.8; [Act s29(1)](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Financial-Intelligence-Centre-Act-2001-Act-38-of-2001.pdf)).
- **Exception 2:** The ban on dealing with UN-sanctioned persons (s26B) applies to everyone and every transaction (para 1.9).

**Who in the precious metals and stones trade is in scope.**
- PCC 58 lists jewellery, ornament and watch dealers likely to sell an item of R100,000 or more "irrespective of the value of the turnover"; numismatic coin dealers; dealers in gold and platinum-group metals as defined in the Precious Metals Act; second-hand dealers in precious metals and stones; and diamond dealer licence holders ([PCC 58, paras 3.1.1-3.1.5](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024.03-PCC-HVGD-guidance.pdf)).
- Draft PCC 126 widens and sharpens this. A dealer can be a miner, manufacturer, beneficiator, refiner, wholesaler, retailer, importer, exporter, broker, cutter, polisher, jewellery manufacturer, or "a buyer or seller in a secondary market". Foreign dealers are included. Silver counts as a precious metal. "Laboratory manufactured" stones count. Rubies, sapphires, tanzanite and emeralds count above R100,000 ([draft PCC 126, paras 2.1-2.6](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2026.9-PCC-DPMS.pdf)). It is a draft (unverified whether the final text keeps this).
- Krugerrand dealers moved from the old Schedule 3 (reporting institutions) into item 20. Their goAML profiles were converted automatically. A KRD that does not meet the test must deregister with reasons ([PCC 58, paras 1.3, 2.5-2.7](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024.03-PCC-HVGD-guidance.pdf)). goAML has a Krugerrand sub-category ([draft PCC 126, para 4.3](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2026.9-PCC-DPMS.pdf)).

**How many registrations one business needs.**
- One per Schedule 1 item. A dealer that also gives credit of R100,000 or more is also an item 11 credit provider and needs a dual registration ([PCC 58, paras 1.5.10.3, 2.3](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024.03-PCC-HVGD-guidance.pdf)).
- Draft PCC 126: one registration per SADPMR permit or licence. Example: a firm with a jeweller's permit, an import permit and a diamond dealer licence registers three times ([draft PCC 126, para 4.2](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2026.9-PCC-DPMS.pdf)).
- Draft PCC 5E (19 March 2026): item 20 dealers register "per licence issued" under the registered name. Where no licence is needed, the head office and each branch register. Each franchise holder registers separately ([draft PCC 5E, paras 9.20.1-9.20.3](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026.3-PCC-Draft-PCC05E_.pdf)). It is a draft that would replace PCC 5D (unverified whether final). The current rule in PCC 5C is that every branch registers as a separate entity unless the FIC approves a delegation structure ([PCC 5C, paras 6.1, 8](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/PCC-5C-Accountable-and-reporting-institutions-registration.pdf)).
- An RMCP must be uploaded for each separate item registration ([Directive 12 feedback note, para 7](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Consultation-feedback-note-Relating-to-draft-Directive-12-on-the-submission-of-RMCPs.pdf)). One RCR is due per Org ID, consolidating all branches that belong to the same legal entity ([PCC 60, paras 2.2-2.10](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2026.6-PCC-60-RCR-_On-RCR-Submission.pdf)).

**Who must be the compliance officer.**
- A legal person (company, CC) must have a compliance function and assign a person "with sufficient competence and seniority" ([Act s42A(2)](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Financial-Intelligence-Centre-Act-2001-Act-38-of-2001.pdf)).
- A business that is not a legal person (sole proprietor, partnership) must appoint a competent person, except "a sole practitioner" ([Act s42A(4)](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Financial-Intelligence-Centre-Act-2001-Act-38-of-2001.pdf)). For a sole proprietor, the owner can take the compliance officer role ([PCC 5C, para 5.2](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/PCC-5C-Accountable-and-reporting-institutions-registration.pdf); [GN 7B, para 181B](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Guidance-Note-7B-%E2%80%93-Implementation-of-various-aspects-of-the-FIC-Act.pdf)).
- The compliance officer registers the entity on goAML and approves other users. A money laundering reporting officer (MLRO) can be added to file reports ([PCC 5C, paras 5.1-5.5](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/PCC-5C-Accountable-and-reporting-institutions-registration.pdf)).

**Exemptions and fees.** I found no size, turnover or employee-count exemption in the Act, Regs or guidance. PCC 58 says "irrespective of the value of the turnover" ([PCC 58, para 3.1.1](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024.03-PCC-HVGD-guidance.pdf)). Registration is free and is not a licence ([Regs 27A(6)-(7)](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Money-Laundering-and-Terrorist-Financing-Control-Regulations.pdf)).

Notes on the table:
- **RMCP content.** The FIC recommends three parts: (1) risk identification and assessment, with risk appetite; (2) controls (CDD, reporting, record keeping); (3) monitoring whether controls work ([GN 7B, para 183A](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Guidance-Note-7B-%E2%80%93-Implementation-of-various-aspects-of-the-FIC-Act.pdf)). PCC 53 groups the content as: governance; risk assessment and rating; CDD; TFS for TF; TFS for PF; PIP controls; account monitoring; reporting; record keeping ([PCC 53, para 1.8](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2022.08-PCC-PCC-53-RMCP.pdf)). Copying the PCC 53 template without customising it is itself non-compliance ([PCC 53, Annexure B preface](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2022.08-PCC-PCC-53-RMCP.pdf)).
- **What the RMCP must say for HVGDs.** PCC 58 lists sector red flags the risk assessment should consider: unusual linked transactions, structuring of cash, large cash in big notes, cash payment then refund by EFT, new clients with no history paying many times, purchases far from home, foreign PEPs, and payments from high-risk countries ([PCC 58, para 4.2](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2024.03-PCC-HVGD-guidance.pdf)). Draft PCC 126 adds a list of higher-risk country types, including countries outside the Kimberley Process for rough diamonds ([draft PCC 126, para 3.2](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2026.9-PCC-DPMS.pdf)). The FIC rated the inherent ML and TF risk of the DPMS sector "high" ([SRA, Dec 2023, section 8](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Sector-risk-assessment-Dealers-in-precious-metals-and-stones.pdf)).
- **CTR data fields** (Reg 22C): reporting institution name, registration or licence number, address, sector, contact person; transaction location, date, rand value, how it was conducted, currency; for each natural person: title, gender, names, ID number, nationality, date of birth, ID source, SA address, phone, occupation, country of residence, alias, source of funds, tax number, employer; for legal persons: name, registration number, the person acting and at least one director; account details where an account was used; and an indicator ([Regs 22C](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Money-Laundering-and-Terrorist-Financing-Control-Regulations.pdf)). Fields marked "full particulars" are compulsory. Others must be filled where "readily available", otherwise "not obtained" ([GN 5C, paras 16-21](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2022.10-Guidance-Guidance-Note-5C-CTRs.pdf)). If cash is both received and paid out above the threshold, two CTRs are due. Foreign currency is converted at the rate at the time of the transaction ([GN 5C, para 26](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2022.10-Guidance-Guidance-Note-5C-CTRs.pdf)).
- **Record location.** Cloud storage is allowed. Records kept outside South Africa are fine only if no foreign law blocks the dealer's or the FIC's access; otherwise copies must be kept in South Africa. Dealers using commercial storage providers should regularly test that they can retrieve their data ([GN 7B, paras 169-174](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Guidance-Note-7B-%E2%80%93-Implementation-of-various-aspects-of-the-FIC-Act.pdf)). Records linked to an ongoing investigation should be kept until law enforcement confirms the case is closed (para 178).
- **Defence for staff.** An employee charged with failing to report has a defence if they followed the RMCP's reporting procedure or reported to the compliance officer ([Act s69](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Financial-Intelligence-Centre-Act-2001-Act-38-of-2001.pdf)). A logged internal escalation therefore protects staff.
