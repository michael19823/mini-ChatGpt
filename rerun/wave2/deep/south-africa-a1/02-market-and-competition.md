# SA high-value goods dealer FICA pack: market size, buyers and competition (deep dive 02)

Date: 10 Oct 2026. Builds on [the A1 report](../reports/south-africa-a1.md). Scope: market size, buyers, competition, channels and regional expansion. Law, product, go-to-market and payments are covered by the other agents.

Main source. The FIC published its 2025/26 annual report on 30 Sep 2026 ([FIC AR 2025/26 PDF](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf); date per [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61895)). I downloaded it and the 2024/25 report ([FIC AR 2024/25 PDF](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf)) and read the tables myself. Page numbers below are the printed page numbers. They match the PDF page numbers. I also read the SADPMR annual reports for 2020/21 and 2021/22 myself.

Language note. South African business and regulatory text is in English. The FIC, SADPMR, trade bodies and vendors all publish in English, so I searched in English only. Afrikaans trade sources did not come up for this topic.

## Summary

- **The buyer base is now counted from the regulator's own tables.** At 31 March 2026 the FIC had **5,581 item 20 registrations**: 4,277 motor dealers, 640 "other goods", and **664 in the jewellery, gold and stone niche** (244 precious stones, 178 precious metals, 242 Krugerrand dealers) ([FIC AR 2025/26, pp. 29 and 38](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)). Registrations grew 8% in the year. A registration is not always a separate firm, so paying firms are fewer (unverified how many fewer).
- **Compliance is poor, and that is the opening.** Only 1,670 item 20 registrants (30%) have ever sent the FIC an RMCP ([p. 36](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)). Only 54% have filed the risk and compliance return (52% of motor dealers, 57% of Krugerrand dealers) ([p. 38](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)). Directive 12 now makes the RMCP a yearly upload, due 31 October for item 20 ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61635)).
- **Jewellers are the most inspected group per head.** The FIC ran 151 inspections on the 600-660 jewellery, gold and stone registrations over two years, about one for every four (my arithmetic from [AR 2024/25, p. 35](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2024-2025.pdf) and [AR 2025/26, p. 40](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)). The top inspection findings are a missing or weak RMCP, poor due diligence, late registration and no evidence of sanctions screening ([AR 2025/26, p. 41](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)).
- **The jewellers' association is about the same size as the niche.** I counted the JCSA's public directory: 581 listings, 565 businesses (213 manufacturers, 186 retailers, 83 wholesalers, 14 refiners), 51% in Gauteng ([JCSA directory](https://www.jewellery.org.za/directory)). The whole jewellery, gold and stone pool, registered or not, is about 700 to 1,200 businesses (my estimate).
- **The niche alone is too small for a business.** At 20% share and about R6,000 a year, 664 registrations give under R1m a year. The product has to sell to all 5,581 item 20 dealers, with jewellers and bullion dealers as the first group, not the only one.
- **Competitors each do one slice, and prices are now known.**
  - The FIC itself: a free editable RMCP template for small non-financial firms (PCC 53, Annexure B). The FIC warns that an uncustomised copy makes the firm "non-compliant" ([PCC 53, pp. 4 and 21](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2022.08-PCC-PCC-53-RMCP.pdf)).
  - AML GO: custom RMCP "from R7,000", RMCP review "from R2,500", screening R1 to R7 a check ([amlgo.co.za](https://amlgo.co.za/)).
  - VerifyNow: a free RMCP "starting document" generator, ID checks from R2.99, sanctions and PEP checks from R5.98, monitoring from R1,999 a year, training R747.50 per learner ([VerifyNow](https://www.verifynow.co.za/pricing)).
  - Moonstone: an RMCP template at R4,995 excl. VAT, once-off ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/new-do-it-yourself-fica-compliance-solution-for-accountable-institutions/)).
  - nCino KYC (formerly DocFox): due-diligence software for dealers, price not published.
  - **Nobody sells a yearly, sector-specific product that writes a tailored RMCP and keeps the calendar, the register and the inspection file.**
- **Templates are a trap.** NADA told motor dealers the FIC "will not accept standard templates" and that the RMCP "must be personalised for each business's operating model" ([NADA, 11 Mar 2025](https://nada.co.za/?p=5097)). The product must build the RMCP from the dealer's own answers.
- **Willingness to pay is anchored at R3,000 to R9,000 a year.** One-off RMCP work costs R4,995 to R7,000+. Fines start at R10,000: the FIC's standard notices charge R25,000 for late registration and R50,000 for a missed return, cut to R10,000 if fixed at once (my reading of FIC notices, e.g. [Miller Gold House](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Administrative-sanction-%E2%80%93-Miller-Gold-House-Pty-Ltd.pdf), [Auctionman](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Administrative-sanction-%E2%80%93-Auctionman-Pty-Ltd.pdf)). 7 diamond and precious-metal dealers paid R10,000 each for a missed first return ([FIC, Dec 2024](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Media-release-Sanctions-and-enforcement-2024.pdf)). In 2025/26 one high-value goods dealer was fined R210,000, of which R105,000 is payable, for RMCP, screening and governance failures (under appeal) ([AR 2025/26, p. 47](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)).
- **Year-3 revenue estimate: about R2m to R3m a year** (base case about R2.3m). This needs the motor and other-goods dealers as well as jewellers, plus consultants who resell it. That is a little below the A1 re-assessment's R3m to R4m.
- **Regional.** The same engine could later serve other goAML countries that cover precious-metal and stone dealers: Kenya (dealers told to register by 11 April 2025, [People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/news/state-orders-dealers-in-precious-metals-stones-to-register-with-frc-by-april)), Mauritius, Botswana (stone dealers and car dealerships were covered already under its 2009 Act, [FI Act 2009](https://policyvault.africa/wp-content/uploads/policy/BWA549.pdf)) and Namibia (coverage unverified). Each needs its own legal content. None is a reason to enter before South Africa works.

## Buyer profile and pain

### Who they are
- **Jewellers and gold dealers.** Mostly small, owner-run businesses. In the JCSA directory, 51% of listings are in Gauteng, 29% in the Western Cape and 11% in KwaZulu-Natal; more are manufacturers (213) than retailers (186) (my count of the [JCSA directory](https://www.jewellery.org.za/directory), Oct 2026). Local gold jewellery fabrication is small: 1,180 kg in 2021/22, up from 683 kg in 2020/21 ([SADPMR AR 2021/22](https://www.sadpmr.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/SADPMR_AR-2022_web-1.pdf)). They already live with a permit regime: a jeweller's permit from SADPMR, and JCSA membership requires a valid permit and, where relevant, a second-hand dealer's licence ([JCSA](https://www.jewellery.org.za/membership)).
- **Diamond and stone dealers.** They are licensed by SADPMR and file the RCR at the best rate (87%). They are used to paperwork and may be the easiest early buyers (my inference).
- **Krugerrand and bullion dealers.** 242 registrations. They were reporting institutions with lighter duties before December 2022 and now carry full duties ([Webber Wentzel](https://webberwentzel.com/News/Pages/worth-their-weight-in-gold-high-value-goods-dealers-fall-within-fica-ambit.aspx)). Only 57% have filed the RCR ([AR 2025/26, p. 38](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)).
- **Motor dealers.** The largest group (77% of item 20). They range from listed groups to one-site used-car lots. Only 52% filed the RCR.
- **Other goods.** Farm equipment, machinery, livestock, art, antiques and boats. The weakest filers (49%).

### How they comply today
1. **goAML, free.** Registration, cash and suspicious reports, and the RMCP upload all go through it. It does not write the RMCP, keep due-diligence files or track deadlines ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/?p=74912)).
2. **A template or a one-off consultant job.** Moonstone template (R4,995), the free VerifyNow generator, or AML GO's custom RMCP (from R7,000) (prices below).
3. **Due-diligence software, for some motor dealers.** nCino KYC markets to dealerships ([nCino KYC](https://blog.kycafrica.ncino.com/the-best-motor-dealerships-across-south-africa-are-already-using-ncino-kyc-africa-heres-why-yours-should-too)).
4. **Nothing.** 70% have no RMCP on file with the FIC, and 46% have not filed an RCR (see tables).

### Evidence of pain
- **Inspection findings in 2025/26.** The main failures were:
  - not developing or implementing an RMCP;
  - RMCPs that "did not meet the FIC Act requirements";
  - not sending in RMCPs when asked;
  - weak due diligence on companies and politically exposed persons;
  - late registration or out-of-date registration details;
  - "lack of evidence of client screening against the targeted financial sanctions list" ([FIC AR 2025/26, p. 41](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)).
  Every one of these is a record-keeping job that software can do.
- **171 of 549 inspections were triggered just by not filing the RCR** ([same, p. 41](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)). Missing a form leads straight to an inspector's visit.
- **Templates fail.** NADA, 11 March 2025: "The FIC has noted that they will not accept standard templates which are being offered in the marketplace"; "this is not simply a tick box exercise but a detailed risk analysis and mitigation document" ([NADA](https://nada.co.za/?p=5097)).
- **goAML access is hard.** The FIC said it "continues to receive numerous enquiries on how to access goAML", mostly about password resets and adding users ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-accepting-rmcps-after-the-12-march-deadline/)). Registrations are often rejected for unclear ID copies or missing company details ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-registration-turmoil/)).
- **Cash reporting traps.** Dealers who told clients to deposit cash into the dealer's bank account assumed no report was due. The FIC disagreed and fined one dealer R66,000 for 6 unreported matters and another R368,000 for 50 ([Moonstone, 2017](https://www.moonstone.co.za/important-fica-reminder-for-motor-dealers/)). That was before item 20, but the rule still applies.
- **Thin RMCPs.** Many dealer RMCPs are only three or four pages ([DealerFloor](https://dealerfloor.co.za/industry-news/seven-key-points-to-become-fully-fica-compliant); [FAnews](https://www.fanews.co.za/article/compliance-regulatory/2/general/1082/fica-reality-check-2026-brings-reckoning-for-sa-s-high-value-goods-dealers/43138)).
- **New filings keep coming in 2026.** Directive 10 (all office locations, due about 29 October 2026), Directive 12 (yearly RMCP, 31 October) and draft PCC 126 ([Acts Online](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2026/08/fic-directive-10-geographic-location-reporting); [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61913)).
- **No forum threads found.** Searches of Reddit and MyBroadband found no dealer threads on goAML. The pain shows up in regulator data and trade press, not in forums.

### What non-compliance costs (FIC 2025/26, my reading of the report)
- The FIC issued 361 "admission of non-compliance" notices in 2025/26: 209 for not filing the RCR and 152 for not registering. 149 were settled with a R10,000 fine each (R1.49m in total). The other 212 went to the FIC Adjudication Panel ([FIC AR 2025/26, p. 43](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)).
- 19 full sanctions on non-financial businesses totalled R635,000. Total FIC fines for the year: R2,125,000 (same page).
- Most named fines were on estate agents and attorneys: R10,000 to R50,000 for a missed RCR, and R10,000 to R175,000 for RMCP failures ([FIC AR 2025/26, pp. 44-46](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)).
- **One high-value goods dealer appealed a sanction in 2025/26.**
  - Charges: RMCP failures (s42(1) and (2)), no sanctions screening (s28A(3)), weak compliance governance (s42A) and registration gaps (s43B).
  - Penalties: R100,000 for the RMCP, R100,000 for screening and R10,000 for registration. R105,000 is payable and R105,000 is suspended ([FIC AR 2025/26, p. 47, Table 11](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)).
  - The dealer is not named because the appeal is pending.
- **The FIC's standard "admission of non-compliance" notices (I read them myself).** I pulled the FIC's sanction documents through its website's media API: 355 PDFs uploaded from 2023 to October 2026 ([FIC media search](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-json/wp/v2/media?search=sanction&per_page=100)). The notices follow one template:
  - Late registration: a R25,000 fine, cut to R10,000 if the firm registers by a set date. Example: Miller Gold House (Pty) Ltd, signed 31 March 2025 ([FIC notice](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Administrative-sanction-%E2%80%93-Miller-Gold-House-Pty-Ltd.pdf)). The notice text names "Credit Providers" as the added category, so the firm's FIC sector is not certain from the notice (the name suggests gold; unverified).
  - Missed risk and compliance return: a R50,000 fine, cut to R10,000 if the return is filed quickly. Example: Auctionman (Pty) Ltd, December 2025 ([FIC notice](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Administrative-sanction-%E2%80%93-Auctionman-Pty-Ltd.pdf)).
  - So the real price of one missed form is R10,000 if the firm reacts at once, and R25,000 to R50,000 if it does not.
- **Jewellers and Krugerrand dealers were named early.** From November 2023 to November 2024 the FIC fined 7 "diamonds and precious metals dealers" R10,000 each (R70,000) for missing the first RCR. It said Krugerrand dealers were among the groups that "remained non-compliant" ([FIC media release, 5 Dec 2024](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Media-release-Sanctions-and-enforcement-2024.pdf)).
- About 90% of motor-dealer penalties are R100k or more (per [FAnews](https://www.fanews.co.za/article/compliance-regulatory/2/general/1082/fica-reality-check-2026-brings-reckoning-for-sa-s-high-value-goods-dealers/43138), Jan 2026; not checked against FIC tables).

### Reading
- Small South African firms are used to paying **R100 to R400 a month** for compliance software (ClearComply) and **R5,000 to R7,000 once** for an RMCP.
- The fine for one missed form (R10,000) is more than a year of a mid-priced product. The fine for a bad RMCP (R100,000 in the dealer case) is ten times more.
- A yearly price of **R3,000 to R9,000** sits between the free and template options and a custom consultant RMCP. That fits the evidence. It is still untested with real buyers.
- Willingness to pay is lower where firms think they will not be inspected. Motor and other-goods dealers file at only about 50%. Jewellers, who are inspected far more often, should feel the risk more (my inference).

Discussion.
- **No one covers duties 2, 3, 4, 7 and 9 together as a yearly product.** These are the duties the FIC's inspection findings focus on.
- **Screening (6) and ID checks (5) are a commodity.** VerifyNow and AML GO sell them for R3 to R7 a check. The product should plug one of them in, or let the dealer record a check done elsewhere, not build its own.
- **Two free options set the floor: the FIC's own PCC 53 template and the VerifyNow generator.** The FIC itself says its template is only a framework and must be customised. That warning is the sales argument. Any RMCP feature must be clearly better: built for the sub-sector (jeweller, bullion, Krugerrand, stones, motor, agri), produced from the dealer's own answers, and kept up to date each year.
- **nCino KYC is the main threat.** It is well funded, already talks to dealers, and runs free webinars. Nothing announced shows an RMCP or calendar feature (unverified roadmap).
- **No jewellery-specific FICA product was found** in this pass or in the A1 passes.

## Implications for positioning and pricing

1. **Position: "Your yearly FIC file, done for you: for high-value goods dealers."** Lead with jewellers, gold, Krugerrand and stone dealers. They have the highest inspection rate, the new PCC 126 rules, and no tailored product. But build sub-sector packs for motor and other goods from the start, because 88% of registrations are there.
2. **Sell the yearly job, not the document.** Free and R5k templates exist, and the FIC rejects standard templates. The value is a tailored RMCP built from the dealer's answers and updated every year, plus:
   - the deadline calendar (31 October RMCP, RCR window, Directive 10 changes);
   - a register of R100k+ sales and R50k+ cash, including cash paid into the bank;
   - a screening and due-diligence log;
   - a training log;
   - a one-click inspection file.
   These match the FIC's 2025/26 inspection findings line by line.
3. **Do not build screening.** Plug in VerifyNow or AML GO at R3 to R7 a check, or let the dealer record a check done elsewhere.
4. **Suggested prices** (excl. VAT, billed yearly by card; to be tested):
   - **Essentials: R290 a month or R2,900 a year.** Applicability check, tailored RMCP builder, calendar, register, training log. Pitched against the free generator and the R4,995 template.
   - **Inspection-ready: R5,900 a year.** Adds the inspection file, a guided yearly RMCP update, Directive 10 and RCR checklists, and multi-user access.
   - **Reviewed: R8,900 a year.** Adds a yearly RMCP review by a partner consultant or attorney (AML GO charges from R2,500 for a review).
   - **Each extra branch or SADPMR permit:** R1,200 a year.
   - **Consultants and accountants:** R1,990 a month for up to 15 dealers.
   - Screening checks are passed through at cost plus a margin.
   The A1 re-assessment suggested about R9,000 a year. The benchmarks support a lower entry tier, with R9,000 as the top tier.
5. **Revenue check (year 3, my estimate):**
   - Jewellery niche: 664 x 20% = about 130 firms x R6,000 = about R0.8m.
   - Motor and other goods: 4,917 x 4% = about 200 firms x R4,500 = about R0.9m.
   - Consultants: 25 x R24,000 = R0.6m.
   - **Total about R2.3m a year (range R1.5m to R3.5m).** That is about US$130k at roughly R17.5 to the dollar (rate unverified).
   - The jewellery niche alone (under R1m) does not carry the business.
6. **Use the FIC's own words in the pitch.** The FIC gives away an RMCP template but says an uncustomised copy is "non-compliant" ([PCC 53, p. 21](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2022.08-PCC-PCC-53-RMCP.pdf)). The product is the customising, done from the dealer's answers and kept current. Its text must be original, because PCC 53 may not be reused commercially.
7. **First pilots from the JCSA directory.** 581 listings with phone numbers, 462 of them in Gauteng and the Western Cape, where most FIC inspections of jewellers happen ([JCSA directory](https://www.jewellery.org.za/directory); [FIC AR 2025/26, p. 40](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)). Start with retailers, wholesalers, refiners and bullion and Krugerrand sellers, not manufacturers who may never sell a R100k item.
8. **Adjacent upsell later.** The same engine fits estate agents (9,695 registrations) and attorneys (21,034), who are under the same FIC regime ([FIC AR 2025/26, p. 29](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)). That is a much bigger pool, but far more crowded (nCino KYC through the Law Society, estate-agent software). It is out of scope here.

## Open questions

- How many separate firms sit behind the 5,581 item 20 registrations (branches, groups)? An FIC PAIA request or a question to the FIC could answer this.
- How many SADPMR jeweller's permits and diamond licences are valid today? The annual reports show only yearly issues. A PAIA request to SADPMR may answer it.
- Will final PCC 126 (comments closed 16 October 2026) raise metal and stone registrations to one per permit, and does it catch scrap-gold buyers?
- What does nCino KYC charge dealers, and does it plan an RMCP or calendar feature?
- What do law firms charge for a dealer RMCP? Only AML GO (from R7,000) publishes a price.
- Would JCSA or NADA endorse or co-brand a member pack? (JCSA's directory now shows 581 listings; how many members are not listed is unknown.)
- Would the VerifyNow free generator's output pass an FIC inspection? If yes, the RMCP feature alone is worth little.
- How many jewellers sell R100k+ items but are not registered? A match of the JCSA directory against the FIC register is not possible, because the FIC does not publish names of registrants.
- Which dealers are covered in Namibia and Botswana, and how many are registered?
