# South Africa: FIC Act RMCP and RCR duties for small Schedule 1 institutions, turned into product requirements

Status: complete as of 2026-10-10 (open questions at the end). Primary sources read in full or in the relevant parts: the FIC Act sections on Acts Online (consolidated text with amendment notes), Directives 8, 10, 11 and 12, the Directive 12 consultation feedback note, Guidance Note 7B (3 Aug 2026), PCC 53, PCC 47A, PCC 60, the FIC's 2026 legal-practitioner RCR questionnaire, the FIC's goAML "How to submit an RMCP" graphic and its 9 Oct 2026 notice, and the Legal Practice Council (LPC) advisory notice of 15 May 2026. Secondary sources are used only for enforcement statistics and cases, and are marked.

Abbreviations: FIC = Financial Intelligence Centre (regulator and FIU). FIC Act = Financial Intelligence Centre Act 38 of 2001. AI = accountable institution. RMCP = Risk Management and Compliance Programme (s42). RCR = Risk and Compliance Return. GN 7B = FIC Guidance Note 7B. PCC = FIC Public Compliance Communication. CDD / EDD / SDD = customer / enhanced / simplified due diligence. TFS = targeted financial sanctions. PF = proliferation financing. DPIP / FPPO = domestic prominent influential person / foreign prominent public official. STR / CTR / TPR = s29 suspicious, s28 cash threshold and s28A terrorist property reports. CJE = commercial juristic entity under the Legal Practice Act 28 of 2014 (LPA). Org ID = the FIC registration number on goAML, the FIC's registration and reporting platform.

## Summary

- **The core duty is old; the yearly filing is new.** Since 2017, s42 has required every AI to "develop, document, maintain and implement" an RMCP. The 2022 amendment added proliferation financing (in force 31 Dec 2022). The RMCP must be approved by the board, senior management or highest authority (s42(2B)), reviewed "at regular intervals" (s42(2C)), made available to staff (s42(3)) and given to the FIC on request (s42(4)) ([FIC Act s42](https://www.acts.co.za/financial-intelligence-centre-act-2001/42__risk_management_and_compliance_.php)).
- **Directive 12 turns "on request" into a yearly upload.** It was gazetted on 4 Sep 2026 (GN 7887, GG 55337), and submissions opened on 7 Sep 2026 ([Directive 12](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Directive-12-On-the-submission-of-risk-management-and-compliance-programmes.pdf)).
  - Items 1 (legal practitioners), 2 (trust and company service providers), 9 (gambling) and 11 (non-bank credit providers) file by **9 October** each year.
  - Items 3 (estate agents), 14 (Postbank), 20 (high-value goods dealers), 21 (SA Mint) and 22 (crypto asset service providers) file by **31 October** each year.
  - New institutions file within **90 days** of starting business.
  - An RMCP re-approved after the yearly deadline must be filed within **10 days** of approval. The FIC's own information sheet says "90 days" instead. The gazette governs, so this conflict must be handled conservatively ([FIC info sheet, copy hosted by Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/wp-content/uploads/library/newsletter/Directive-12-on-the-Submission-of-Risk-Management-and-Compliance-Programmes-RMCPs.pdf)).
- **What the RMCP must contain is now tightly defined.** GN 7B (3 Aug 2026) replaced GN 7 and 7A ([GN 7B](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Guidance-Note-7B-%E2%80%93-Implementation-of-various-aspects-of-the-FIC-Act.pdf)). Its rules:
  - The RMCP must cover all of s42(2)(a)-(s) or say why a paragraph does not apply (s42(2A)).
  - It must describe the programme itself, not "merely reference other documents" (para 181G).
  - It should have three parts: risk assessment, mitigation and monitoring (para 183A). It must rest on an entity-wide ML/TF/PF risk assessment done first (para 183B).
  - Approval cannot be delegated, even by a sole proprietor (paras 181C-E).
  - Using a revised RMCP that has not been approved is non-compliance (Example 5).
  - PCC 53 gives small DNFBPs a template. The FIC warns that an RMCP copied from it without changes is non-compliant ([PCC 53, Annexure B](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2022.08-PCC-PCC-53-RMCP.pdf)).
- **Related duties a kit should bundle.**
  - FIC registration and 90-day updates (s43B), plus Directive 10's head-office and branch details, which existing registrants must add by about 29 Oct 2026.
  - A compliance function (s42A) and ongoing training (s43).
  - Employee screening (Directive 8 of 2023).
  - TFS screening, CDD and five-year record keeping.
  - Reports via goAML, and immediate notice of any reporting failure (Directive 3A of 2025).
  - The periodic RCR. The only one in force, Directive 11 of 2026, was a single return covering three years. It was due 30 Jun or 31 Jul 2026 ([Directive 11](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Directive-11-%E2%80%93-Risk-and-compliance-return.pdf)).
- **The FIC itself supervises all these items.** Schedule 2 now lists only the FSCA and SARB bodies, and only for financial-sector items ([Schedule 2](https://www.acts.co.za/financial-intelligence-centre-act-2001/schedule_2_list_of_supervisory_bodies.php)).
- **Enforcement is active and evidence-driven** ([Moonstone on the FIC 2025/26 annual report](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61901)).
  - In 2025/26 the FIC issued 549 inspection reports: 169 on legal practitioners and 76 on credit providers.
  - It picks inspection targets mainly from RCR data. It treated 171 institutions as high risk only because they had not filed an RCR.
  - The top findings were no RMCP, an RMCP that does not meet the Act, an RMCP not produced on request, and no documented evidence of TFS screening.
  - It issued 361 admission-of-non-compliance notices. A missed return drew a R10,000 fine.
  - The largest law-firm case is R7.7m against Kunene Ramapala Inc. That included R3.8m for having no implemented RMCP and R3.92m for having no TFS screening ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/r7-7m-fine-stands-as-fic-appeal-board-rules-against-law-firm/)).
  - The legal maximums are R10m for a natural person and R50m for a legal person ([s45C](https://www.acts.co.za/financial-intelligence-centre-act-2001/45c__administrative_sanctions.php)).
- **What the portal leaves undone.**
  - goAML accepts a PDF named `YYYYMMDD_RMCP.pdf` through "My Org Details" and sends an acknowledgement ([FIC graphic](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/HTSRMCP.png); [FIC notice 9 Oct 2026](https://www.fic.gov.za/2026/10/09/important-information-on-submission-of-rmcp-submissions-9-october-2026/)).
  - A successful upload proves only the format and naming, not adequacy ([consultation feedback, paras 18-20](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Consultation-feedback-note-Relating-to-draft-Directive-12-on-the-submission-of-RMCPs.pdf)).
  - Nothing official helps the firm draft, tailor, approve, version, time the 10-day rule, keep the evidence inspectors ask for, or handle several Org IDs.
- **No regional differences.** This is one national Act with one national supervisor and one portal, in English.
- **Upcoming changes.**
  - The FIC says further directives may extend yearly filing to all AIs.
  - The AML Bill 2026 is in Parliament, with no change to s42 found.
  - The next FATF mutual evaluation is expected to be completed in October 2027.
  - Draft PCC 126 (precious metals and stones dealers) is out for comment until 16 Oct 2026.
- **Product.** 72 testable requirements follow, each traced to its legal source. The core is:
  - a scoped profile per Org ID;
  - a risk-assessment wizard;
  - an RMCP generator with an s42(2) coverage check;
  - non-delegable approval with frozen versions;
  - deadline clocks for 9 Oct, 31 Oct, 90 days and 10 days;
  - a goAML filing pack (the customer uploads it; the product never holds credentials);
  - evidence logs and a one-click inspection pack.

## Who is obliged

Legal basis: Schedule 1 of the FIC Act as substituted by Notice 2800 (GG 47596), in force 19 Dec 2022 ([Schedule 1](https://www.acts.co.za/financial-intelligence-centre-act-2001/schedule_1_list_of_accountable_institutions.php)). Directive 12 applies to the items below. For item 11, banks, mutual banks and co-operative bank credit providers are excluded ([Directive 12, para 4](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Directive-12-On-the-submission-of-risk-management-and-compliance-programmes.pdf)).

- **No small-firm exemption.** Size changes only how detailed the RMCP must be. "An RMCP for an accountable institution which does not provide a wide range of products and/or services ... could be relatively simple" ([GN 7B, para 185](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Guidance-Note-7B-%E2%80%93-Implementation-of-various-aspects-of-the-FIC-Act.pdf)). Every s42(2) paragraph must still be covered or explained (s42(2A)).
- **Only size-type relief: the compliance person.** A legal person must have a compliance function and a person "with sufficient competence and seniority" (s42A(2)). A non-legal person must appoint a competent person, except a sole practitioner (s42A(4)) ([s42A](https://www.acts.co.za/financial-intelligence-centre-act-2001/42a__governance_of_anti-money_.php)).
- **One RMCP filing per Org ID.**
  - One submission per Schedule 1 item registration ([consultation feedback, para 7](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Consultation-feedback-note-Relating-to-draft-Directive-12-on-the-submission-of-RMCPs.pdf)).
  - Branches that are not AIs in their own right are covered by one submission. Standalone branch AIs file their own (paras 5-6).
  - Group-wide RMCPs are submitted with entity extracts as annexures (para 33).
  - A firm registered under two items files twice.
- **Possible carve-out for litigation-only attorneys (unverified).** Exemption 10 in the MLTFC Regulations exempts item 1 functions from Parts 1 and 2 of Chapter 3 (CDD and record keeping). The exception is listed transactional work: property, company, trust and account work, managing client assets, financial or real-estate representation, and litigation fee deposits of R100,000 or more in 12 months ([Exemption 10 text on Acts Online](https://www.acts.co.za/financial-intelligence-centre-act-2001/r1595_schedule_part_4_exemptions_for_attorneys_and_administrators_of_property.php)). Acts Online shows no withdrawal note. GN 7B says the 2017 amendments "necessitated the withdrawal of many exemptions"; their content may now only guide lower-risk ratings ([GN 7B, paras 42, 73](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Guidance-Note-7B-%E2%80%93-Implementation-of-various-aspects-of-the-FIC-Act.pdf)). Whether Exemption 10 itself survived is unconfirmed. Even if it did, it does not cover Part 4 (RMCP, registration, training). It would only justify "not applicable" entries for CDD in a litigation-only firm.
- **Size of the obliged population.** 60,115 institutions and reporters were registered with the FIC at 31 Mar 2026. The FIC supervises more than 80% of the AIs ([Moonstone on the annual report](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61901)). Buyer counts are covered in file 02.

## Filing channels and formats

- **One portal, three different filings.** goAML is the FIC's "registration and reporting platform". It handles registration, Directive 10 location data, the RMCP upload and the s28, 28A and 29 reports ([Directive 12, para 6](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Directive-12-On-the-submission-of-risk-management-and-compliance-programmes.pdf)).
  - The 2026 RCR was filled in directly on a separate RCR platform reached through the FIC website ([Directive 11, para 5.4](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Directive-11-%E2%80%93-Risk-and-compliance-return.pdf)), using goAML credentials ([PCC 60, para 4.11](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2026.6-PCC-60-RCR-_On-RCR-Submission.pdf)).
  - The FIC says the RCRs under Directives 6 and 7 (a Microsoft Forms link), the Directive 11 RCR and the Directive 12 RMCP are different obligations. A submission made through the wrong channel may be penalised ([consultation feedback, paras 15-16](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Consultation-feedback-note-Relating-to-draft-Directive-12-on-the-submission-of-RMCPs.pdf)).
- **RMCP upload steps** ([FIC graphic](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/HTSRMCP.png); [Moonstone summary of the info sheet](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61994)):
  1. Save the approved RMCP as a PDF named `YYYYMMDD_RMCP.pdf`, where the date is the approval date (FIC example: `20250304_RMCP.pdf`).
  2. Log into goAML, open the profile at top right, then "My Org Details".
  3. In the comments box below the address, type "RMCP submission". This is mandatory: without it the "Submit Request" button stays inactive ([FIC notice, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.fic.gov.za/2026/10/09/important-information-on-submission-of-rmcp-submissions-9-october-2026/)).
  4. Attachment > Upload the PDF.
  5. Submit Request > Continue. The FIC then issues an acknowledgement of receipt (same notice).
- **Content of the file.**
  - Submit the RMCP documentation "as approved" (consultation feedback para 22).
  - A group-wide RMCP goes in with entity extracts as annexures (para 33).
  - The FIC promised a user guide on naming and format (para 24); the graphic above appears to be it.
  - No file-size limit, page limit or rule on password protection is published (unverified).
- **What the upload proves.** Only that the format and naming are right, not that the RMCP is adequate. The FIC and supervisors will use the uploaded RMCPs for guidance, monitoring, inspections and enforcement (consultation feedback paras 18-20).
- **No API and no agent filing.**
  - Sharing goAML credentials is prohibited under Directive 2 of 2014 ([PCC 60, para 4.11](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2026.6-PCC-60-RCR-_On-RCR-Submission.pdf)).
  - Third-party service providers may not submit the RCR for the institution (PCC 60, para 4.10).
  - Directive 12 does not say expressly whether an accountant may upload the RMCP from their own goAML user. Treat it as not allowed (unverified).
  - A product can prepare and check the pack, but the institution's own registered user must click Submit.
- **RCR mechanics (2026).** Save several times; download a copy after submitting; no retraction; fix errors through a written compliance query ([PCC 60, paras 4.15, 4.18, 4.19](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2026.6-PCC-60-RCR-_On-RCR-Submission.pdf)). Paper and e-mail are not accepted, and a wrong Org ID makes the return invalid ([LPC advisory notice](https://lpc.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Advisory-Notice-Risk-and-Compliance-returns.pdf)).
  - The legal-practitioner questionnaire has four parts: general risk (1.1-1.63), PF (2.1-2.11), TF (3.1-3.32) and sector-specific questions (4.1 onwards) ([FIC questionnaire](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Legal-practitioner-questionnaire.pdf)).
- **Language and copyright.**
  - Every FIC document is in English.
  - FIC guidance and PCCs may be reproduced "in an unaltered form only for non-commercial use" ([GN 7B, preface vi](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Guidance-Note-7B-%E2%80%93-Implementation-of-various-aspects-of-the-FIC-Act.pdf); [PCC 53 copyright notice](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2022.08-PCC-PCC-53-RMCP.pdf)). A paid product must paraphrase and link, not copy.

## Supervisors and enforcement evidence

- **Who supervises.** The FIC supervises items 1, 2, 3, 9, 11, 14, 20, 21 and 22 directly.
  - Schedule 2 now lists only the FSCA (items 4, 5, 12), and the SARB, Prudential Authority and FinSurv (items 6, 7, 7A, 8, 10, 13, 19, 23). Former supervisors such as the gambling boards were deleted with effect from 19 and 31 Dec 2022 ([Schedule 2](https://www.acts.co.za/financial-intelligence-centre-act-2001/schedule_2_list_of_supervisory_bodies.php)).
  - For attorneys, the FIC has been the supervisor since 19 Dec 2022; the Law Societies held the role until 31 Oct 2018 ([Moonstone on the Len Dekker judgment](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-cannot-rely-on-pre-2022-non-compliance-in-attorney-sanctions-high-court-finds-2/)).
- **Professional regulators relay the duty but do not enforce it.** The LPC issues advisory notices to practitioners on the FIC duties. Its RCR notice told them to make sure "your RMCP is current, implemented, and properly documented" ([LPC](https://lpc.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Advisory-Notice-Risk-and-Compliance-returns.pdf)). The lead report says the LPC asks for the FIC Org ID in fidelity fund certificate statements ([LSSA guide, p.5](https://www.lssa.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Final-Draft-RMCP-Guidelines-6-5-25-Final.pdf), from lead report, not re-read).
- **How targets are picked.** All figures in this bullet and the next are from the FIC 2025/26 annual report as summarised by [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61901).
  - The FIC selects institutions for inspection mainly from its analysis of RCRs.
  - Of the 549 inspections, 171 were of institutions deemed high risk only because they had not filed an RCR.
  - 101 were rated high risk, 89 medium-high, 92 medium, 81 low and 15 low-medium.
  - The FIC also ran 161 lighter "compliance reviews": 43 of legal practitioners, 24 of credit providers, 23 of high-value goods dealers, 22 of TCSPs, 9 of gambling businesses and 8 of CASPs. These focused on RMCP drafting and spotting suspicious transactions.
- **What inspectors find.**
  - Institutions with no RMCP, an RMCP that does not meet the Act, or an RMCP not produced on request.
  - Weak CDD on legal entities and PEPs.
  - Late or stale registration.
  - "insufficient documented evidence that clients had been screened against the targeted financial sanctions list".
  - The FIC received 11,147 RMCPs during the year.
- **What inspectors check on the RMCP** ([GN 7B, paras 190AA-190CC](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Guidance-Note-7B-%E2%80%93-Implementation-of-various-aspects-of-the-FIC-Act.pdf)):
  - whether the board, senior management or highest authority approved it;
  - whether they understand the risks and translated them into controls;
  - a "holistic assessment" of whether Parts 1-3 are covered, described and approved, including reviews and amendments.
- **Sanctions in numbers (2025/26)** ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61901)).
  - 361 admission-of-non-compliance notices: 209 for unfiled RCRs and 152 for non-registration.
  - Under that process the return must be filed within seven working days and the penalty paid within a further seven. A missed return drew a R10,000 fine.
  - 149 matters were finalised for R1.49m.
  - In separate full enforcement, 28 sanctions were imposed on non-financial institutions. 19 were finalised at R635,000 and 9 are under appeal.
- **Cases.**
  - *Kunene Ramapala Inc (law firm).* Inspected in June 2023. Fines: R3.922m for no TFS screening, R3.8m for no documented and implemented RMCP, R50,000 for a late Directive 6 RCR, and R20,000 for breaching Directives 1, 2 and 4. The total was capped at 10% of turnover. The firm had registered with the FIC about seven years late. The Appeal Board said "an administrative sanction cannot be avoided merely because non-compliance was rectified after the fact" ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/r7-7m-fine-stands-as-fic-appeal-board-rules-against-law-firm/)).
  - *Len Dekker Attorneys Inc (High Court, 25 Mar 2026).* The FIC may not count non-compliance from before 19 Dec 2022 in the "duration" of a breach. All relevant remedial measures must be considered under s45C(2)(c) ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-cannot-rely-on-pre-2022-non-compliance-in-attorney-sanctions-high-court-finds-2/)).
  - *FSCA, three FSPs, R735,000 combined.* Including R300,000 for having no RMCP (search summary of [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/no-rmcp-no-excuse-fsca-fines-three-fsps-a-combined-r735-000/), unverified).
- **Sanction factors.** The FIC must weigh the nature, duration, seriousness and extent of the breach, previous non-compliance, remedial steps and other factors ([s45C(2)](https://www.acts.co.za/financial-intelligence-centre-act-2001/45c__administrative_sanctions.php)).
  - Appeals go to the Appeal Board within 30 days. The appeal is decided only on the written evidence that was before the FIC ([s45D](https://www.acts.co.za/financial-intelligence-centre-act-2001/45d__appeal.php)).
  - So evidence that does not exist at inspection time is close to worthless later. This is the strongest argument for software that keeps dated evidence.
- **Questionnaires and checklists usable as a spec.**
  - The FIC's RCR questionnaire per sector (legal practitioners: 4 parts, more than 100 questions) ([FIC](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Legal-practitioner-questionnaire.pdf)).
  - PCC 53 Annexure A, a reference table of s42.
  - PCC 53 Annexure B, the template structure.
  - PCC 53 Annexure C, risk indicators.
  - GN 7B ch. 4 ([PCC 53](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2022.08-PCC-PCC-53-RMCP.pdf)).

## Regional differences

- **None in the duty itself.** The FIC Act, its Regulations and the directives are national. The FIC is the single supervisor for these items, and goAML is the single channel ([Schedule 2](https://www.acts.co.za/financial-intelligence-centre-act-2001/schedule_2_list_of_supervisory_bodies.php); [Directive 12](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Directive-12-On-the-submission-of-risk-management-and-compliance-programmes.pdf)).
- **Licensing is partly provincial.** Item 9 gambling businesses are licensed by the National Gambling Board or a provincial licensing authority ([Schedule 1, item 9](https://www.acts.co.za/financial-intelligence-centre-act-2001/schedule_1_list_of_accountable_institutions.php)). The provincial boards are no longer FIC Act supervisory bodies (Schedule 2 deletions). A gambling module needs a "licensing authority" field, not different rules.
- **Geography matters only as a risk factor.** GN 7B and PCC 53 ask firms to rate geographic risk by country, province, region or town ([PCC 53, Table 1](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/2022.08-PCC-PCC-53-RMCP.pdf)). The product needs location fields for the risk matrix and Directive 10, not regional rule sets.
- **Language.** One English version is enough. FIC documents and the goAML interface are in English (my observation from the documents read).

## PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS

Each requirement is testable. "Basis" gives the legal source.
- "Act" = FIC Act; "Reg" = MLTFC Regulations (GN R1595 of 2002).
- "D8", "D10", "D11", "D12" = Directives 8 of 2023, 10, 11 and 12 of 2026. "D3A" = Directive 3A of 2025.
- "CF" = Directive 12 consultation feedback note; "Info" = FIC Directive 12 information sheet; "HTS" = FIC "How to submit an RMCP" graphic.
- "GN 7B", "PCC 53", "PCC 47A", "PCC 60" = FIC guidance documents.

Links are in the sections above.

**A. Scope, profile and set-up**

1. The system must store, per customer, each Schedule 1 item it is registered under (1, 2, 3, 9, 11, 14, 20, 21, 22) and the Org ID for each. Each item gets its own RMCP record, deadline and filing pack. Test: a firm registered under items 1 and 2 shows two RMCPs, two deadlines and two packs. Basis: D12 paras 4-5 and Annexure A; CF para 7.
2. The system must run scope questions per item and warn when the profile looks out of scope. Checks:
   - Item 1: own-account attorney or advocate with a fidelity fund certificate, or a CJE. An attorney employed by a registered CJE gets "no separate registration".
   - Item 11: bank-group credit providers are excluded from D12.
   - Item 20: payments of R100,000 or more for an item valued at R100,000 or more, single or linked.

   Test: a jeweller with no item at or above R100,000 sees "item 20 may not apply; take advice". Basis: Act Sch. 1 items 1, 11, 20; PCC 47A 2.2, 3.2; D12 para 4.
3. The system must record the legal form: legal person with a board; legal person without a board; non-legal person; or sole practitioner. It must derive the approver and compliance-person rules from that form:
   - a legal person needs a compliance function and a competent senior person;
   - a non-legal person needs a competent assistant unless it is a sole practitioner;
   - the approver is the board, else senior management, else the highest authority.

   Test: a "sole practitioner" profile drops the separate compliance-appointment task but keeps approval and training. Basis: Act s42A(1)-(4), s42(2B); GN 7B 181B-181E.
4. The system must not switch off any s42(2) element because of size. Small firms may get shorter text, but every element needs content or an "N/A because ..." reason. Test: a one-person firm cannot reach "ready for approval" with an element blank. Basis: Act s42(2A); GN 7B 185, 185A.
5. The system must hold the Directive 10 location data for the head office, each SA and foreign branch, and each subsidiary's head office and branches: name, licence no., registration no., address, and the compliance contact's surname, first name and contact details. It must also flag each branch as "standalone AI" or not. Test: export produces a complete Directive 10 data sheet. A standalone branch gets its own RMCP and deadline. Basis: D10 para 5.1; CF paras 5-6.
6. If the customer is in a group or ticks "complex structure", the system must show that PCC 53 is not meant for it and recommend expert review. It must also generate a group-extract annex. Basis: PCC 53 1.6-1.7; GN 7B 183D, 187; CF para 33.

**B. Deadlines and reminders**

7. The system must set the yearly RMCP deadline by item: 9 Oct for items 1, 2, 9, 11; 31 Oct for items 3, 14, 20, 21, 22. Reminders go to the compliance person and the approver at 60, 30, 14, 7 and 1 days. Test: an item 20 profile shows 31 Oct 2027; an item 1 profile shows 9 Oct 2027. Basis: D12 Annexure A.
8. The system must show the weekday of every deadline and warn when it falls on a weekend or public holiday. By default it advises filing on the previous working day, because D12 states no roll-forward (unverified). Test: 31 Oct 2026 shows "Saturday: file by Fri 30 Oct". Basis: D12 Annexure A.
9. The system must take a "business commencement date" and set two clocks of 90 days each: FIC registration and first RMCP submission. Test: commencement on 1 Feb 2027 gives 2 May 2027 for both. Basis: Act s43B(1); Reg 27A; D12 para 7.
10. Every RMCP approval dated after the yearly deadline must start a 10-calendar-day filing clock (day 1 is the day after approval). The screen must note that the FIC info sheet says 90 days and that the gazette's 10 days is used. No materiality filter is allowed. Test: approval on 15 Nov 2026 gives a due date of 25 Nov 2026; the dashboard turns red on day 8. Basis: D12 para 8; CF paras 28, 30; Info.
11. Any edit to registration data (address, compliance contact, branches, licence numbers) must start a 90-day "update goAML" task. Existing registrants must see the Directive 10 deadline (about 29 Oct 2026). Basis: Act s43B(4); D10 paras 5.2-5.3.
12. The system must schedule an RMCP review no later than 12 months after the last approval (customers may choose shorter). It must also open a review task when any of these happens: a new product, service, channel or technology; a change in the entity risk assessment; or a legal change flagged by the vendor's content team. Test: on day 366 without a review, the status is "overdue". Basis: Act s42(2C); PCC 53 2.4 and Annexure B 1.7; GN 7B 37A, 184A, 190.
13. The system must export all deadlines as an iCal feed and send e-mail reminders. Basis: operational support for rows 7-12.
14. The system must keep an RCR register: the directive, period, deadline, status, date filed and the downloaded copy of the submitted return. It must not invent future RCR dates. A new RCR directive is added by the content team when it is gazetted. Basis: D11 paras 5.1-5.3; PCC 60 4.18.

**C. Risk assessment**

15. The system must provide an entity-wide risk-assessment wizard. It must cover clients, products and services, delivery channels, geography (country, province, town), third-party service providers and other factors. ML, TF and PF must be addressed separately. The RMCP cannot reach "ready for approval" until the assessment is complete. Test: building the approval pack without a finished assessment is blocked. Basis: Act s42(1), s42(2)(a); GN 7B 183B-183C; PCC 53 3.1-3.2.1.
16. The assessment must record inherent risk, controls, residual risk and a risk-appetite statement, for the entity and for each business area. Basis: GN 7B 183A (Part 1), 183C; PCC 53 3.2.1.
17. The system must offer a sector indicator library: for lawyers, PCC 47A 4.3; for all DNFBPs, PCC 53 Annexure C; plus GN 7B indicators. Indicators must be paraphrased, carry a source link, and require a "why it applies to us" note. Test: no indicator text is a verbatim copy of more than one FIC sentence. Basis: PCC 47A 4.3; PCC 53 Annexure C; GN 7B 37; copyright notices in GN 7B and PCC 53.
18. The assessment must cite the applicable national and sector risk assessments by name and date. The content team keeps this list current. Basis: GN 7B 183E.
19. The system must provide a "new product, service, channel or technology" assessment form. It must be completed and approved before the launch date entered, and it is logged. Test: a launch date earlier than the approval date raises a warning. Basis: GN 7B 37A; PCC 53 3.2.2, 3.2.6.
20. The system must let the customer set a client risk-rating method and store it in the RMCP:
    - factors and characteristics;
    - scores from 1 to 3;
    - total-score bands mapped to SDD, standard CDD and EDD;
    - an automatic high-risk list (e.g. FPPO);
    - an automatic decline list (e.g. TFS-listed person);
    - rating intervals.

    Test: a score of 12 maps to EDD; a TFS-listed client maps to "do not proceed". Basis: PCC 53 3.2.7-3.2.10 and Table 1; GN 7B 60; Act s42(2)(m).
21. The method must not allow a rule that rates a client low risk only because of low income, unless a written reason is given. Basis: GN 7B 40A.

**D. RMCP document generator**

22. The system must hold a coverage matrix of all 20 elements of s42(2) ((a)-(q), (qA), (r), (s)). Each element must be mapped to a document section or to "N/A" with a reason or an alternative control. The matrix is exported as an annex. Test: deleting the s42(2)(k) termination section makes validation fail. Basis: Act s42(2), (2A); GN 7B 180, 181F, 185A; PCC 53 Annexure A.
23. The document must follow the three-part structure: (1) risk identification and assessment, including risk appetite; (2) mitigation (CDD, TFS, reporting, record keeping, etc.); (3) monitoring of control effectiveness. Basis: GN 7B 183A.
24. For DNFBP items, the document must contain the nine PCC 53 themes: governance; risk framework; CDD; TFS for TF; TFS for PF; PIPs; account monitoring; reporting; record keeping. Basis: PCC 53 1.8.
25. The substantive description must sit in the RMCP itself. A section that only refers elsewhere ("see our CDD manual") must fail validation. Any supporting document must be listed in a "documents forming part of this RMCP" annex and attached. Basis: GN 7B 181G, 183G; CF para 23.
26. Every generated paragraph must trace to at least one customer answer. The system must refuse approval while placeholder names, sample entity names or untouched default examples remain. Test: an RMCP built from all-default answers is labelled "not tailored, cannot be approved". Basis: PCC 53 Annexure B ("strongly cautions against ... copying"); GN 7B 181H-181J.
27. The governance section must name the approvers and their roles, the compliance function, and the compliance officer with qualifications, experience and seniority. It must state that the board, senior management or highest authority is ultimately responsible. Basis: Act s42A; GN 7B 181, 184; PCC 53 2.1-2.3.
28. The document must describe regular reporting to the approvers, escalation of breaches, and remediation steps. Basis: GN 7B 184A; PCC 53 2.7-2.8.
29. The CDD section must cover PCC 53 4.1-4.12:
    - who is a client;
    - no anonymous clients (s20A);
    - CDD by client type and risk;
    - extra due diligence for legal persons, trusts and partnerships;
    - ongoing due diligence intervals;
    - EDD and SDD conditions;
    - onboarding approval;
    - inability to complete CDD and termination (s21E);
    - client profiling;
    - doubtful information;
    - suspicion on once-off transactions under R5,000.

    Basis: Act s42(2)(b)-(m); PCC 53 4; Reg 1A.
30. The TFS sections must name the list sources (UNSC 1267 lists under POCDATARA s25; the s26A list on the FIC website), the screening tool and process, the freezing and reporting steps, and the people covered (client, beneficial owner, representative, payment data). PF must have its own section. Basis: Act s26A-26C, s28A; PCC 53 5-6.
31. The PIP section must cover DPIPs, FPPOs, family members and close associates; senior-management approval; source of funds and wealth; and data sources. Basis: Act s42(2)(l); PCC 53 7.
32. The monitoring section must cover manual or automated monitoring and the examination of complex, unusually large or unusual-pattern transactions, with written findings. Basis: Act s42(2)(e), (g), (h); PCC 53 8.
33. The reporting section must cover:
    - the end-to-end process for s28, s28A and s29 reports via goAML, including who submits;
    - the time limits: CTR above R49,999.99 within 3 days, TPR within 5 days, STR within 15 days;
    - written findings on whether to report;
    - handling of s27, s32 and s34 requests and s35 monitoring orders;
    - a tipping-off rule;
    - immediate written notice to the FIC of any reporting failure.

    Basis: Act s42(2)(o)-(p); Reg 22B, 24; PCC 53 9; D3A para 2.2.
34. The record-keeping section must say what is kept, in what format and where, for how long (5 years from the relevant trigger), and the third-party keeper's Reg 20 details if any. Basis: Act s42(2)(n), s22, s22A, s23, s24; Reg 20; PCC 53 10; GN 7B 168-174.
35. The foreign-operations and group sections (s42(2)(q), (qA)) must appear only when the profile has foreign or group operations. Otherwise they are marked "N/A" with a reason. Basis: Act s42(2)(q), (qA), (2A); GN 7B 187-188.
36. The document must contain training (s43) and employee-screening (D8) sections, and a section on how the RMCP is put into practice and shared with staff. Basis: Act s42(2)(r), s42(3), s43; D8 para 2.5; PCC 53 2.5-2.6.
37. The system must output an editable DOCX and a text-searchable PDF. Every page must carry the version number, the approval date and the Org ID. Basis: D12 para 6 (PDF upload); HTS.

**E. Approval and version control**

38. The approver selection must follow the legal-form rule in requirement 3. A committee, the compliance officer or an employee cannot be the final approver of a legal person with a board. Test: choosing "compliance officer" as approver for a company with a board is rejected. Basis: Act s42(2B); GN 7B 181A-181E, Example 1.
39. The approval pack must include a short summary of key risks and controls. Each approver must confirm that they read the full RMCP (time-stamped attestation). An optional resolution and minutes template is included. Basis: GN 7B 181H-181K, Example 3.
40. The approval must be recorded with name, role, date and signature (e-signature or uploaded wet-ink scan). The approval date becomes the filing date stamp. Basis: Act s42(2B); PCC 53 Annexure B 1.6; HTS step 1.
41. Each approved version must be frozen: stored read-only with a hash. Any edit creates a new draft. The dashboard must always show the version "in force" and flag drafts that are being used before approval. Test: editing after approval leaves the approved PDF unchanged, and the in-force marker stays on the old version until the new one is approved. Basis: GN 7B Example 5, 190; Act s42(2C).
42. A filing pack can be built only from an approved version, and the PDF must include the approval page. Basis: D12 para 8; CF para 22; GN 7B 181L.

**F. goAML filing pack (customer files; product never holds credentials)**

43. The system must name the file `YYYYMMDD_RMCP.pdf` from the approval date. It produces one PDF per Org ID, with group extracts as annexures inside it. Test: approval on 20 Sep 2027 gives `20270920_RMCP.pdf`. Basis: HTS; Info; CF para 33.
44. The system must show the five goAML steps, with a copy button for the mandatory comment "RMCP submission". Basis: HTS; FIC notice of 9 Oct 2026.
45. The system must never ask for, store or use goAML usernames or passwords, and must not submit to goAML for the customer. Test: a code and UI review finds no credential field. Basis: Directive 2 of 2014 as cited in PCC 60 4.11; PCC 60 4.10.
46. A filing can be marked "filed" only when the customer uploads the FIC acknowledgement or a screenshot. The system records the date and time, Org ID, file name and hash of the filed PDF. Test: without proof the status stays "prepared, not filed". Basis: FIC notice of 9 Oct 2026; D12 para 9.
47. Pre-flight checks before release:
    - the file is a PDF;
    - it is not password-protected;
    - the text is searchable;
    - it includes the approval page;
    - the file-name date equals the recorded approval date;
    - it is under a configurable size limit (the FIC limit is unknown).

    Basis: HTS; CF paras 18-20, 24.
48. The filing screen must state that a successful upload proves only format and naming, not adequacy. Basis: CF paras 18-20.
49. The interface must keep the RMCP upload, the RCR and the Directive 10 location update as three separate tasks, and warn against uploading an RMCP as an RCR. Basis: CF paras 15-16; RCR filing errors reported by the FIC ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-flags-filing-errors-as-rcr-deadline-closes/), from lead report).

**G. Governance, staff, training and screening**

50. The system must keep a staff register. It must flag staff "involved in transactions to which the Act applies" and record each one's acknowledgement of the current RMCP version. Test: a new RMCP version resets acknowledgements to "pending". Basis: Act s42(3); GN 7B 183F; PCC 53 1.5.
51. The system must keep a training plan and log: date, topic, trainer, attendees, material and test result. It must flag relevant staff with no training in the last 12 months (customers may choose another interval). Basis: Act s43, s62; GN 7B 184A.
52. The system must keep an employee-screening log: competence and integrity checks at hiring and periodically by risk, and TFS checks at hiring and on each s26A(3) notice. It records method and outcome, with export on request. Basis: D8 paras 2.3-2.7.
53. The system must produce a compliance-officer appointment letter and keep a competence record (qualifications, experience, seniority). Basis: Act s42A(2)(b), (4); PCC 53 2.2; GN 7B 184.

**H. Operational evidence logs (light, or import from a KYC tool)**

54. Client risk register: client reference, date, factor scores, total, CDD level, approver for high risk, next review date. CSV import from existing KYC tools must be supported. Basis: PCC 53 3.2.5, 3.2.8, 3.2.10, 4.8.
55. TFS screening evidence log: date, list or version screened, people screened, result, reviewer and action. It must show existing clients not re-screened since the last list change. Basis: PCC 53 5-6; KR Inc case (R3.92m); 2025/26 inspection finding on missing screening evidence.
56. Unusual-transaction and reporting-decision log, with written findings. Access is limited to the compliance officer and approvers, to support the tipping-off rules. Basis: Act s42(2)(h); PCC 53 8.2, 9.2, 9.4.
57. Remediation log: finding, source (inspection, review, self-identified), action, owner, due date and completion evidence. Basis: Act s45C(2)(c); Len Dekker judgment (remedial steps must be considered).

**I. Inspection and RCR readiness**

58. One-click inspection pack (ZIP with an index). It must contain:
    - every approved RMCP version with its approval;
    - the risk assessments;
    - the s42(2) coverage matrix;
    - registration and Directive 10 data;
    - filing proofs;
    - training, screening and remediation logs.

    Test: generated in under 60 seconds for a 10-person firm. Basis: Act s45B(2)(b), (d), (e); GN 7B 181L, 190AA-190CC; Act s45D(3), (3A) (an appeal is decided on the existing record).
59. The system must map the FIC's sector RCR questionnaire sections to stored data and produce a draft answer sheet. The customer's compliance officer then enters the answers on the RCR platform. The product never submits. Test: for item 1, parts 1-4 of the legal-practitioner questionnaire each show the source data used. Basis: D11 para 5.2; FIC legal-practitioner questionnaire; PCC 60 4.10-4.11.
60. On request, the system must produce a s42(4) "copy for the FIC": the in-force approved RMCP with its approval page. Basis: Act s42(4), s61(c); PCC 53 1.4.

**J. Accountant and consultant workspace**

61. A consultant account must manage many institutions. Each institution has its own items, Org IDs, deadlines and documents, and a dashboard sorts by next deadline. Basis: D12 Annexure A; CF para 7.
62. The consultant may draft, but approval must be done by the institution's own approver, signed into their own account. Test: a consultant user cannot press "approve". Basis: Act s42(2B); GN 7B 181C-181E.
63. White-label PDF output (consultant's logo) must not hide the institution's name, Org ID or approver. Basis: D12 para 6; GN 7B 181L.

**K. Data protection, hosting and security**

64. The customer must have "free and easy" access to all records at all times. That includes a full export in legible formats (PDF, CSV, DOCX) and a read-only export window of at least 90 days after cancellation. Basis: Act s24 (third-party keeping allowed if access is free and easy); GN 7B 170.
65. If the product stores records the customer must keep under the Act (CDD, transaction, screening), the system must generate the Reg 20 notice to the FIC. The notice gives the vendor's name, trading name, the person controlling access, the address where records are kept, the address from which control is exercised, and the customer's liaison person. Test: the notice names the hosting region and address. Basis: Act s24(3); Reg 20; GN 7B 170.
66. Records must be kept for at least 5 years from the relevant trigger (end of relationship, transaction concluded, STR submitted). Deletion inside that period must be blocked, and longer periods may be configured. Basis: Act s23.
67. The system must keep a tamper-evident audit trail (who, what, when; hashes of approved documents). Access needs MFA and is role-based. Basis: GN 7B 172.
68. If data is hosted outside South Africa, the vendor must offer a binding data-processing agreement that meets POPIA s72 and a written operator contract under POPIA s21, with immediate breach notification. It must also ensure no foreign law blocks SA access, or offer an SA-region copy. Basis: [POPIA s72](https://popia.co.za/section-72-transfers-of-personal-information-outside-republic/); [POPIA s21](https://popia.co.za/section-21-security-measures-regarding-information-processed-by-operator/); GN 7B 173.
69. STR-related notes must be visible only to the compliance officer and approvers, never in staff-wide views or consultant white-label exports. Basis: PCC 53 9.4 (tipping-off).

**L. Legal-content governance**

70. Every template clause, check and deadline rule must store its source (Act section, directive paragraph, GN 7B or PCC paragraph) and the source's date. When a source changes, every customer RMCP using that clause is flagged "review needed". Test: changing the GN 7B 181G rule flags all RMCPs. Basis: GN 7B preface iv; Act s42(2C).
71. The product must not reproduce FIC guidance text verbatim in paid output. It paraphrases and links. Basis: GN 7B preface vi; PCC 53 copyright notice; PCC 60 cover.
72. Every output must state that the product is not legal advice and that the approvers remain solely responsible for adequacy. An optional expert-review add-on must be offered. Basis: GN 7B 183; Act s45C(4)(b) (personal liability).

## Open questions

1. **10 or 90 days for amended RMCPs?** The gazette says 10 days (D12 para 8); the FIC info sheet says 90. Ask the FIC through a compliance query, and keep the 10-day default until it answers.
2. **Is Exemption 10 for attorneys still in force?** Acts Online shows no withdrawal, while GN 7B (para 42) says many exemptions were withdrawn without naming them. If it applies, litigation-only practices may mark CDD and record-keeping elements N/A, but the RMCP duty stays. Confirm with the FIC or a FICA attorney.
3. **May a third party (accountant or consultant) upload the RMCP from its own goAML user?** Directive 12 is silent. PCC 60 bans third-party submission only for the RCR. Confirm, because this shapes the consultant plan.
4. **Are there file-size, page or format limits for the goAML attachment?** None found.
5. **Do weekend deadlines roll forward?** 31 Oct 2026, 9 Oct 2027 and 31 Oct 2027 fall on weekends. The Interpretation Act's day-counting rule may apply to the 10- and 90-day periods but not to fixed dates (unverified).
6. **When is the next RCR, and will the questionnaires change?** No directive has been issued after Directive 11.
7. **Does the FIC review uploads automatically?** It says it will use RMCPs for monitoring and inspections, but its process is unknown.
8. **Does a vendor that writes tailored RMCPs for law firms risk "practising law"?** Legal advice is not reserved work for all purposes in South Africa, but the LPA position on drafting for attorneys was not checked (unverified).
9. **Acts Online wording of s42(4).** It shows "internal rules" while s61(c) says "copy of its RMCP". Check the Government Printer's consolidated text.
10. **Does item 3 still read "Estate Agency Affairs Act 1976"?** That Act was replaced by the Property Practitioners Act 2019, so the FIC's interpretation of item 3 should be checked (relevant to the estate-agent overlap).
