**Reading the numbers.**
- The FIC received only 10,675 RMCPs from the Directive 12 sectors in 2025/26, against 46,622 registrations. That is 23%. The FIC asked everyone for an RMCP in March 2025 ([FIC letter, 4 Mar 2025](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2025.3-GN-RMCP-Letter-Request_250304.pdf)). So about three in four registrations either have no RMCP or did not send it.
- By sector, the RMCP response was lowest for gambling (450 of 4,676, 10%), legal practitioners (4,218 of 21,034, 20%) and estate agents (2,059 of 9,695, 21%). It was highest for crypto (48%), trust and company service providers (40%) and credit providers (40%) (my calculations from FIC AR PDF p.29 and p.36).
- The core market for a product sold to small firms is therefore about **9,300 law firms, about 2,000-2,600 TCSPs (mostly accounting practices), about 2,700 credit providers (with up to 5,400 more on the NCR register), about 4,300 motor dealers, about 1,300 other high-value goods dealers, about 4,600 gambling outlets and 362 crypto firms**. Without estate agents that is roughly **25,000 firms**; with estate agents (9,695 registrations) about 35,000.

## Buyer profile and pain

**Size.** Almost all buyers are micro firms.
- Law firms: 6,969 of 9,307 firms (75%) are sole practitioners and 98% have fewer than 10 attorneys ([LSSA](https://www.lssa.org.za/about-us/about-the-attorneys-profession/statistics-for-the-attorneys-profession/)).
- Credit providers: most new NCR applicants in 2024/25 "were from sole-director businesses", mainly in Gauteng, Limpopo and KwaZulu-Natal ([NCR AR 2024/25, p.29](https://ncr.org.za/documents/pages/Annual%20Reports/NCR%20Annual%20Report%202024_2025_signed.pdf)).
- Motor dealers (4,277 of the 5,581 HVGD registrations) range from franchise dealers to small used-car lots (split not published).

**How they comply today.**
- Many do nothing until pushed. Only 23% of the Directive 12 registrations sent the FIC an RMCP in 2025/26 (my calculation from [FIC AR 2025/26, p.29 and p.36](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)).
- The FIC's main inspection findings in 2025/26 were: no RMCP developed or implemented; RMCPs that did not meet the Act; and ignoring the request to submit RMCPs (FIC AR, PDF p.40).
- Those who act use a free template (FIC PCC 53, LSSA guide, REBOSA for estate agents), a paid template (Moonstone), a KYC vendor's service (nCino KYC, eFICA) or a consultant. No survey of the split exists (unverified).
- Regulators and associations keep warning against templates. NADA told motor dealers in March 2025 that the FIC "will not accept standard templates offered in the marketplace" (search summary of [NADA](https://nada.co.za/?p=5097)). The FIC says one-person firms do not need "a 200-page document" ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-urges-businesses-to-simplify-compliance-focus-on-risks-not-paperwork/)).

**Software they already use.**
- Law firms: practice and trust-accounting software (AJS, LegalSuite, Lexpro, GhostPractice), some with nCino KYC integrations ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/?p=57648)). Prices are not published for South Africa (unverified).
- Estate agents: property CRMs and compliance tools such as Realty Comply ([Realty Comply](https://www.realtycomply.com/)).
- All: goAML for registration, cash threshold reports and RMCP upload. Some use KYC check vendors (VerifyNow, eFICA, nCino KYC).

**Pain, with evidence.**
- *Pressure is rising.* The FIC issued 549 inspection reports in 2025/26: 169 on legal practitioners, 111 on estate agents, 76 on credit providers, 61 on precious metals/stones dealers and 37 on motor dealers (FIC AR, Table 8, PDF p.40). It hosted 33 webinars and 16 roadshows for 14,077 attendees and answered 24,840 phone queries (FIC AR, PDF p.26 and p.34). Accounting Weekly reports 556 inspections in 2024/25, with remedial notices to 59% of those inspected (search summary of [Accounting Weekly](https://www.accountingweekly.com/trending-news/high-court-clips-fic-power-to-backdate-penalties), unverified).
- *Sanctions are real, if usually small.* 361 admission-of-non-compliance notices; 149 settled for R1.49m in total (about R10,000 each); 212 went to the Adjudication Panel; 19 sanctions on non-financial sectors totalled R635,000 (FIC AR, PDF p.43). Named cases in the report include an estate agent fined R175,000 for RMCP failures and legal practitioners fined R25,000-R50,000 for not filing the RCR (FIC AR, sanctions table).
- *Low filing rates.* 2026 RCR, 31 July group: legal practitioners 9.7% filed by 15 July ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-flags-filing-errors-as-rcr-deadline-closes/)). Trust service providers: only 417 of 789 had filed their RCR by 10 July 2026, and 493 had uploaded an RMCP (search summary of a [FISA notice](https://www.fisa.net.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/260626_TCSP_Submission-of-2026-RCR-1.docx.pdf), unverified).
- *Confusion over channels.* RMCPs go through goAML; Directive 11 RCRs use a separate system, and earlier RCRs used Microsoft Forms; one does not satisfy the other (search summary of [Moonstone, p=61994](https://www.moonstone.co.za/?p=61994)). The FIC flagged RMCPs uploaded instead of RCRs ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-flags-filing-errors-as-rcr-deadline-closes/)).
- *Forums.* I found no Reddit or MyBroadband threads on RMCP filing (two searches). The visible "voice of the customer" is in trade press, association notices and paid webinars, such as IEASA's "How to submit RMCP" webinar ([Quicket](https://www.quicket.co.za/events/395674-ieasa-webinar-how-to-submit-rmcp-the-step-by-step-guidance-on-the-new-directive/)).

**Reading.**
- The market already accepts **R3,500 a year** (eFICA) for a self-service builder and **R5,000-R8,500** for a drafted or template RMCP. A one-off LEAD webinar costs R1,200.
- The free generator (VerifyNow) caps what a plain PDF is worth. A buyer will pay for things the free tools do not give: a document the firm can defend at inspection, the approval and filing record, and reminders.
- Fines of R25,000-R175,000 for filing and RMCP failures are 7-50 times a R3,500 subscription. That is a clear enough sales message for firms that have been inspected or warned.

## Competitor table and discussion
