# SA RMCP filing kit: go-to-market, payments, company setup and financials (deep dive 04)

Date: 10 Oct 2026. Builds on [the B4 report](../reports/south-africa-b4.md), [01-law-and-requirements.md](01-law-and-requirements.md) and [02-market-and-competition.md](02-market-and-competition.md). Money is in South African rand (R). "Excl. VAT" means before 15% VAT. "My estimate" marks planning numbers I derived; "(unverified)" marks facts I could not confirm.

Status: complete (10 Oct 2026). Searches were in English; the FIC, SARS and trade material on this duty is published in English (the market file's Afrikaans search returned only English pages).

## Summary

- **Price below eFICA and sell more than a PDF.** Solo **R2,490 a year excl. VAT** (R2,863.50 with 15% VAT), Practice R4,490, accountants **R900 per client entity a year** (minimum 10). Anchors: eFICA's self-service RMCP Builder at R3,500 a year (from Nov 2026), Moonstone's R4,995 template, a free VerifyNow generator, and typical settled FIC fines of about R10,000 (R25,000-R175,000 in named cases) ([efica.co.za](https://efica.co.za/); [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/new-do-it-yourself-fica-compliance-solution-for-accountable-institutions/); [FIC AR 2025/26](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf)). The pitch is "your FIC year, done and provable": tailored RMCP, approval minute, goAML-ready file, 10-day amendment clock, inspection pack, multi-client view.
- **Seasons.** RMCP uploads are due 9 October (lawyers, TCSPs, gambling, credit providers) and 31 October (dealers, estate agents, crypto) every year. About half of each year's new sales should fall in August-October (model assumption). The 2026 rush has passed, so the first full season is 2027. Until then, sell GN 7B revisions, new-practice packs and the accountant plan.
- **Channels, in order:** (1) accounting practices as buyers and resellers via the Tax Faculty, SAIPA, SAICA, Accounting Weekly and FISA; (2) sole practitioners outside nCino's LSSA base via search, GoLegal and De Rebus; (3) new practices via LEAD's practice-management course; (4) credit providers via CASA; (5) motor dealers via NADA. POPIA s69 restricts cold email, including B2B, so lead with content, webinars and partners ([MJ Kotze Inc](https://mjkinc.co.za/popia/companies-and-b2b)). Year-1 marketing budget: **R180,000 (about US$11,000)**.
- **Payments: sell through Paddle from the founder's foreign company.** Paddle covers South Africa at 15% VAT for B2B and B2C, prices in rand, and charges 5% + US$0.50, about 6% of net revenue ([Paddle VAT list](https://paddle.com/support/which-countries-does-paddle-charge-vat-for); [Paddle currencies](https://developer.paddle.com/concepts/sell/supported-currencies); [Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)). SA cards may pay foreign suppliers for services and subscriptions up to **R100,000 per transaction** since 8 April 2026 ([SARB Circular 12/2026](https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/what-we-do/financial-surveillance/financial-surveillance-documents/2026/12-2026.pdf)). Paddle will not process legal advice, so a partner attorney bills any review add-on directly. Stripe on the foreign company is the back-up (UK pricing: 3.15% + 20p for non-EEA cards, +2% FX) ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/gb/pricing)).
- **Tax friction is low with Paddle.** A foreign e-services seller must register for SA VAT only above **R2.3m of SA sales in 12 months** (from 1 April 2026), and the B2B exclusion does not help a seller with mixed customers ([SARS guide VAT-REG-02-G02](https://www.sars.gov.za/vat-reg-02-g02-supply-of-electronic-services-by-foreign-suppliers-and-foreign-intermediaries-external-guide/); [SARS FAQ Q6](https://www.sars.gov.za/lapd-vat-g16-vat-faqs-supplies-of-electronic-services)). Without Paddle, non-VAT-registered buyers would owe 15% "imported services" VAT themselves (VAT215 within 60 days). There is **no withholding tax on SaaS fees**; the 15% royalty WHT only bites if the contract licenses software, and most treaties cut it to 0-10% ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/south-africa/corporate/withholding-taxes); [SARS DTA table](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Docs/WTR/Withholding-Tax-on-Royalties-Summary-of-DTA-Rates-Rest-of-the-World.pdf)).
- **No local company at launch.** If later needed (rand EFT demand, a partner that requires it, SA staff): CIPC fees are R50 + R175 and there is no minimum capital. Remote set-up costs about R5,000-R15,000 (my estimate). A resident public officer, an SA address and a bank account (3-6+ weeks) are the hard parts. Running costs are about R30,000-R50,000 a year ([MJ Kotze Inc](https://mjkinc.co.za/doing-business-in-south-africa/company-registration-foreigners)).
- **Contracts.** Many buyers are "consumers" under the CPA (juristic persons below R2m turnover or assets). Sole practitioners also get the s14 rules: a renewal notice 40-80 business days before expiry, and cancellation on 20 business days' notice ([CPA s14](https://www.acts.co.za/consumer-protection/14_expiry_and_renewal_of_fixed_term_agreements); [CGSO guidance](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2025/10/cgso-fixed-term-agreement-guidance)). Cap liability at 12 months' fees. State that approval and adequacy stay with the customer. Buy PI insurance (about R15,000 a year budgeted).
- **36-month model (founder builds with AI agents, no founder pay).** Base: 751 direct customers plus 772 accountant-managed entities, **ARR R3.06m (about US$186k) at month 36**. Year-3 profit before founder pay is about R2.0m. Cash-positive from month 10. **Peak cash need about R130,000 (US$8,000).** Low: ARR R1.1m, peak need R270,000, cash-positive only in month 32. High: ARR R5.4m.
- **Exit.** Likely buyers are KYC/AML platforms (eFICA, VerifyNow, nCino, Instarc), regulatory-reporting firms and legal publishers. nCino paid US$75m for DocFox (2024) ([The Digital Banker](https://thedigitalbanker.com/ncino-set-to-acquire-docfox-for-75m/)). At 2.5-4x ARR, the base case is worth about R7.7m-R12.2m (US$460k-740k) at month 36. Regional expansion is weak: estate agents at home (+45% registrations) beat Namibia.
- **Kill criteria:** under 3 pre-orders from 30 conversations by 10 Nov 2026; under 25 direct customers and 3 accountant practices by 30 Apr 2027; ARR under R0.33m after the October 2027 season; first-year renewal under 50%.

- **What not to do.** Do not sell "an RMCP generator"; that is free elsewhere. Do not price per document; the amendment clock and inspection pack are what keep customers renewing.

### VAT (15%) on the price

- South Africa's VAT rate is 15%; the 2026 Budget left it unchanged ([Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr, Budget 2026 VAT summary](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/news/publications/2026/South-Africa/Tax-Exchange-Control/Tax-and-Exchange-Control-25-February-2026-Budget-summary-VAT); search summary).
- Software sold online by a foreign company is an "electronic service" whatever its nature: SARS says "from 1 April 2019, VAT became payable on any services delivered by electronic means" ([SARS VAT FAQ on electronic services, Issue 4, Q7](https://www.sars.gov.za/lapd-vat-g16-vat-faqs-supplies-of-electronic-services)).
- Show prices excluding VAT on the website for business buyers, and the VAT-inclusive price at checkout. Which party charges VAT depends on the payment set-up (see Payments and tax friction).
- About three quarters of law firms are sole practitioners ([LSSA](https://www.lssa.org.za/about-us/about-the-attorneys-profession/statistics-for-the-attorneys-profession/)). Many are below the VAT registration threshold, which rose from R1m to R2.3m on 1 April 2026 ([Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/news/publications/2026/South-Africa/Tax-Exchange-Control/Tax-and-Exchange-Control-25-February-2026-Budget-summary-VAT), search summary). For them VAT is a real 15% cost, so the VAT-inclusive Solo price (R2,863.50) is what they compare.

### Channels in priority order

1. **Accounting practices as buyers and resellers (item 2).** 2,574 TCSP registrations, mostly accounting practices, file by 9 October themselves and look after small credit providers, dealers and other clients ([FIC AR 2025/26](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf), via the market file). No competitor offers them a multi-client view. Reach them through CPD webinars with the Tax Faculty (SAIT), SAIPA and SAICA, Accounting Weekly / Accounting Academy, and FISA for trust practitioners ([Tax Faculty RMCP webinar](https://taxfaculty.ac.za/events/how-to-implement-an-rmcp-in-your-firm-after-registering-with-the-fic); [Accounting Weekly](https://www.accountingweekly.com/financial-intelligence-centre/fic-2026-rcr-deadline-is-coming-are-you-ready); [FISA notice](https://www.fisa.net.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/260626_TCSP_Submission-of-2026-RCR-1.docx.pdf)). Offer: the practice's own RMCP free with 10 client entities.
2. **Sole practitioners and small law firms outside nCino's base.** 6,969 of 9,307 firms are sole practitioners ([LSSA](https://www.lssa.org.za/about-us/about-the-attorneys-profession/statistics-for-the-attorneys-profession/)). Channels: search ("submit RMCP goAML", "Directive 12", "GN 7B RMCP"), the free check, GoLegal's newsletter and site, De Rebus, and BLA/NADEL regional events. GoLegal sells website, newsletter and social ads and claims about 16,000 newsletter subscribers (2020 figure; rate card on request) ([GoLegal advertise](https://www.golegal.co.za/advertise/); search summary). Do not try to displace nCino in LSSA-partner firms.
3. **New practices.** Every attorney opening a practice must complete LPC-approved practice-management training, and then file an RMCP within 90 days ([LEAD PMT](https://www.lssalead.org.za/legal-practitioners/practice-management-training/), via the market file). Offer LEAD a free "FIC starter" module for PMT attendees, or sponsor a PMT session.
4. **Credit providers (item 11).** CASA has "more than 1,800 non-bank credit providers" ([Moonstone on CASA](https://www.moonstone.co.za/microfinance-sa-rebrands-as-casa-to-broaden-focus-across-credit-sector/)). Member webinar and member discount; referral deal with micro-lender software such as ACPAS ([ACPAS FICA blog](https://www.acpas.co.za/FICABlog.html)).
5. **Motor and other high-value goods dealers (item 20).** NADA told dealers the FIC "will not accept standard templates" (search summary of [NADA](https://nada.co.za/?p=5097)). A motor-dealer edition and a NADA/RMI webinar before the 31 October deadline.
6. **Compliance consultants and template sellers as resellers.** Moonstone (R4,995 toolkit), Probeta and small consultancies sell static documents; offer them the accountant plan with white-label output ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/new-do-it-yourself-fica-compliance-solution-for-accountable-institutions/)).
7. **Later: practice-software vendors** (LegalSuite, GhostPractice, Lexpro) for integration or referral; AJS already integrates nCino ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/?p=57648)).

**Cold email is restricted.** POPIA s69 bars electronic direct marketing without consent unless the person is an existing customer; a non-customer may be approached once, on the prescribed form. Commentators read this as covering B2B email to firms, since POPIA protects juristic persons too ([MJ Kotze Inc on B2B](https://mjkinc.co.za/popia/companies-and-b2b); [Juta, B2B direct marketing](https://cms.juta.co.za/documents/42/Issue_no_2_B2B_Direct_Marketing_July_2022.pdf); search summaries). So the plan relies on content, partners, webinars and search, plus at most one compliant approach per prospect, not cold-email sequences.

- **Webinars as the main event.** One 45-minute CPD-style webinar a month in season, co-hosted with a partner, with a named FICA attorney on the panel. In-season: weekly.
- **Renewal.** Send the CPA-compliant renewal notice 40-80 business days before expiry ([CGSO guidance via acts.co.za](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2025/10/cgso-fixed-term-agreement-guidance)), together with "what changed in FIC rules this year".
- **Local help.** From mid-2027, a part-time South African customer-success contractor for phone and WhatsApp support in season (in the model).

## Payments and tax friction

### Do South African cards work for cross-border online payments?

- **Yes, for amounts like ours.** The Reserve Bank lets South African residents with credit or debit cards pay foreign suppliers for "imports over the Internet, services or subscriptions". The per-transaction limit rose from R50,000 to **R100,000** on 8 April 2026 ([SARB Exchange Control Circular 12/2026](https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/what-we-do/financial-surveillance/financial-surveillance-documents/2026/12-2026.pdf)). A R2,864 Solo plan or a R10,350 accountant plan is far below that.
- **No paperwork for the buyer.** A card payment needs no balance-of-payments form; a SWIFT transfer does (bank practice, unverified).
- **Practical friction.** Some cards must be enabled for online or international use in the banking app, and 3-D Secure is standard (unverified; bank-specific). Expect a few failed first attempts; the checkout should show a "card declined? enable online international payments in your banking app" tip.

### Option A (recommended at launch): Paddle as merchant of record

- **South Africa is covered.** Paddle lists South Africa at **15% VAT for both B2B and B2C** sales, and says "B2B customers are responsible for reclaiming indirect tax back on their tax returns" ([Paddle VAT countries](https://paddle.com/support/which-countries-does-paddle-charge-vat-for)).
- **Rand pricing works.** ZAR is a Paddle payment currency (minimum charge R12.75) and a payout currency, though not a balance currency. Bank transfers are only for USD, EUR and GBP ([Paddle supported currencies](https://developer.paddle.com/concepts/sell/supported-currencies)).
- **Fee.** 5% + US$0.50 per checkout transaction, no monthly fee ([Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing)). On a Solo sale of R2,863.50 incl. VAT that is about R143 + R8 = **R151**, or **6.1% of the net price** (my calculation).
- **What it removes.** Paddle is the seller of record. It charges and pays SA VAT, issues the tax invoice and takes card fraud and chargebacks. The founder's foreign company never registers for SA VAT, and the buyer has no "imported services" VAT to self-assess (see below).
- **Software only.** Paddle does not accept "human services that are not related to a software offering (e.g. ... legal advice ...)" ([Paddle: what you may not sell](https://paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/what-am-i-not-allowed-to-sell-on-paddle); search summary). Sell the software through Paddle; let the partner attorney bill the review add-on directly. Ask Paddle in writing before launch whether the "new-practice starter" counts as software.
- **Gaps.** No EFT in rand. Buyers must pay by card or PayPal (PayPal availability in SA: unverified). Paddle reviews each new seller's website and product before approval (Paddle practice, unverified).

### Option B: Stripe on the founder's foreign company

- **Stripe and South Africa.** Stripe lists South Africa as "Extended network", the same status as Ghana, Kenya and Nigeria ([Stripe global](https://stripe.com/global)). That route is for South African businesses through Paystack; it does not stop a foreign Stripe account from charging South African cards.
- **Fees on a UK Stripe account** ([Stripe UK pricing](https://stripe.com/gb/pricing)): international (non-EEA) cards 3.15% + 20p; +2% if currency conversion is needed; Stripe Billing 0.7%; Stripe Tax 0.5% per transaction where registered. If prices are in rand and paid out in pounds, the stack is about **5.9-6.4% + 20p**, about the same as Paddle, but the founder carries the VAT work and chargebacks.
- **ZAR presentment.** Stripe lets sellers charge in many currencies, including rand (unverified for 2026; check the Stripe currency list).
- **VAT duty for the founder's company.**
  - A foreign supplier of electronic services must register for SA VAT once its SA sales pass **R2.3 million in any 12 months** (up from R1 million on 1 April 2026). It may register voluntarily above R120,000 ([SARS external guide VAT-REG-02-G02, rev. 07, para 2(g) and 4.6](https://www.sars.gov.za/vat-reg-02-g02-supply-of-electronic-services-by-foreign-suppliers-and-foreign-intermediaries-external-guide/)).
  - Since 1 April 2025 a foreign supplier that sells **only to VAT-registered vendors** is outside the rules. A supplier with both vendors and non-vendors as customers must register and charge VAT on all of them once over the threshold, and must be able to prove which customers are vendors ([SARS VAT FAQ, Issue 4, Q6](https://www.sars.gov.za/lapd-vat-g16-vat-faqs-supplies-of-electronic-services)). Our buyers are a mix, so the B2B exclusion does not help.
  - Registration is by VAT101 with no SA bank account needed. Returns are monthly or two-monthly, in rand. Records must be kept in South Africa unless SARS approves otherwise ([SARS guide, 4.4, 4.6, 4.8](https://www.sars.gov.za/vat-reg-02-g02-supply-of-electronic-services-by-foreign-suppliers-and-foreign-intermediaries-external-guide/)).
  - The base case passes R2.3 million of SA sales in about year 3 (see Financial model). So Option B means a VAT registration by then, or earlier if done voluntarily.

### Buyer-side tax friction

- **Imported-services VAT (when the seller is not SA-registered).** If an unregistered foreign seller supplies a buyer that uses the service "otherwise than for the purposes of making taxable supplies", the buyer must declare 15% VAT itself. A non-vendor uses form VAT215 within 60 days (for invoices from 24 Dec 2024) when the invoice exceeds R100 ([SARS VAT FAQ, Q70-Q71](https://www.sars.gov.za/lapd-vat-g16-vat-faqs-supplies-of-electronic-services)).
  - In plain terms: a small law firm that is not VAT-registered and buys from an unregistered foreign seller owes SARS 15% on its own form. Few will know this; compliance-minded buyers will not like it.
  - A VAT-registered firm that uses the tool for its taxable business has nothing to declare (my reading of Q70).
  - Paddle (Option A) or SA VAT registration (Option B) removes this friction.
- **Withholding tax: none in the normal case.**
  - South Africa has no withholding tax on fees for services paid to non-residents ([PwC tax summaries](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/south-africa/corporate/withholding-taxes)). The proposed WHT on service fees never came into force (search summary of [MJ Kotze Inc](https://mjkinc.co.za/software-technology-law/withholding-tax-international-software-payments)).
  - The 15% royalty WHT (Income Tax Act s49A-49G) applies to payments "for the use of or right to use" IP. A hosted SaaS subscription is generally not a royalty (search summary of [MJ Kotze Inc](https://mjkinc.co.za/software-technology-law/withholding-tax-international-software-payments)). Draft the terms as access to a hosted service, with no licence to copy or install software.
  - If a payment were treated as a royalty, most treaties cut the rate: 0% for beneficial owners in the UK, Ireland, Netherlands, Germany, Sweden, Cyprus, Hungary and the US; 5% Croatia and Bulgaria; 10% Poland, Portugal, Malta and the Czech Republic; 15% Romania ([SARS DTA royalty rate table, June 2023](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Docs/WTR/Withholding-Tax-on-Royalties-Summary-of-DTA-Rates-Rest-of-the-World.pdf)). The founder's country matters only in this edge case.
- **Exchange control for the buyer.** Card payments are fine (above). A SWIFT transfer needs the bank to report the payment; costs are on the buyer (below).

### Bank transfer norms and costs

- South African firms normally pay suppliers by EFT in rand (common practice, unverified). A cross-border SWIFT payment is expensive for small amounts:
  - Standard Bank business accounts: outgoing international payment 0.502% (min R210, max R760) or 0.700% (min R300, max R995), depending on channel ([Standard Bank Business Current Account Pricing Guide 2026](https://www.standardbank.co.za/static_file/South%20Africa/PDF/Business%20Pricing/2026/Business%20Current%20Account%20Pricing%20Guide%202026.pdf); search summary).
  - Capitec: R250 per outgoing foreign payment (SHA/BEN), R500 (OUR) ([Capitec forex fee schedule 2026](https://www.capitecbank.co.za/globalassets/pages/documents-library/business/forex-fee-schedule-2026.pdf); search summary).
  - On a R2,864 Solo plan, a R210-R300 SWIFT fee adds 7-10%. **Do not offer SWIFT for Solo.** Offer it only for accountant plans above about R9,000, invoiced in EUR, GBP or USD through Paddle (bank transfer supported in those currencies).
- **Local rand options (need a local company and bank account).** Paystack in SA charges 2.9% + R1 on local cards and R3 per bank transfer (search summary of [Paystack fee note](https://intercom.help/trovahealth/en/articles/10697542-what-are-paystack-s-fees-and-how-can-i-calculate-them-south-africa)). PayFast charges 3.2% + R2 on cards and 2% (min R2) on Instant EFT, with no set-up or monthly fee ([PayFast fees](https://payfast.io/?p=25639)). Both are cheaper than Paddle, but both need a South African entity and bank account (PayFast settles in rand to an SA bank account; search summary, unverified).

### Payments recommendation

1. **Launch on Paddle**, priced in rand, VAT added at checkout, yearly plans plus a monthly card option. Total cost about 6% of net revenue.
2. **Keep Stripe as a back-up** on the foreign company, in case Paddle refuses the product category or the account.
3. **Watch EFT demand.** If more than about 25% of qualified leads refuse to pay by card (kill-or-pivot signal for the payment set-up, not the business), open a local company and add PayFast Instant EFT (see Company setup).
4. **Accountant plans** can also be invoiced in EUR/GBP/USD for bank transfer via Paddle.

## Company setup (needed or not, costs)

### Recommendation

**No South African company at launch.** Sell from the founder's existing foreign company through Paddle. The reasons:
- Card payments from SA buyers to foreign sellers are allowed up to R100,000 per transaction ([SARB Circular 12/2026](https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/what-we-do/financial-surveillance/financial-surveillance-documents/2026/12-2026.pdf)).
- Paddle handles SA VAT, so the foreign company needs no SA VAT number ([Paddle](https://paddle.com/support/which-countries-does-paddle-charge-vat-for)).
- There is no withholding tax on SaaS fees in the normal case ([PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/south-africa/corporate/withholding-taxes)).
- No licence is needed to sell compliance software; the FIC registers accountable institutions, not their software vendors (my reading of [01-law-and-requirements.md](01-law-and-requirements.md); unverified).
- A foreign company must register in SA as an "external company" only if it "conducts business" there: being party to an SA employment contract, or a course of conduct in SA over six months that shows it means to carry on business there. It must then register within 20 business days ([Companies Act s23, Acts Online](https://www.acts.co.za/companies-act-2008/23_registration_of_external_companies_and_registered_office); search summary). Selling online from abroad with no SA staff or office is generally treated as outside this, but no source addresses online sales directly (unverified). **Do not hire an SA employee on the foreign company**; use independent contractors or an employer-of-record.

**Open a local (Pty) Ltd only if one of these happens:**
1. More than about 25% of qualified buyers refuse card payment and want rand EFT or debit order.
2. A channel partner (LSSA/LEAD, a practice-software vendor, CASA, a bank) will only contract with, or pay, a South African entity, or asks for a B-BBEE certificate.
3. The founder wants an SA employee (customer success) rather than contractors.
4. The founder moves to South Africa.

A local company pays for itself only above about R1.5m-R2m a year in sales, or if it unlocks a channel.

## Contracts and liability

### Who the contract is with
- With Paddle, the buyer's purchase contract is with Paddle (merchant of record), and the software terms are with the founder's company. With Stripe, both are with the founder's company.
- Put the governing law and courts of the founder's home country in the terms, but assume SA consumer law can still apply to small buyers (below).

### South African consumer law reaches many of these buyers
- **CPA and small juristic persons.** The Consumer Protection Act does not protect a juristic person whose asset value or annual turnover is R2 million or more ([SAICA on the CPA](https://saica.org.za/resources/legislation-and-governance/consumer-protection-act); [threshold notice via PolicyVault](https://www.policyvault.africa/policy/determination-of-threshold-in-terms-of-the-consumer-protection-act/?version=0); search summaries). Many sole practitioners (natural persons) and small credit providers and dealers are below R2m, so **they count as consumers**.
- **Fixed-term rules (CPA s14).** For consumers who are natural persons (s14 does not apply between juristic persons):
  - the consumer may cancel on 20 business days' notice, subject to a reasonable cancellation charge;
  - the supplier must give written notice of an auto-renewal **40-80 business days before expiry**, with any changes and the option to cancel;
  - the default maximum term is 24 months ([Acts Online, CPA s14](https://www.acts.co.za/consumer-protection/14_expiry_and_renewal_of_fixed_term_agreements); [CGSO guidance via acts.co.za, Oct 2025](https://acts.co.za/news/blog/2025/10/cgso-fixed-term-agreement-guidance); search summaries).
  - **Product consequence:** send a renewal notice 60 business days (about 12 weeks) before each yearly renewal, offer a pro-rata refund on early cancellation, and keep terms at 12 months.
- **Unfair terms.** CPA ss48-51 limit unfair, unreasonable or unjust terms, and a clause limiting the supplier's liability must be drawn to the consumer's attention in plain language (CPA s49; unverified, not opened). Use a short, bold liability box at checkout, not only in the terms.
- **ECTA.** Natural-person buyers online get the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act's website disclosures (s43) and a cooling-off right (s44) (unverified, not opened). Paddle's buyer terms cover refunds; mirror a 7-day no-questions refund for Solo.

### Liability and what the product promises
- **The product is not legal advice.** The Legal Practice Act reserves for practitioners court appearances and drawing up documents for court proceedings, and acts other laws reserve for attorneys ([LPA s33, Acts Online](https://www.acts.co.za/legal-practice-act-2014/33__authority_to_render____)). Drafting a firm's RMCP is not on that list (my reading). The product must not call itself a law firm or hold itself out as a legal practitioner (s33(2)).
- **Approval stays with the customer.** GN 7B says approval of the RMCP cannot be delegated, even by a sole proprietor (see [01-law-and-requirements.md](01-law-and-requirements.md)). The terms and every PDF should say that the approver is responsible for adequacy, that the tool helps them build and evidence the programme, and that a goAML upload does not mean the FIC accepts the RMCP.
- **Liability cap.** Cap at fees paid in the last 12 months. Exclude indirect loss and regulatory fines, but not for gross negligence or fraud (SA courts may not uphold an exclusion of gross negligence against consumers; unverified). Promise a **content currency commitment** instead: "we update the questions and text within 30 days of any new FIC directive or guidance note and tell you what changed".
- **Partner attorney review add-on.** Contract the review directly between the buyer and the attorney (the attorney's own engagement letter); the platform takes no fee at first. Check the LPC Code of Conduct on fee sharing with non-practitioners and on touting before launch (open question).
- **Data protection.** The customer is the responsible party; the vendor is an operator. Offer a POPIA s21 operator agreement and an s72 cross-border transfer clause, as set out in requirement 68 of [01-law-and-requirements.md](01-law-and-requirements.md). Keep no client CDD data in the MVP, only firm-level answers and staff names.

### Insurance
- Buy professional indemnity / technology errors-and-omissions cover in the founder's home country, naming South African customers in the territory. A UK contractor-type policy for up to £1m starts around £83-97 a year ([Simply Business](https://www.simplybusiness.co.uk/business-insurance/professional-indemnity/); search summary); a SaaS company with regulatory-compliance output will pay more. I budget **R15,000 (about £600) a year** (my estimate) and get a broker quote before launch.

### Documents to prepare (budget R25,000-R40,000 once, my estimate)
1. SaaS terms (SA-consumer-proofed: CPA s14 renewal notice, s49 liability notice, ECTA disclosures).
2. Privacy notice and POPIA operator agreement.
3. Accountant/reseller agreement (white-label, client consent, no shared goAML credentials).
4. Partner attorney agreement (review add-on).
5. Content disclaimer and "what this is and is not" page.
A South African technology lawyer should review 1-3 (about R15,000-R25,000; my estimate, unverified).

### Unit economics and what the numbers mean
- **Cash need is small** because the founder builds with AI agents and takes no pay at first: about R130,000 (US$8,000) in the base case, R270,000 (US$16,500) in the low case. Plan **R300,000 (about US$18,000)** of runway to be safe.
- **Blended acquisition cost, year 1 base:** marketing R180,000 + referral commissions about R24,000 + customer-success contractor R60,000, over 270 new accounts, is about **R980 per account**, excluding founder time (my calculation). A Solo customer pays R2,790 in year 1 (after the founding period), so payback is under six months.
- **Lifetime value, base:** with 70% then 80% renewal a direct customer stays about 4.5 years on average, worth about R12,500 in revenue and about R10,500 after payment, hosting and content costs (my calculation). LTV/CAC is high on paper. The real limit is the founder's selling time in the August-October peak and the small pool.
- **Founder income.** The base case could pay the founder R40,000 a month from year 2 and still end month 36 with about R2.5m cash. The low case cannot support founder pay within 36 months.
- **Seasonality drives cash.** About 40-50% of each year's cash arrives in August-October. Costs run all year, so the low case dips again each November-July.
- **The low case is visible early**: by month 6 it has about 27 direct customers against 67 in the base (see kill criteria).

## Regional expansion

**Domestic expansion first; regional expansion is weak.**

1. **Same country, more segments (months 12-24).** Add estate agents (item 3: 9,695 registrations, 31 October deadline), which the separate estate-agent idea covers. They share the duty, the portal and most of the product ([FIC AR 2025/26](https://www.fic.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FIC-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf), via the market file). Estate agencies already pay R999 a month for compliance software (Realty Comply, unverified), so a R2,490 RMCP plan is cheap for them. This is the biggest near-term upside: about 45% more registrations.
2. **If the FIC extends yearly filing to all accountable institutions.** The FIC says further directives may cover all other accountable institutions, including financial institutions ([Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fic-revises-annual-rmcp-deadlines-in-final-directive-12/)). The 13,493 other registrations (60,115 minus 46,622; my calculation from the market file) are mostly FSPs and other financial firms. A "financial services" module would follow such a directive.
3. **Namibia (month 24+, low cost).** English, the same legal roots, goAML, and FIC Namibia sector guidance for legal practitioners, estate agents and dealers. But it has no yearly RMCP upload and its yearly compliance return covers banks only ([FIC Namibia guidance](https://www.fic.na/how-we-do-it/guidance/); [Directive 03 of 2023](https://www.fic.na/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Directive-03-of-2023-FIA-Compliance-Returns.pdf), via the market file). About 780 active lawyers (2017; [Law Society of Namibia](https://lawsocietynamibia.org/?p=1176)). Paddle does not list the Namibian dollar as a payment currency ([Paddle currencies](https://developer.paddle.com/concepts/sell/supported-currencies)), so price in rand; the Namibian dollar is pegged 1:1 to the rand (unverified). Revenue potential is small (my estimate: under R200,000 a year).
4. **Botswana, Kenya, Mauritius: not worth it** before year 3. Different laws and supervisors, no yearly upload trigger ([market file, Regional expansion](02-market-and-competition.md)).

**What travels.** The interview engine, approval log, amendment clock and multi-client dashboard are country-neutral. Only the content and the deadline calendar are South African. If the founder also builds the Bosnia AML kit or a similar product elsewhere, the same code base can serve both (owner's portfolio view).

## Exit and partnerships

**Partnerships (start in year 1):**
- *Content partner:* a FICA attorney or boutique firm that reviews content, appears on webinars and offers paid reviews (billed directly).
- *Distribution partners:* the Tax Faculty / SAIT, SAIPA, Accounting Academy, FISA, CASA, NADA, LEAD (new practices), GoLegal.
- *Referral partners:* KYC and screening vendors that lack an RMCP tool, such as Instarc (Tallinn-based; raised €1.25m in September 2026 for an SA KYC/CDD and onboarding platform; no RMCP drafting mentioned) ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/09/07/instarc-bags-e1-25m-to-crack-south-africas-compliance-market/)), AML GO and micro-lender software (ACPAS).
- *Template sellers as resellers:* Moonstone, Probeta.

**Likely acquirers (years 3-5):**
- **KYC/AML platforms selling to the same institutions** that want the RMCP layer: eFICA (Charter Group), VerifyNow, nCino KYC, Instarc, SearchWorks. nCino bought DocFox, the SA-built onboarding/KYC company, for US$75 million in cash (closed March 2024) ([The Digital Banker](https://thedigitalbanker.com/ncino-set-to-acquire-docfox-for-75m/); [nCino 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1902733/000190273324000152/ncno-20240731.htm)). Global buyers do buy SA compliance software, though at a scale far above this product.
- **Regulatory-reporting firms.** Regnology bought Heywood Business Analysts, an SA banking-compliance software firm, in March 2025 (search summary of [FE International](https://www.feinternational.com/blog/regnology-heywood-regtech-ma-case-study)).
- **Legal publishers and practice-software vendors:** LexisNexis SA, Juta, LegalSuite, GhostPractice (no deals found; candidates only).
- **Compliance services firms:** Moonstone, compliance consultancies.

**Valuation.** Bootstrapped SaaS under US$1m ARR sells for about 2.5-4x annual revenue, or 4-6x seller's discretionary earnings when the owner still runs it ([Beancount guide, Jul 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/11/bootstrapped-saas-valuation-multiples-2026-acquire-com-indie-founders-guide)). A study of 615 live listings found a typical ask of about 2.6x revenue ([BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/saas-valuation-multiples-2026)). Both are marketplace-adjacent sources.

## Open questions

1. **eFICA's RMCP Builder at launch (Nov 2026):** features, multi-entity support, uptake among its 500+ clients. Its website returned HTTP 503 on 10 Oct 2026.
2. **Paddle approval:** will Paddle accept a compliance-document tool, and does it treat the new-practice starter as software? Ask before building checkout.
3. **Stripe ZAR presentment and fees** for the founder's actual Stripe country; I used the UK price list.
4. **Share of buyers who refuse card payment.** Measure in pilots; this decides whether a local company is needed.
5. **Founder's home country:** tax residence of the selling company, its DTA with South Africa (relevant only if fees were ever treated as royalties), and its own VAT/OSS treatment of Paddle payouts.
6. **External-company test for online sellers** (Companies Act s23(2)(b)): get a one-page opinion from an SA corporate lawyer (about R5,000; my estimate).
7. **LPC Code of Conduct on fee sharing and touting**, before any revenue share with partner attorneys.
8. **Partner fees:** what the Tax Faculty, SAIPA, CASA, NADA, GoLegal and De Rebus charge for co-hosted webinars and adverts; no rate cards were published.
9. **Next RCR call date** and whether the FIC will make the RCR yearly.
10. **Real CPCs** for FIC search terms in South Africa (Google Keyword Planner).
11. **Remote incorporation quotes** from two SA law firms, plus a nominee public-officer quote, in case a local company becomes necessary.
