# The Bahamas: country research

**Market size:** a small economy (about 400k people, estimate). The economy rests on tourism and offshore financial services, with fisheries (spiny lobster) as the main goods export.
**Search budget used:** 10 searches (small-market cap). WebFetch was not used.
**Accessibility:** Accessible. The country is not under sanctions, the USD/BSD peg is 1:1, there are no internet restrictions, and foreign SaaS can be sold directly. Software needs no local licensing. Selling to regulated financial institutions would mean vendor due diligence, which is not the case for DNFBPs.

**Bottom line:** I found **no strong standalone opportunity** for a solo founder. The best lead is AML/CFT compliance tooling for small DNFBPs (designated non-financial businesses and professions). The country's CFATF 5th-round onsite visit is scheduled for 19–30 Oct 2026. The pool of buyers is small, so this only works as one module of a **pan-Caribbean DNFBP compliance product** (Cayman, BVI, Bermuda, Barbados, TCI and others). Every other workflow I screened is either annual, already handled by a free government portal, or too small to sell into.

## Opportunities

### Opportunity: DNFBP AML/CFT "exam-ready file" for small Bahamian firms (Caribbean-wide module)

**Industry:**
Legal services, real estate brokerage/land development, precious-metals dealers and small accounting firms supervised by the Compliance Commission of The Bahamas.

**Buyer:**
The managing partner or MLRO/compliance officer at a small law firm (1–10 lawyers), a real estate brokerage or a land developer.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The CFATF 5th-round mutual evaluation onsite visit is scheduled for 19–30 Oct 2026, assessed under the updated FATF methodology with effectiveness tested against 11 Immediate Outcomes.
- Officials have said mid-size and smaller licensees "may not have adequate resources to invest in compliance". The Attorney General's Office plans to engage an industry consultant and run training.
- After the onsite visit, follow-up remediation and stepped-up supervisory examinations are likely (estimate).
- The National AML Strategy 2025–2028 was issued Dec 2024.

**Current workflow:**
1. The firm registers with the Compliance Commission and completes the Commission's Risk Assessment Questionnaire (structure, clients, products, transactions, delivery channels, governance) through the Commission's online portal.
2. The firm maintains a firm-wide risk assessment and AML policies, usually as Word templates from a consultant.
3. For each client or transaction, staff collect ID and source-of-funds documents into email and paper files, screen names by hand or with a low-cost screening tool, and record the risk rating in a spreadsheet.
4. When the Commission schedules an onsite examination, staff assemble the evidence by hand (training logs, CDD files, STR register).

**Pain:**
- Penalties for failing to register are up to $50,000 for individuals and $200,000 for financial institutions.
- The Commission has run risk-based onsite exams (41 law firms, 13 real estate/land developers and 1 accounting firm in the early cycle). It also detected dozens of unregistered firms (48 law firms, 19 DPMS and 7 real estate firms in 2018–2020).
- Public statements say smaller licensees are under-resourced.
- I found no direct user complaints (none searched or found), so the pain is inferred.

**Existing solutions:**
- Global screening and KYC SaaS: Sanction Scanner, sanctions.io, ComplyAdvantage (the latter is a generic category example and was not verified for Bahamas marketing).
- AML training: VinciWorks.
- Big 4 and local consultants: EY Bahamas publishes AML material.
- The AG's Office's own consultant and training, which is free to the industry.
- Word/Excel templates.
- The Compliance Commission's own portal for risk questionnaires.

**The gap:**
Screening tools stop at name-checks. Nothing I found maps the Bahamian requirements (FTRA, POCA, the Compliance Commission Codes of Practice for real estate brokers and land developers) into one per-client CDD workflow. Such a workflow would produce the evidence pack an examiner asks for, and it would be priced for a 3-person firm. Treat this as an unverified gap. The search budget did not allow deep vendor diligence on regional players.

**Possible product:**
A light case file for each client or matter: CDD checklist driven by Bahamian Codes of Practice, risk rating, document expiry tracking and an STR/internal-report register. It would have a one-click "examination pack" export. A jurisdiction rules layer would let the same product be sold in Cayman, BVI, TCI, Bermuda and Barbados.

**MVP:**
- Real estate brokers only: client and transaction intake form, the Code of Practice CDD checklist, a risk score and a PDF exam pack.
- Screening through a third-party API.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
$49–149/month per firm (estimate), plus a fixed-fee "exam readiness" setup of $500 (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**
- The Bahamas Real Estate Association member directory.
- The Bahamas Bar Association.
- The Compliance Commission's registration lists (whether these are public is unverified).
- BICA practitioner list, for accountants.

**Risks:**
- The trigger date is passing right now, and demand may collapse after the onsite visit.
- The total addressable market in the Bahamas is likely only a few hundred firms (estimate).
- Consultants and free AG training are substitutes.
- Firms may stay on templates until they are cited.
- This needs a multi-jurisdiction rules layer to be a business.

**Kill condition:**
- Fewer than about 300 reachable DNFBPs across the first 3 Caribbean jurisdictions.
- Or interviews show firms are satisfied with consultant templates plus a $20/month screening tool.

**Score:** 4/10. As a Bahamas-only product it is about 3/10. As a pan-Caribbean DNFBP module it could reach about 5/10.

### Opportunity: Business-licence turnover-statement workpaper for BICA practitioners

**Industry:**
Accounting practices.

**Buyer:**
Small BICA-licensed accounting firms that prepare client turnover statements and reviewed accounts.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- Business licence fees are turnover-based, and renewal is due by 31 January.
- Businesses with turnover of $250k or more need a turnover statement from a BICA-licensed practitioner.
- The tiered regime is reported as review for $250k–$5m turnover and audit above that, with an accounts deadline of 31 March.
- The 2024–2025 renewal season had widely reported problems.

**Current workflow:**
1. The accountant collects client sales records.
2. The accountant reconciles them to VAT returns.
3. The accountant prepares a turnover statement and review report in Word or Excel.
4. The client uploads the documents to the DIR portal.

**Pain:**
- BICA's president publicly cited several challenges affecting renewals.
- The tax chief targeted 80–85% compliance, which implies a large non-compliant tail.

**Existing solutions:**
- Accountants' existing practice and audit tools (CaseWare-type, unverified locally), plus Excel.
- The DIR online portal.

**The gap:**
A VAT-return to turnover-statement reconciliation template. This is narrow and seasonal.

**Possible product:**
Seasonal workpaper SaaS that reconciles VAT filings to declared turnover and generates the statement.

**MVP:**
An Excel-in, PDF-out reconciliation tool.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
$20–40 per client engagement per year (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**
The BICA member/practitioner directory.

**Risks:**
- Annual frequency only.
- Few buyers (likely low hundreds of practitioners, estimate).
- Reconciliation is a core skill accountants won't outsource.

**Kill condition:**
Practitioners say their existing audit software templates already cover it.

**Score:** 2/10

## Rejected after competitor research

- **NIB C10 contribution filing:** killed by NIB's free Employer Self-Service portal, which handles C10 uploads and online payment, plus existing payroll bureaus. Source: https://www.nib-bahamas.com/NIB-News/NIB-Urges-Employers-to-File-C10-Contribution-Statements-For-Quick-Benefit-Payments-/_p6
- **VAT e-invoicing readiness:** there is no e-invoicing mandate and no timeline. Filing goes through the free DIR portal, and accounting software and accountants cover returns. Sources: https://www.e-invoice.app/country/BS, https://quaderno.io/guides/bahamas/vat/
- **Short-term rental tax compliance:** substitutes are booking platforms (remittance role unverified), property managers and accountants. The sources I found (bnbcalc.com) are low quality and contradict each other on rates, so nothing here is reliable.
- **Pillar Two / DMTT:** limited to large MNE groups and served by the Big 4 (KPMG TaxNewsFlash, Aug 2026).

## Attractive problem, poor distribution

- **Lobster export EU CATCH / traceability packs:** a mandatory, new digital catch certificate applies from Jan 2026, alongside MSC and US buyer traceability. The industry earns about $75m a year and involves about 7,000 fishermen, but the paying buyers are only a handful of exporters/processors in the Bahamas Marine Exporters Association, and the EU CATCH system itself is free. This is viable only as part of a regional Caribbean seafood-exporter product. Sources:
  - https://www.mlcalliance.org/post/new-eu-catch-certificate-requirements-for-lobster-exports
  - https://www.bahamastradeinfo.gov.bs/buy/key-products/seafood/
  - https://fisheryprogress.org/node/7758
  - https://fisheries.msc.org/en/fisheries/bahamian-spiny-lobster-fishery

## Too competitive

- Generic KYC/sanctions screening for Bahamian financial institutions. Global vendors (Sanction Scanner, sanctions.io and others) already serve this market. Central Bank SFIs buy through enterprise procurement; the Central Bank itself tendered an AML risk-assessment solution in 2024.

## Source-quality notes

- Several search hits were unreliable: blog.jingtea.com, expanship.com and bnbcalc.com look auto-generated, and ird.gov.ai is **Anguilla**, not the Bahamas. I did not rely on them for any claim.
- Customs (Click1) results returned only Barbados and Jamaica ASYCUDA material, so I made no Bahamas claim on customs.
