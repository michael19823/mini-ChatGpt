# Belgium

Research depth: deep pass, 59 web searches (WebFetch blocked, so evidence comes from search-result snippets). Searches were run in Dutch, French and English. Belgium is a high-wage, well-served B2B software market. Its regional split (Flanders, Wallonia, Brussels) creates real compliance fragmentation, but most large, obvious workflows already have Belgian vendors, social secretariats or free government tools. Anything not seen in a source is marked "unverified" or "estimate".

## Opportunities

### Opportunity: Flemish chain-liability due-diligence file for cleaning, meat and parcel subcontracting chains

**Industry:**
Contract cleaning, meat processing (slaughter and boning subcontracting), parcel delivery and, secondarily, construction. Flanders region.

**Buyer:**
Operations or HR/admin managers at mid-size cleaning companies, meat processors and parcel subcontractors that use direct subcontractors. Also professional clients (facility managers) that hire cleaning firms. Social-secretariat and accounting-firm advisers are a channel.

**Trigger / Why now:**
The new Flemish due-diligence duty (zorgvuldigheidsplicht) on chain liability for illegal employment of third-country nationals took effect on 1 Jan 2026 in four risk sectors: construction, cleaning, meat, and parcel couriers working for postal services. The grace period ended 30 Jun 2026, and sanctions apply from 1 Jul 2026. Penalties are criminal (6 months to 3 years and/or EUR 4,800-48,000) or administrative (EUR 2,400-24,000). The Flemish government's planned digital check application was shelved ("voorlopig afgevoerd"), so documents must be collected directly from the subcontractor. The reporting portal (meldloket) for missing or forged documents is now live.

**Current workflow:**
1. Before work starts, the client or contractor asks each direct subcontractor for its ID and contact data and a signed written declaration that it does not and will not employ illegally residing foreigners.
2. For each foreign worker or self-employed person, it collects a passport copy, proof of residence, and either a work authorisation and DIMONA proof, or (if posted from another EEA country) the Limosa-1, the A1/CoC and the EEA residence permit.
3. It checks validity and expiry dates, chases missing items and re-checks when crews change.
4. If documents are missing or clearly forged, it reports to the Flemish Social Inspection via the meldloket in time. Otherwise it loses the liability exemption.
5. It keeps the file for up to 5 years after the cooperation ends for inspection.

**Pain:**
The obligation is new and mandatory, and it repeats per subcontractor, per worker and per document expiry. Penalties are heavy and liability is joint. Every social secretariat and law firm published guidance (CLB, Attentia, Liantis, Grant Thornton, Altius, Eubelius, Vanhavermaet, VLAIO). Guidance says "you can be supported by a social secretariat or a lawyer", which means the work is manual today. Cleaning chains are layered: attendance registration thresholds drop to zero with 2 or more subcontractor levels, so multi-tier chains are common.

**Existing solutions:**
- Conneqtr Zen: Belgian construction-site admin SaaS backed by BESIX. It stores legal documents for check-in and qualifications. Construction-focused.
- NineID: published a guide on the 2026 rules. Its product scope is unverified.
- AutoCheckIn, Werfcheck, Traxgo (ConstruBadge): construction presence registration, not document due diligence.
- Suivo: content on A1/Limosa for foreign contractors working in Belgium. Its product scope is unverified.
- Federal RSZ apps "Correcte Ondernemer" (30bis withholding check) and "Correcte Werknemer" (Limosa check). Free, but they don't cover residence or work-permit files or the Flemish declaration.
- Social secretariats and lawyers (templates, contract clauses). Generic vendor-compliance tools (e.g. a "Contractor Compliance" listing on Capterra BE).

**The gap:**
No tool was found that is built for non-construction chains (cleaning, meat, parcels). Such a tool would need to:
- send a per-subcontractor document request in NL/FR/EN/PL/RO/BG/PT;
- collect the signed Flemish declaration;
- run document checklists per worker scenario (third-country national employed in Belgium vs posted from the EEA);
- track expiry dates;
- prepare a meldloket report when documents are missing or forged;
- keep an inspection-ready 5-year archive.

Construction tools bundle this with site presence, which cleaning and meat firms don't need in the same form. The gap is unverified until NineID's and Suivo's products are checked.

**Possible product:**
"Due-diligence file per subcontractor": a portal where the subcontractor uploads the required documents. The contractor gets a traffic-light status per subcontractor and worker, expiry alerts, a pre-filled meldloket report and an inspection export (PDF/ZIP).

**MVP:**
- A web form per subcontractor with a magic link, with scenario-based document checklists (from the VLAIO and legal-guide lists).
- An e-signed declaration template.
- An expiry dashboard and email reminders.
- An inspection export.

No government API is needed.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
EUR 49-199 per month by number of subcontractors, or EUR 5-10 per subcontractor per month. Also a white-label licence for social secretariats and accounting firms. All estimates.

**How to find first customers:**
- Cleaning: members of the Algemene Federatie voor Schoonmaakbedrijven (ABSU/UGBN, membership list unverified), or KBO/Crossroads Bank NACE 81.2 filtered for Flanders.
- Parcel: the public list of more than 1,200 BELparcel registrations (BIPT).
- Meat: AFSCA/FAVV-approved establishments list (slaughterhouses and cutting plants). Also Febev members (unverified).
- Channel: accounting firms and smaller social secretariats (Besox, Sodalis, Accuria, CLB) that published guidance but appear to sell advice rather than software (unverified).

**Risks:**
- Social secretariats or Conneqtr could add a module.
- The Flemish government could revive its check app.
- Enforcement intensity is unknown.
- Personal data (passports, residence permits) under GDPR requires careful hosting.
- Flanders only. Federal chain liability is separate, so this is not national.

**Kill condition:**
- Conneqtr, NineID, Suivo or a large social secretariat already offers a cheap, non-construction-specific due-diligence portal.
- Or 10 interviews with cleaning, meat or parcel firms show they use few third-country-national subcontractor workers and handle the check with a one-page clause.

**Score:** 5/10

### Opportunity: Parcel Act compliance kit for small parcel-delivery subcontractors

**Industry:**
Last-mile parcel delivery (subcontractors of bpost, PostNL, DPD, GLS and others).

**Buyer:**
Owner-managers of small and mid-size parcel subcontractors (typically a handful to a few dozen drivers, an estimate), and the intermediate contractors above them.

**Trigger / Why now:**
The Parcel Act (Pakjeswet) phased in obligations from 2024 to 2026:
- BIPT/BELparcel registration (since 1 May 2024);
- a half-yearly report on MyBelparcel (by 31 Jan and 31 Jul) listing direct subcontractors, parcels delivered and fees paid;
- an appointed coordinator and a vigilance plan mapping the subcontractor chain;
- minimum compensation per hour;
- an RSZ notification of subcontractors (from 1 Apr 2025);
- maximum daily and weekly distribution times (from 1 Jul 2026).

Parcel couriers are also one of the four Flemish chain-liability risk sectors from 2026.

**Current workflow:**
1. The coordinator keeps a spreadsheet of sub-subcontractors, drivers, parcels and fees.
2. Twice a year, they re-key it into MyBelparcel.
3. They write and update a vigilance plan (template-based) and a list of the inspection checklist items.
4. They check that rates paid to sub-subcontractors respect the minimum compensation.
5. They collect chain-liability documents for any third-country-national drivers.

**Pain:**
Several overlapping obligations land on very small, low-margin firms. Inspections and court cases against subcontractors are documented (PostNL subcontractors were fined in Antwerp). Securex says the regulator invited employers to use a model document, which shows that firms need templates.

**Existing solutions:**
- The BELparcel portal (free, manual entry).
- Social secretariats (Securex, Partena, Sodalis, Besox) with guidance and templates.
- The postal companies' own time-registration systems, which they must make available to subcontractors.
- Fleet and route apps (unverified for compliance features).

**The gap:**
Nobody seen offers a compliance pack for subcontractors that combines:
- the chain register;
- a minimum-compensation check;
- a pre-filled half-yearly BELparcel export;
- vigilance-plan versioning;
- the chain-liability document file.

This is unverified beyond the absence of search hits.

**Possible product:**
A light web app. You maintain your chain and payments once, and it produces the half-yearly report data, the vigilance plan, the minimum-pay check and the inspection binder.

**MVP:**
A register of subcontractors and drivers, a CSV of parcels and fees per semester, a report-ready summary in BELparcel field order, a vigilance-plan generator from the official template, and the chain-liability document upload from opportunity 1.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
EUR 29-79 per month per company (estimate). Margin pressure in the sector limits price.

**How to find first customers:**
- The public BELparcel list of registered companies (more than 1,200 registrations).
- Sector associations (unverified).
- The postal companies' subcontractor onboarding, as a partnership channel.

**Risks:**
- The 2025-2029 federal coalition agreement says the parcel legislation will be "evaluated and simplified", with redundant rules removed.
- Time registration was already suspended in 2025.
- Buyers are price-sensitive and churn-prone.
- The large postal companies may provide tools to their subcontractors.

**Kill condition:**
The simplification law removes half-yearly reporting or the vigilance plan. Or the postal companies already give subcontractors a portal that produces the BELparcel report.

**Score:** 4/10

### Opportunity: Syndic building-compliance calendar and art. 3.94 sale-information pack

**Industry:**
Co-ownership property management (syndics of VMEs/ACPs).

**Buyer:**
Small professional syndic offices (BIV/IPI-registered), plus volunteer syndics of small buildings.

**Trigger / Why now:**
In Flanders, every VME must hold an asbestos attest for the common parts by 31 Dec 2026. The syndic is responsible and liable for informing the VME in time. Every existing apartment building also needs an EPC for the common parts. Recurring inspections include lifts (every 6 months), electrical installations in common parts (every 5 years) and lift risk analysis (every 15 years), all as cited in notarial sale documents. On every apartment sale, art. 3.94 of the Civil Code requires the syndic to send information within 15 days to the agent/seller and within 30 days to the notary. This covers funds, arrears, decided works, litigation and attests, and the syndic bills about EUR 200-220 per request.

**Current workflow:**
1. The syndic tracks attests and inspections per building in the syndic accounting software, a spreadsheet or memory.
2. When a notary's request arrives, staff assemble balances, decided works and attests into a letter or PDF.
3. They re-send the same attests to agents, notaries and buyers by email.

**Pain:**
The asbestos common-parts deadline is a hard 2026 trigger. The 3.94 pack is per transaction and has a deadline. Consumer complaints about syndic costs and responsiveness exist (Test Aankoop). The evidence of internal pain is indirect.

**Existing solutions:**
- Synsafe (easi): Belgian syndic platform.
- Syndic24: small and volunteer syndics.
- Easy Syndic (manages about 2,400 co-ownerships, so it is a syndic firm, not software).
- Other Belgian syndic accounting packages (names and features unverified).
- syndicdata.be: a public data platform on syndics.

**The gap:**
Possibly an add-on that sits next to whatever accounting software the syndic uses: a per-building attest register with expiry alerts and a one-click 3.94 pack generator. This is unverified, because major syndic packages may already generate the 3.94 letter.

**Possible product:**
An attest vault and calendar per building, plus a 3.94 response generator that combines accounting exports and stored attests into the standard notarial format.

**MVP:**
Upload attests with expiry dates per building, deadline alerts, and a 3.94 PDF built from a CSV of owner balances.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
EUR 2-4 per building per month, or EUR 10 per generated 3.94 pack (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**
The BIV/IPI register of real-estate agents-syndics, syndicdata.be listings, and syndic federations (CIB, unverified membership list).

**Risks:**
Syndic software incumbents can add this trivially. Syndics may already have templates. The asbestos deadline creates a one-time backlog rather than recurring revenue.

**Kill condition:**
The top 3 Belgian syndic packages already include an attest register and 3.94 generation.

**Score:** 4/10

### Opportunity: EPC kNR/NR field-to-VEKA workflow for energy experts type D (Flanders)

**Industry:**
Energy-performance certification of non-residential buildings, Flanders.

**Buyer:**
Independent energy experts type D (solo practitioners and small firms).

**Trigger / Why now:**
From 1 Jan 2026, every non-residential unit (including small units below 1,000 m²) must have an EPC NR or kNR. Fines run EUR 500-5,000. Type D exams have been organised since Jan 2025. Owners must also register meter readings annually for the EPC NR label, which a type D expert or an internal building manager can do.

**Current workflow:**
1. The expert quotes the job.
2. They make a mandatory site visit, covering all rooms and taking photo evidence.
3. They re-enter the data into the VEKA EPC-NR application and energy-performance database.
4. They issue the certificate and invoice the client.

**Pain:**
VEKA quality controls and published "common mistakes" lists show error-prone data collection. No direct complaints were found.

**Existing solutions:**
The mandatory VEKA EPC-NR software and database (free). Generic field-inspection apps. UK-style EPC field apps (e.g. RdSAP GO) show the product pattern, but no Flemish NR equivalent was found (unverified).

**The gap:**
An evidence and checklist capture app in VEKA input order, plus a quote-to-invoice workflow. VEKA offers no API, so the output is a structured sheet for manual entry.

**Possible product:**
A mobile capture app with a per-component photo checklist and an export in VEKA input order.

**MVP:**
A PWA form with a photo checklist, a PDF evidence dossier, and quote and invoice templates.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
EUR 30-60 per month per expert (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**
The VEKA list of recognised energy experts, and type D training cohorts.

**Risks:**
A small niche, no integration, a one-off 2026 backlog, and a low willingness to pay.

**Kill condition:**
Fewer than about 300 active type D experts, or an existing Flemish EPC field app already covers NR.

**Score:** 3/10

## Rejected after competitor research

- **Asbestos-inventory field app (Flanders).** Killed by OVAM's own offline-capable mobile-first PWA, which feeds the OVAM asbestos database directly (https://acagroup.be/nl/customer-success-stories/mobile-first-app-asbestattest-ovam/).
- **Funeral-home death-registration interoperability (Flanders).** Killed by eLys (Athumi), mandatory in all Flemish municipalities from 1 Jan 2026. In Jan 2026, 281 of 285 municipalities and 358 funeral directors were already on it, and it supports integration with funeral software. Funeral Manager (Nivelles) is the Belgian SaaS incumbent. Closure (NL) is expanding into Belgium for post-death contract cancellations. One residual complaint: municipalities still ask funeral directors for data twice (via eLys and by email). That is too small to build on. Sources: https://www.vlaanderen.be/lokaal-bestuur/nieuwsberichten/aangifte-van-overlijden-volledig-digitaal-vanaf-1-januari-2026 , https://docs.athumi.eu/elys/help/ik-ben-uitvaartondernemer , https://www.vvsg.be/node/7032
- **Veterinary antibiotic registration (Sanitel-Med/VAMREG, extended 1 Jan 2026).** Killed by practice-software integrations (Vetocare VAMREG integration) and by AB Register and Bigame as third-party submitters.
- **Horeca GKS 2.0 cash register (from 1 Jul 2026).** Requires certified POS. Killed by POS vendors.
- **EUDR for Belgian chocolate SMEs.** Killed by osapiens, TraceX and similar. Also, micro and small operators are only in scope from 30 Jun 2027, and downstream SMEs mostly don't file statements.
- **Customs IDMS migration.** Killed by certified customs-declaration software vendors and the forwarders' in-house teams.
- **Peppol e-invoicing helper.** Hundreds of approved packages and access points.
- **DIMONA/DmfA tooling.** Social secretariats.
- **Manure transport documents (VLM).** The approved AGR-GPS service providers and the Mestbank portal cover it.

## Attractive problem, poor distribution

- **Post-death administration for bereaved families.** DELA's 2026 barometer found 58% got lost in the administrative maze. But the buyer is a one-time consumer, and Closure plus the funeral homes already occupy the space.
- **EPC NR for shop and restaurant owners.** One-time purchase. The energy expert is the better buyer.
- **Wallonia/Brussels funeral digitalisation.** Still paper, but the market is small and a regional platform will probably come from government (unverified).

## Too competitive

- Check In and Out at Work in construction (from 1 Apr 2027): AutoCheckIn, Werfcheck, Conneqtr Zen, Traxgo/ConstruBadge, social secretariats.
- Cleaning-sector attendance registration (PC 121): the free RSZ app, CleanManager, Appreo, Flexsys.
- Peppol e-invoicing.
- EUDR traceability.

## Overall

The best Belgian lead is the **Flemish chain-liability due-diligence file outside construction** (5/10). It has a hard 2026 trigger, the government abandoned its check app, the obligation is per subcontractor and per document, and there is a clear channel through social secretariats and accounting firms. It is still Flanders-only, and a Belgian incumbent (Conneqtr) or a social secretariat could close it. Validate with about 10 interviews with cleaning and meat-processing firms before building. Belgium remains a crowded market with no opportunity above 5/10.
