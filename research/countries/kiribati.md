# Kiribati: Country Research

**Market class:** Microstate (about 120k–130k people). The public sector makes up more than half of GDP and the private sector provides about 30% of employment. Search budget was 4, and all 4 were used.
**Accessibility:** No US, EU or UK sanctions apply. A foreign solo founder could legally sell software here. In practice, payment rails and connectivity are limited (unverified detail, not researched further). This is not an inaccessible market. The economy is just too small.

**Bottom line:** I found no viable standalone indie-software opportunity in Kiribati. Two things drive this. The number of firms that would have to buy a compliance tool is tiny: the Kiribati Chamber of Commerce & Industry lists only 200+ members. And the compliance workflows that do exist are either run by the government on donor-funded systems or are already covered by an incumbent. At most, Kiribati could be a line item in a Pacific-wide product built for Fiji, PNG or Samoa.

## Industries screened

| Industry | Workflow looked at | Verdict | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fisheries (tuna, domestic purse seine/longline JVs) | Catch and logbook electronic reporting, VMS, licence conditions, EU FPA reporting | Reject | The government-side integrated VMS/ERS control centre was contracted to Satlink (2019). Vessels report under regional PNA/FFA/WCPFC systems. Only a handful of domestic JV operators would buy. |
| Customs brokers / importers | Import declarations in ASYCUDAWorld | Reject | ASYCUDAWorld went live in Dec 2021 (UNCTAD). There were only about 3,800 import declarations a year (2019), which is too few for broker SaaS. |
| SMEs / accountants: VAT | Monthly VAT return (RET 020, 12.5% VAT) filed with Internal Revenue Board | Poor distribution | The return is still a blank PDF form on mfed.gov.ki, which points to a manual workflow. But a few hundred VAT registrants cannot support a standalone product. |
| Non-resident digital service providers | Kiribati VAT registration via a local VAT representative | Reject | This is a niche within global indirect-tax tools (Avalara, Fonoa-type vendors, commenda.io). Kiribati is a tiny add-on country for them. |
| Retail / wholesale (import-dependent stores) | Stock, pricing, VAT invoicing | Reject | This is a generic POS/accounting need. Generic tools (Xero/QuickBooks or local POS) fill it, and there is no regulatory trigger. |

## Opportunities

None meet the brief's bar: identifiable buyers at scale, weak incumbents, a why-now trigger and a clear distribution channel. I am not presenting any opportunity in the template, so no idea is padded out to fill the section.

**Add-on note (not scored as standalone):** A "Pacific small-state VAT/withholding return preparer" for accountants serving several Pacific Island countries might include Kiribati alongside Tuvalu, Nauru and the Solomon Islands. This only makes sense if a parent product is first validated in Fiji or PNG. Kiribati's own evidence is the PDF return form ([mfed.gov.ki blank VAT return](https://www.mfed.gov.ki/sites/default/files/BLANK%20VAT%20Returns.pdf)). Indicative standalone score: 2/10.

## Rejected after competitor research

- **Fisheries e-logbook / catch-reporting compliance for domestic fleets.** Killed by Satlink, which is contracted by the Ministry of Fisheries and Marine Resources for an integrated VMS + ERS control centre. Regional PNA/FFA/WCPFC reporting systems and donor-funded (World Bank) fisheries programmes also cover this. The buyer pool is only a few JV operators.
- **Customs-declaration prep for importers/brokers.** Killed by ASYCUDAWorld (UNCTAD, live Dec 2021) plus very low volume (about 3,796 declarations in 2019).
- **Kiribati VAT for non-resident digital sellers.** Killed by global indirect-tax compliance vendors that already list Kiribati.

## Attractive problem, poor distribution

- VAT return preparation and reconciliation for local SMEs and accountants. The work is manual and PDF-based, but there are only a few hundred businesses. The only practical channel is the Kiribati Chamber of Commerce & Industry (200+ members).

## Too competitive

- None in the usual sense. The market is too small rather than too contested.
