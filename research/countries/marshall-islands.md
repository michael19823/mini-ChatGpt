# Marshall Islands (RMI): Opportunity Research

**Researched:** 2026-10-05 · **Search budget used:** 4 of 4 (microstate) · **Classification:** microstate / tiny economy

## Summary verdict

There is **no viable standalone indie-software opportunity** in the Marshall Islands. The domestic
economy is very small: the 2021 census counted about 42,000 people (from memory, not re-checked
in this run), with a few hundred formal private businesses concentrated in Majuro and Ebeye. Most
revenue comes from Compact of Free Association grants, fishing-licence fees and the offshore
ship/corporate registry. The two workflows with real volume are global ones that happen to carry
the RMI flag. **Port-State fisheries paperwork** is handled by MIMRA and regional (PNA/FFA)
systems. The **RMI ship registry** is administered by International Registries, Inc. (IRI) in
Reston, VA, and its users are ship managers worldwide, so it is not a Marshallese market.

The only real "why now" is tax reform. The March 2026 wage-tax amendment (Bill 103) took effect
with April 2026 payrolls. An IMF-recommended move from a gross-revenue tax to VAT plus a profit
tax is under discussion, but it has been reported as delayed, with no enacted date. If a VAT is
ever enacted, it could create a short-lived compliance need, but the buyer pool (a few hundred
firms) is too small to support a product on its own. It should be treated as an **add-on** to a
Pacific-wide product (for example one that covers FSM, Palau, Kiribati and Nauru together), not
as a market.

No sanctions or access barriers apply. The USD economy, the US postal system and US-linked
banking make it easy for a foreign founder to sell into. The problem is size, not access.

## Opportunities

No opportunity reaches a buildable score. One candidate is recorded below for cross-country
comparison only, as a possible add-on to a regional product.

### Opportunity: Pacific gross-revenue-tax / future-VAT filing add-on (RMI module)

**Industry:**
Small-business tax compliance / accounting

**Buyer:**
Small accounting practices and bookkeepers in Majuro and Ebeye, plus owner-managers of retail,
construction and service firms that file quarterly gross-revenue tax returns.

**Trigger / Why now:**
In March 2026 Bill 103 changed wage-tax withholding (effective April 2026 payrolls). In 2025 the
IMF recommended replacing the gross revenue tax and import-based consumption taxes with a VAT
plus a net-profit tax (with a presumptive regime for small firms). Implementation has been
reported as delayed, and no enacted date was found.

**Current workflow:**
1. The business keeps its sales in a spreadsheet or basic accounting package (QuickBooks/Xero, or none).
2. A bookkeeper computes quarterly gross revenue and applies the tax ($80 on the first $10,000/yr, then 3% on the excess).
3. The return is filed on a paper/PDF form with the Division of Revenue and Taxation, and payment is made separately (filing method unverified).
4. The bookkeeper recalculates wage withholding per employee under the new exemption.

**Pain:**
Moderate at best. The computation is simple. The real pain would arrive only if a VAT is enacted
(registration, invoicing rules, returns), and none has been.

**Existing solutions:**
Local accountants doing the work by hand; Xero/QuickBooks with manual tax codes; consultancies
such as Healy Consultants for offshore and foreign firms; and donor-funded revenue-modernisation
programmes (IMF/PFTAC, an Australian-funded tax adviser per a DFAT ToR) that will likely shape any
future e-filing system.

**The gap:**
There is no RMI-specific tax template in generic accounting tools (unverified). That gap is
trivial to fill with a spreadsheet.

**Possible product:**
A Pacific micro-economy tax-return pack: country rule sets (RMI, FSM, Palau, Kiribati) that map
Xero/QuickBooks exports to each jurisdiction's return format, with a VAT module switched on only
once a law is enacted.

**MVP:**
A Xero export mapped to the RMI quarterly gross-revenue-tax return plus a wage-tax schedule, as a PDF.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
$15–30/month per business, or $100–200/month per accounting practice. The total RMI addressable
market is probably under $20k ARR (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**
The RMI Chamber of Commerce, Majuro accounting firms, and Ministry of Finance / Revenue taxpayer
outreach. All of these are small and relationship-driven.

**Risks:**
VAT may never be enacted. The government may launch donor-funded free e-filing. The market is
tiny, and in-person relationships matter.

**Kill condition:**
No VAT law passed by 2027, or a donor-funded revenue system with free e-filing announced.

**Score:** 2/10

## Rejected after competitor research

- **Fisheries agent port-entry / transshipment paperwork (Majuro).** Killed by the
  government-provided **MIMRA Web App**, which agents must already use for port-entry requests.
  Inspections run under MIMRA's 2026 inspection plan, and regional PNA/FFA/WCPFC monitoring
  systems also apply. Only a handful of agents operate, and transshipment volume fell in 2024.
  Sources: https://www.rmimimra.com/index.php/about-us/port-entry ,
  https://www.rmimimra.com/images/Resources/RMI%20Inspection%20Plan%202026.pdf ,
  https://www.samoanews.com/node/131285
- **RMI-flag maritime compliance tooling.** Killed by **IRI** (the registry administrator, with
  its own client tools and FleetPSC programme) and by the crowded global ship-management /
  PSC-readiness SaaS market. The buyers are global ship managers, not Marshallese firms.
  Sources: https://www.register-iri.com/marshall-islands-report-january-2026 ,
  https://www.register-iri.com/wp-content/uploads/FleetPSC-Flyer-Reston-A4-March-2026.pdf
- **Wage-tax (Bill 103) payroll updates.** Killed by local accountants and generic payroll
  software. It is a one-time parameter change for a few hundred employers.
  Source: https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/20/marshall-islands-wage-tax-brackets-2026-small-employer-guide

## Attractive problem, poor distribution

- **A future RMI VAT/profit-tax transition.** It would be a real mandatory trigger if enacted, but
  the buyer pool is tiny and relationship-driven. It is viable only as a module in a
  Pacific-islands product sold through regional accounting networks or PFTAC-linked revenue
  offices.

## Too competitive

- RMI-flag ship compliance (IRI's own tools, plus global maritime SaaS).

## Add-on note

RMI is best served as a jurisdiction module inside a product built for a larger Pacific or US-affiliated
island market (FSM, Palau, Guam/CNMI). It should not be targeted on its own.
