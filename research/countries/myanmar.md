# Myanmar: Country Research

**Research date:** 2026-10-04 · **Searches used:** 5 (handled as an inaccessible / conflict-affected market)
**Verdict: NOT ACCESSIBLE to a foreign solo founder in practice. No opportunities recommended. Research stopped after the accessibility check, as agent-instructions section 4 requires.**

---

## 1. Accessibility check (decisive)

| Factor | Finding | Effect on a foreign solo founder |
|---|---|---|
| Sanctions (US/EU/UK) | Sanctions are targeted, not comprehensive. The EU renewed restrictive measures on 27 Apr 2026 until 30 Apr 2027: 106 individuals and 22 entities, an arms embargo, a dual-use ban, and a ban on technology/software for intercepting or monitoring communications. OFAC SDN designations continue, for example the DKBA and scam-centre-linked companies (Nov 2025). The UK has its own Myanmar regime (guidance updated Mar 2026). | Selling ordinary B2B SaaS to private SMEs is not banned outright. But the founder must screen every customer against SDN/EU/UK lists. Many larger Myanmar firms are military-linked conglomerates, which makes this a constant legal risk. |
| AML / payments | Myanmar is still on the FATF "call for action" blacklist after the June 2026 plenary, one of only three countries with Iran and North Korea. Correspondent banks have de-risked, and cross-border transfers are slow, heavily documented and often rejected. | Stripe, Paddle and major card processors do not serve Myanmar. Collecting USD subscriptions is impractical. Payments in MMK through KBZPay or WavePay need a local entity and do not reach a foreign founder's account. |
| Internet controls | The Cybersecurity Law has been in force since 1 Jan 2025 and fully in effect since 30 Jul 2025. Unauthorised VPN installation carries up to 6 months in prison. The law allows shutdowns on state-security grounds and requires digital platforms to keep users' personal details for the authorities. | A foreign SaaS that holds Myanmar user data falls under the platform data-retention and disclosure rules. Regional shutdowns make service unreliable in conflict areas. |
| Political / economic | Military-run state, ongoing armed conflict, the 2025–26 junta-run elections, currency controls and forced conversion of export earnings. | No predictable regulatory "why now". Rules change by decree. Customers' ability to pay is low. |

**Conclusion:** A foreign solo founder cannot reliably get paid, cannot easily screen out sanctioned counterparties, and would hold user data under a surveillance-oriented law. Myanmar is treated as **inaccessible** for this study.

## 2. Industries screened (light pass, for the record)

| Industry | Workflow looked at | Verdict | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employers / payroll bureaus | Monthly SSB return to the township office, PIT withholding, Burmese payslips | Rejected | Local vendors (QHRM, which claims 350+ employers) and Mercans already cover it. Prices are about MMK 200k–500k per year for a 20-person firm, so willingness to pay is very low. |
| Accountants / all taxpayers | Commercial tax and CIT returns on the IRD e-filing portal (efiling.ird.gov.mm) | Rejected | The portal is still mostly pilot/voluntary, apart from LTO/MTO CIT filing. There is no public API, local accounting firms do the work manually and cheaply, and payment rails block a foreign vendor. |
| Agricultural exporters (rice, beans, coffee) | Export documentation and EU traceability (EUDR) | Rejected | The EU market is shrinking because of sanctions and reputational risk, and buyers avoid Myanmar origin. A foreign tool would also need sanctions screening of counterparties. |
| Border traders / importers | Border trade bank-payment proof (rule since Oct 2022) and import licences | Rejected | The rule is driven by decree and changes often. The buyers are informal or cash-based, and some trade runs through armed-group-controlled areas. |
| Garment exporters (CMP) | Buyer due-diligence and labour-compliance evidence | Rejected | The sector is contracting as EU/UK brands exit. Brand-side compliance platforms such as audit/ESG SaaS already serve the remaining buyers. |

## 3. Opportunities

**None recommended.** Given the accessibility check above, no idea meets the brief's Final Decision Rule: there is no way to collect payment, no reliable distribution channel, and no stable regulatory "why now".

---

## 4. Rejected after competitor research

- **SSB/PIT payroll compliance for SMEs:** killed by **QHRM** (local, Myanmar-specific, already handles the SSB cap, township register export and Burmese payslips) and **Mercans** (managed payroll with direct SSB relationships). Low price points confirm weak willingness to pay.

## 5. Attractive problem, poor distribution

- **IRD e-filing re-entry from accounting software to the portal:** a real duplicate-entry pattern for LTO/MTO filers. But there is no API, the buyers are reachable only through local accounting firms, and there are no payment rails for a foreign vendor.

## 6. Too competitive

- HR/payroll for Yangon SMEs: QHRM, Mercans, plus regional HRIS players.

## 7. Possible add-on to a neighbouring market

- None advisable. Thai cross-border tools (for example for Mae Sot border trade) could in theory touch Myanmar counterparties, but FATF blacklist due diligence and sanctions screening make this a liability rather than a feature.
