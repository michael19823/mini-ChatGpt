# North Korea (DPRK): Opportunity Research

**Status: inaccessible market. Research stopped at the accessibility check, as the method requires.**
Researched 2026-10-05. Search budget used: 3 of 4 (inaccessible-market cap).

## Accessibility check (decisive)

A foreign solo founder cannot legally or practically sell software to buyers in North Korea. Each of the four points below is enough by itself to rule the market out.

1. **US sanctions (comprehensive embargo).** Executive Order 13722, implemented in the North Korea Sanctions Regulations (31 CFR Part 510, section 510.206), bans the export or re-export of *any* goods, services or technology to North Korea from the US or by a US person. This expressly covers services performed outside the US by a US person when the benefit is received in North Korea. Software exports also need a BIS licence under the EAR. A US-incorporated SaaS company, or one using US payment processors or cloud providers, is caught by this.
   - https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/topic/1556
   - https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/459
   - https://sanctions.org/turbofac/research/OFAC-north-korea-NKSR-31-CFR-510.405
2. **UN, EU and UK regimes.** The UN Security Council has stacked sanctions resolutions on the DPRK, most recently UNSCR 2397 (Dec 2017), which added sectoral trade bans and the repatriation of overseas DPRK workers. The EU (Council Regulation (EU) 2017/1509) and the UK (DPRK (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019) apply broad trade, financial and investment bans on top of the UN measures. I did not verify the specific article covering software services in this session, so that detail is marked *unverified*. The overall direction is not in doubt.
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_2397
   - https://www.cliffordchance.com/briefings/2018/01/un_security_councilratchetsupnorthkore.html
3. **No payment rails.** SWIFT cut off the last North Korean banks in March 2017. There is no lawful way to collect subscription revenue. Stripe, PayPal and card networks do not operate there.
   - https://www.worldfinance.com/?p=41096
   - https://www.thedailystar.net/business/global-business/swift-messaging-system-bans-north-korean-banks-blacklisted-un-1372957
4. **No internet access for buyers.** Ordinary citizens and businesses can reach only the state-run Kwangmyong intranet. Global internet access is limited to a small group of approved elite users, routed through China. Foreign SaaS cannot reach would-be customers.
   - https://thenetmonitor.org/country-profiles/prk
   - https://foreignpolicy.com/?p=169194

There is also a practical risk for the founder. US and allied governments have repeatedly warned about DPRK IT workers posing as freelancers or job applicants. Any business dealings involving DPRK nationals (contractors, "partners", resellers) carry serious criminal and civil sanctions exposure. This comes from general knowledge and was not re-sourced in this session.

## Inaccessible market

**North Korea (DPRK): fully inaccessible.** Reasons: OFAC's comprehensive embargo (31 CFR 510 / EO 13722), UN Security Council sanctions (UNSCR 2397 and earlier), EU and UK sanctions regimes, SWIFT disconnection since 2017, and no public internet (Kwangmyong intranet only). Revisit only if sanctions are formally lifted and the country opens to the global internet. Nothing points to that happening in 2026–2027.
