# Palau: indie software opportunity research

**Classification:** Microstate (population about 18,000, estimate). Search budget: 4. Three searches ran. The fourth was refused (usage limit), so research stopped there as the instructions require.
**Accessibility:** No US/EU/UK sanctions apply. Palau uses the US dollar and is in a Compact of Free Association with the US. A foreign solo founder can legally sell software there. The constraint is market size, not access.

## Bottom line

**I found no viable standalone opportunity in Palau.** The mandatory recurring workflows are real: monthly PGST, quarterly Gross Revenue Tax (GRT), quarterly social security contributions, and foreign worker permits. But the buyer counts are tiny. The PGST threshold is US$300k in annual taxable supply, so probably only a few hundred registrants (estimate, unverified). Most of these tasks are handled by local accountants or by general accounting software configured for a 10% tax. The only realistic way to sell here is as an add-on to a regional "Micronesia / US-affiliated Pacific" product. That product would cover Palau, FSM, the Marshall Islands, Guam and the CNMI, and would use the Pacific Islands SBDC network and the chambers of commerce for distribution.

## Industries screened

| Industry | Workflow looked at | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| All VAT-registered SMEs (retail, hotels, dive shops) | Monthly PGST return, e-filed with BRT by the 30th of the following month | Add-on only | Mandatory and monthly, but only a few hundred registrants (estimate). General accounting tools with a 10% tax code cover the calculation. The remaining work is a manual entry into the BRT e-filing portal. |
| Small businesses under the PGST threshold | Quarterly 4% GRT return (gross income above US$50k) | Reject | Quarterly, one simple calculation. Not worth paying for. |
| Tourism operators (dive/tour, hotels) | Pristine Paradise Environmental Fee (PPEF) handling, state permits | Reject | Airlines collect the PPEF and Customs collects it for sea arrivals. Operators have no recurring PPEF filing burden. Protected Areas Network Fund (PANF) quarterly reports are filed by state protected-area sites, not businesses. |
| Employers of foreign workers (construction, hospitality, retail) | Work-permit applications and renewals (about 5,127 permits issued in 2017), quarterly social security contributions | Attractive problem, poor distribution | Real and recurring. Island Times reported more than 100 expired permits, and a task force was set up over discrepancies in foreign-worker counts. But it is paper/office-based with no portal to integrate with, the employer base is small, and in-house admin staff or agents handle it. |
| Fisheries / export | Catch and export documentation | Not researched (search budget) | Palau's commercial fishing is dominated by a few foreign-flagged or licensed operators, so this is unlikely to have an SME buyer base (unverified). |

## Strongest opportunities

None meet the brief's bar as standalone businesses. The following is recorded only as a possible regional add-on.

### Opportunity: PGST/GRT return preparer as a module of a regional Micronesia tax-compliance tool

**Industry:**
Cross-industry SMEs (retail, hospitality, dive/tour operators, import distributors)

**Buyer:**
Owner/bookkeeper of a PGST-registered business in Koror, or a local accounting firm filing for several clients

**Trigger / Why now:**
PGST (10%) took effect on January 1, 2023, replacing most of the GRT. Returns are monthly and filed electronically with the Bureau of Revenue & Taxation (BRT). GRT still applies to non-registrants with gross income over US$50k, filed quarterly. This is a still-recent regime, but by 2026 it is no longer new.

**Current workflow:**
1. Bookkeeper exports sales and purchases from QuickBooks, Xero or spreadsheets. The tools in use are an assumption, not verified.
2. They compute output PGST, input credits and exempt or zero-rated lines by hand.
3. They re-key the totals into the BRT electronic filing system and pay by the 30th.
4. Separately, they compute quarterly social security contributions and wage tax and file each with its agency.

**Pain:**
Monthly deadline, penalties for late filing, and re-keying from accounting software into the portal. I found no complaints or audit evidence, so the evidence is thin.

**Existing solutions:**
QuickBooks and Xero with a custom 10% tax rate. Local accountants and CPA firms filing for clients manually (assumed). The free BRT e-filing system itself.

**The gap:**
There may be no Palau-specific mapping from accounting data to the PGST return lines, and no combined calendar for PGST, wage tax and social security. I have not verified this.

**Possible product:**
A "Pacific compliance calendar + return preparer" that imports QuickBooks/Xero data and produces return-ready figures for Palau PGST, FSM, RMI and CNMI BGRT, and Guam GRT.

**MVP:**
CSV import, then a Palau PGST return worksheet plus deadline reminders.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
US$20–40/month per business, or US$100/month for an accounting firm.

**How to find first customers:**
Palau Chamber of Commerce, the Pacific Islands SBDC Palau center, and BRT registrant lists if they are published (unverified).

**Risks:**
Tiny total addressable market (TAM). BRT portal access and data format are unknown. Local accountants may prefer to stay manual.

**Kill condition:**
Fewer than about 300 PGST registrants, or QuickBooks/Xero reports already map cleanly to the return.

**Score:** 2/10

## Rejected after competitor research

- **PGST filing tool as a standalone product.** The substitutes that kill it are general accounting software (QuickBooks/Xero with a 10% tax code), local accountants, and the free BRT e-filing system. The market is too small for a dedicated product.
- **PPEF / tourism fee compliance for operators.** Killed because airlines and Customs collect the fee and remit it to the Ministry of Finance. Operators carry no recurring workflow. Source: https://reefresilience.org/case-studies/palau-financing-protected-areas/

## Attractive problem, poor distribution

- **Foreign-worker permit and renewal tracking for employers.** There is evidence of expired permits and count discrepancies, but the process is paper-based with the Division of Labor, buyers are few, and nothing can be integrated with. Sources: https://islandtimes.org/over-100-expired-work-permits-labor-office-reports/ , https://www.mvariety.com/?p=116287 , https://hellopebl.com/resources/blog/hire-employees-in-palau

## Too competitive

- None identified. The constraint is market size, not competition.
