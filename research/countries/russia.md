# Russia: country research (global indie-hacker opportunity study)

Research date: 2026-10-05. Searches used: 4 (inaccessible-market budget).

## Verdict: not accessible to a foreign solo founder. No opportunities recommended.

Russia has plenty of the workflows the brief is looking for: mandatory state information systems, product labeling and traceability, veterinary certification, alcohol accounting and e-invoicing. But a foreign (US/EU/UK-based) solo founder cannot legally or practically sell B2B workflow software there in 2026. Per the agent instructions (section 4), research stopped after the accessibility check.

### Accessibility check

| Barrier | Finding | Effect on a foreign solo SaaS founder |
|---|---|---|
| US sanctions | OFAC determination under EO 14071 (issued 12 July 2024, effective 12 Sept 2024) bars US persons from providing IT consultancy and design services, and IT support and cloud services for enterprise management software and design/manufacturing software, to any person in Russia. | A US person cannot build, host or support B2B workflow SaaS for Russian customers. |
| EU sanctions | The 12th package (Dec 2023) bans selling, supplying or providing enterprise management software to the Russian government and to any entity established in Russia. The ban explicitly covers ERP, CRM, BI, SCM, CMMS, project management, and accounting, fleet, logistics and HR components. | Covers nearly every product shape this study targets (compliance workflow, logistics, HR/payroll, accounting add-ons). |
| UK sanctions | The UK aligned with the EU in December 2023. The Russia (Sanctions) (EU Exit) (Amendment) Regulations 2025 (in force 24 Apr 2025) extend the ERP/software ban to intangible transfers: downloads and cloud services. | Same as above for UK persons. |
| Payment rails | Visa and Mastercard suspended Russian operations in 2022 (widely reported; no source fetched in this session), and major Russian banks are cut off from SWIFT. Stripe, Paddle and similar processors do not serve Russia. | No practical, legal way to collect recurring subscription revenue from abroad. |
| Data localization | 152-FZ requires Russian citizens' personal data to be stored on servers in Russia. Rules tightened from 1 July 2025. Fines are RUB 1–6M for a first localization offence and RUB 6–18M for repeats, and turnover-based fines up to 3% are coming in 2026. | You would need Russian hosting and a local legal entity, which conflicts with the Western sanctions above. |
| Import substitution | Foreign software is banned for public agencies from 1 Jan 2025. Critical-infrastructure owners must switch to domestic software by 1 Nov 2028, and fines are planned. Buyers in regulated sectors prefer software on the Russian software registry. | Even where it would be legal, buyers are being pushed toward registry-listed domestic vendors. |

Conclusion: **inaccessible.** For US/EU/UK persons, selling is legally blocked. Collecting payment is practically blocked for everyone. Russian law adds localization and domestic-software preferences that a foreign solo founder cannot meet.

## Opportunities

None. No opportunity meets the brief's final decision rule, because a foreign solo founder has no legal or practical way to sell or get paid.

## Rejected after competitor research

- Not reached. Every idea was rejected at the accessibility stage first. For context only (unverified this session): the domestic 1C ecosystem, SKB Kontur and Tensor (SBIS) are the incumbents across Russian compliance workflows (EDO, labeling, Mercury, EGAIS, reporting to the tax authority). They would be the main competitors even without sanctions.

## Attractive problem, poor distribution

- Russian state-system compliance (Mercury, Chestny ZNAK, EGAIS). The pain is real and recurring, but there is no viable distribution or payment path for a foreign founder.

## Too competitive

- The same workflows would very likely be too competitive anyway, given the 1C partner network plus Kontur and SBIS (unverified estimate).

## Add-on to a neighbouring market?

- No. Serving Russian customers from Kazakhstan, Armenia, etc. would still breach US/EU/UK persons' obligations and carries circumvention risk. Do not pursue.
