# Tuvalu: Country Research

**Classification:** Microstate (population about 9,500 in 2025 per Internet Society Pulse). Search budget: 4 searches (all used).

**Bottom line:** I found no viable standalone indie-software opportunity in Tuvalu. The formal private sector is tiny. Most licensed businesses are small retailers and family shops, plus a few state-owned enterprises; I could not verify the number of registered businesses. The most heavily regulated sector with recurring reporting is industrial tuna fishing. That sector's reporting already runs through donor-funded regional systems from SPC, FFA and PNA, and the buyers are foreign fleets and the government. Tuvalu should be treated, at most, as an add-on market for a Pacific-wide product built for Fiji, Samoa or Vanuatu.

**Accessibility:** Tuvalu is not under sanctions and internet access works: about 81% of the population are internet users, and Starlink is resold through Tuvalu Telecommunications Corporation (TTC). The barriers are practical, not legal. There are very few buyers, the economy uses the Australian dollar and runs mostly on bank transfers, and procurement is government-heavy, much of it donor-funded through ADB, the World Bank and UNCTAD.

## Industries screened

| Industry | Workflow looked at | Verdict | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fisheries (industrial tuna) | Licensing, catch logsheets, ALC/VMS reporting under the new Fisheries Management Regulations 2025 | Reject | Real new regulatory trigger, but SPC's TUFMAN2 and its e-reporting apps (OnBoard, Tails), FFA/iFIMS and the PNA systems already cover it. The buyers are foreign fleets and the government. |
| Customs brokers / importers | Import declarations, duty calculation | Reject | Only a handful of importers. Pacific customs runs on UNCTAD's ASYCUDA, and I could not confirm that Tuvalu has rolled out ASYCUDA World. Too few brokers to pay for a product. |
| Accountants / tax compliance | Business tax and payroll filings to the revenue department | Reject | Very few accounting practices. Tax reform is donor-led (ADB technical assistance). Filing volume is far too small to support a subscription. |
| Retail / wholesale (small shops, fusi co-ops) | Inventory, stock reorders, licence renewals | Reject | Generic POS problem. Generic POS tools and spreadsheets already cover it, and there is no mandatory reporting trigger. |
| Government vendors / donor projects | Procurement bids and donor reporting (ADB, World Bank, GCF climate projects) | Poor distribution | Donor reporting is genuinely painful, but the buyers are project units sitting inside ministries. Sales go through donor procurement, which is not an indie channel. |

## Opportunities

None meet the bar. I am not listing scored opportunities because there are too few buyers for a standalone product. Under the brief's benchmark of about 5,000 reachable buyers paying $200–500 a month, Tuvalu probably has fewer than 50 potential buyers for any one vertical (estimate, unverified).

**Possible add-on angle (not scored):** a regional "Pacific small-state compliance pack" for importers or fishing-licence agents. It would be built first for Fiji, then extended to Tuvalu, Kiribati and Nauru. The Pacific ASYCUDA network and the shared PNA/FFA fisheries regimes create some regional standardization, but competitor diligence would have to happen at the regional level, not in Tuvalu.

---

## Rejected after competitor research

- **Fisheries licence and logsheet compliance tool for vessels licensed in Tuvalu.** The Tuvalu Fisheries Authority's *Fisheries Management Regulations 2025* (published in 2026) set out permit and licensing procedures, vessel reporting obligations and mandatory Automatic Location Communicators (ALCs). This looked like a textbook regulatory trigger. It was killed by **SPC's TUFMAN2**, a cloud tuna data system for Pacific Island countries that takes data directly from SPC's e-reporting apps (**Tails, OnBoard, OnShore, OLLO**), by **iFIMS** (integrated observer and catch e-reporting), and by the **PNA / FFA vessel-registration and VMS systems**. These are free or donor-funded tools, mandated at regional level, with fleet-level buyers outside Tuvalu.
- **Customs declaration helper.** Killed by **ASYCUDA**, the regional standard run by UNCTAD with a Pacific programme. I could not confirm whether Tuvalu currently uses ASYCUDA World. There are too few brokers either way.

## Attractive problem, poor distribution

- **Donor-project reporting and climate-finance monitoring and evaluation** (for example the World Bank and ADB projects in Tuvalu). The workflow is recurring and painful, but buyers are reached through donor procurement and there are only a few project units.

## Too competitive

- None at Tuvalu's scale. Fisheries is "solved" by free regional public tools, not by commercial competitors.
