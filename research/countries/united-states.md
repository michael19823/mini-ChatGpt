# United States: research report (deep pass, 2026-10-05)

**Scope and caveats:** this is a deep pass with about 90 WebSearch calls (WebFetch blocked), and it screened 35+ industries. The brief's benchmark ideas (Florida grease manifests, funeral EDRS, solar permitting, certified payroll, fire inspection reporting) are not re-reported. Anything not tied to a source is marked *estimate* or *unverified*. The US is the most heavily served software market in the study, and most workflows that looked attractive already had a vertical vendor. Only one candidate scored above 5.

## Opportunities

### Opportunity: School-Choice Funding Receivables and Reconciliation for Private Schools

**Industry:**
Private K-12 schools that accept ESAs, vouchers and tax-credit scholarships

**Buyer:**
Business manager, bookkeeper or head of school at a small to mid-size private school (often faith-based, 100-600 students). Secondary buyers are diocesan or network finance offices that run many schools.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- Texas Education Freedom Accounts (TEFA) launch in 2026-27 with a $1B budget, 150,000+ applicants and $10,474 per private-school student, run by Odyssey. 700+ schools are approved and paid in 25% installments.
- The federal Educational Choice for Children Act tax-credit scholarship starts Jan 1, 2027 in opt-in states. Money flows through many scholarship-granting organizations (SGOs), so schools will get funds for the same student from several SGOs plus state programs.
- 75 programs in 33 states now fund more than 1M students.
- Florida private schools sued Step Up For Students in Feb 2026 over delayed, reduced or missing payments.
- Arizona's ESA had about 90,000 unprocessed reimbursement requests and vendors waiting months for payment.

**Current workflow:**
1. The school confirms each student's award and program (e.g., FES-UA, FTC, TEFA, AZ ESA, several AZ STOs or PA SSOs) and sets expected amounts per term.
2. Each quarter, staff create invoices or payment requests in each platform (Step Up EMA, Odyssey, ClassWallet). In Florida, payment also needs parent approval in EMA.
3. Staff chase parents who have not approved and track which students are pending, approved, paid, reduced or rejected.
4. Staff download deposit reports or receive checks from many SGOs/STOs, match them to students, and post them to FACTS, TADS, Blackbaud or QuickBooks.
5. Staff handle exceptions: amount changes, withdrawals and pro-rated refunds back to the program, double-counted students, and audits.

**Pain:**
- Florida school leaders testified to "chronic payment delays, fluctuating scholarship amounts, technical failures"; one school was still owed $50,000 from the prior year, and the lawsuit followed.
- In Florida, 27,000+ students were flagged as double-counted in Q1 2025-26.
- Arizona schools and vendors waited months for payment.
- Revenue depends directly on this workflow, because ESA/voucher money can be most of a small school's tuition revenue (*estimate*).

**Existing solutions:**
- FACTS (Nelnet) markets school-choice features that apply awards to tuition accounts, track payments from families, SGOs and state programs, and segment funds by source.
- Blackbaud publishes school-choice guidance for its tuition products.
- TADS and Smart Tuition are other tuition management tools (school-choice depth unverified).
- Program-side school portals: Step Up EMA, Odyssey, ClassWallet.
- On the SGO side, Vouchive serves ECCA scholarship-granting organizations.
- Spreadsheets and part-time bookkeepers.

**The gap:**
The incumbents are ledgers. Nothing found pulls the status of each award from each program portal, chases the parent-approval bottleneck, matches deposits from many SGOs/STOs at the student level, and flags short-pays and withdrawal refunds before the term closes. It is unverified whether FACTS imports platform remittance files directly or relies on manual posting; this is the key diligence question.

**Possible product:**
A school-choice receivables desk. It imports each program's award and payment exports (EMA, Odyssey, ClassWallet, SGO remittances), keeps a per-student expected-vs-received ledger, automates reminders to parents, and pushes clean postings to FACTS, TADS or Blackbaud.

**MVP:**
Florida (Step Up) and Texas (TEFA/Odyssey) only. CSV import of award and payment reports, a student-level variance dashboard, parent-approval reminder emails/SMS, and a FACTS-ready posting export.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
$100-300/month per school, tiered by number of scholarship students (*estimate*). Alternatively $3-5 per scholarship student per month for the school year. Diocesan multi-school licences.

**How to find first customers:**
- Public participating-school lists: Texas Comptroller TEFA school list (700+), Florida DOE scholarship-participating private school directory (2,100+), AZ ESA vendor lists.
- State private-school associations (TEPSAC member accreditors, FCIS, Florida Catholic Conference).
- Catholic diocesan finance offices.
- ACSI regional chapters.

**Risks:**
- FACTS (used by thousands of schools) adds direct platform integrations.
- Program administrators change, as Texas chose Odyssey and states switch vendors.
- Policy and litigation risk to the programs themselves.
- Platform terms on data exports.
- Many schools are in only one program, which lowers fragmentation per school.

**Kill condition:**
Interviews show FACTS or Blackbaud already import EMA, Odyssey and ClassWallet remittances automatically, or business managers say reconciliation takes under 2 hours per month.

**Score:** 6/10

### Opportunity: BEAD Subgrantee Post-Award Compliance Packets

**Industry:**
Rural broadband (small ISPs, electric and telephone co-ops, municipal providers)

**Buyer:**
Grants/compliance manager or CFO at a regional ISP or co-op holding one or more state BEAD subgrants

**Trigger / Why now:**
All BEAD final proposals are approved, and subgrant agreements are being signed through 2026. Reporting and monitoring run from January 2026 through December 2030. Each state broadband office sets its own quarterly report template, milestone reimbursement documentation and BABA evidence rules. Montana issued separate terrestrial and LEO quarterly report instructions in mid-2026.

**Current workflow:**
1. The provider pulls construction progress, BSLs passed/connected, subscriber counts and jobs data from GIS, billing and finance systems.
2. Staff fill in each state's quarterly report template, narrative and milestone tables.
3. Staff collect manufacturer BABA certification letters for every material.
4. Staff assemble milestone reimbursement packages; payment is released only when the milestone is complete and documented.
5. Staff respond to desk reviews and audits; final closeout is due within 120 days of the period of performance.

**Pain:**
Reimbursement and possible forfeiture depend on documentation (state monitoring plans). A provider active in two or three states keeps parallel formats. Hours per quarter are unquantified (*unverified*).

**Existing solutions:**
- Engineering and grant consultants (common, unverified by name here).
- VETRO FiberMap has built-in BEAD export templates for many state programs.
- 3-GIS (fiber asset management).
- Sonar (ISP billing) publishes a BEAD toolkit and post-award compliance guidance.
- Calix supports BABA for its equipment.
- Spreadsheets.

**The gap:**
GIS tools export network data, and consultants write narratives. No product found keeps a per-state, per-milestone evidence binder (BABA letters, invoices, photos, BSL status) that generates each state's report and reimbursement request.

**Possible product:**
A BEAD compliance binder with state template packs. It ingests GIS/billing exports and a BABA certificate library, and outputs each state's quarterly report and milestone reimbursement package.

**MVP:**
Templates for 3 states with many small subgrantees, BABA certificate tracker, and a quarterly report generator from CSV.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
$500-1,500/month per subgrantee during construction (*estimate*). Cheaper than a consultant's quarterly retainer.

**How to find first customers:**
Public state award lists. Texas awarded 17-22 providers and Louisiana 12-13; NTIA and state broadband office sites list all subgrantees. NTCA, WISPA and NRECA member lists.

**Risks:**
- Small buyer count: perhaps 400-800 subgrantees nationally, many of them large carriers or SpaceX (*estimate*).
- Consultants bundle reporting.
- VETRO or Sonar extend their tools.
- Federal rule changes (NTIA has already restructured BEAD once).

**Kill condition:**
Fewer than about 200 small subgrantees nationally, or state offices accept a single NTIA-standard format.

**Score:** 4/10

### Opportunity: Healthcare Staffing Agency Multi-State Registration and Rate Reporting

**Industry:**
Healthcare staffing (nurse and aide agencies supplying nursing homes, assisted living and hospitals)

**Buyer:**
Owner, controller or compliance lead at a 1-20 office healthcare staffing agency operating in several states

**Trigger / Why now:**
- About two dozen states now license or register healthcare staffing agencies (list as of March 2024 per one vendor source).
- Several states require recurring rate data:
  - New York requires quarterly financial reports under PHL Art. 29-K, with registration renewal for Aug 2026-Jul 2027.
  - Illinois requires quarterly reports to the Department of Labor of average bill rate, pay rate and labor costs by county and provider type.
  - Minnesota and Massachusetts require annual reports and set rate caps.
- More bills are pending (e.g., Nebraska LB 308 with a public rate database).

**Current workflow:**
1. Finance exports payroll and invoices.
2. Staff aggregate by state, county, facility type and job category.
3. Staff fill in each state's template or portal and track registration renewals and fees.
4. Staff keep caregiver background-check evidence per state rules (Maine, Pennsylvania).

**Pain:**
Missing a report can cost the registration in that state. Aggregating by county and category is spreadsheet-heavy (*unverified*; no complaint evidence found).

**Existing solutions:**
- Licensing services (Harbor Compliance-type providers; Zenind publishes state guides).
- Background-check vendors (Streamline Verify).
- Staffing ATS/payroll systems (no state-report feature found, *unverified*).
- Outside CPAs.

**The gap:**
A rules pack that turns one payroll and billing export into each state's rate report on schedule, plus a registration calendar.

**Possible product:**
A state report generator for staffing agencies, with category mapping and due-date tracking.

**MVP:**
NY and IL quarterly reports from CSV, plus a calendar for 10 states' renewals.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
$99-299/month per agency (*estimate*).

**How to find first customers:**
State registries of registered agencies (NY DOH registration list, IL nurse agency licensees, PA DOH registry). American Staffing Association healthcare section.

**Risks:**
Only a few states need recurring rate reports. Agencies may treat it as a quarterly CPA task. Rate-cap politics could shrink the industry.

**Kill condition:**
Fewer than 5 states require recurring rate reporting, or agencies say the report takes under an hour.

**Score:** 4/10

### Opportunity: Weights & Measures "Placed in Service" Report Router (Scales, Fuel Meters, EV Chargers)

**Industry:**
Registered service agencies: scale service companies, petroleum equipment/meter technicians, EVSE installers

**Buyer:**
Owner or office manager of a scale or meter service company working across counties or states

**Trigger / Why now:**
This is a standing, fragmented mandate with a new growth segment. Since Jan 1 2021, California requires RSAs to report commercial EV charger installs and repairs to county sealers. 2026 CDFA guidance adds traceable ISO 17025 calibration requirements for EVSE standards. Maryland runs EVSE registration. Every state has its own form, and California splits reporting across county sealers.

**Current workflow:**
1. The technician repairs or installs a device and applies a seal and sticker.
2. The office fills in that state's (or county's) Placed in Service form with device ID, NTEP CC, make/model/serial and seal details.
3. The office emails, faxes or mails the form within 24 hours (Idaho, Sacramento County, Sutter County), sometimes in triplicate (South Dakota).
4. The office keeps calibration certificates for test standards.

**Pain:**
The 24-hour deadline applies to every qualifying repair. Forms are paper or PDF in most states (Idaho, Utah, Oregon, Maine, Michigan guides). Penalties and enforcement intensity are unverified.

**Existing solutions:**
Generic field-service apps and paper forms. No scale-service software with automatic per-jurisdiction placed-in-service submission was found (*unverified*).

**The gap:**
Producing the jurisdiction-correct form from one service record and sending it to the right sealer or state address.

**Possible product:**
A mobile service ticket that auto-selects the right state or county form, fills it in, emails it, and logs proof of submission.

**MVP:**
California (state form plus county routing) and 5 neighbouring states, with email delivery and an audit log.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
$29-79/month per technician (*estimate*).

**How to find first customers:**
Public RSA registries (Michigan, Maryland scale and meter lists, CDFA RSA lists), the scale dealer network (e.g., Rice Lake dealers), EVSE installer lists.

**Risks:**
- Low willingness to pay.
- States may build portals.
- Field-service tools could add a form library.
- Total RSA count is unknown (*estimate*: low thousands).

**Kill condition:**
Interviews show the form takes under 5 minutes and nobody pays to remove it.

**Score:** 4/10

### Opportunity: SNAP Restriction Item-File Maintenance for Independent Stores

**Industry:**
Independent convenience stores and grocers (SNAP-authorized retailers)

**Buyer:**
Owner or back-office manager of a 1-20 store independent chain, or a POS reseller serving them

**Trigger / Why now:**
USDA approved SNAP food-restriction waivers for 23 states (soda, candy, energy drinks and more). The first took effect Jan 1 2026, and 19 are expected by end-2026. Each state defines restricted items differently (e.g., Iowa ties restrictions to its tax classes, while Texas uses a 5 g added-sugar or artificial-sweetener rule). The waivers cover more than 21,000 items. Retailers get a 90-day grace period and then risk losing SNAP authorization. Convenience stores are about 45% of SNAP outlets.

**Current workflow:**
1. The retailer obtains a list: state-provided (Florida, Oklahoma, Louisiana via NIQ Brandbank), NIQ via NGA, or Sifter.
2. Staff match it to the store's own item file, including private label, store-coded items and new SKUs.
3. Staff set SNAP-eligible flags per state in the POS, then re-check when lists or new items change.

**Pain:**
NGA and NACS say most states did not provide a usable list and that retailers face loss of authorization after two violations. FMI, NGA and NACS estimate $1.56B upfront compliance cost.

**Existing solutions:**
NIQ (NGA partnership), Sifter Solutions (state contractor for Arkansas, offering retailer POS maintenance files), state-published lists, POS and back-office vendors (Petrosoft and others), wholesaler item files.

**The gap:**
Matching a messy single-store item file to the state list and keeping it current, for stores whose POS vendor does not push updates.

**Possible product:**
Upload the POS item export, get back a corrected SNAP-flag file per state, and receive monthly change alerts.

**MVP:**
Texas and Florida (large states with April 2026 waivers), CSV in and out for the 3 most common c-store POS formats.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
$15-40/month per store (*estimate*).

**How to find first customers:**
USDA FNS SNAP retailer locator data (public), state c-store associations, POS resellers.

**Risks:**
- Legal: on June 22 2026 a DC federal court vacated the waivers for Colorado, Iowa, Nebraska, Tennessee and West Virginia, and other states may follow.
- Free state lists and NIQ compete.
- POS vendors may absorb the work.
- Low price per store.

**Kill condition:**
An appeal or new rulings vacate most waivers, or major c-store POS vendors push state flags automatically.

**Score:** 4/10

### Opportunity: California Rent Registry and Rental Registration Tracker for Small Property Managers

**Industry:**
Residential property management

**Buyer:**
Small property managers or landlords with 20-500 units across several California cities (and other registry cities such as Seattle and Ann Arbor)

**Trigger / Why now:**
Rent registries are multiplying:
- Los Angeles RSO requires annual registration plus a rent registry, with fees of about $38.75 per unit.
- Oakland requires annual rent and tenancy reporting (2026 deadline Mar 2).
- LA County's unincorporated-area registry user guide was dated Jul 2026.
- San Leandro's registry starts Jan 31 2027 at $149 per unit.

Each city also has rental registration and inspection cycles (Seattle RRIO, Ann Arbor).

**Current workflow:**
1. The manager exports the rent roll from AppFolio, Buildium or a spreadsheet.
2. Staff re-key unit rents, tenancy start dates and amenities into each city's registry portal, and pay fees.
3. Staff report new tenancies where required and track inspection or renewal deadlines.

**Pain:**
Cities can block rent increases or eviction processes for non-registration (LA RSO "to lawfully demand or accept rent"). Fees and penalties apply. The work is annual per unit with event-driven updates, so frequency is low.

**Existing solutions:**
City portals. Municipal-side vendors (Cloudpermit and others). Generic compliance trackers (DoorLoop hub content, Process.st). Property-management suites (registry export features *unverified*).

**The gap:**
Rent roll to multi-city registry upload with per-city field mapping and a deadline calendar.

**Possible product:**
A registry filing assistant: upload the rent roll, get per-city filing files or assisted entry, a fee calendar and stored proof.

**MVP:**
LA RSO, Oakland, LA County and San Leandro, fed from AppFolio and Buildium CSV.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
$1-2 per unit per year plus $49/month minimum (*estimate*).

**How to find first customers:**
City registry and landlord association lists (AAGLA, East Bay Rental Housing Association, CAA chapters).

**Risks:**
Annual frequency. Property-management suites could add exports. Cities change portals.

**Kill condition:**
AppFolio or Buildium already export registry-ready files, or managers spend under a day a year on it.

**Score:** 4/10
