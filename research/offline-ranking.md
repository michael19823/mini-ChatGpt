# Offline-Industries Pass: Global Ranking

*2026-10-05. This ranking covers all 199 reports in `research/offline/`, written with the
regulator-first method in `research/method/offline-instructions.md`. It looks only at "quiet"
industries: small, regulated, mostly paper-based businesses that rarely post online, which the
first pass of the study missed.*

## Headline

- **353 scored opportunities in 166 countries.** The other 33 countries had nothing worth scoring.
  Most of those are microstates, sanctioned or closed markets, or very small economies.
- **Scores are lower and flatter than in the first pass.**

  | Score | Opportunities |
  |---|---|
  | 7 | 2 |
  | 6–6.5 | 11 |
  | 5–5.5 | 44 |
  | 3–4 | 230 |

  In the first pass, the top country-level score was 7.5.
- **Two buyer groups stand out:**
  - Rural agri-input and storage businesses. These have the two best ideas, both driven by brand-new
    2026 rules in India and the Philippines.
  - Small dealers that must keep police or anti-money-laundering registers: scrap yards, pawnshops,
    gold shops and money changers. These recur in 36–39 countries each.
- **The Florida grease pattern is real and widespread.** The pattern is "one service job, several
  receiving bodies, each with its own format". It appeared 36 times in 32 countries: septic,
  backflow, boreholes, lifts, signs, memorials and water meters. None of these scores above 6
  locally. Its value lies in reusing one routing engine across many countries.
- **None of these ideas has been fact-checked yet.** In the first pass, 8 of the top 10 lost
  0.5–2 points when a plan agent re-checked them. Expect the top scores here to settle around 5–6
  after a similar check. That would put them level with the reassessed first-pass leaders, not
  above them.

What the patterns say:

1. **New laws make the best openings.** Every idea scoring 6 or more has a rule dated 2024–2026
   with a near deadline. Many "quiet" industries are quiet because nothing has changed for them,
   and those scored 2–4.
2. **Dealer registers are the most portable product.** Scrap, pawn, gold and money-changer
   registers share one core: identify the seller, record the item, flag thresholds, build an
   inspection pack. They are needed in 70+ countries, and only the report formats change. Global
   scrap SaaS is built for US and UK law, and local tools handle stock, not evidence.
3. **The router pattern is common but thin locally.** Each country's version is small, typically
   a few hundred to a few thousand service firms. It works only as one engine sold country by
   country, or to a vendor as an add-on.
4. **Household employers are a weak market.** The pain is real (30 ideas), but these buyers don't
   pay for software, and where they would, an app already exists (AskMandla in South Africa,
   Singapore and Hong Kong services). Agencies that place workers are better buyers than the
   households themselves.
5. **Most top ideas need a local partner.** Of the 15 above, only Spain, Australia and the
   Philippines pawnshop pack can realistically be sold remotely in English or Spanish. The
   Nigeria, Kenya, Colombia, Indonesia and Brazil ideas all need someone on the ground.
6. **The biggest risk is a free government tool.** That risk recurs as in the first pass. India
   (iFMS, SATHI), Colombia (SINIGAN), Nigeria (lawmapay) and Australia (on-trac) all have a
   government platform that could expand to cover the gap.

**Verdict:** the offline pass found new, real openings, especially the India and Philippines 2026
rules and the multi-country dealer-register pattern. It doesn't change the first-pass
recommendation on its own. The top three offline ideas deserve the same development-plan check the
first-pass top 10 received before they're compared like for like.

## Caveats

- **Model mix and Opus review.** 20 reports were written by Sonnet subagents and end with
  `Research model: Sonnet`; all the others were written by Opus. Opus reviewed every Sonnet
  report (`research/offline/_sonnet-review.md`, plus an `## Opus review` section in each one).
  - **Sound:** 14 small or inaccessible markets reached the same honest null result Opus would.
  - **Cut:** 3 of the 7 Sonnet ideas scored 4 or higher. Portugal's gold-buyer register fell
    4 → 2, because Gouwin and Ponto25 already produce the police declarations. Portugal's
    hunting zones and Lithuania's farm journal fell 4 → 3, and Kosovo's livestock lead fell
    3 → 2 because it cited Albania's law.
  - **Unchanged:** Egypt's bakery lead (5) and Jamaica's two leads (4).

  No Sonnet idea was in the top 15, so the ranking above doesn't change. Lesson for future
  passes: Sonnet is fine for screening small markets, but any Sonnet idea scored 4 or higher
  needs an Opus check of its competitors and trigger before it enters a ranking.
- All research used web-search snippets only, because WebFetch was blocked. Market counts marked
  "unverified" or "estimate" in the reports should not be relied on.
- Scores are each agent's own, against the brief's 10 criteria plus the offline-channel rule. They
  were not normalised across agents beyond the pattern grouping above.
