Method: I read every report in full. For the four reports with an opportunity scored 4 or higher (Egypt, Jamaica, Lithuania, Portugal), I checked the claims each score depends on with 4–5 WebSearch calls per report. For Kosovo I used 1 search, on a law number that looked wrong. The other reports got a desk check only. In total I used 19 searches. For comparison I read four Opus offline reports: Latvia, Spain, Morocco, and Trinidad and Tobago.

## Overall judgment

Sonnet was good enough for the null-result countries, but not for the countries where a score depended on competitor diligence. In the 14 small or inaccessible markets (Comoros, Holy See, Yemen and others), the Sonnet reports reach the same honest "nothing here" verdict an Opus report would. They flag unverified counts well, and I found no invented URLs. The problems sit where the scores matter. Two of the four reports scored 4 or higher missed incumbents that one targeted search finds. In Portugal that search was "software ourivesaria PJ", and the gap it missed was the exact gap the opportunity rested on. Kosovo attached a neighbouring country's law to its only lead. Compared with Latvia, Spain and Morocco, the Opus reports run the "product + industry" competitor search as a matter of routine: Opus Spain found ORONET for the same gold-buyer obligation that Sonnet Portugal left "unknown". They read the legal text more closely for who carries the duty, and their search effort is more even (Opus: 19–31 searches for comparable countries). Sonnet's effort varied widely: 32–34 searches for Egypt and Portugal, but only 2–6 for many small markets. Sonnet also tends to give 4/10 to leads whose own sub-scores average about 3. Recommendation: Sonnet is fine for first-pass screening of small or low-prior markets. Any Sonnet opportunity scored 4 or higher needs a short Opus (or second-pass) check of the competitor and the trigger before it enters the ranking.
