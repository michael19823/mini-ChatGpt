# Afghanistan: Offline-Industries Pass

**Date:** 2026-10-05
**Search budget used:** 7 of 8
**Verdict:** **No opportunities recommended.** The accessibility gate from the country report (`research/countries/afghanistan.md`) still holds. The US renewed Executive Order 14064 on 20 Jan 2026. GL 20 still bars transfers to Taliban-controlled entities. Afghan banks remain cut off from card and SWIFT rails. Internet access is politically fragile after the fibre shutdown of Sep–Oct 2025. The regulator-first method does find quiet industries here that are regulated and enforced: money changers, pharmacies, scrap and steel, livestock. In every case, though, the regulator is a de facto authority run by a sanctioned group. The "workflow" is paper licensing, counter visits and inspections with sealing, not digital reporting. And the operators cannot pay a foreign vendor through any legitimate rail. This is a short, honest report, as the prompt allows for inaccessible markets.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

None. No candidate passes the brief's distribution and willingness-to-pay tests, or this pass's founder-access test:

- **Founder access:** a non-local solo founder cannot sell here. Even a local founder faces sanctions screening on every buyer and a regulator that is itself a blocked party.
- **Offline channel:** the only aggregators are guild-like bodies (money changers' unions, the scrap merchants who met the MoF). They operate under the de facto authorities, so working through them creates sanctions exposure.
- **Willingness to pay:** buyers pay in cash or hawala. Nothing collectable by a foreign vendor was identified.

## 3. Rejected

- **Money-changer licence and AML record-keeping (DAB):** the most "regulated quiet industry" found. It has capital rules, sealing campaigns and arrests. Killed because it is AML/sanctions-core, DAB discourages online services, and the market is hawala.
- **Pharmacy licence/inspection readiness (MoPH):** enforcement is real, with 87 closures out of 1,776 inspected. Killed because the pain is licensing status, not recurring paperwork, and there is no rail or legal path to sell.
- **Scrap/steel supply records:** killed because there is no register obligation, only an export-policy fight.
- **Livestock cross-border permits:** killed because the bans are episodic and decree-driven, with no recurring filing.
- **Possible add-on outside the country:** Afghan livestock and scrap traders who cross the border are better reached through Pakistan's own compliance markets. Use the Pakistan report.

## 4. Method notes

- What worked: English searches for regulator press pages. moph.gov.af publishes inspection and closure counts. Pajhwok and Afghanistan International cover DAB licensing and enforcement.
- What didn't work: searches for registers, forms and trade associations for livestock, scrap and trader licensing. These return 2021-era or Pakistan-side material. No public operator registers were found. Dari and Pashto searches were not attempted within the 8-search budget.
- The gating facts (sanctions, payment rails, internet) dominate. A deeper search would not change the verdict.
