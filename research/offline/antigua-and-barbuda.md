# Antigua and Barbuda: Offline-Industries Pass

**Date:** 2026-10-05 · **Market class:** Microstate (population about 100k) · **Search budget used:** 8 of 8 · **Country report:** `research/countries/antigua-and-barbuda.md` (its only idea, an ABST e-invoicing add-on, is not repeated here)

## Bottom line

None of the quiet industries in Antigua and Barbuda supports a standalone software product. The regulators exist: the Transport Board, the St. John's Development Corporation (SJDC), the Fisheries Division, the Veterinary and Livestock Division, the Social Security Board, the Medical Benefits Scheme and the Labour Department. But each obliged population is counted in the tens or low hundreds, not thousands. Most workflows are annual licence renewals at a counter. Where a recurring filing exists (the monthly payroll levies), it is generic across all employers and already handled by accountants or by the payroll module of off-the-shelf accounting software. The least-bad idea is a done-for-you household-employer payroll service (score 2/10). It works only as one jurisdiction inside a multi-island OECS household-payroll service, and it needs a local partner.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Household Employer Payroll-Levy Service (as one OECS jurisdiction, not standalone)

**Industry:**
Households as employers (domestic workers, nannies, caregivers, gardeners); also villa owners and expatriate/CIP residents who employ staff

**Buyer:**
The household head, often a CIP investor or expatriate resident unfamiliar with local filings, or a villa property manager acting for an absentee owner

**Trigger / Why now:**
No new trigger. The obligation is long-standing: monthly Social Security, Medical Benefits and Education Levy returns and payment by the 15th. The only weak "why now" is the growing number of CIP-linked residents and villa owners who employ staff and want compliance done for them (unverified).

**Current workflow:**
1. The household registers as an employer with the Social Security Board and the MBS (forms at the counter).
2. Each month it calculates contributions on wages, up to the EC$6,500/month insurable ceiling.
3. It fills in separate returns and pays each body at its office or bank by the 15th.
4. Foreign workers also need an annual work-permit renewal at the Labour Department.

**Pain:**
Three bodies, monthly, all on paper. But the penalties seem rarely enforced against households, and many domestic arrangements are informal (inferred; no enforcement evidence found).

**Existing solutions:**
- Local accountants and bookkeepers who file for a monthly fee
- Villa and property management companies that already run staff payroll
- EOR / global payroll vendors that list Antigua (Rivermate, Playroll, HiveDesk), which are aimed at companies and too expensive for one nanny
- Doing it yourself at the counter

**Offline evidence:**
No household-payroll product or guide aimed at Antiguan households was found. Employer guidance comes only from EOR vendors' generic pages and the US SSA summary.

**Offline channel:**
CIP agents and the lawyers who process citizenship-by-investment files, villa property managers, and expatriate community groups. All need a local partner.

**Market count:**
No public count of household employers. Estimate: low thousands, of whom maybe a few hundred would pay.

**The gap:**
No affordable "nanny payroll" (an HomePay-style service) exists for OECS households. A software-only tool still leaves the payment and counter filing undone.

**Possible product:**
A done-for-you monthly service: the household enters hours and wages; the service calculates SS, MBS and Education Levy, produces the payslip and returns, and a local partner files and pays them. Built to be cloned for St Kitts, St Lucia, Grenada and Dominica.

**MVP:**
A spreadsheet-plus-WhatsApp service run with one local bookkeeper for 10 households.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
US$25–40/month per household (estimate). It is a service, not software; households would not pay for software alone.

**How to find first customers:**
Through CIP agents (licensed agent list from the Citizenship by Investment Unit) and villa management firms.

**Risks:**
- A tiny market, and low willingness to pay
- Weak enforcement gives households little reason to comply
- Needs a local partner to file and pay
- Accountants already offer this

**Kill condition:**
Fewer than 5 of 20 CIP agents or villa managers say their clients would pay US$25/month, or SS and MBS don't accept filings made by an agent for the household.

**Founder access:**
A non-local solo founder could not sell this without a local bookkeeper partner.

**Score:** 2/10 (Antigua alone); perhaps 3–4/10 as part of a multi-island OECS household-payroll service

## 3. Rejected

- **Taxi/minibus licensing helper.** The Vehicle and Road Traffic (Amendment) Act 2025 raised licence fees and confused operators (Antigua Observer, https://antiguaobserver.com/?p=308653 and https://antiguaobserver.com/?p=309199). But renewal is annual, it is handled through associations (UTC and others) and at the Transport Board counter, and the pain is about cost, not paperwork. The sector is getting two seats on the Transport Board (https://antiguaobserver.com/?p=320626), so the problem is being solved politically, not with software.
- **Heritage Quay vendor registration.** About 150 vendors register with SJDC (https://sjdcanu.com/vendors_mall); a one-off registration with no recurring filing.
- **Fisheries catch reporting.** The Fisheries Division samples the catch itself at 32 landing sites (FAO NPOA-IUU, https://www.fao.org/fi/static-media/FI/DOCUMENT/IPOAS/national/AntiguaAndBarbuda/NPOA_IUU.pdf; CIJN, https://www.cijn.org/antigua-and-barbuda-more-research-needed/). Fishers have no filing burden to sell into.
- **Livestock registration and strays.** Old statutes (FAOLEX, https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/ant43590.pdf), and the owners are smallholders with no money to spend.
- **Scrap metal register.** Couldn't confirm Antigua's law; there are very few dealers.
- **Domestic-worker work permits.** Annual, and already handled by lawyers and agents.

## 4. Method notes

- Regulator-first queries returned mostly UK or regional statutes (for example, the UK Scrap Metal Dealers Act) instead of Antiguan ones. Antigua's laws are hard to search; laws.gov.ag did not appear in results.
- The *Antigua Observer* was the most useful source for operator pain (transport). Expect the local press, not regulators, to be the evidence base in OECS microstates.
- FAO and FAOLEX cover the animal and fishery laws well but have no counts.
- No query produced an operator count from a register. Every count here is an estimate.
- For the OECS, the household-payroll pattern is the only one that might work across islands. Run it once as a cross-OECS track rather than per country.
