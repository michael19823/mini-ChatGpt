# Austria: offline-industries pass (as of 2026-10-05)

Scope: quiet, regulator-facing industries that the deep country pass (`research/countries/austria.md`) did not cover. That report already handles heating/AC databases (chimney sweeps, installers), posting of workers, asbestos, waste EDM, Pfand, 24-hour care, kindergartens, pharmacies, vets, funeral homes and pesticide records. None of those are repeated here.

I used 37 WebSearch calls, almost all in German. WebFetch was blocked, so the evidence comes from search-result summaries and official PDF titles, not from full-page reads. Austria is accessible to a non-local founder in legal terms (EU, SEPA). But every quiet industry below is rural, German-speaking and reached through Land authorities or chamber and association channels, so **any of these ideas needs a local, German-speaking partner to sell**.

Overall verdict: Austria's quiet industries are well covered by **free public or association systems**. Examples are DLS-Online, AMA databases, the Land hunting systems (JIS-Online, JAFAT), FinanzOnline EKA, the Land-funded OÖ water-cooperative service centre and the VIS bee register. Austria's main structural feature, nine Länder with their own rules, does create fragmentation, but mostly in low-revenue, volunteer-run or hobby segments. Nothing reaches the "build" bar. One idea (water cooperatives) is worth a few interviews.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: TWV compliance and member-admin kit for volunteer water cooperatives

**Industry:**  
Small drinking-water cooperatives and water associations (Wassergenossenschaften, Wasserinteressentschaften). Also small private suppliers that are food businesses (alpine huts, guesthouses with their own spring).

**Buyer:**  
The volunteer chair (Obmann) or treasurer (Kassier) of a cooperative supplying about 20–2,000 people. Also the certified Wassermeister where one exists.

**Trigger / Why now:**  
The EU Drinking Water Directive 2020/2184 was transposed by TWV amendments BGBl II 2024/57 (15 Feb 2024) and 2024/122 (16 May 2024):
- **§5a** brings in a risk assessment and risk management based on EN 15975-2. The first one is due by **12 Jan 2029** for suppliers above 100 m³/day or 500 people.
- **§5b** adds catchment risk assessment.
- New parameters (PFAS and others) apply from 2026.
- Lab results must be sent electronically to the authority's data system.
- Operators must inform consumers about non-compliance and about the return to normal operation.

In Styria, 45% of about 600 cooperatives (<10 m³/day) must send a member to one-day basic training, and 35% (10–100 m³/day) need a certified Wassermeister.

**Current workflow:**  
1. The Wassermeister or a volunteer walks the system (springs, reservoir) and notes readings in a paper logbook or Excel.
2. An accredited lab (e.g. AGES, hus-salzburg, Agrolab) takes samples according to the test plan and sends the findings to the cooperative and electronically to the Land.
3. The chair keeps the findings in a folder, informs members by notice board or letter, and handles any non-compliance by phone with the BH.
4. Once a year the treasurer reads meters (often members self-report), calculates Wasserzins in Excel or a billing program, and sends invoices.
5. For the risk assessment, the cooperative hires an engineering office or lab (hus-salzburg sells "Risikobewertung"), or asks the OÖ Land service centre.

**Pain:**  
Styria's TW newsletter states that new reporting obligations put a "disproportionate" burden on volunteer-run cooperatives compared with large municipal utilities. It asks for "clear guidelines, training and help with reporting obligations and electronic processes". Local press (Tips, Lasberg) reports fears that the new EU directive means up to a tenfold increase in testing and higher water prices. Direct complaints about software or double entry were **not found**.

**Existing solutions:**  
- Land OÖ "Wassergenossenschaftlicher Bau- und Servicedienst": free, Land-funded technical, legal and organisational help for OÖ WASSER members, plus a hotline.
- Styrian Land information events and newsletters.
- Labs and engineering offices: hus-salzburg (risk assessment), Mach & Partner ZT (digital tools for water supply).
- "Wassermeister" self-monitoring software (wassermeister.eu/.net; Austrian presence and pricing unverified), Igel Wassergeld (billing for associations and cooperatives), KISTERS, waterloo.io, DWA Hirthammer logbooks.
- Municipal finance software (k5/Gemdat) where the municipality runs the supply.
- Excel and paper folders.

**Offline evidence:**  
- Volunteer officers and tiny organisations (OÖ: 2,097 cooperatives serving about 200,000 people).
- Paper founding and statute templates from the Land.
- Support is delivered through a phone hotline and in-person information evenings (265 participants in Styria).
- No Austrian software review pages were found for this segment.

**Offline channel:**  
- The OÖ WASSER Genossenschaftsverband eGen (2,097 members) and its annual meetings.
- The Styrian Land Trinkwasser information events and newsletter list.
- ÖVGW and Wassermeister training courses (the basic course is mandatory for many).
- Accredited labs that already invoice every cooperative and could resell or bundle.
- Each Land's Wasserbuch (public water-rights register) as the prospect list.

**Market count:**  
>5,500 water suppliers in Austria (Land OÖ, Trinkwassertag 2017). Cooperatives: OÖ 2,097 (Tips 2025/26) and Styria 682 (Land Steiermark). Other Länder unverified. About 13% of the population is supplied by house wells and cooperative systems.

**The gap:**  
Nobody offers a cheap tool aimed at volunteers that keeps the TWV logbook, lab findings, test-plan reminders, consumer-notice templates, the risk-assessment worksheet (EN 15975-2 structure) and simple member billing in one place. Professional tools target utilities with staff, and the free help is advice, not a system.

**Possible product:**  
A mobile-first "Genossenschafts-Wasserbuch". It covers inspection checklists, a reservoir log, lab-PDF intake, deadlines (test plan, Wassermeister refresher), a member register with meter readings and Wasserzins invoices, and a guided risk-assessment template that the cooperative's engineer or lab finalises.

**MVP:**  
Logbook, lab-PDF archive with deadline reminders, member list and an annual invoice run, for OÖ cooperatives only.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
EUR 10–30/month per cooperative (estimate). A small cooperative's whole budget is the members' water fees. A per-cooperative licence sold through the umbrella association or a lab (EUR 100–300/year) is more realistic. Buyers would pay for **done-for-you help** (risk assessment by the lab, billing by the treasurer) more readily than for software.

**How to find first customers:**  
Through the OÖ WASSER umbrella association (ask to present at regional meetings), Land Wasserbuch entries and Styrian info-event lists. One accredited lab as reseller partner.

**Risks:**  
- The OÖ Land service centre or the association builds or licenses its own tool (the association is subsidised by the Land).
- Very low ARPU.
- Volunteers resist new tools.
- Smaller cooperatives (<100 m³/day) are outside the 2029 risk-assessment deadline, so the "why now" is weak for most of them.
- A local, German-speaking seller is required.

**Kill condition:**  
The OÖ WASSER association already provides members with a free digital logbook or billing tool. Or fewer than 3 of 10 interviewed chairs say they would pay at least EUR 100/year.

**Score:** 4/10 (pain 5, frequency 6, mandatory 8, fragmentation 5, competition 5, incumbent gap 5, accessibility 7, WTP 2, MVP 7, distribution 6). Founder access: needs a local partner.

### Opportunity: Maintenance-report router for small sewage plant (Kleinkläranlagen) service firms

**Industry:**  
Maintenance firms for small sewage plants (biological plants ≤50 PE). Also plant manufacturers that offer maintenance contracts.

**Buyer:**  
The owner or office manager of a regional maintenance firm (e.g. Bioklar-type firms in OÖ/NÖ, Energie AG OÖ's inspection service).

**Trigger / Why now:**  
There is no 2025–2026 trigger. The obligation is old and steady:
- Under §134 WRG, the operator must have an expert inspect the plant every ≤5 years and submit the report to the water authority.
- Maintenance reports plus effluent lab findings go to the "responsible authority" in a format that depends on the authority.
- NÖ requires Land accreditation for maintenance firms.

Germany standardised digital maintenance protocols (DiWa). No Austrian equivalent was found, so the "why now" is weak.

**Current workflow:**  
1. A technician visits the plant (once or twice a year), takes a sample and fills in a paper or app protocol.
2. The lab analyses the effluent sample.
3. The office merges the protocol and lab findings into a report and sends it to the plant owner and, by post or email, to the relevant BH. The format and recipient differ by Land and district.
4. The office tracks which owners are due for the 5-year §134 inspection and reminds them.

**Pain:**  
The firms describe sending reports to the authority "depending on the authority's rules" (bioklar.at). No complaints were found. Pain is **inferred, not evidenced**.

**Existing solutions:**  
- Firms' own forms plus email.
- German KKA tools and the DiWa format (German authorities only).
- Generic field-service software.
- Energie AG OÖ's own inspection service.

**Offline evidence:**  
- Paper protocols are signed on site.
- Postal or email submission to BHs.
- Owner courses run by the Land OÖ for operators of plants <50 PE.
- No Austrian software listings were found.

**Offline channel:**  
- ÖWAV training courses for plant operators and maintenance staff.
- The NÖ accredited-firm list.
- Plant manufacturers and dealers.
- Land OÖ owner courses.

**Market count:**  
About 27,500 plants ≤50 PE in Austria, 6,200 of them still mechanical-only (2016, BMLRT k2W study). The number of maintenance firms is **unknown** (estimate: under 150).

**The gap:**  
Nothing turns one maintenance visit plus a lab result into the correct report format for each BH and Land, and tracks the 5-yearly §134 due dates per plant.

**Possible product:**  
A field app plus office back-end. The technician fills in the protocol, the lab PDF is attached, and the system generates the right report per authority and keeps a due-date register per plant.

**MVP:**  
Protocol, owner report, BH email submission and a due-date register, for one Land (OÖ).

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
EUR 50–150/month per firm (estimate). A firm with 1,000 plants charges about EUR 150–300 per visit (unverified).

**How to find first customers:**  
NÖ accredited-firm list, Google Maps search for "Kläranlagen Wartung" by Land, plant manufacturers' dealer lists, ÖWAV course attendee networks.

**Risks:**  
Very small number of buyers. Firms may already use generic field-service tools. Authorities accept plain email PDFs, so the "router" adds little.

**Kill condition:**  
Interviews show that every authority accepts the same PDF by email. Or there are fewer than 50 firms nationally.

**Score:** 3/10 (pain 3, frequency 6, mandatory 8, fragmentation 6, competition 6, gap 4, accessibility 5, WTP 4, MVP 7, distribution 3). Founder access: needs a local partner.

The chambers of agriculture (LK OÖ, LK Kärnten) publish free guides. The farmer's tax-free allowance is 15–27 litres, so there is almost no money to spend on compliance. A yield calculator would be a free lead-magnet at best. ## 4. Method notes

What worked:
- German queries aimed at Land PDFs (land-oberoesterreich.gv.at forms, Styrian "TW Newsletter", tirol.gv.at Merkblätter).
- Chamber-of-agriculture (lko.at) guides.
- RIS/BGBl references.

These gave the obligations and counts quickly.

What failed:
- Market counts for niches (maintenance firms, drillers, Viehhändler). There are no public registers that searches can reach.
- Searches for operator complaints, which returned almost nothing.
- Product-name searches for niche Austrian software.

Austria's pattern is that the **Land or the association provides the free tool or service**, which is the main substitute in almost every quiet industry.
