# The Bahamas: offline-industries pass

**Search budget used:** 8 WebSearch calls (the cap set for this country). WebFetch was not used.
**Context:** a small market (about 400k people, estimate) spread across many islands, with most regulated activity in New Providence (Nassau) and Grand Bahama. The country report (`research/countries/bahamas.md`) already covers DNFBP AML, BICA turnover statements, lobster EU CATCH, NIB C10, STR tax and DMTT. Those are not repeated here.

**Bottom line:** this is a short report and the result is honest but thin. The quiet industries exist, and several have clear paper-based obligations. In almost every case, though, the operator count is tiny, and either the receiving body is a single counter (one Road Traffic franchise unit, one Straw Market Authority, one Department of Marine Resources) or the obligation is a once-a-year licence. That kills fragmentation and frequency. The one lead with a fresh 2025 trigger is the **Scale 8 domestic-worker work permit**, which now needs a Labour Certificate. Even that is a done-for-you service, not software, and it needs a local operator. I found **no opportunity a non-local solo software founder should build**.

## 2. Strongest opportunity (weak)

### Opportunity: Scale 8 domestic-worker permit renewal desk (Labour Certificate + NIB letter + Immigration filing)

**Industry:**
Households employing foreign domestic workers (maids, nannies, gardeners, handymen), plus the small immigration agents and attorneys who file for them.

**Buyer:**
Household employers in Nassau and the Family Islands, including second-home owners who are often abroad. The secondary buyer is small law firms and immigration agents that file Scale 8 permits in volume.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- From 1 September 2025, every Scale 8 work permit application, new or renewal, must include a valid Labour Certificate, and the certificate must be renewed every 2 years.
- This adds a second agency (Department of Labour) to an annual Immigration cycle that already needs proof of NIB contributions.
- The Scale 8 permit fee is reported at $2,000 a year, with a $200 non-refundable processing fee (Fragomen / Immigration fee scale). The standard processing time is 8–12 weeks, and there is a paid expedited option.

**Current workflow:**
1. The employer tracks the permit expiry date, usually from memory or a paper diary (assumption).
2. The employer applies to the Department of Labour for a Labour Certificate (from Sep 2025, every 2 years).
3. The employer gets an NIB letter confirming contributions are up to date. This requires the household to be registered as an employer (Form R1) and to be paying.
4. The employer assembles the Immigration renewal pack (forms, passport, photos, police record, medical, fees) and files it in person or through an attorney or agent.
5. The employer waits 8–12 weeks, or pays for expedited processing. A lapse leaves the worker out of status.

**Pain:**
- Three agencies, an annual deadline, and a $2,000+ fee at stake each year.
- A missed renewal or incomplete pack means the worker is out of status and the fee or time is lost.
- This pain is **inferred from the official requirements**. I found no complaints and no reports of delays after September 2025.

**Existing solutions:**
- Immigration attorneys and law firms (for example, Higgs & Johnson publishes employment and immigration guidance).
- Informal immigration "runners" and agents (unverified, but common in the region).
- Fragomen-type global firms, which serve corporate permits, not households.
- The Immigration Department's own forms and guidance, which are free.
- No household-employer software was found.

**Offline evidence:**
- Applications are filed through the Immigration Department's forms and offices.
- NIB registration starts with a paper Form R1 and an in-person ID check.
- There are no SaaS listings for Bahamian domestic employment.
- The buyers (households) have no online community to speak of.

**Offline channel:**
- Immigration agents and small law firms that already file Scale 8 permits (a white-label tracker or pack builder).
- Property managers and concierge firms serving second-home owners (for example in the Exumas, Abaco and Eleuthera).
- Church and community networks of the domestic workers themselves (unverified).

**Market count:**
Unknown. No published count of Scale 8 permits was found. An estimate is low thousands of permits a year. Kill or confirm this with an Immigration annual report or a parliamentary answer.

**The gap:**
No one tracks the three-agency cycle (Labour Certificate every 2 years, NIB letter, annual Immigration renewal) for the household. Agents do it per case, by hand.

**Possible product:**
A renewal desk: a calendar of each permit's Labour Certificate, NIB and Immigration dates, a document checklist per worker, and reminders to the employer. It would be sold mainly as a done-for-you filing service by a local agent, with the software as the agent's back office.

**MVP:**
A spreadsheet-grade tracker plus a WhatsApp/SMS reminder at 120, 90 and 60 days before expiry, with a document checklist PDF. It would be given to 3–5 immigration agents to test.

**Willingness to pay:**
Households would pay for a **done-for-you service** (perhaps $150–400 per renewal on top of government fees, estimate), not for software. Agents might pay $20–50 a month for a tracker (estimate). That is too little to matter.

**Founder access:**
A non-local solo founder **cannot** realistically sell this. It needs in-person filing in Nassau and relationships with the Immigration and Labour departments. A local agent is the natural operator.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
A $250 per-renewal service fee (estimate), or a $30-a-month tracker for agents (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**
- Immigration agents and attorneys (Bahamas Bar Association listings).
- Property-management firms on the Family Islands.

**Risks:**
- The government could digitise the process. The Bahamas has been moving immigration services online (unverified for Scale 8).
- Agents already do this.
- The market is very small.
- The service needs a local person.

**Kill condition:**
- The Scale 8 permit count is under about 2,000 a year.
- Or Immigration launches an online renewal portal with reminders.
- Or interviews show agents already track renewals well enough in a diary or Excel.

**Score:** 3/10. A real trigger and a recurring cycle, but it's a local service business with a tiny pool and no software margin.

## 3. Rejected

- **Scrap metal and second-hand dealer registers.** There is a real duty under the Pawnbrokers and Secondhand Dealers Act 2011 (ID checks, records, chain of custody, police inspection), and Customs inspects exports. But there are only a handful of dealers and no recent enforcement campaign found. The substitute is a paper ledger plus Customs' own inspection. Sources: https://tribune242.com/news/2013/mar/25/scrap-dealer-blasts-wild-calls-over-copper-ban, https://tribune242.com/news/2013/jun/25/cash-for-gold-enforce-the-existing-laws, https://www.tribune242.com/comments/cr/124/27821/
- **Jitney and taxi franchise compliance.** There is a single receiving body (the Road Traffic Department Franchise Unit, which works by counter and phone), and renewal is annual. The owner-drivers have no software budget. Sources: https://www.roadtraffic.gov.bs/?p=5085, https://roadtraffic.gov.bs/?p=3391
- **Straw Market vendor fees.** The obligation is fee payment to one Authority, and enforcement is by lockout. Only the Authority itself could buy a system, through government procurement. Sources: https://www.tribune242.com/comments/cr/124/15455/, https://www.tribune242.com/comments/cr/124/68195/, https://ewnews.com/?p=97622
- **Commercial fishing vessel licences.** The licence is annual, collected in person at the Department of Marine Resources. The fishermen are not buyers, and the exporters are covered in the country report. Source: https://bahamas.gov.bs/wps/wcm/connect/mof_content/internet/all+services/c/commercial+fishing+licence
- **Live-animal and meat import permits (BAHFSA).** These are already described as streamlined and automated, and customs brokers handle them. Source: https://agriexchange.apeda.gov.in/ImportRegulations/Bahamas.pdf
- **NIB registration for household employers.** NIB's free Employer Self-Service portal is the substitute (also rejected in the country report).

## 4. Method notes

- **Worked:** official Bahamian government sites (immigration.gov.bs, roadtraffic.gov.bs, bahamas.gov.bs service pages) for obligations, and Tribune242 / EWNews for enforcement history.
- **Didn't work:**
  - Searches for registers and counts. None of the receiving bodies publish licence lists, so every market count is an estimate.
  - Scrap and pawnbroker searches, which returned Barbados and Botswana material alongside 2011–2013 Bahamian news. There was nothing from 2024–2026.
- **Structural problem:** almost every obligation has a single national receiving body. The brief's "one job, many receiving authorities" pattern does not occur in a single-tier unitary microstate.
- **Budget:** 8 searches were enough to screen. Field trades (well drillers, septic, backflow) and licensed premises were not screened.
