# Bahrain: offline-industries pass

Budget: 20 WebSearch calls (19 used). Bahrain is a microstate of about 1.5M people, and its regulated
quiet trades are small in absolute numbers. This is a short report. Only one idea comes close to the
bar, and even that one is marginal on market size. Anything not tied to a source below is marked
**unverified** or **estimate**.

## 2. Strongest opportunity

### Opportunity: MO 105/2025 sale register and AML file for Bahraini jewellers

**Industry:**
Gold and jewellery retail and workshops (Gold City, Manama souq, Muharraq souq)

**Buyer:**
Owner of a single-branch jewellery shop who acts as their own compliance officer (allowed for
one-branch firms). Secondarily, the compliance officer at multi-branch chains.

**Trigger / Why now:**
MOIC Ministerial Order No. 105 of 2025 (published late 2025, in force the day after publication)
covers gold and jewellery licensees and auditors. It bans cash payments for any single deal of
BD 3,000 or more and requires a customer ID copy for cash purchases over BD 2,000. Every sale needs
a receipt with client details, value, item description and date, and records must be kept for at
least 5 years. Multi-branch firms must appoint a Bahraini management compliance officer, a deputy
and a compliance officer per branch, all with an approved AML certificate. MOIC enforces this
through inspection campaigns on gold markets.

**Current workflow:**
1. The sale is rung up on a jewellery POS or a handwritten receipt book. The gold rate and making
   charge are calculated by hand or in the POS.
2. For cash sales over BD 2,000, staff photocopy or phone-photograph the CPR/passport and file it.
3. The owner checks by eye whether a customer is splitting purchases to stay under thresholds.
4. Suspicious cases go to the FIU (Financial Intelligence Directorate) as an STR. The channel is
   unverified for jewellers.
5. Before or during a MOIC inspection, the owner assembles receipts, ID copies, an AML policy and
   training certificates from paper files.

**Pain:**
Paper ID copies and receipt books kept for 5 years, with inspection exposure. The receipt duty is
explicitly enforced: per MOIC, failing to issue a sales receipt leads to legal procedures under
the precious-metals laws. The threshold logic (BD 2,000 ID / BD 3,000 cash ban) is per transaction
and per customer, which is error-prone on paper. No complaint threads were found, as expected for
this sector.

**Existing solutions:**
- ZSolTech: a GCC jewellery ERP/POS with AML/KYC "embedded into every transaction" and Arabic
  support. It is the closest direct competitor, enterprise-leaning.
- Indian jewellery POS (Omunim/Online Munim, and others) used by Indian-owned shops in the GCC.
  These are GST-focused and unverified for Bahrain AML.
- AML consultancies: amlbahrain.com ("AML Defence Mechanism for Jewellers in Bahrain"), Fin-Soul
  (AML policy and procedure services), Themis/Aman (Bahrain AML checklist). They sell policies,
  training and outsourced reviews as a service.
- MOIC's own free jewellery-sector AML guide (PDF) and awareness sessions.
- Paper receipt books from stationers, plus a phone camera.

**Offline evidence:**
MOIC communicates through PDF guides, an email address (aml_awareness@moic.gov.bh) and inspection
campaigns. Hallmarking is done at the counter, with "representatives registration forms" for
shop staff. Shops are concentrated physically in a few souqs. No Bahrain-specific jeweller
software listings were found apart from ZSolTech.

**Offline channel:**
Walk the Manama Gold City and Muharraq souq; the shops are physically clustered. Go through the
MOIC Precious Metals and Gemstones directorate's representative registration and awareness
sessions. Partner with AML consultancies (amlbahrain.com, Fin-Soul) as the done-for-you layer.
Reach jewellery POS resellers in the souq.

**Market count:**
About 1,000 jewellery firms ("almost 900" firms equals about 90% compliance, per GDN). The number
of branches is higher but unknown.

**The gap:**
Small single-branch shops are unlikely to buy a full jewellery ERP just for AML. What they need is
an "AML register": a tablet or phone app beside their existing POS that captures the customer's
CPR (scan), applies the BD 2,000 and BD 3,000 rules, flags aggregation by the same customer,
prints a compliant receipt, and produces a 5-year inspection-ready file and an annual risk
self-assessment.

**Possible product:**
A bilingual (Arabic/English) per-sale AML register and receipt app for jewellers, with an
inspection-pack export. Sold bundled with a consultancy that supplies the policy and training
certificate.

**MVP:**
A mobile web app with: an ID capture form, threshold rules from MO 105/2025, a receipt PDF with
the mandated fields, a customer history search, and an "inspection file" PDF export. There is no
integration with MOIC or the FIU.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
Estimate: BD 10–20/month per shop (about USD 27–53). With a consultancy bundle (policy, training,
annual review), BD 300–600/year. The buyer will probably pay for done-for-you compliance more
readily than for software alone.

**How to find first customers:**
Physical walk-ins at Gold City; MOIC awareness sessions; consultancy partners; jewellery POS
resellers.

**Risks:**
Small total market (about 1,000 × BD 15 × 12 is roughly BD 180k/year at 100% share). ZSolTech and
POS vendors can add the same fields. MOIC may launch its own e-register. Selling to souq owners
needs Arabic and in-person trust, so a **non-local solo founder would need a local partner**.
Expansion to Kuwait (which banned cash gold deals in Nov 2025), Qatar and UAE DPMS is the real
upside, but that turns it into a GCC product competing with UAE AML vendors (amluae.com and
others).

**Kill condition:**
Shops say their current POS already prints the required fields and stores ID scans. Or MOIC
inspectors do not check per-sale records in practice. Or MOIC publishes a free e-register.

**Score:** 4.5/10
(Pain 5, frequency 8 per sale, mandatory 8, fragmentation 3, competition 5, incumbent gap 5,
buyer access 7 because of physical clustering, willingness to pay 4 because it is service-led,
MVP 8, distribution 5 because it is offline but local-dependent.)

## 3. Rejected

- **Household employers of domestic workers.** The Enhanced WPS (mandatory from February 2026,
  monthly wage files via the LMRA portal) explicitly excludes domestic workers. Contract
  registration is a one-off via LMRA or an agency. There is no recurring household filing.
  (Sources: KPMG/EY/Mondaq alerts on WPS 2.0; GDN on the LMRA domestic contract.)
- **Enhanced WPS for SMEs.** Not a quiet industry. It covers all private employers, and banks and
  payroll vendors file it.
- **Domestic-worker recruitment agencies.** Only 84 licensed, and LMRA holds the data.
- **Commercial fishermen.** 2,871 licences, but the 2025 rule is a crewing requirement with no
  filing. Low willingness to pay. Bahraini-only.
- **Livestock and slaughter.** Only 2 licensed abattoirs. There is no trader register duty.
- **Tobacco digital stamps.** NBR's own app covers the retailer side.
- **Scrap, second-hand, beekeepers, pest control.** No Bahrain obligation found within budget.
- **Driving instructors.** A public register exists, but there is no recurring reporting duty.

## 4. Method notes

What worked: English Gulf press (gdnonline.com, newsofbahrain.com) for counts and enforcement;
moic.gov.bh FAQ and PDF pages; Big-4 regulatory alerts (KPMG, EY) for labour triggers. What didn't
work: Arabic queries mostly returned Kuwaiti or Saudi results (for example, a "BD 3,000 ID" hit was
actually a Kuwaiti rule). Western seed categories (scrap, second-hand, beekeeping, pest control)
produced no Bahrain hits. In Bahrain, quiet trades are mostly regulated by MOIC, LMRA and SCE,
and the state increasingly runs the portal itself.
