# Bangladesh: Offline (quiet) industries pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. I made 28 WebSearch calls, and 5 of them were refused with "usage limit" errors. I resumed once at the coordinator's request and stopped after the next refusal, so this report is shorter than planned. WebFetch was not used. Anything I could not confirm from a search result is marked "unverified" or "estimate".

The accessibility caveat in `research/countries/bangladesh.md` still applies to everything below. SMEs can send only up to USD 3,000 a year abroad for business expenses, and prices are low and in BDT. So **every idea here needs a local partner or local entity**, and none can realistically be sold by a non-local solo founder.

### Opportunity: Fortnightly food-grain stock register for licensed rice mills and grain traders

**Industry:**
Rice milling (automatic and husking) and wholesale paddy, rice, wheat and flour trading.

**Buyer:**
The owner or manager of a licensed rice mill or aratdar (wholesale commission agent), especially millers who hold government procurement contracts.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The Food Products (Prevention of Prejudicial Activity) Act 2023 made hoarding punishable by up to life imprisonment ([UNB](https://unb.com.bd/category/Bangladesh/cabinet-clears-new-anti-hoarding-law-lifetime-jail-term-proposed-for-offenders/113513)).
- Food-grain licence rules require a stock report every 15 days. Without a licence, nobody may stock more than 1 t for trade ([Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/node/726388); [TRW](https://tahmidurrahman.com/?p=248855)).
- The Food Minister has said nobody may trade paddy or rice without a food-grain licence ([Daily Star Bangla](https://bangla.thedailystar.net/business/news-552141)). District food offices publish licence and stock forms as PDFs on their pages of the national portal, for example the Feni Sadar food office ([portal PDF](https://file-chittagong.portal.gov.bd/files/food.sadar.feni.gov.bd/page/bcc80904_219f_11e7_8f57_286ed488c766/4e40f3b5f35d5c2bf28d9cc18e70acab.pdf)). These forms suggest the process is paper-based (I could not read the PDF).
- The government has a record of revoking millers' licences, for example 2,000 millers over failed procurement supply ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/331729)).

**Current workflow:**
1. The mill keeps paddy purchase and rice sale books by hand or in Tally/Excel (estimate).
2. Every 15 days, the clerk adds up opening stock, purchases, milling output and sales by grain type.
3. The clerk fills in the food office's stock-report format and hand-delivers it to the upazila or district food controller (unverified whether any online channel exists).
4. During mobile-court or food-office inspections, the physical stock must match the register. If it doesn't, the owner risks hoarding charges.

**Pain:**
- The penalty is severe, under the 2023 Act.
- The report is due fortnightly.
- The physical stock must reconcile with the books.
- I found no direct operator complaint about the report itself (unverified).

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper ledgers (khata) from local stationers.
- Tally or Excel kept by the mill's accountant.
- Generic local inventory and POS apps (for example, Hishabee and other Bangla bookkeeping apps; I did not check whether any has a mill or grain module).
- The food office's own paper format.

**Offline evidence:**
- Filing goes to district and upazila food offices.
- I found no portal or software listing for "food-grain licence stock report".
- Enforcement is by mobile court and on-site inspection.

**Offline channel:**
- District rice-mill owners' associations (for example, the Bangladesh Auto Major and Husking Mill Owners Association; its exact name is unverified).
- Licence lists from district food controllers.
- Procurement-contract lists, since millers sign supply contracts each season.
- Phone and WhatsApp outreach in mill clusters such as Dinajpur, Naogaon and Kushtia.

**Market count:**
- ~19,000 rice mills ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/node/331729)). Dinajpur alone had 703 operational husking mills in 2025.
- Licensed grain traders: count not found.

**The gap:**
A Bangla-language stock book that turns purchases, milling yield and sales into the food office's fortnightly report, and shows an "inspection-ready" stock position against the licence limit.

**Possible product:**
A mobile Bangla app plus printable report. The clerk enters daily paddy-in, rice-out and sales, and the app prints the fortnightly form and a stock-limit warning.

**MVP:**
A daily entry screen, an auto-generated fortnightly report PDF in the food-office format, and a running stock-vs-limit display.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
BDT 500–1,500 a month per mill (estimate). The owner will more likely pay a local accountant or agent for a done-for-you report than pay for software.

**How to find first customers:**
Mill-owner associations in the north-west districts, food-office licence lists and procurement-contract lists.

**Risks:**
- The Directorate of Food may launch its own online stock reporting.
- Millers may prefer opacity, since hoarding is profitable.
- Low WTP.
- Needs a local Bangla-speaking seller.

**Kill condition:**
Ten millers say the fortnightly report takes their clerk under an hour, or the food office already accepts it online.

**Score:** 4/10. WTP is service-only or very low. A non-local founder could not sell it.

### Opportunity: Dealer ledger for government-appointed fertiliser dealers (Policy-2025)

**Industry:**
Subsidised fertiliser distribution: urea, TSP, DAP and MOP.

**Buyer:**
A union- or municipality-level fertiliser dealer who runs up to three ward retail centres.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The Integrated Fertiliser Dealer Appointment and Distribution Policy-2025 took effect on 16 Nov 2025.
- It merges BCIC and BADC dealers, abolishes sub-dealers and informal retailers, and gives each dealer three ward retail centres to run.
- Dealers now carry more stock-movement and sales accountability ([Daily Star](https://tds-images.thedailystar.net/business/news/govt-unifies-fertiliser-distribution-new-dealer-policy-4046556); [Bonik Barta](https://en.bonikbarta.com/business/T6Qf6QzFyYjxs7QC)).
- Dealers have protested the policy, and media report shortages under the new system ([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/agriculture/419479/fertilizer-shortages-deepen-as-new-dealer-system); [Desh Rupantor](https://www.deshrupantor.com/676292/)).

**Current workflow:**
1. The dealer lifts an allocation from the BCIC or BADC buffer godown.
2. The dealer moves stock to the warehouse and three ward centres.
3. Staff sell to farmers at the fixed price and record sales in registers (format unverified).
4. Upazila agriculture officers and the monitoring committee inspect, and the dealer reports stock and sales.

**Pain:**
- The main abuses named in the press are paper ones: off-market sales, fertiliser drawn under a dead dealer's name, and cross-district diversion ([Daily Star Bangla](https://bangla.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/agriculture/news-719101)).
- An honest dealer has to prove sales to farmers.
- Now that dealers run several centres, they must reconcile stock across them.

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper registers.
- DAE's own monitoring.
- The government's planned farmer smart-card and digital platform for ~2.75 crore farmers ([TBS Bangla](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangla/bangladesh/news-details-511976)).
- Generic bookkeeping apps.

**Offline evidence:**
- Sales are made at rural counters to farmers.
- Inspection is done by upazila officers.
- I found no dealer software listings.

**Offline channel:**
- Upazila agriculture offices.
- Fertiliser dealer associations (the Bangladesh Fertilizer Association exists; its dealer-side body is unverified).
- BCIC and BADC buffer godowns, where every dealer collects stock.

**Market count:**
~11,000 dealers (5,800 BCIC + 5,200 BADC, before consolidation) ([Daily Star](https://tds-images.thedailystar.net/business/news/govt-unifies-fertiliser-distribution-new-dealer-policy-4046556)).

**The gap:**
A multi-centre stock and sales ledger that produces whatever monthly report the upazila monitoring committee wants, with farmer-wise sale proof.

**Possible product:**
A Bangla app for dealer and ward-centre staff covering lifts, transfers between centres and farmer-wise sales, plus the inspection report.

**MVP:**
Allocation lift entry, transfers to three centres, a daily sales tally, and a printable stock statement.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
BDT 500–1,000 a month (estimate). The government may mandate its own tool instead.

**How to find first customers:**
Dealer lists published at upazila agriculture offices, and BCIC and BADC dealer lists.

**Risks:**
- The farmer smart card and a government system would make this redundant.
- The policy is contested and may be revised.
- Dealers may not want traceability.

**Kill condition:**
The Ministry of Agriculture rolls out a mandated dealer app or card-based point of sale, or the report format remains a single paper page that nobody checks.

**Score:** 3.5/10. This is software, but with low WTP. A local founder is required.

### Opportunity: Hong Kong Convention per-ship document pack for green ship-recycling yards

**Industry:**
Ship recycling at Sitakunda, Chattogram.

**Buyer:**
The HSE or compliance manager at a Hong Kong Convention-compliant (green) yard, or the yard owner.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The Hong Kong Convention entered into force on 26 Jun 2025.
- The Ministry of Industries told the BSRB to issue DASRs only after approving each yard's SRFP ([FE](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/home/ministry-moves-to-enforce-hong-kong-convention-in-ship-recycling?amp=true)).
- A 2026 accident killed 9 workers on a ship whose IHM recorded no hazards, even though 6 agencies had declared it safe ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/6e5ig0dpsv); [Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/news/shipbreaking-yards-green-paper-deadly-ground-4248676)).
- 12 worker deaths had been recorded by August 2026, so scrutiny is rising.

**Current workflow:**
1. The yard receives the ship's IHM and cash-buyer documents.
2. The yard or its consultant prepares the Ship Recycling Plan.
3. Approvals are obtained from the BSRB and other agencies (DoE, Explosives, Customs).
4. Gas-free and hot-work certificates, worker training records, waste manifests and the completion statement are kept, mostly on paper or in Excel (unverified).

**Pain:**
- Several agencies must approve each ship.
- Faulty IHM data can be fatal.
- An "approved on paper" status carries reputational risk for European shipowners selling to the yard.

**Existing solutions:**
- Classification societies and HKC consultants: ClassNK, IRClass and GMS-type cash-buyer services (unverified for Bangladesh specifically).
- Consultants who prepare SRFPs.
- Yard-internal Excel and paper.

**Offline evidence:**
- Filing goes to a single ministry board.
- I found no yard-management software listing.

**Offline channel:**
- The Bangladesh Ship Breakers and Recyclers Association (BSBRA).
- The BSRB list of compliant yards.
- Cash buyers who already deal with every yard.

**Market count:**
23 green yards (17 notified to the IMO) plus 8 on trial, so about 30 ([Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/news/shipbreaking-yards-green-paper-deadly-ground-4248676)).

**The gap:**
A per-ship evidence pack that links IHM line items to hot-work permits, gas tests, training records and waste disposal, so a yard can show a shipowner or the BSRB a full audit trail.

**Possible product:**
A per-ship compliance file with templates for SRP, permit-to-work, gas-free logs and waste manifests, and an export to the BSRB format.

**MVP:**
A permit-to-work and gas-free log tied to ship sections, plus a waste manifest log, with a PDF audit pack per ship.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
USD 300–1,000 per ship, or USD 300–800 a month per yard (estimate). Yards are export-linked, so they may be able to pay.

**How to find first customers:**
BSBRA membership, the IMO-notified yard list, and introductions through European shipowners' recycling teams.

**Risks:**
- About 30 buyers caps revenue at roughly USD 100–300k a year.
- Relationship-driven buyers.
- Consultants and class societies may bundle this service.
- Some yards prefer paper "compliance".

**Kill condition:**
The top 10 yards already use a class-society or consultant platform, or BSRB issues its own e-portal for SRP approvals.

**Score:** 4/10. A service plus software model is more realistic. A non-local founder could reach shipowners, but yard sales need a local presence.

## 3. Rejected

- **Domestic-worker payroll and contracts.** Domestic workers entered the Labour Act in Nov 2025, but there is no registration, payslip or contribution mechanism, and households won't pay.
- **Gold jewellers' stock and VAT register.** Gold Policy 2026 is still a draft, and fixed per-bhori VAT simplifies the calculation. Revisit once the policy is final and a register duty is confirmed.
- **LPG retailer licensing.** A one-off Explosives licence with no recurring data duty. The substitute is a trade licence plus non-compliance.
- **Easy-bike registration.** The policy is a draft, the buyers are poor owner-drivers, and the work is a one-time registration done at BRTA.
- **Money changers.** Only 235 licensees, a central-bank reporting format and AML exposure.
- **Brick-kiln compliance.** Illegality comes from location and fuel, not from paperwork. Enforcement means demolition.
- **Pesticide retailers and feed mills.** Annual licence only, with no enforced recurring register found (unverified).
- **Overseas recruiting agencies.** They looked like a fit for the "one job, many receiving authorities" pattern (BMET clearance plus destination-country visa portals). They are rejected because BMET's A-RMS, RAIMS and OEP are free and already used by about 85% of agencies, and commercial recruitment software such as Palazon exists.
- **Not screened** because the search budget ran out: cattle haats, Hajj agencies, private security firms, driving schools, and the rawhide and tannery trade. Potato cold storages were only partly screened, and their obligations are unverified. All of these are worth a follow-up pass.

## 4. Method notes

- Regulator-first queries worked well in English Bangladeshi news sources: TBS, FE, Daily Star, Dhaka Tribune and UNB. They gave me licence counts and enforcement events.
- Bangla queries returned useful news, for example on the fertiliser policy, but not official forms. dpp.gov.bd gazette PDFs turn up but were not readable without WebFetch.
- Official form-level detail (report formats, whether filing is online) could not be verified. Most "offline evidence" here is therefore inferred from news and marked unverified.
- District food-office pages on portal.gov.bd host the actual licence and stock-report forms. This is a useful pattern for finding official forms in Bangladesh, but the PDFs need WebFetch to read.
- The search budget was cut short by "usage limit" refusals: 28 calls in total, 5 of them refused.
