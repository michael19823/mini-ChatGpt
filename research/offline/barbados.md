# Barbados: Offline-industries pass

**Researcher:** offline-industries agent (Barbados) | **Date:** 2026-10-05 | **Search budget used:** 8/8

## Bottom line

Barbados (population about 280k, estimate) does have regulated quiet industries: precious-metal and
second-hand-metal dealers licensed by a Magistrate and registered with the Commissioner of Police,
farmers and produce dealers under a praedial-larceny receipt-book regime, licensed fishers and
fishing vessels, PSV (ZR route taxi) permit holders, and street vendors under the National Vending
Act 2021. In every case, though, the operator count is tiny (dozens to low thousands), the "system"
is a government-issued book, permit or database rather than something a vendor can plug into, and
willingness to pay is low. **No standalone Barbados opportunity scores above 3/10.** The only idea
worth keeping is a dealer transaction register sold across several Caribbean jurisdictions, with
Barbados as one of them. The 8-search budget allowed one query per group, so counts are mostly
estimates.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Caribbean dealer transaction register for precious-metal and second-hand-metal dealers (Barbados as one jurisdiction)

**Industry:**
Cash-for-gold buyers, jewellers that buy used gold, second-hand-metal and scrap dealers

**Buyer:**
Owner-operator of a licensed precious-metal or second-hand-metal dealership

**Trigger / Why now:**
No new 2026 trigger in Barbados. The Precious Metals and Second-Hand Metals Act and the Royal Barbados Police Force's registration notices are long-standing. The regional "why now" is Trinidad and Tobago's Scrap Metal Act 2022, which was proclaimed in part and followed by Legal Notice No. 59 of 2025. That makes a multi-island product plausible, with Barbados added as a small extra market.

**Current workflow:**
1. Register the business name or company, then apply to the district Magistrate for a licence.
2. Take a copy of the licence to Police Headquarters within 7 days.
3. For each purchase, record the seller and the item and check them against the Act's acceptance rules (the exact register fields were not verified), probably in a paper book.
4. Make records available for police inspection.

**Pain:**
Penalties under the Act are severe (fines of BBD 50,000 and 10 years' imprisonment for metal theft, plus penalties on dealers who accept property without following the guidelines). The government brought the law in to curb cash-for-gold crime.

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper register books (stationers; unverified)
- Generic pawn/POS software (e.g. US pawn-shop systems; Barbados fit unverified)
- Trinidad and Tobago's Ministry of Trade "Managefy" licensing app for scrap dealers (seen in search results; scope unverified)

**Offline evidence:**
Licensing goes through a Magistrate and the copy is carried to Police HQ in person. No Barbados software listings or forums for this trade were found.

**Offline channel:**
The Royal Barbados Police Force publishes registration notices; jewellery shops in Bridgetown can be visited in person; in Trinidad and Tobago, the Ministry of Trade's licensed-dealer list.

**Market count:**
Unknown for Barbados. Dozens of dealers (estimate). No public register was found.

**The gap:**
An ID-capture-plus-register app that produces the police-inspection record in each island's format.

**Possible product:**
A tablet app that captures the seller's ID, a photo of the item and its weight at the counter, enforces the holding and acceptance rules of each jurisdiction, and exports a police-ready register.

**MVP:**
A digital purchase book with ID photo, item photo and a printable or PDF register for one jurisdiction (Trinidad and Tobago first), then Barbados.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
USD 20–50/month per shop (estimate)

**How to find first customers:**
Walk-in visits to Bridgetown jewellers and gold buyers; police notices; Trinidad and Tobago licensing lists.

**Risks:**
A very small Barbados market; dealers who avoid records on purpose won't buy; a non-local founder can't sell in person, so this needs a local partner.

**Kill condition:**
Fewer than about 30 licensed dealers in Barbados, or the police not inspecting registers in practice.

**Score:** 3/10 (only as part of a multi-island product; a non-local solo founder would need a local partner)

**Willingness to pay:** low; a few larger jewellers might pay for software, but most would want nothing at all.

## 3. Rejected

- **Praedial-larceny digital receipt book:** this looked like a regulatory trigger (registration of everyone who buys or sells produce, Ministry-format receipt books, reintroduced Certificates of Purchase). It was killed because the Ministry sets the format and issues the books, police check paper receipts, and the buyers are small farmers and hucksters with little to spend. Any digital version would be a government or Barbados Agricultural Society project. Sources: https://gisbarbados.gov.bb/blog/new-legislation-to-protect-farmers-soon/ ; https://www.cbc.bb/news/local-news/police-issue-warning-on-proof-of-produce-amid-praedial-larceny-crackdown/ ; https://gisbarbados.gov.bb/blog/praedial-larceny-act-being-reviewed-purchase-certificates-to-be-reintroduced/
- **Household-employer NIS/PAYE service:** a real obligation, but the substitutes are the free NIS portal (https://www.nis.gov.bb/portal/), accountants and EOR providers. The market is small.
- **ZR / PSV permit holders:** no recurring filing; the conflict is over policy. Sources: https://www.ta.gov.bb/News/Latest/NOTICE-Sale-of-Permits.aspx ; https://nationnews.com/?p=186054
- **Street vendors under the National Vending Act:** the dispute is about licence renewal and location; there's no money in it. Source: https://barbadostoday.bb/2025/12/17/whither-the-vendors-act-four-years-on/
- **Fishers:** the Fisheries Division collects the data itself (LRS database, Access, Excel); the fleet is small. Sources: https://www.fao.org/4/y5402e/y5402e06.htm ; https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/s3/2025-08/Barbados-Final-2025-508.pdf
- **Livestock / pig farmers:** no recurring mandatory filing found. Source: https://barbadostoday.bb/2025/05/31/govt-rolls-out-plan-for-pork-industry/

## 4. Method notes

- Searches that worked were government press-release sites (gisbarbados.gov.bb) and local news (Nation, Barbados Today, CBC) combined with the name of the Act or a police notice. They surfaced the obligations quickly.
- A search for "scrap metal" plus "Barbados" returned only Trinidad and Tobago and UK results, so no separate Barbados scrap regime was found.
- No public licence registers or counts were found for any industry. All counts are estimates.
- Not researched: tattoo and barber health licences, food-handler permits, burial and cemetery rules, well and septic contractors.
