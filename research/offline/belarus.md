# Belarus: Offline-Industries Pass

**Date:** 2026-10-05 · **Searches used:** 8 of 8 · **Existing report:** `research/countries/belarus.md`

## Verdict: inaccessible market. No opportunities scored.

The country report already found that Belarus fails the accessibility gate, and nothing in this pass changes that:

- **EU sanctions:** since 1 July 2024, Regulation 765/2006 as amended by 2024/1865 bans supplying enterprise-management software, IT consultancy, and accounting, bookkeeping and tax-consulting services.
- **US export controls:** since September 2024, BIS requires a licence to export enterprise-management software to Belarus.
- **Payments:** Paddle and Lemon Squeezy do not support Belarus, and Belarusian bank cards are restricted for paying foreign online services.

A non-local solo founder cannot legally sell to, or collect payment from, the quiet industries below. A local Belarusian founder could, but the country reports assume a foreign founder. Quiet industries are also the worst case for remote selling, because their channels are associations, district executive committees and markets. All of those need someone on the ground who speaks Russian or Belarusian.

I still screened the quiet industries through regulator sources, so that the record exists if the sanctions picture changes.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

**None scored.** All candidates fail the accessibility gate (EU and US software and services restrictions, and no workable payment rails).

If the gate ever lifts, the only candidate worth revisiting is the **agro-ecotourism compliance kit** for the new Decree No. 275 Regulation (effective 20 Nov 2026). It would include a guest register, the documents the new Regulation requires, and the paperwork for state financial support. The market is about 1,400 homesteads, willingness to pay is likely only for a done-for-you service, and the channel would be district executive committees and the Belarusian agro-ecotourism association "Отдых в деревне" (Rest in the Village; existence not re-verified). Even without sanctions, it would score about 4/10 or lower on market size and willingness to pay.

## 3. Rejected

- **Agro-ecotourism Decree No. 275 kit:** a real trigger, but the market is small and poor, and it is inaccessible because of sanctions and payment rails.
- **Craft-levy artisans:** a flat Br10/month fee paid through bank or ERIP, with no recurring reporting.
- **Scrap-metal dealers:** state-ordered volumes and few licensed organisations, not small dealers.
- **Precious-metal scrap and pawnbrokers:** a closed list of about 32 organisations.
- **Market traders:** the substitutes are supplier paperwork and the 1C or EDI marking stack, which the country report already rejected.
- **Household employers:** no distinct obligation surfaced, and payroll and bookkeeping services fall under the EU ban.

## 4. Method notes

- Russian-language queries on official sources worked well: pravo.by, ilex.by, gb.by, etalonline.by and neg.by.
- Queries aimed at Belarus-specific animal and beekeeping rules returned Kazakhstan, Russia and Ukraine instead. Next time, add a filter for "Республика Беларусь" or site:pravo.by.
- Official statistics on licensee counts (scrap, markets) were not reachable through search snippets.
- The binding constraint is sanctions, not research depth, so I stopped at the 8-search budget.
