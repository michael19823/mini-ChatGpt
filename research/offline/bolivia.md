# Bolivia: quiet-industries pass

*Date: 2026-10-05. Method: 12 WebSearch calls (Spanish first), then stopped; the thin results did not justify the rest of the 18-call budget. WebFetch not used. Everything comes from search-result summaries, not full pages. Anything not confirmed there is marked unverified or estimate. Bolivia is a small, low-ticket, largely state-digitised market. The existing report (research/countries/bolivia.md) already covers DGSC chemicals, SIAT RCV, payroll, mineral traders and fuel stations, so those are not repeated.*

## 2. Strongest opportunities

No quiet industry in Bolivia reached a buildable level. One is worth keeping on a watch list.

### Opportunity: Veterinary antimicrobial e-prescription and sales ledger (watch list, not build)

**Industry:**
Agroveterinary shops and farm veterinarians selling antimicrobials.

**Buyer:**
Owner of an agroveterinaria, or a practising veterinarian who prescribes. Unverified: any numbers.

**Trigger / Why now:**
SENASAG's CC-RAM committee met in Jan 2025 to plan veterinary antimicrobial e-prescription and formed a working group on the technical and legal requirements. No issued rule, date or platform was found, so this is a forecast only.

**Current workflow:**
1. A farmer or vet requests a product, and any sale record is handled on paper, at the counter.
2. SENASAG registration covers the establishment and the product; no per-sale prescription register was found.
3. Under a future rule, the prescriber and seller would need to record each antimicrobial sale. (Hypothesis.)

**Pain:**
None evidenced yet; no enforcement or complaints found.

**Existing solutions:**
None found. SENASAG could build the e-prescription itself, as it did with Gran Paitití and the 111-service "PPADRON WEB" module (Publiagro, April 2024). Generic pharmacy or inventory software is the likely substitute (unverified).

**The gap:**
Only exists if SENASAG publishes a file or portal spec and leaves record-keeping to the private sector.

**Possible product:**
Counter-sale register with prescription capture and a report in SENASAG's format.

**MVP:**
Prescription-linked sales log with export. Cannot be built until the rule exists.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
US$10-20 per month per shop (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**
SENASAG-registered establishment lists, if public (unverified), and veterinary colleges. Local founder needed.

**Offline evidence:**
Veterinary retail is counter trade; no software listings surfaced in the searches.

**Offline channel:**
Veterinary college association, agricultural fairs (Agropecruz, per Publiagro), supplier distributors. None verified.

**Market count:**
Unknown.

**Risks:**
The rule may never be issued; the state may build the tool; very low price level.

**Kill condition:**
No SENASAG resolution by mid-2027, or a state-built free platform.

**Score:** 3/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Household employer payroll and CNS filing:** the obligation is real (Ley 2450; DS 4589; contract and booklet rule) and handled at counters with paper forms. But no enforcement, no count of employers, no business buyer, and a consumer price ceiling of a few dollars. Sources: https://www.lexivox.org/norms/BO-L-2450.xhtml, https://oig.cepal.org/sites/default/files/2021_dto4589_bol.pdf, https://eju.tv/?p=415756, https://www.gestora.bo/archivos/bajar?id=3087.
- **Livestock movement (GMA) and RUNSA:** state-run digital system, PDF accepted; operators reach it through SENASAG or accredited persons. Source: https://www.senasag.gob.bo/index.php/transparencia/regensa-en-linea/478-capitulo-3-15-movimiento-animal.
- **Beekeepers:** small, poor, logbook only. Source: https://www.senasag.gob.bo/images/areas_senasag/SA/sanidad_apicola/Registro%20de%20Establecimientos%20Apicolas.pdf.
- **Minibus and trufi operators:** syndicate-driven and filing-light. Source: https://www.lexivox.org/norms/BO-DS-28710.xhtml.
- **Scrap, pawn and second-hand dealers:** no register found.

## 4. Method notes

- Regulator-first searches in Spanish found laws and SENASAG documents but almost no counts: Bolivian regulators rarely publish registrant numbers, so the "market count" criterion usually failed.
- The search engine returned many Peru, Chile, Argentina and Spain results for generic Spanish queries; adding "Bolivia" plus agency names (SENASAG, ATT, CNS) helped.
- Searches on cemeteries, scrap dealers and pawnshops returned nothing Bolivian and relevant. Treat as unverified, not as proof of absence.
- Verdict: Bolivia has no quiet-industry opportunity above 3/10. It remains an add-on market for a Peru or Colombia product, as the existing report concludes.
