# Brunei: offline-industries pass

**Searches used:** 18 of 20, in English and Malay. WebFetch not used.

**Bottom line:** Brunei has about 450,000 people (estimate). Most quiet trades either do not exist at scale here or are absorbed by the state:
- Pawnbroking is mostly Islamic Ar-Rahnu run by banks.
- Customs runs through the BDNSW single window.
- Halal is centralised in MUIB/BKMH, which the country report already covers.
- Farms are heavily supported by the Department of Agriculture and Agrifood (DAA).

The one quiet segment with a public register, a mandatory record-keeping duty and a 2026 trigger is the **licensed employment agencies that recruit foreign domestic workers ("amah")**. That segment is roughly 100 firms. The best idea scores 3/10, and no quiet industry here meets the build threshold. Brunei could be an add-on market only, most plausibly for a tool built for Malaysian (Sabah/Sarawak) or Singaporean maid agencies. A non-local solo founder could reach the agencies by phone from the public list, but the deals would be relationship-driven and conducted in Malay. A local partner is advisable.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Amah-agency compliance file (worker lifecycle and statutory registers)

**Industry:**
Licensed employment agencies recruiting foreign domestic workers (Category A and C licences)

**Buyer:**
Owner/manager of a small Brunei employment agency, usually family-run, that places Indonesian and Filipino domestic workers with households

**Trigger / Why now:**
- From 1 Jan 2026, foreign nationals on selected passes, including domestic helpers on a Work Pass, must hold a medical plan on entry and while residing in Brunei. Takaful Brunei's quick guide, dated 14 Jan 2026, has a recommended "Essential Protection Bundle" for domestic helpers.
- This adds a policy-per-worker tracking item on top of the existing Foreign Worker Licence, the JobCentre clearance and TAP endorsement steps, and work-pass renewals.

**Current workflow:**
1. The household asks the agency for a helper. The agency files the Foreign Worker Licence form (LD1 new, LD4 additional, LD5 renewal; Malay-language PDFs) with the Labour Department.
2. The agency arranges the job order, the source-country side, the medical and the Immigration work pass. From 2026 it also arranges the mandatory medical plan.
3. The agency keeps statutory records in the prescribed Forms 4 and 5 (Employment Agencies Regulations 2004, s.24) and tracks fees against the B$1,500 cap.
4. Renewals, replacements ("ganti") and early terminations go through the same paper loop. Expiry dates are tracked in exercise books or Excel (assumption, unverified).

**Pain:**
- Many expiry dates per worker: licence, pass, passport and now the medical plan.
- Prescribed registers must be kept, and the Labour Department enforces against unlicensed agencies (Borneo Post).
- There is no direct evidence of agency complaints, so the pain is unverified.

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper Forms 4/5 and Excel.
- The Labour Department and Immigration portals and counters.
- Regional maid-agency software from Singapore and Malaysia. Names were not verified for Brunei, and none was found listing Brunei.
- Takaful/insurers' own enrolment for the medical plan.
- Generic HR/EOR vendors (Pebl, G-P) serve corporates, not amah agencies.

**Offline evidence:**
- The Labour Department publishes the agency list and all application forms as PDFs to be printed and submitted.
- The prescribed records are numbered paper forms.
- No Brunei agency software listing was found.

**Offline channel:**
- Phone or WhatsApp every agency on the Labour Department's published "Senarai Agensi Pekerjaan yang berdaftar" PDF.
- Partner with Takaful Brunei and other insurers selling the domestic-helper bundle, since they now touch every agency.

**Market count:**
- 104 registered agencies in 2017 (Labour Dept). The current list is on labour.gov.bn, but the count was not extracted.
- Only a subset hold Category A/C (unverified).

**The gap:**
A per-worker file holding every expiry (licence, pass, passport, medical plan), which prints the Form 4/5 registers and pre-fills the LD forms. This gap is unverified.

**Possible product:**
A lightweight agency back-office covering workers, households (employers), placements and expiry alerts, with exports of the statutory registers and pre-filled Labour Department forms.

**MVP:**
A worker roster with 5 date fields, 60/30-day WhatsApp/email reminders, and a printable Form 4/5 register.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
B$40–80 per agency per month (estimate). The buyer is more likely to pay for software plus setup (data entry from existing books) than for software alone.

**How to find first customers:**
- The Labour Department's registered-agency PDF list, worked by phone.
- Insurer partnerships.
- Selling the same product to Sabah/Sarawak agencies.

**Risks:**
- About 100 buyers at most.
- The government may build a single foreign-worker portal.
- Agencies may already use Malaysian or Singapore software.
- Deals are relationship-driven and in Malay.
- **Founder access:** a non-local founder can reach the agencies, but closing deals likely needs a local partner.

**Kill condition:**
Fewer than 40 active Category A/C agencies, or agencies say the Labour Department does not inspect Form 4/5 records.

**Score:** 3/10 (Brunei alone). Only worth it as an add-on to a Malaysia/Singapore maid-agency tool.

### Opportunity: Farm records pack for Brunei Pesticide-Free / Brunei GAP certification

**Industry:**
Small fruit and vegetable farms

**Buyer:**
Farm owner, often an agri-entrepreneur supported by DARe or DAA

**Trigger / Why now:**
The Brunei Pesticide-Free (BPF) Certification was launched by DAA in July 2026. It assesses planting practices, farm management, monitoring systems and post-harvest handling, and 4 farms were certified by August 2026. Brunei GAP has run since 2014.

**Current workflow:**
1. The farm applies to DAA.
2. It keeps records of inputs, planting and harvest, typically in notebooks or Excel (assumption).
3. DAA officers assess the farm on site.
4. The farm uses the label once certified.

**Pain:**
Record-keeping for the assessment. There is no evidence of failures.

**Existing solutions:**
- DAA guidance and officer support.
- DARe help with Brunei GAP certification.
- Generic farm-record apps and Malaysian MyGAP tools.

**Offline evidence:**
On-site officer assessments. No software listings were found.

**Offline channel:**
DAA's extension officers and DARe agri programmes.

**Market count:**
4 BPF-certified farms (Aug 2026). The number of Brunei GAP holders is unknown and probably tens (estimate).

**The gap:**
Audit-ready input, planting and harvest logs mapped to BPF/GAP checklists. The need is unverified.

**Possible product:**
A mobile farm diary that exports the BPF/GAP evidence file.

**MVP:**
A form-based daily log plus a PDF export per assessment criterion.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
B$10–20 per month (estimate). These farms are more likely to want it free and government-funded.

**How to find first customers:**
DAA's certified-farm announcements and DARe.

**Risks:**
- The schemes are voluntary.
- The government subsidises farms, so DAA would more likely build or give away a tool.
- **Founder access:** needs a local.

**Kill condition:**
DAA supplies its own record templates (likely).

**Score:** 1/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Slaughtering-centre licence and records:** Real BDFA licensing (Form G .docx, B$500/B$250 fees), but Phase I covers only large centres, which are likely fewer than 20 (estimate). Halal inspection is done by officers on site. Sources: https://bdfa.gov.bn/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Form-G-Application-form-for-Slaughtering-Centre-Licence.docx, https://bdfa.gov.bn/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Wholesome-meat-fees-regulations-2011.pdf
- **Pawnbrokers / gold buyers:** The Pawnbrokers Order 2002 exists, but the market is dominated by banks' Ar-Rahnu. Source: https://www.academia.edu/63349032/ (Ar-Rahnu in Brunei); https://www.agc.gov.bn/AGC%20Images/LOB/pdf/Chp.63.pdf
- **Jewellers' AML (DPMS):** Too few shops. Regional AML vendors (e.g. Arctic Intelligence) already cover Brunei. Source: https://arctic-intelligence.com/countries/compliance-brunei
- **Childcare centres:** 45 centres (2022), and multi-agency checks happen mainly at licensing. Source: https://thescoop.co/2022/01/05/child-care-centres-set-to-reopen-from-january-17/
- **Household employers of domestic workers (direct):** Households act through agencies. They are consumers, not software buyers.
- **Scrap metal, second-hand goods, taxis, tamu traders, pest control, small fishers:** No Brunei-specific recurring filing obligation was found. Where operators exist, they are few.

## 4. Method notes

- What worked: Malay queries pointing at Labour Department forms ("borang", "agensi pekerjaan berdaftar") and BDFA documents. These surfaced official PDFs and the public agency register.
- What failed: English queries for police dealer registers, hawker or taxi licensing and pesticide licensing. These returned Malaysian, NZ or HK results, which suggests these regimes either do not exist in Brunei or are not online.
- Parliament Q&A transcripts (hansard.queeniecy.com) look like a useful source for counts, but they could not be read without WebFetch.
