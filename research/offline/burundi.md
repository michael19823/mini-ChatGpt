# Burundi: Offline-Industries Pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. Budget: 8 WebSearch calls, all used. WebFetch was not used. Everything here comes from search-result snippets. Unverified items are marked *unverified* or *estimate*. This is a deliberately short report. Burundi is a very small, low-income market under tight forex controls (see `research/countries/burundi.md`), and most quiet industries here are informal, so their operators can't buy software.

## Bottom line

None of the quiet industries in Burundi clears the bar for a software business run by a non-local solo founder. Regulator records show three things:

- **Obligations exist on paper:**
  - cooperative production declarations to the OBM (Office Burundais des Mines et des Carrières);
  - livestock health certificates from the communal vet;
  - annual activity reports from religious bodies to the Ministry of the Interior.
- **Most of these obligations are filed by only a small share of the obliged bodies.** In 2021, about 300 of 7,460 associations filed annual reports with the Ministry of the Interior.
- **The buyers are informal, cash-poor or politically exposed.** At best, a local done-for-you compliance service could work. Two such service ideas are written up below, scored low.

## 2. Strongest opportunities (all weak)

### Opportunity: Annual-report and foreign-donation declaration service for religious bodies

**Industry:**  
Religious confessions, organisations and movements (mostly Pentecostal/evangelical churches; also mosques and smaller movements)

**Buyer:**  
Legal representative or secretary-general of a registered church or religious organisation, especially one with foreign donors.

**Trigger / Why now:**  
Law 1/30 of 16 Sep 2022 on religious confessions. The 24-month compliance window ended on 16 Sep 2024, so the law is now fully in force. Its requirements include:
- a baccalaureate-level legal representative;
- rules on church financing;
- an annual activity report with prescribed contents (arts. 42–43);
- a declaration to the Minister of any gifts or legacies from abroad.

The Ministry of the Interior has issued public compliance reminders, and ABP reported in May 2024 that religious bodies were being urged to adopt the framework. Practising after a suspension or registration denial carries 6 months to 5 years of prison plus a fine (US IRF report).

**Current workflow:**  
1. The church secretary compiles a paper or Word report of activities, members, finances and projects (*unverified detail*).  
2. Foreign-donation receipts are listed by hand and declared to the Ministry.  
3. The documents are filed in person at the Ministry of the Interior in Gitega/Bujumbura (*unverified*), with follow-up by phone and visits.

**Pain:**  
The sanctions are severe: suspension and criminal penalties. Many churches have weak administration; the minimum-education requirement for leaders suggests this. Filing compliance is historically very low: only about 300 of 7,460 associations filed reports in 2021.

**Existing solutions:**  
- Hand-written or Word reports.  
- Umbrella bodies: Catholic dioceses, the Anglican church, CNEB (Conseil National des Églises du Burundi) — *CNEB role in filing unverified*.  
- Local accountants and lawyers.  
- Donor-side reporting tools used by foreign partner churches/NGOs.

**Offline evidence:**  
Ministry communication goes through press releases and conferences (for example, the minister's conference with religious leaders on 29 Dec 2022). No online filing portal was found.

**Offline channel:**  
- Umbrella federations (CNEB, the Muslim community COMIBU — *unverified that either would cooperate*).  
- The Ministry of the Interior's own sensitisation meetings.  
- Diaspora and foreign partner churches that fund Burundian churches and want clean donation declarations.

**Market count:**  
636 registered religious bodies (2021; US State Dept IRF report citing the Ministry of the Interior). The number with foreign funding is *unknown*.

**The gap:**  
A structured annual-report template that matches arts. 42–43, plus a donation register that produces the foreign-gift declaration. The foreign partner could pay for it.

**Possible product:**  
A French/Kirundi template-and-register tool, sold mainly as a done-for-you service by a local agent. It produces the annual report and the foreign-donation declaration from one simple income/activity log.

**MVP:**  
- A printable Word/PDF template that mirrors arts. 42–43.  
- A spreadsheet donation register that auto-fills the declaration.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
USD 50–150 per church per year as a service (*estimate*), ideally paid by the foreign partner church or NGO.

**How to find first customers:**  
The Ministry of the Interior's list of approved religious bodies (*unverified that it is published*), umbrella federations, and foreign mission partners.

**Risks:**  
Political sensitivity: the government has suspended churches and closed some, and a foreign vendor handling church finance data would be scrutinised. The Ministry may change the format at will. The money is in small amounts and partly in BIF. A local founder is required.

**Kill condition:**  
The Ministry publishes a free standard form or portal, or umbrella federations already file for their members.

**Willingness to pay:**  
Only for a done-for-you service, not for software.

**Founder access:**  
Needs a local. A non-local solo founder could not sell this.

**Score:** 2/10

### Opportunity: Production and site register for artisanal mining cooperatives (OBM declaration pack)

**Industry:**  
Artisanal gold and 3T (tin, tantalum, tungsten) mining cooperatives

**Buyer:**  
Cooperative president or secretary; indirectly, licensed buying houses (comptoirs) and donor traceability projects.

**Trigger / Why now:**  
- Under the Mining Code (2013 code, art. 94; revised in 2018), approved cooperatives hold artisanal permits.  
- From 2022 artisanal gold activity resumed under new OBM measures: 30% of production goes to the State before any sale, and production is reported to the OBM, local administration and security services along the route to the central bank.  
- Burundi has adopted ITIE (EITI), iTSCi and OECD due-diligence standards.

**Current workflow:**  
1. Production is weighed on site and noted on paper, if at all.  
2. The cooperative reports to the OBM, local administration and security services, and the gold is escorted to the BRB.  
3. Separately, iTSCi tags and logbooks apply for 3T.

**Pain:**  
The Minamata ASGM action plan states that most sites keep no registers of production, personnel, accidents or mercury use. Missing records put the permit at risk (*inference*).

**Existing solutions:**  
- iTSCi (ITSCI programme, for 3T).  
- OBM paper forms.  
- Donor and NGO projects (ActionAid study, Minamata NAP).  
- Comptoirs' own records.

**Offline evidence:**  
Records are absent or on paper; physical escort to the BRB; no software vendors found.

**Offline channel:**  
OBM cooperative lists (*unverified that they are published*), comptoirs, and Minamata/UNEP and other donor programmes.

**Market count:**  
Not found. Approved cooperatives probably number in the low hundreds (*estimate*).

**The gap:**  
A simple register of production, personnel, accidents and mercury use that also produces the OBM declaration.

**Possible product:**  
An offline phone register that prints the OBM declaration and a mercury/safety log. It would be sold to donor projects as a tool for their cooperatives.

**MVP:**  
A printable register book plus a spreadsheet that produces the monthly declaration.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
Donor contract per programme (*estimate*); cooperatives themselves would not pay.

**How to find first customers:**  
The UNEP/Minamata ASGM programme and EITI/iTSCi implementers in Burundi.

**Risks:**  
Gold is a state- and security-controlled flow, and data visibility is politically sensitive. iTSCi is incumbent for 3T. Donor procurement cycles are slow.

**Kill condition:**  
The OBM or iTSCi already provides the registers, or donor programmes are inactive.

**Willingness to pay:**  
Donor-funded only.

**Founder access:**  
Needs a local partner and donor relationships.

**Score:** 2/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Livestock movement certificates:** issued by the communal vet; the trader pays per certificate and has no reason to buy software. The state is the only buyer.
- **Moto-taxi and bicycle-taxi operators:** banned from most of Bujumbura since March 2022 (about 20,000 vehicles affected).
- **Lake Tanganyika fishers:** enforced by seizing gear, not by filing; very poor buyers.
- **Domestic-worker employers:** INSS affiliation is formally mandatory, but there is no enforcement evidence and the sector is informal.
- **Veterinary product sellers and pharmacies (retail):** laws exist, but no enforced recurring register was found.
- **Forex bureaus:** licences revoked, so there is no market.

## 4. Method notes

- **What worked:** French queries naming the law or the regulator (OBM, Ministry of the Interior, "loi confessions religieuses 2022"). They surfaced local press (Iwacu, ABP, Le Renouveau, Jimbere) and legal databases (FAOLEX, AMPEID).
- **Best source for counts:** US State Dept religious-freedom reports, which cite Ministry of the Interior registry numbers.
- **What didn't work:** queries for 2025–26 registers or licence lists. Burundian regulators publish almost nothing online, so operator counts were mostly unobtainable.
- **Not tried:** Kirundi queries, because of the 8-call budget.
