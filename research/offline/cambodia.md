# Cambodia: offline (quiet) industries pass

Researched 2026-10-05. I used 14 WebSearch calls of the 18 allowed (7 English first pass, then 3 Khmer-language and 4 regulator-first English follow-ups at the coordinator's request). The Khmer queries returned almost nothing relevant (mostly irrelevant English results), so Khmer coverage is still weak. WebFetch was not used. Counts and facts I could not source are marked "unverified" or "estimate".

**Headline:** I found no quiet industry in Cambodia that clears the bar. The registers that exist (MLVT, MAFF, MoH, NBC) are mostly one-off licences, held at a ministry counter, with no recurring structured filing. Searches turned up no public operator counts, no enforcement campaigns tied to recurring paper reports, and no recent regulatory triggers for these groups. The main recurring-filing triggers in the country (the 2025 MLVT payroll and labour Prakas, GDT e-filing) already sit in the existing country report and hit larger, louder buyers.

**Founder access:** a non-local solo founder would need a Khmer-speaking partner for every sector below. Most of these operators are informal or micro-scale, so phone and in-person selling in Khmer would be required.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

None. I cannot recommend any idea from this pass with a straight face. Every candidate failed on at least two of these: no recurring filing, no countable register, no evidence of paper pain, buyer too informal or too few. The existing country report's three opportunities (LaCMS/NSSF/GDT payroll pack, overtime and contractor tracker, GDT filing workbench) remain the best Cambodian leads, but they target formal employers and accountants, not quiet industries.

The one item worth a follow-up, with low confidence (about 3/10 if it held up):
- **Animal-movement and slaughterhouse permits (MAFF Prakas 178 and 226).** If interviews with a few pig and cattle traders or slaughterhouse owners showed per-movement permits are done on paper at provincial offices at volume, it could resemble a "one job, many authorities" workflow. I found no evidence of this. Buyer count, volume and willingness to pay are unverified. Distribution would be through MAFF provincial offices, which I cannot name from a source.

## 3. Rejected

- Recruitment agencies and domestic work: licence only, long renewal cycle, small count.
- Moto-taxi / tuk-tuk: informal, one-off registration, low WTP.
- Money changers and gold shops: no register or count found.
- Agro-input dealers: one-off registrations, no count.
- Rice millers and exporters: too few, large, already on the national single window.
- Private clinics and pharmacies: licensing and advertising enforcement, not recurring reporting.

## 4. Method notes

- English searches on "licence" and "register" returned law-firm summaries and old news, not registers or operator counts. Cambodia's regulators publish few lists in indexed form.
- The Cambodia National Trade Repository (cambodiantr.gov.kh) and MAFF's file server (serv.maff.gov.kh) hold procedure descriptions and forms. They are the right next place to look, but they were only surfaced as search result titles, not read in detail.
- Khmer queries (MAFF animal permits and slaughterhouses, MLVT labour-contractor enforcement, gold and used-vehicle dealer registers) returned only one MAFF Prakas 415 listing at library.ncdd.gov.kh (not read) and nothing on police dealer registers or enforcement. The search engine handles Khmer poorly, so this is not proof that nothing exists.
- Regulator-first English queries found Prakas 117 (Ministry of Commerce, effective 8 Jan 2026) and a nationwide scrap-yard inspection order, neither of which makes a quiet-industry opportunity. I did not find any police dealer register, scrap or gold-dealer reporting obligation, trade association member directory or paper-register supplier for any screened group.
- Not done: reading cambodiantr.gov.kh and library.ncdd.gov.kh pages directly (WebFetch not allowed), association searches, and supplier searches. A Khmer-speaking researcher should do these before ruling the country out completely.
- I stopped at 14 of 18 searches because the remaining ideas were unlikely to return register data.
