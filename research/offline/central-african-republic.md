# Central African Republic: offline-industries pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. Search budget: 8 WebSearch calls, all used. Languages: French, with no English results that mattered. Sango-language sources were not searchable.

## Bottom line

**No quiet-industry opportunity reaches a buildable score.** CAR regulators publish almost nothing online: no gazette search, no licensing registers, no downloadable forms. What leaks out comes through local press (ACAP, Oubangui Médias, Corbeau News, Radio Ndeke Luka) and old FAO, IPIS and Kimberley Process studies. Every quiet industry checked has at least one of three problems. Some are run by state monopolies or by networks with armed-group or security-force links (mining, cattle, moto-taxis). Some have only tens of formal operators (pharmacies). Some have no enforced recurring filing at all (domestic workers, butchers). The one weak candidate below is included to show what the best of a poor set looks like. It is not a recommendation.

This agrees with the existing country report: serve CAR only as an add-on to a Cameroon or CEMAC product, if at all.

## 2. Strongest opportunity (weak)

### Opportunity: Moto-taxi union member and papers register (Bangui)

**Industry:**
Informal transport: moto-taxis

**Buyer:**
The leadership of a moto-taxi union (syndicat des taxis-motos), which sits at the Ministry of Transport moto counter. The alternative buyer is an owner of a small fleet of 5–20 bikes rented out daily.

**Trigger / Why now:**
Repeated control campaigns by the Ministry of Transport. The 2023 operation confiscated bikes with no administrative papers, and the Directorate General of Transport called it a success. Circulation bans have been imposed for security reasons. There is no dated 2025–2026 regulation. The search found no new obligation.

**Current workflow:**
1. The owner collects papers (customs clearance, tax, registration, insurance, union card) at a shared moto counter.
2. The union keeps member lists and dues on paper or in notebooks (assumption, unverified).
3. During a roadside control, missing papers lead to confiscation, and the owner has to retrieve the bike in person.

**Pain:**
Confiscation means lost daily income. Papers expire and are kept physically.

**Existing solutions:**
- The paper union card and the counter itself
- Union officials and intermediaries ("démarcheurs") who chase papers for a fee (assumption)
- WhatsApp groups (assumption)

**Offline evidence:**
Filing happens only at a physical counter with officials from four agencies present. No portal or software listing was found.

**Offline channel:**
Union offices and the Ministry of Transport moto counter in Bangui. This needs a local partner on the ground.

**Market count:**
About 38,000 moto-taxis in Bangui (press estimate, unverified). The number of unions is unknown.

**The gap:**
There is no shared expiry tracker for papers or dues. But the expiry dates the state actually enforces are informal, and most bikes reportedly belong to uniformed personnel, who are not exposed to confiscation in the same way.

**Possible product:**
An SMS/USSD reminder service plus a union-side member register that records paper expiry dates and dues payments, with payment by Orange Money.

**MVP:**
A spreadsheet-backed SMS reminder for the members of one union.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
XAF 200–500 per rider per month, collected by the union (estimate). That is under €1 per rider. Any revenue would come from volume and the union's cooperation, and this would be sold as a service, not as software.

**How to find first customers:**
In-person contact with union leaders at the Bangui moto counter, through a local partner.

**Risks:**
- Security
- The political and uniformed-personnel ownership structure
- Unions might use the register for extraction
- Mobile money margins
- A non-local founder cannot sell this

**Kill condition:**
The unions do not track expiry or dues at all, or the state launches its own registration drive or digitisation (as Senegal and Congo have done regionally).

**Score:** 2/10. Founder access needs a local. Willingness to pay is near zero, and this would be a service rather than software.

## 3. Rejected

- **Diamond/gold collector notebook digitisation:** the paper cahier de collecteur is mandatory and there were about 350 licensed collectors (IPIS, 2011–12). However, the 2024 Mining Code moves collection to GEMINCA-employed buyers, and the state plans its own electronic permit/GIS system (Agence Ecofin). The state is the substitute, and sanctions and governance risk is prohibitive. Sources: https://ipisresearch.be/wp-content/uploads/2013/08/20130813_CARFRENCH.pdf, https://www.radiondekeluka.org/68806-centrafrique-apres-des-mois-dattente-le-nouveau-code-minier-enfin-adopte-par-lassemblee-nationale, https://www.agenceecofin.com/actualites/0605-128131-la-rca-mise-sur-la-gouvernance-numerique-pour-transformer-son-secteur-minier, https://www.kpcivilsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/KPCSC_Grassroots_research_CAR.pdf
- **Livestock passport and transhumance certificate tooling:** the obligation is real (FAO), but the trade is a conflict economy. Sources: https://www.crisisgroup.org/fr/rpt/africa/central-africa/central-african-republic-chad/317-violence-et-transhumance-en-centrafrique-le-temps-dagir, https://corbeaunews-centrafrique.org/centrafrique-la-commercialisation-du-betail-alimente-le-conflit/, https://www.fao.org/4/v4440t/v4440T0p.htm
- **Pharmacy and depot compliance:** Bangui officines are capped at about 20. The December 2025 crackdown hit informal "mini pharma" sellers, who are not software buyers. Sources: https://acap-rca.com/2025/12/04/le-collectif-des-vendeurs-des-produits-pharmaceutiques-denonce-la-fermeture-de-leurs-officines/, https://www.sangonet.com/afriqg/PAFF/Dic/santC/medicam-gachis-rca.html
- **Butchers and abattoirs, household employers, second-hand dealers:** no enforced recurring filing was found.

## 4. Method notes

- French local-press queries ("Centrafrique" + sector + "agrément" / "contrôle") were the only productive pattern. The useful sources were ACAP, Oubangui Médias, Corbeau News, Radio Ndeke Luka, and IPIS/FAO studies.
- Queries for registers, forms and "2026" returned nothing CAR-specific. Search engines filled the gap with France, Morocco, Senegal and Tunisia. CAR has no searchable gazette or licensing lists.
- Counts come from old NGO studies or press estimates. Treat them all as unverified.
