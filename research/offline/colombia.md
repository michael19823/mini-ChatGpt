Date: 2026-10-05. Scope: industries whose operators rarely post online, found through regulators, registers and official reports. This does not repeat the opportunities in `research/countries/colombia.md` (RNDC shipper side, RDA/IHCE, ICA/ReteICA, gas OIAs, glosas).

## 2. Opportunities

### Opportunity: SINIGAN V6 back-office for livestock auctions and cattle traders ("one sale, every guía and bono")

**Industry:**
Cattle trade: livestock auctions (subastas ganaderas), commission agents and traders (comerciantes de ganado), feedlots buying across municipalities.

**Buyer:**
The operations or admin manager of a subasta ganadera (Asosubastas members). Secondary buyers are cattle traders who move lots weekly, and the agricultural-supply stores or ganadero committees that already process guías for farmers.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- ICA replaced SINIGAN V5 with **SINIGAN V6**.
  - Users had to register by 15-Jan-2026.
  - V5 stopped on 1-Feb-2026.
  - The first V6 movement guide was issued on 20-Apr-2026, after ICA extended the deadlines.
- V6 makes digital registration mandatory, uses individual animal records, and attaches a **bono de venta** to every change of ownership.
- The search tool reports that V6 interoperates with forest-monitoring systems in line with **Ley 2585 de 2026 on deforestation-free livestock**. That is unverified: my search on the law was refused.

**Current workflow:**
1. The farmer (or a helper) logs into SINIGAN V6 with their ID and the farm's RUV (vaccination record). They check that the farm and its inventory migrated correctly from V5.
2. For every movement to an auction, slaughterhouse or another farm, someone requests a GSMI. Requirements: the farm registered (form 3-101), vaccination cycle up to date, **ear-tag numbers of each animal**, and a transporter already registered in SINIGAN. This is done in the app, the web or in person at an ICA office (COP 11,450 in person).
3. At the auction each lot changes hands. A bono de venta must be registered for each buyer (COP 5,700 virtual, COP 8,800 in person). A new guía is then needed to move the animals out.
4. When the platform fails, ICA issues handwritten contingency guides (Santander). These later have to be reconciled into the system.
5. Inventory mismatches (migration errors, lost animals, blocked users) are fixed through ICA offices.

**Pain:**
- Asosubastas reported operations down about **40%** in the first 3 weeks: about **34,000 head** not moved, an impact of about **COP 73,000 million**.
- Fedegán publicly blamed ICA for failures in the movement module.
- Press reports describe:
  - user blocks, inventory loss and record inconsistencies;
  - payments not reflected;
  - 60% of cattle in Antioquia not moving;
  - complaints from ganaderos in Córdoba and Arauca.
- Failure means animals can't legally move or be sold.

**Existing solutions:**
- ICA's own free SINIGAN V6 app and web (the mandatory channel).
- ICA offices (in-person guías).
- Farm-management apps that keep herd records (several are advertised on Contexto Ganadero; I didn't check whether they integrate with V6).
- Identigan publishes SINIGAN V6 how-to guides. It is a vendor of identification products, and whether it has a software product is unverified.
- Auctions' in-house auction software, not checked.
- Manual helpers and digitadores at auctions and committees.

**Offline evidence:**
- Handwritten contingency guides.
- In-person counter fees published by ICA.
- Rural connectivity problems cited by guilds.
- The users are older ranchers. The guild press (Contexto Ganadero, El Meridiano, La Voz del Cinaruco) is the main record of pain, not forums.

**Offline channel:**
- **Asosubastas**, which already works directly with ICA on auction guías.
- Fedegán / FNG's vaccination network (which visits about 600 k farms each cycle).
- Departmental ganadero committees.
- The agricultural-supply stores near auctions.
- WhatsApp groups of auction buyers. Auctions publish their weekly calendars and phone numbers.

**Market count:**
- 638,941 cattle farms with 29.19 M head (2024).
- 118,559 SINIGAN V6 users.
- The number of auctions isn't public in what I found (**unverified; estimate: dozens to low hundreds of auction yards**).
- The number of cattle traders isn't known.

**The gap:**
- ICA's tool is a per-user transactional portal. It doesn't help an **auction or trader handling many owners' animals in one day**. The missing pieces:
  - pre-checking each lot before the auction (farm valid, vaccination current, ear tags present in the seller's V6 inventory, transporter registered);
  - generating the list of bonos and outgoing guías per buyer;
  - tracking contingency (paper) guides until they are regularised;
  - keeping an evidence file for disputes.
- I found no public API, so V6 integration would be assisted-manual or browser automation. That is the main uncertainty.

**Possible product:**
A lot-centric workspace for auctions and traders:
- Import the catalogue: seller, ear tags, buyer.
- Run pre-validation checklists.
- Produce, per buyer, the exact set of bonos and guías to register, with the data ready to paste or fill.
- Track each transaction's status, including paper contingency guides, until it is reconciled in SINIGAN.

**MVP:**
A web app plus WhatsApp intake for one auction:
- Upload the auction catalogue (Excel).
- Per-lot checklist (RUV, vaccination cycle, ear tags, transporter registration).
- A print/PDF "dossier" per buyer.
- A queue of pending bonos and guías with status.
- No V6 integration at first: it's a data-preparation layer.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- COP 600k–1.5 M per month per auction (about USD 150–350), or about COP 1,000–2,000 per head processed.
- For traders, COP 100–200k per month.
- Willingness to pay: auctions would pay for software if it cuts lost sessions. Small traders would more likely pay for a **done-for-you guía service** (gestoría), so expect service plus software.

**How to find first customers:**
- Asosubastas' member list and each auction's published weekly calendar.
- Introduction through departmental ganadero committees.
- Visit auction days in person (Antioquia, Córdoba, Santander, Meta, Casanare).

**Risks:**
- No API, and ICA could restrict automation.
- ICA may stabilise V6 so the acute pain fades.
- Auctions may already have in-house systems.
- Rural trust needs a **local founder or partner**; a non-local solo founder would struggle.
- Ley 2585 obligations are unverified.

**Kill condition:**
Kill the idea if any of these holds:
- 5 auction managers interviewed say V6 is now stable and their staff handle bonos and guías in under an hour per auction day;
- Asosubastas or Fedegán already provides members with a lot-preparation tool;
- ICA forbids third-party preparation or automation.

**Score:** 6/10 (Pain 8, Frequency 9, Mandatory 10, Fragmentation 3, Competition 6, Incumbent gap 6, Buyer access 6, WTP 5, MVP 7, Distribution 6. Founder access: needs a local.)

### Opportunity: Compliance back-office for recycler organizations (ORO): SUI monthly tons, ECA weighing and REP evidence

**Industry:**
Waste pickers' organizations that provide the "aprovechamiento" activity of the public cleaning service.

**Buyer:**
The legal representative or administrator of a recycler organization (asociación / cooperativa de recicladores) registered in RUPS, often with an external accountant or adviser.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- **Decreto 1381/2024** gives recycler organizations 15-year exclusivity for aprovechamiento and sets a progressive regularisation regime.
- **Decreto 670/2025** adds funding sources beyond the tariff.
- SSPD reporting resolutions continue, for example Res. 20251000182585 (aseo/SUI), which required retroactive reporting by 30-Jun-2025.
- Revenue (the aprovechamiento tariff paid through the cleaning bill) depends on the reported tons.

**Current workflow:**
1. Recyclers bring material to the ECA, where it is weighed and classified. Weights are usually logged on paper or in Excel.
2. Sales to buyers and industry are invoiced (e-invoice), and the organization keeps the sale support documents.
3. In the first 3 business days of every month the organization uploads the tons to the SUI. Superservicios publishes the figures, and these drive the tariff.
4. A mass balance between tons weighed, tons sold and tons reported is kept for audits and for the Contraloría.
5. Separately, packaging producers' REP plans (Res. 1342/2020) need certificates. These are issued by the **transformer** (Annex I format), using traceability data that ultimately comes from the recyclers.

**Pain:**
- Superservicios' sector reports and its 2026 prioritisation report cover inconsistencies in reported data and supports.
- Organizations need paid advisers.
- Wrong reports put tariff revenue and their status as a provider at risk.

**Existing solutions:**
- **Genesis Traza**: built with recycler associations in Bogotá. It centralises SUI uploads, mass balance, e-invoicing and Contraloría reports. This is a direct competitor.
- Ciclos: a circular-economy platform listed on MIT Solve; I didn't confirm its scope.
- cieTrade: an international recycling ERP, too enterprise for this buyer.
- E-invoicing tools (Alegra and others).
- Excel plus a paid adviser.

**Offline evidence:**
- Members are waste pickers, many with low digital literacy.
- Organizations depend on advisers and paper weigh tickets.
- Superservicios publishes guidance on identifying the recycler population and characterising the organizations.

**Offline channel:**
- National and city recycler associations (e.g., Bogotá's associations, the ARB; names unverified).
- Superservicios' formalisation workshops.
- Municipal PGIRS (waste-plan) offices.
- The advisers who already file for several organizations: sell to one adviser, reach 10 organizations.

**Market count:**
- 1,215 ORO in the formalization regime plus 130 not, with 76,707 recyclers reported (SSPD report for 2024).
- 2,820 RUPS providers engaged solely in aprovechamiento (Oct 2025).

**The gap:**
- Unverified whether Genesis Traza covers it: **REP-certificate evidence packs for buyers and transformers** (which ton came from which route or recycler).
- Mobile weighing capture at small ECAs outside Bogotá.
- If Genesis already covers these, there is no gap.

**Possible product:**
- A mobile weigh-ticket app for ECAs that feeds:
  - the monthly SUI file;
  - the mass balance;
  - a per-sale traceability pack that transformers and REP plans can use.
- Priced for small organizations outside Bogotá.

**MVP:**
- Weigh-ticket capture (by recycler and material).
- Monthly SUI upload file plus a mass-balance check.
- A PDF traceability annex per sale.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- COP 150–400k per month per organization (USD 35–100), or a fee per adviser seat.
- Willingness to pay is constrained. Many organizations would pay only if the adviser bundles it (**service plus software**).

**How to find first customers:**
- The RUPS list (public, Superservicios).
- Superservicios' aprovechamiento sector report (organizations by department, e.g., 79 providers in Antioquia).
- Recycler associations and their advisers.

**Risks:**
- Genesis Traza and similar tools already cover the core.
- Superservicios may change the SUI formats.
- A politically sensitive sector.
- Low ability to pay.
- Needs a local.

**Kill condition:**
Kill it if Genesis Traza (or a peer) already serves organizations outside Bogotá at a comparable price and includes weigh-ticket capture. That looks likely.

**Score:** 4/10 (Pain 7, Frequency 9, Mandatory 9, Fragmentation 4, Competition 3, Incumbent gap 3, Buyer access 8, WTP 3, MVP 7, Distribution 6.)

### Opportunity: Used-cooking-oil (ACU) collector compliance router: per-pickup constancias and multi-authority January reports

**Industry:**
Used-cooking-oil collection (gestores de ACU), which mostly feeds biodiesel and oleochemicals.

**Buyer:**
The owner or operator of a registered ACU gestor that collects from restaurants, hotels, casinos and food plants across several authority jurisdictions.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- **Weak.** Res. 316/2018 is not new. Authorities keep enforcing it and updating their lists; Cornare's gestor list is dated 31-Jan-2026.
- I found no 2025–2026 change. This is the main weakness.

**Current workflow:**
1. The gestor registers separately with each environmental authority where it collects (CAR, Cornare, CVC/DAGMA, SDA, AMVA, etc.).
2. At each pickup the gestor issues a constancia in the Ministry format, usually on paper or a PDF.
3. In the first 15 days of January the gestor files an annual report to **each** authority, listing every generator served and the kg collected from each.
4. Each generator (restaurant) also files its own annual report to its authority with copies of all its constancias.
5. The two sides must reconcile, and authorities can cross-check.

**Pain:**
- The rule creates exactly the "one job, many authorities" pattern of the Florida grease benchmark.
- I found no complaint evidence. Pain is inferred from the structure of the rule.

**Existing solutions:**
- Each authority's own forms and registration pages (Cornare, CVC, CAR, Corpoboyacá, CARDER, CVS, Corpourabá).
- Hooy, an Acodres Santander app that collects ACU from homes. It's consumer-facing, not compliance.
- General route/collection apps.
- Excel.

**Offline evidence:**
- Registers are published as PDFs per authority.
- Constancias are on paper.
- Collectors are small trucking operations.

**Offline channel:**
- The public gestor lists published by each authority (Cornare PDF list of Jan-2026, CVC list, SDA list).
- Biodiesel and oleochemical buyers that aggregate gestores.
- **Acodres** (the restaurant guild) for the generator side.

**Market count:**
- Cali (DAGMA): 19 gestores and 589 registered generators, with 561,356 kg reported in 2021.
- National gestor count is **unverified (estimate: a few hundred)**.
- Generators: tens of thousands of restaurants are obliged in principle, but registration is low (estimate).

**The gap:**
Nothing turns pickup records into:
- authority-specific January reports for a gestor operating in several jurisdictions;
- a generator-facing portal with the constancias and a ready-made January report, which the gestor could offer as a reason for restaurants to choose it.

**Possible product:**
- A pickup-logging app that issues the Ministry-format constancia by WhatsApp or PDF.
- It auto-builds each authority's annual report.
- Each restaurant gets a link to its own constancias and its own pre-filled annual report.

**MVP:**
Mobile pickup log, then a PDF constancia, then a January export per authority (Excel in each authority's layout), plus a generator link.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- COP 150–300k per month per gestor (USD 35–75).
- Optionally an add-on the gestor resells to generators.
- Willingness to pay is modest. Gestores would pay mainly if it wins them restaurant accounts.

**How to find first customers:**
- Call through the authorities' public gestor lists.
- Introductions through biodiesel plants that buy ACU.

**Risks:**
- Small market.
- An annual filing deadline.
- Authorities may launch their own online reporting.
- Low enforcement may mean low urgency.

**Kill condition:**
Kill it if, nationally, fewer than about 300 gestores exist, or most operate in a single jurisdiction (no multi-authority pain), or the large biodiesel buyers already give gestores such a tool.

**Score:** 4/10 (Pain 4, Frequency 6, Mandatory 8, Fragmentation 8, Competition 8, Incumbent gap 7, Buyer access 7, WTP 3, MVP 9, Distribution 6. Founder access: could be sold remotely by phone/WhatsApp from the public lists, but Spanish is required.)

## 3. Rejected

- **Mineral and gold traceability for traders (RUCOM).**
  - The trigger is real: the platform becomes mandatory on 19-Nov-2026.
  - But the ANM has already authorized 6 technology operators (OTTMs), including MinTrace (Cuántico), so the gap is closed by design.
  - The sector also carries illicit-finance risk.
  - Sources: https://www.portafolio.co/energia/colombia-activa-control-total-a-la-mineria-y-lanza-sistema-obligatorio-para-rastrear-minerales-desde-origen-494542, https://anm.gov.co/rucom-2026-31-de-mayo-fecha-limite-para-renovar-y-evitar-restricciones-en-la-comercializacion-de
- **Domestic-worker employers (Ley 2466/2025).** New duties (written contract, PILA for by-the-day workers). Symplifica and Nómina en Línea (Aportes en Línea) already cover contracts, social-security enrolment, payroll and settlement. It's a B2C trap.
- **Community water systems (Decreto 960/2025).** The new decree aims to *reduce* SUI reporting for community providers, and the buyers can't pay. If anything, sell through municipalities or departmental water plans (PDA), which is enterprise and public procurement.
- **Scrap dealers and compraventas.** No current Colombian digital register or recurring police filing found, only the 1970 duty to show invoices. There is nothing to automate.
- **Beekeepers, agricultural-input stores, tattoo studios, cemeteries.** These are one-off registrations or inspection-only duties, with no recurring submission found.
- **Pool logbooks (Res. 1618/2010).** Plausible, but no evidence of a filing obligation or of pain. Not pursued.

## 4. Method notes

- **Worked well:**
  - Regulator press releases plus the guild press (Contexto Ganadero, Agronegocios) for SINIGAN V6. Pain, numbers and dates were all public.
  - Superservicios sector reports and RUPS bulletins for exact counts.
  - Environmental-authority (CAR) pages for ACU lists and forms.
- **Worked poorly:**
  - Police-register queries (chatarrerías, compraventas) returned nothing Colombian. The Colombian police code has no dealer-register regime comparable to Chile or Argentina.
  - Counts of auctions, apiaries and agricultural-input stores aren't published in searchable form.
- **Not reached:** the search budget was cut off after 35 queries by a usage-limit error. Ley 2585/2026 (deforestation-free livestock) is the most important follow-up: it may add a verification duty on top of SINIGAN for slaughterhouses and traders.
