# Côte d'Ivoire: Offline-industries pass (as of 2026-10-05)

> **Constraints.** I used 20 WebSearch calls (French first) and could not use WebFetch, so every finding below comes from search-result snippets. The pages behind them were not opened.
> - "Verified" means the fact appeared in a snippet returned in this session, and its URL is listed.
> - "Unverified" or "estimate" means my own assumption.
>
> No names, numbers or URLs have been invented. This pass does not repeat the opportunities in `research/countries/cote-d-ivoire.md`: pharmacy tiers-payant, FNE purchase-side checks and ARS 1000 for cocoa.

**Bottom line:** Côte d'Ivoire's quiet industries are real and regulated, but in most of them the receiving body is either:
- a **central state platform** (police HORA for hotels, CCC/SNT for cocoa, SIMEP for gold, CCAK for cashew); or
- an authority that **barely enforces** (pesticide retailers, security firms, domestic employers).

Three regulator-driven 2026 triggers stand out, all in road transport and households:
1. **AMUGA/Ministry of Transport compliance operation from 1 Aug 2026.** It targets wôrô-wôrô and unmarked taxis in Greater Abidjan.
2. **DGTTC inspection of 569 driving schools (June 2026).** A new ministerial order (arrêté) on opening and running a driving school was adopted in a workshop on 25–26 June 2026.
3. **The ILO-backed campaign to formalise domestic workers.** Households employing staff must register with CNPS and declare wages every quarter.

None of the three is strong. The best fit is a **done-for-you service with software behind it**, sold through unions and associations. That needs a local partner.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Document-renewal and compliance tracker for wôrô-wôrô and taxi owners

**Industry:**
Urban paratransit in Grand Abidjan: communal taxis (wôrô-wôrô), unmarked taxis and, later, gbaka minibuses.

**Buyer:**
The vehicle owner who rents to drivers against a daily cash payment, especially owners of 3 to 20 vehicles. Transport unions and cooperatives are secondary buyers.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- From **1 Aug 2026**, Grand Abidjan public-transport vehicles must be administratively compliant. This is a special operation announced by the Ministry of Transport and backed by AMUGA (KOACI, June and July 2026).
- Costs named for compliance: technical inspection, insurance, registration and renewal of authorisations. An authorisation to operate and exceptional measures were announced for wôrô-wôrô and unmarked taxis.
- AMUGA moved to enforcement against illegal occupation of transport facilities in May 2026.
- From Feb 2026, irregular number plates have been sent to the impound lot (fourrière).

**Current workflow:** *(presumed. Validate in interviews.)*
1. The owner keeps the registration document (carte grise), inspection certificate (valid 6 months), insurance certificate, vignette, patente and the yearly municipal authorisation (25,000–75,000 FCFA by commune) in a folder or with the driver.
2. The owner or driver queues at the inspection centre, insurer, town hall and tax office as each item expires.
3. The driver pays a daily municipal tax (500–1,500 FCFA) and a daily payment to the owner (for example 12,000–15,000 FCFA a day in Yopougon). Payments are cash or Mobile Money and are tracked in a notebook.
4. When police or AMUGA stop a vehicle with an expired item, it is fined or impounded and the vehicle earns nothing until released.

**Pain:**
An impounded or unauthorised vehicle loses its daily payment, and several documents with different validity periods renew at different counters. The pain is backed by the enforcement campaign and the impound measures. **No owner complaints about document tracking specifically were found.**

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper folders and notebooks; WhatsApp reminders between owner and driver.
- Union and syndicate offices, which handle some formalities for a fee (*unverified*).
- Generic custom fleet apps from Abidjan web agencies. Kolonell quotes **2–6 million FCFA** for a taxi or VTC fleet app with Wave/Orange Money payouts.
- Insurers' and inspection centres' own reminders (*unverified*).

**Offline evidence:**
- Every document is issued at a physical counter.
- Owners are often individuals with no web presence.
- No off-the-shelf tracking product for wôrô-wôrô owners appeared in search, only custom-build agency offers.

**Offline channel:**
- Taxi owners' and drivers' unions at commune stations (gares).
- Inspection centres and insurance brokers that sell public-transport cover, which could offer the tracker as a retention tool.
- WhatsApp groups of owners in each commune.

**Market count:**
Unknown. No official count of wôrô-wôrô vehicles or owners was found in this session. Needs AMUGA or Ministry of Transport data.

**The gap:**
A cheap per-vehicle tracker combining:
- every expiry date (inspection every 6 months, insurance, vignette, municipal authorisation, patente);
- a daily-payment ledger per driver;
- a "ready for control" status;
- optional done-for-you renewals.

Custom fleet apps cost millions of FCFA and target VTC fleets, not individual wôrô-wôrô owners.

**Possible product:**
WhatsApp-first, with photos of each document turned into expiry reminders and a per-driver payment ledger reconciled against Mobile Money. A local agent handles renewals for a fee.

**MVP:**
A form to photograph documents, typed-in expiry dates, WhatsApp reminders 15 days before each expiry, and a daily-payment log. A single commune pilot (Yopougon or Cocody).

**Pricing hypothesis:**
1,000–2,000 FCFA per vehicle per month for software (*estimate*). Realistic revenue comes from a renewal-handling commission of 2,000–5,000 FCFA per formality (*estimate*). Owners will pay for avoided impoundment, not for software.

**How to find first customers:**
Commune taxi-station unions, insurance brokers and inspection-centre queues.

**Risks:**
- The 2026 operation could be a one-off campaign, after which enforcement fades.
- The state or AMUGA could build its own vehicle registry and authorisation system.
- Owners' willingness to pay is very low.
- It cannot be sold without a local operator.

**Kill condition:**
- Ten owner interviews show they already rely on a union or insurer to handle renewals.
- Or impoundment for expired documents is rare after the campaign.

**Score:** 4/10

- Founder access: needs a local. A non-local solo founder cannot sell this.
- Willingness to pay: service, not software.

### Opportunity: Compliance register and back office for driving schools under the 2026 order (arrêté)

**Industry:**
Driving schools (auto-écoles).

**Buyer:**
The owner-manager of an approved driving school, typically with 1–5 instructors and 1–3 vehicles.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- **June 2026:** the DGTTC inspected **569 driving schools** (302 in Abidjan, 267 in the interior). It found:
  - unapproved schools;
  - non-compliance with the official training programmes;
  - lax licence-award conditions.
- Announced measures: **tariff harmonisation**, institutional support and a permanent consultation framework.
- **25–26 June 2026 workshop:** adopted a draft order on opening and running a driving school, revised the training framework for instructors and examiners, and examined licence-award conditions.
- The automated theory exam was extended nationwide.
- In Feb 2026, the driving schools themselves publicly called for urgent licence reform.

**Current workflow:** *(presumed)*
1. A candidate enrols at the school. The school compiles the paper dossier (ID, photos, medical certificate) and pays exam fees.
2. Lesson hours are logged in a notebook or not at all.
3. The school registers candidates for the theory exam (now automated) and the practical exam through the DGTTC.
4. At inspection, the school must show its approval, instructor qualifications, vehicles, and compliance with the programme and tariffs.

**Pain:**
- Inspection-driven: proof of compliance with the official programme and harmonised tariffs, which the new order will likely formalise.
- Unapproved competitors are being cleaned out, so compliant schools have an incentive to document compliance.
- Evidence of pain comes from the regulator and the association; **no school-level complaints about record keeping were found.**

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper registers and notebooks.
- No local driving-school software surfaced in search; directory listings show schools only.
- French driving-school software exists (built around France's digital training logbook, not Ivorian rules).
- Generic Excel files.
- DGTTC exam-booking channels (format unverified).

**Offline evidence:**
- Driving schools appear only in business directories, with no software listings.
- Inspection is physical.
- The sector speaks through the UNAECI association and an employers' union of driving schools.

**Offline channel:**
- UNAECI, which has asked to be involved in every stage of the reform.
- The driving schools' employers' union.
- DGTTC consultation framework meetings.
- Phone or WhatsApp outreach to the 569 inspected schools listed in directories (annuaireci.com).

**Market count:**
**569 schools** inspected by the DGTTC (AIP, June 2026). The approved subset is smaller (unverified).

**The gap:**
A lesson-hours register per candidate that matches the official programme, a candidate dossier checklist and exam-registration tracker, and an inspection-ready export. All of this depends on what the arrêté requires.

**Possible product:**
A simple tablet/WhatsApp register where instructors log each lesson against the official curriculum, candidates' dossiers are tracked to exam, and an inspection pack (approval, instructors, vehicles, hours, tariffs) prints in one click.

**MVP:**
Candidate list, lesson log, dossier checklist and a PDF inspection pack, piloted with 5 UNAECI schools.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
10,000–20,000 FCFA per school per month (*estimate*). This is 569 × 15,000 × 12 ≈ 100M FCFA a year at full penetration, about US$170k, and only as an upper bound.

**How to find first customers:**
UNAECI members and the 569-school inspection list.

**Risks:**
- The arrêté may not require any lesson register.
- The DGTTC may build its own platform, since it already automated the theory exam.
- The market is small.
- Schools may prefer to stay informal.

**Kill condition:**
- The published arrêté contains no recurring record-keeping or reporting duty.
- Or the DGTTC announces its own driving-school management portal.

**Score:** 4/10

- Founder access: needs a local, French-speaking partner. A remote founder could build it, but selling requires association meetings.
- Willingness to pay: software at a low price, possibly bundled with the association.

### Opportunity: Done-for-you CNPS registration and payslips for household employers

**Industry:**
Households as employers: housekeepers, nannies, drivers, guards and cooks.

**Buyer:**
Expatriate and upper-middle-class households in Abidjan, plus the domestic-staff placement agencies that place staff with them, as resellers.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The Ministry of Employment and Social Protection, with the ILO, runs a national campaign to formalise domestic workers (Fraternité Matin).
- Decree 2024-902 (consolidated version 21 Mar 2025) restates that persons employing only domestic staff must declare them to CNPS.
- CMU contributions of 1,000 FCFA per person per month apply.

**Current workflow:**
1. Most households do not declare at all.
2. Those that do register at a CNPS agency, CEPICI or e-CNPS. The "gens de maison" employer file needs a registration form, worker declaration, ID, photo, proof of address and bank details (RIB).
3. Wages are declared to CNPS **quarterly** and contributions paid.
4. Payslips, leave and termination calculations are done by hand or not at all. Some placement agencies handle entry and exit declarations.

**Pain:**
Legal exposure exists on paper (a mandatory declaration within 8 days of hiring), but enforcement on households is weak. The real pain is for expatriates and employers who face disputes at termination (*unverified*).

**Existing solutions:**
- e-CNPS (the free official portal).
- Placement agencies (SION Universal, SPPCI and others).
- Accountants.
- EOR and payroll firms (Pentabell, Rivermate), which target companies, not households.
- Generic payslip tools.

**Offline evidence:**
- Registration is done at a counter.
- Workers are largely informal, so the ILO-backed campaign exists.
- No household-payroll product for Côte d'Ivoire surfaced.

**Offline channel:**
- Placement agencies as resellers.
- Expatriate associations and the welcome desks of international schools and embassies (*unverified*).
- Relocation firms.

**Market count:**
Unknown. No count of CNPS-registered domestic employers was found.

**The gap:**
A household-specific flow covering:
- one-time CNPS employer registration;
- monthly payslips at the legal minimum wage or above;
- quarterly declaration prepared and paid;
- leave and termination calculator.

**Possible product:**
A paid service (registration plus quarterly filing) running on a small payroll engine for household contracts.

**MVP:**
A payslip generator plus a quarterly CNPS calculation and reminder, with human filing on e-CNPS. Pilot with one placement agency.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
5,000–10,000 FCFA per household per month, plus a 25,000–50,000 FCFA registration fee (*estimate*).

**How to find first customers:**
Placement agencies listed on annuaireci.com and africarrieres; expatriate community groups.

**Risks:**
- No enforcement, so demand only comes from conscientious employers.
- Small reachable segment.
- An accountant can do it cheaply.

**Kill condition:**
Placement agencies report that fewer than about 10% of their clients ask for CNPS declaration.

**Score:** 3/10

- Founder access: needs a local to file and visit the CNPS.
- Willingness to pay: only for a done-for-you service.

## 4. Method notes

What worked:
- French queries naming the regulator and the year ("DGTTC … 2026", "AMUGA … 1er août") surfaced AIP, KOACI and Fraternité Matin news on inspections and campaigns.
- servicepublic.gouv.ci gave the official procedure pages for agréments and CNPS registration.
- civlii.laws.africa gave the consolidated decrees.

What didn't work:
- No public licensing registers with counts turned up, apart from the 569 driving schools. Counts mostly come from press coverage of inspections.
- Queries about software and competitors returned French (France) results or Abidjan custom-build agency blogs (Kolonell), not local products.
- Several sectors (funerals, scrap, security) only returned pre-2020 press.
