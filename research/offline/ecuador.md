# Ecuador: Offline (Quiet) Industries Pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. I used 19 of the 20 allowed WebSearch calls. I did not use WebFetch. Anything I could not confirm from an official or press source is marked "unverified" or "estimate". This pass does not repeat the opportunities in `research/countries/ecuador.md` (EUDR cocoa/coffee traceability, ARCSA pharmacy reports, MDT-2025-122 construction safety).

Overall finding: most of Ecuador's quiet industries don't leave a separate paper trail with sector regulators. Instead, they are caught by **cross-cutting national obligations**: the SRI (tax), the UAFE (the anti-money-laundering unit) and the SEPS (the regulator for cooperatives and community finance). These obligations reach small operators through their RUC (tax ID). Since August 2025 the SRI can suspend a RUC automatically, which makes these obligations painful. The three leads below all follow that pattern.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Regularisation-plus-compliance kit for community savings banks (cajas comunales)

**Industry:**
Community and savings banks in the popular and solidarity finance sector.

**Buyer:**
The treasurer, president or manager of a caja comunal or caja de ahorro. A secondary buyer is the second-tier networks and NGOs that support many cajas, such as RFD (Red de Instituciones Financieras de Desarrollo) members and development projects.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- Resolution JPRF-F-2025-0154 (20 May 2025) requires cajas to become legal entities, adapt their bylaws, register in the public register and get SEPS authorisation by **20 November 2026**.
- On 14 October 2025 UAFE named cajas comunales, cajas de ahorro and bancos comunales as new obligated subjects. That brings a registration code, a compliance officer and monthly RESU/NO-RESU reports.
- SEPS resolution 2025-0148 lists a UAFE certificate among the requirements.
- Only 36 had regularised after one year.

**Current workflow:**
1. The treasurer keeps member savings and loans in a notebook or Excel.
2. For regularisation, a lawyer or volunteer drafts adapted bylaws, reserves the name on the SEPS portal (a PDF user guide dated 21 Jan 2026 covers this) and assembles the member roster.
3. They obtain the UAFE registration code and appoint a compliance officer, then every month submit a RESU (or NO-RESU) in the UAFE-prescribed structure.
4. Under the regulation they must keep individual savings accounts per member, with no member above 5% of deposits. This is checked by hand.

**Pain:**
Primicias reports that cajas are still taking third-party deposits despite the ban, and that few have regularised. After the deadline, unregularised cajas are illegal. Their volunteer officers have no accounting staff.

**Existing solutions:**
- SEPS portal and guides (free, procedural only)
- Lawyers and consultants (for example Quevedo & Ponce publish on the new rules)
- Core-banking systems built for cooperatives (too heavy and expensive)
- University-thesis accounting systems (academic)
- Excel

I found no commercial caja-specific product in search. That is a gap but not proof of absence: local cooperative-software vendors probably exist (unverified).

**Offline evidence:**
- SEPS's channel is PDF guides and an "inscripción en nómina" counter procedure.
- Cajas are volunteer-run, community-based and often rural or indigenous.
- Media describe irregular, informal operation.

**Offline channel:**
- The public SEPS list ("Nómina de Cajas Comunales y Cajas de Ahorro"), which gives names and provinces
- RFD and other second-tier networks
- NGO and development programmes that sponsor cajas
- Cooperative federations
- Phone or WhatsApp outreach using the SEPS list

**Market count:**
572 cajas on the SEPS list (Feb 2025). At least 1,600 operating per RFD's director (May 2026, Primicias).

**The gap:**
No cheap tool combines a member ledger (savings, loans, the 5% concentration check) with the SEPS regularisation checklist and documents, and then the monthly UAFE NO-RESU/RESU file.

**Possible product:**
A Spanish caja ledger that also generates the regularisation dossier and the monthly UAFE report. It would be sold as software-plus-service: setup by a partner accountant, then a monthly fee.

**MVP:**
- Member register and savings/loan ledger, with the 5% concentration alert
- Checklist for regularisation documents
- Monthly RESU/NO-RESU file in the UAFE structure

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- USD 15–30/month per caja, or USD 150–300 for a regularisation package.
- More realistically, licences paid by an NGO or network on behalf of 20–100 cajas.
- Willingness to pay: weak for software alone. A done-for-you package is more likely to sell.

**How to find first customers:**
The SEPS list, RFD, and NGO programmes.

**Founder access:**
Needs a local partner. Trust, rural reach and Kichwa-speaking communities make remote selling by a non-local founder unrealistic.

**Risks:**
- After 20 Nov 2026 most cajas may simply close or go underground instead of buying anything.
- SEPS may supply a free tool.
- Very low ability to pay.
- The deadline may be extended (unverified).

**Kill condition:**
- The networks or NGOs already supply a free ledger, or
- fewer than about 300 cajas actually regularise, or
- the UAFE obligation is waived for small cajas.

**Score:** 5/10

### Opportunity: Monthly UAFE RESU / NO-RESU filer for small non-financial obligated subjects

**Industry:**
Small non-financial UAFE obligated subjects:
- used-car and vehicle traders
- jewellers and precious-metal dealers
- small builders and real-estate firms
- vehicle-armouring shops (obligated since April 2025)
- factoring firms and football clubs
- provincial political parties, reinsurance intermediaries and insurance producer-advisers (added Oct 2025)

**Buyer:**
The owner, or the external compliance officer (oficial de cumplimiento) who serves several small obligated subjects.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- SRI resolution NAC-DGERCGC25-00000018 (6 Aug 2025) adds an "obligated to UAFE" flag to the RUC certificate. Since 10 September 2025, failing to obtain the UAFE registration code within 30 business days leads to automatic RUC suspension. Lifting it needs a UAFE compliance certificate.
- UAFE widened the list again in October 2025.

**Current workflow:**
1. The SRI flags the business at RUC opening or update. The owner registers on the UAFE platform and appoints a compliance officer.
2. Every month, by the 15th, someone pulls transactions of USD 10,000 or more from invoices or sales records, builds the RESU file in the structure set by UAFE's manual, and uploads it through SISLAFT. If there were no such transactions, they file a NO-RESU.
3. The officer also has to keep KYC files and a prevention manual.

**Pain:**
- The filing is monthly, even when the answer is "nothing to report".
- A lapse can bring RUC suspension, which stops invoicing.
- Small firms hire an external officer.

**Existing solutions:**
- External compliance officers and law firms (NMSLaw, Andersen and EY publish alerts)
- Firms such as ciro.com.ec, which publishes a 2026 guide to UAFE reports (its product scope is unverified)
- Bank-grade AML suites (overkill for small firms)
- UAFE's own SISLAFT upload

**Offline evidence:**
- The work is done by a human compliance officer and filed through a government upload.
- Search found no SME RESU software.
- Advice reaches firms through law-firm alerts and chamber newsletters (CCQ).

**Offline channel:**
- Chambers of commerce (CCQ's tax bulletin covers this)
- Accountants and external compliance officers as resellers: one officer serves many firms
- The vehicle importers' association (AEADE) and used-car lots
- Jewellers' guilds (unverified)

**Market count:**
Unverified. The RUC flag exists, but I found no published number of non-financial obligated subjects. Estimate: several thousand.

**The gap:**
A cheap tool that reads the firm's SRI electronic invoices (XML) and flags customers whose single or cumulative transactions reach USD 10,000. It would then fill the RESU structure, or confirm and file NO-RESU, and keep the KYC file per flagged customer.

**Possible product:**
"Upload or connect your SRI invoices; get your monthly UAFE file and KYC checklist." The main buyer is the external compliance officer who handles 10–50 clients.

**MVP:**
- SRI XML invoice parser
- Threshold aggregation per customer
- RESU file generator in the UAFE manual's format
- NO-RESU reminder
- Multi-client dashboard for compliance officers

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- USD 20–40/month per obligated firm, or
- USD 150–300/month for an officer with many clients.
- Willingness to pay: plausible, because firms already pay officers. The software saves the officer time.

**How to find first customers:**
Compliance officers registered with UAFE (they are found through law and accounting firms), CCQ, and AEADE.

**Founder access:**
Possible remotely if partnered with one compliance officer. Spanish is required, and UAFE format details need local validation.

**Risks:**
- Accounting ERPs or e-invoicing vendors may add the feature.
- UAFE may change the format or the threshold.
- The market count is unverified.
- Officers may prefer their own Excel templates.

**Kill condition:**
- The RESU format is trivial (a short web form), so there is no software value, or
- local ERPs already export RESU, or
- fewer than about 1,000 non-financial obligated SMEs exist.

**Score:** 5/10

### Opportunity: Partner-to-operator settlement and invoicing for transport cooperatives (SRI 2026 rule)

**Industry:**
Commercial ground-transport operators (cooperatives and companies): school and institutional transport, tourism, heavy and light cargo, mixed transport. Taxis are excluded.

**Buyer:**
The operator's manager or secretary-treasurer, and the cooperative's contracted accountant.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- SRI resolution NAC-DGERCGC26-00000024 took effect on publication, 10 July 2026, and repealed the 2017 regime.
- Partners and shareholders may now register only the activity "transport service exclusively from partners to operators" and may invoice **only their operator**. The operator invoices end clients.
- A later reform added a new withholding scheme (UQA).

**Current workflow:**
1. Each partner owns a vehicle and does trips for clients that the operator contracts, or that the partner brings in.
2. The operator now invoices the client and collects payment.
3. Each partner issues an e-invoice to the operator for their share. The operator withholds tax under the new scheme and pays the partner.
4. The accountant reconciles client invoices, partner invoices, withholdings and payments per partner each month in Excel.

**Pain:**
- It is a new monthly many-to-one reconciliation; a cooperative may have dozens of partner-owners.
- Partners who used to invoice clients directly have to change.
- Errors affect deductibility and withholding.

**Existing solutions:**
- E-invoicing vendors, which are already publishing about the rule: TuFacturero, TribuExcel, Boletín Contable, LLB Solutions, ACS
- The SRI's free invoicing tool
- General accounting ERPs
- Accountants

**Offline evidence:**
- Cooperatives are run by elected partners. Trip assignment happens on paper or WhatsApp.
- Guidance comes through accountant bulletins, not product pages.

**Offline channel:**
- Provincial federations of cargo and school-transport cooperatives
- Transport-cooperative accountants, since one accountant serves several cooperatives
- ANT and municipal (GAD) lists of operators holding permits
- SEPS register of non-financial cooperatives

**Market count:**
Unverified. Transport co-ops are the largest group of non-financial popular-and-solidarity cooperatives (54.7%, UTA thesis). I found no ANT count of operators by modality. Estimate: thousands of operators.

**The gap:**
E-invoicing vendors will issue the individual documents. What's missing is the **per-partner monthly settlement**: trips → client invoice → expected partner invoice → withholding → net payable. That includes chasing missing partner invoices and flagging partners still invoicing clients directly.

**Possible product:**
A settlement sheet for transport operators. It imports the operator's issued and received SRI XML, matches partner invoices to the trips and client invoices, computes withholdings and produces a payout statement per partner.

**MVP:**
- XML import (issued and received)
- Partner roster
- Monthly matching and missing-invoice list
- Withholding calculation
- Payout PDF per partner

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- USD 30–80/month per operator, or
- USD 150/month for an accountant with several operators.
- Willingness to pay: moderate, routed through accountants.

**How to find first customers:**
Transport-cooperative federations, accountants who specialise in transport cooperatives, and municipal permit lists.

**Founder access:**
Needs a local accountant partner. A remote sale to cooperative boards is unlikely.

**Risks:**
- Strongest competition risk of the three: e-invoicing and ERP vendors moved within days of the resolution.
- The SRI may amend the rule again; it has already been reformed once.
- The market count is unverified.

**Kill condition:**
- Major Ecuadorian e-invoicing vendors (TuFacturero, Contífico-type ERPs) ship partner-settlement modules by Q1 2027, or
- interviews show cooperatives settle partners in cash outside the books.

**Score:** 5/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Household employers of domestic workers:** there are 63,340 IESS affiliations (Dec 2025), falling. The IESS portal and accountants are free or cheap substitutes, and each household pays very little. ([Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/economia/trabajadoras-remuneradas-hogar-trabajo-domestico-ecuador-derechos-afiliaciones-iess-119217/))
- **Scrap and copper dealers:** I found no national register or recurring report, only municipal permits and police raids on illegal collection centres. ([Expreso](https://www.expreso.ec/guayaquil/descubren-centros-acopio-ilegales-cobre-guayaquil-228591.html))
- **Beekeepers:** only 2,175 producers, and the zoosanitary certificate is occasional and issued by the state. ([gob.ec](https://www.gob.ec/arcfz/tramites/solicitud-certificado-zoosanitario-produccion-movilidad-explotacion-apicola))
- **Livestock traders and fairs:** Agrocalidad issues the movement guide itself, so no intermediary paperwork is left.
- **Taxi operators:** permit renewal is rare (about 10 years), and taxis are exempt from the 2026 SRI rule.
- **Used-car lots, transfer paperwork:** the bottleneck was ANT being closed (Jan–Mar 2026), not a workflow software can fix. Their UAFE duty is covered by opportunity 2. ([Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/sociedad/suspension-temporal-compra-venta-carros-nuevos-usados-ant-115093/))
- **Gold buyers and pawnbrokers:** BCE authorisation is a one-off web form. I found no pawnbroker register. Mining-related reputational risk.

## 4. Method notes

- What worked: following **cross-cutting national resolutions** (SRI NAC-DGERCGC, UAFE's widening list of obligated subjects, SEPS/JPRF) down to the quiet sectors they newly catch. Ecuadorian law-firm and accountant blogs (NMSLaw, Lexis, Boletín Contable) plus Primicias reliably surface the resolutions.
- What didn't: sector-specific "registro de chatarreros", livestock and slaughterhouse queries returned mostly Colombian or Argentine results. Operator counts (UAFE obligated subjects, ANT transport operators) are not published in searchable form. All three leads need a count from a public-information request (LOTAIP) or the SEPS list before interviews.
- Founder access: all three leads need a local accountant or compliance-officer partner. None is a cold, remote SaaS sale.
