# Egypt: Offline / Quiet-Industries Pass (as of 2026-10-05)

Scope: 32 WebSearch calls (Arabic first; two in extended mode). WebFetch is blocked, so every claim comes from search-result summaries and snippets, not from reading the official pages. Anything not stated in a result is marked *unverified* or *estimate*. This report replaces the earlier 5-search partial run and reuses its gold-shop, domestic-worker, scrap-import and tutoring-centre findings. It does not repeat the existing country report (EPTTS pharma serialization, ACI air freight, ETA monitor for accountants).

Honest headline: Egypt's quiet trades are mostly regulated through **state-run subsidy and licensing systems** (bread, ration cards, farmer cards, nursery licences), not through police registers. The state often supplies its own portal or POS, so the open slot for a small vendor is usually a *back-office layer*, and willingness to pay is low. Nothing here scores above 5/10.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Baladi Bakery Flour-and-Cash Reconciliation under Direct Deduction

**Industry:**
Subsidised baladi bread bakeries.

**Buyer:**
The bakery owner (often an older owner-operator; some bakeries are run by heirs), and the owner's accountant.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The Ministry of Supply moved to "direct deduction" on 1 August 2026, after postponing from 1 July (Al-Watan, Akhbar El Yom, Dostor snippets).
- Flour moved to the free price, EGP 19,240 per ton, bought from the tied mill. The subsidised loaf stays at EGP 0.20 and 90 grams.
- A bank account is mandatory. Some owners needed extra time for licence transfers and inheritance settlements (Akhbar El Yom).
- The Bakeries Division is still asking for changes to the deduction mechanism and for compensation for higher operating costs.

**Current workflow (partly inferred):**
1. The bakery buys its flour allotment from the mill and pays at the free price.
2. Loaves are dispensed against ration cards on the ministry's system.
3. The system computes the entitlement from loaves dispensed and settles to the bakery's bank account.
4. The owner reconciles flour bought, loaves recorded, cash received and the system's balance by hand or in a notebook (*unverified*).
5. Disputes about missing flour, unrecorded loaves or delayed credit go to the local supply directorate.

**Pain:**
Owner statements to the press ask for a delay and a better deduction mechanism. Fines were postponed to ease the move into the system (Cairo24 snippet). Actual error rates and hours spent are *unverified*.

**Existing solutions:**
- The ministry's own system and the (four) payment providers competing to run bakery e-payments (Dostor).
- Generic bakery accounting (Qoyod page "accounting software for bakeries"). Its page says general accounting is not enough because production must link to stock and the till.
- Accountants who already do tax and wage work for the bakery (*unverified*).

**The gap:**
Hypothesis only: a view that matches mill delivery notes, flour bought, loaves recorded and bank credits, and flags mismatches before the owner disputes them. It cannot be built without export access or manual entry from the state system, which is the main unknown.

**Possible product:**
A phone-first reconciliation sheet for the owner: enter flour deliveries and read the bank and system balances, and get a daily mismatch flag plus a printable dispute pack for the supply directorate.

**MVP:**
A manual-entry reconciliation app in Arabic with WhatsApp-shareable dispute summaries. No integration with the ministry system in the first version.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
EGP 150-400 per bakery per month (*estimate*). Many owners may only pay for a done-for-you service via their accountant.

**How to find first customers:**
- The General Bakeries Division (الشعبة العامة للمخابز) and Cairo's bakeries division (about 4,000 subsidised bakeries in Cairo per a snippet).
- Mills and flour depots, which know every tied bakery.
- Supply directorates in each governorate.
- A local, Arabic-speaking seller is essential.

**Market count:**
About 32,000 subsidised bakeries (Egypt Telegraph, Dostor snippets; the exact current register was not seen).

**Offline evidence:**
The division speaks for owners, who use a bank mandate and the state card system rather than any software. No bakery software vendor pages were found for the direct-deduction rules (*absence in one search only*).

**Offline channel:**
The bakeries divisions of the chambers of commerce, flour depots, and governorate supply directorates.

**Risks:**
- The state or its payment providers add a reconciliation view and close the gap.
- Owners will not pay for software where the state system already pays them.
- Margins are thin and the state may change the rules again.
- A non-local founder cannot sell this.

**Kill condition:**
Kill it if the four payment providers already give owners a daily statement matching flour to loaves, or if five owner interviews show they trust the bank statement and need nothing else.

**Score:** 5/10 (strong trigger and reach; low willingness to pay, a state-owned core system and an unverified pain level).

### Opportunity: Nursery Licence-Regularisation Tracker

**Industry:**
Private nurseries (ages 0-4), mostly owner-run.

**Buyer:**
The nursery owner-manager, or a consultant who prepares nursery licence files (*unverified that such consultants exist*).

**Trigger / Why now:**
- Ministry circular (July 2025) granting a 6-month temporary licence while owners regularise with civil protection, local development and the new-urban-communities body (Maspero, Al-Watan, Al Mal snippets).
- A high committee collects monthly directorate reports on temporary licences (Egypt Telegraph snippet).
- January 2026: the Ministers of Solidarity and Housing discussed easing nursery licensing nationwide (Ahram, Masrawy, Amwal Al Ghad titles).

**Current workflow (inferred from the circular):**
1. The owner submits ID, qualification, criminal record and health certificates, staff files, a child-protection policy and ownership or lease papers to the directorate.
2. A temporary licence is issued for 6 months.
3. The owner must collect approvals from civil protection, local development and the housing body before it expires.
4. Staff files and capacity limits must be kept and shown on inspection.

**Pain:**
The scale of unlicensed nurseries is large (about 32,000 of 48,225 per the census snippet) and the state has chosen amnesty-style regularisation, which implies owners struggle to complete the dossier. The cost to owners and the share expiring unfulfilled are *unverified*.

**Existing solutions:**
- Nursery management apps for parents and teachers (Nursery Pro, "Baraem" and freelance builds on Khamsat/Mostaql per the snippets).
- Directorate staff and informal consultants (*unverified*).
- None found that tracks the three-authority approval chain.

**The gap:**
A document-and-deadline checklist for the regularisation chain and the inspection-ready staff files. Possibly too narrow, and temporary by design.

**Possible product:**
A mobile checklist that holds the dossier, tracks expiry dates and civil-protection requirements, and prints the pack for the directorate.

**MVP:**
Checklist plus document vault plus expiry reminders by WhatsApp.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
EGP 100-300 per month (*estimate*). More plausible as a one-off dossier service.

**How to find first customers:**
Governorate solidarity directorates (which hold the applicant lists), nursery owner groups on social media (*unverified*), and parent-facing app vendors as partners.

**Market count:**
Up to about 48,000 nurseries, of which about 32,000 unlicensed (Egypt Telegraph/Al-Mal snippets). Older ministry figures (14,281 licensed, 10,488 unlicensed) use a different base.

**Offline evidence:**
The licence process runs through directorate committees and printed dossiers. The 2026 articles describe nursery owners being asked to "announce themselves".

**Offline channel:**
Directorate offices, local nursery associations (*unverified*), and parents' groups.

**Risks:**
- The regularisation window may end or be extended without a new fixed cycle.
- Annual or one-time work, so low frequency.
- Owners prefer informal arrangements.
- Local seller only.

**Kill condition:**
Kill it if the temporary-licence window has closed with no recurring inspections, or if owners say the directorate already hands them a complete checklist.

**Score:** 4/10.

### Opportunity: Gold Shop E-Receipt (carried over, downgraded)

**Industry:**
Gold retailers and workshops.

**Buyer:**
Shop owner, via the outside accountant.

**Trigger / Why now:**
The Gold Division and the Egyptian Tax Authority were preparing an awareness programme on e-invoice and e-receipt in July 2026 (Amwal Al Ghad, Akhbar El Yom). A 2022 report says all gold-sector taxpayers were already obliged to use e-invoicing (Hapi Journal snippet), so the real status of enforcement in the sector is *unverified*.

**Existing solutions (new this run):**
Daftra, Dexef and Qoyod each publish gold-and-jewellery software pages with karat, weight, stones and e-invoice support. This is the 80-90% incumbent case that the brief says to downgrade.

**The gap:**
Only the e-receipt mode for retail counters and weight-based buy-back netting, which was not confirmed missing.

**Market count:**
More than 3,500 gold and silver shops and workshops (Al-Ain snippet; *unverified*).

**Offline channel:**
Gold chamber sessions, the Sagha market, accountants.

**Score:** 3/10. Three incumbents were found, so the earlier 4/10 drops. A local founder would be required.

## 3. Rejected

- **Ration-card grocers (about 26,000):** the failure is the state's POS capacity and system crashes, not the grocer's paperwork ([El Watan](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/1213492)).
- **Well drillers:** law and fines confirmed ([Youm7](https://www.youm7.com/6081041)) but no register, count or recurring filing found.
- **Pesticide and fertiliser shops:** Farmer Card is state-run; shop licensing facts come from a 2020 article only.
- **Tuk-tuk and microbus operators:** a state financing programme and per-governorate line licensing, informal owners, no filing.
- **Butchers, poultry, fishing boats, beekeepers:** only licensing law and issuance statistics found, no recurring operator-side report or count.
- **Money changers:** few large regulated entities.
- **Domestic workers, scrap-import paperwork, tutoring centres:** unchanged from the earlier run (proposal only; importer and customs workflow; contested licensing).
- **Cemeteries and driving schools:** not screened properly; the searches returned non-Egyptian sources.

## 4. Method notes

- Arabic regulator-first queries worked best for finding *new* state systems (bread direct deduction, nursery temporary licences). They returned mostly news-site snippets, so counts are secondhand. Official portals were never read.
- Gulf and Libyan sites crowded out Egyptian results for cemeteries, scrap and driving schools. Adding "مصر" and the authority's name helps, but it did not fix these three.
- Query "برنامج + industry" found incumbents quickly (it killed the gold lead).
- Searches that would have helped but were not run: the supply ministry's official bakery instructions, a nursery owners' association, a scrap-dealer register, a count of pesticide shops.
