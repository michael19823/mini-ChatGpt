# El Salvador: Offline-industries pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. I used 20 WebSearch calls and no WebFetch. Every claim comes from search-result summaries. Anything I could not confirm is marked **unverified** or **estimate**. This report does not repeat the opportunities in `research/countries/el-salvador.md` (AML for professionals, DTE v2.0).

**Bottom line:** El Salvador's quiet industries are mostly either informal with no enforcement (domestic employers, scrap, tattoo, street trade) or already pulled onto free government portals: SIMPLE.SV for cattle brands, MAG/CENDEPESCA counter licences and the SSF's SPU for payroll. The one live regulatory trigger is the **Autoridad Salvadoreña del Agua (ASA)**. Its April 2026 guideline makes every surface- or groundwater user install metering and send extraction data to the ASA, by telemetry or, for small users "temporarily", by electronic file. Juntas de agua and tanker-truck (pipa) distributors already owe semi-annual extraction and distribution reports. That gives one modest opportunity (4/10). The buyers are poor, the ASA may build its own portal, and the money sits mostly in meter hardware.

## 2. Opportunities

### Opportunity: ASA extraction-reporting kit for juntas de agua and small well users

**Industry:**
Community water boards (juntas de agua / ADESCOs), private well owners (farms, agro-industry, hotels, condominiums) and tanker-truck distributors.

**Buyer:**
The treasurer or president of a junta de agua (volunteer board, often with one paid fontanero or operator). The plant or farm manager of a private well user. The drilling or metering contractor who installs the equipment, as a reseller.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The Ley General de Recursos Hídricos (2022) created the ASA and a duty for every water user to report. Water providers must keep a monthly distribution log and file a semi-annual report of volumes extracted and distributed.
- A 2025 lineamiento extended registration and semi-annual reports to tanker (pipa) distributors.
- The **ASA guideline published in the Diario Oficial on 8 Apr 2026** requires every natural or legal person extracting surface or groundwater, "regardless of location, quantity or purpose", to install flow/volume meters and data storage and to send the data to the ASA's servers. Telemetry is the default. **File submission is allowed temporarily for smaller users.** The guideline also sets sanctions. Phase-in deadlines are **unverified**.

**Current workflow (inferred; not confirmed by interviews):**
1. The operator reads a mechanical meter (if one exists) and writes the reading in a notebook. Many juntas have no source meter.
2. The treasurer tallies monthly volumes and connections in a ledger or Excel.
3. Every six months, someone fills in the ASA report, often with help from an NGO, an ANDA/ASA technician or the municipality, and submits it through the Sistema de Gestión de Trámites or in person.
4. From 2026, small users must produce the extraction data file in the ASA's format. Larger users buy a telemetry box from a meter vendor.

**Pain:**
Mandatory, recurring (monthly log plus semi-annual report, and now continuous data) and new. Registration progress shows that capacity is low: only about 14% of about 2,500 juntas had registered by the July 2023 deadline. Local radio called it a "fracaso a nivel nacional", and by 2025 or 2026 only "more than 600" were registered. Sanctions exist under the new guideline, but amounts and enforcement are **unverified**.

**Existing solutions:**
- **The ASA's own systems:** the online Sistema de Gestión de Trámites and the SIHI hydrological information system. It may add a free upload form; whether it already has one for the 2026 data files is **unverified**.
- **Telemetry hardware vendors**, regional ones such as Compres, plus local meter distributors. They bundle data transmission for larger users.
- **Junta billing software** from the region: JAAP (Ecuador), SIAPAP, SisAgua. They handle billing, not ASA reporting, and I found no Salvadoran-specific product.
- **NGOs and the ASA's "apoyo integral"** to juntas: a free human substitute.
- Excel and paper notebooks.

**Offline evidence:**
Volunteer rural boards, an 86% non-registration rate at the deadline, ASA forms published as downloadable PDFs, and registration driven by field campaigns rather than online self-service. I found no Salvadoran software listing for junta administration.

**Offline channel:**
- The ASA's own junta-registration campaigns and its list of registered juntas (600+).
- Junta networks and federations (for example the alliance that lobbied the ASA to ease legalization, named in El Mundo; exact organization **unverified**).
- WASH NGOs that fund rural systems.
- Well-drilling firms registered with the ASA and meter distributors, who could bundle the reporting app with the meter.
- Municipal water units.

**Market count:**
About 2,500 juntas (estimate cited in El Mundo), of which 600+ are registered with the ASA (Diario El Salvador). Junta-supplied households number over half a million (El Salvador.com, 2024). The number of private well authorizations and pipa distributors is **unverified**.

**The gap:**
A cheap tool that turns an operator's monthly meter readings (typed on a phone, or a photo of the meter) into the ASA monthly log, the semi-annual report and the 2026 extraction data file. It could optionally also produce the junta's member billing.

**Possible product:**
A Spanish, WhatsApp-friendly mobile web app. The operator logs readings per source and per sector. The app validates them, keeps the monthly distribution log and generates the ASA semi-annual report and the electronic file for small users. A cheap billing module for juntas keeps them using it between reports.

**MVP:**
Reading capture (manual entry plus a photo), monthly log, and an export in the ASA file format for one junta. Built only after obtaining the ASA's file specification.

**Willingness to pay:**
Juntas would pay only a token amount; they are non-profits that pay a $0 canon. Private well users and pipa firms would pay more, but they will probably buy a meter vendor's telemetry bundle. A realistic model is **service plus software sold through meter or drilling contractors**, or funding by an NGO or donor per junta.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
$5–10/month per junta, or $50–100/year (**estimate**). $20–40/month for a private well user on the file route. Or a $100–300 one-off bundle with meter installation through a contractor.

**How to find first customers:**
Ask the ASA for its list of registered juntas, or get it through a transparency request. Work through WASH NGOs and drilling contractors. Phone and WhatsApp outreach to junta treasurers.

**Founder access:**
A non-local solo founder could build it, but selling needs a local partner: junta work is relationship-based, rural and done in person, and an NGO or contractor partnership is essential.

**Risks:**
The ASA ships a free upload portal or mobile app, as Salvadoran agencies usually do (see DTE, SPU, Aduana 360). The file route may be phased out in favour of telemetry, where hardware vendors capture the value. Enforcement on juntas may be soft, since they are politically sensitive. The market is small and poor.

**Kill condition:**
The ASA's file format turns out to be a simple web form in its own portal, or ASA staff or NGOs fill in junta reports for free, or interviews show that juntas file nothing and face no penalty.

**Score:** 4/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Domestic-worker employer compliance.** The ISSS special regime is voluntary. About 5% of more than 140,000 workers are enrolled. The domestic-work law is still pending and C189 is not ratified. No trigger. (ISSS form page: https://www.isss.gob.sv/servicios/instrucciones-para-el-llenado-del-formulario-aviso-de-inscripcion-de-empleador-sector-domestico/ ; ORMUSA: https://observatoriolaboral.ormusa.org/trabajo-domestico-un-trabajo-invisibilizado/ ; https://www.elsalvador.com/noticias/nacional/trabajadoras-del-hogar-ignoradas-por-estado-salvadoreno/1235546/2025/)
- **Cattle brand and sale paperwork.** Substitute: the MAG's procedure on **SIMPLE.SV**; transfer costs $33.90 with immediate delivery. (https://www.simple.sv/tramite/solicitud-de-matricula-de-fierro-para-herrar-ganado/ciudadano ; https://www.elsalvador.com/noticias/nacional/requisitos-venta-ganado-mag/1244514/2025/)
- **Bus and microbus subsidy compliance.** The compensation requires GPS with real-time transmission to the VMT; hardware vendors do this. Operators are squeezed and paid up to 6 months late. 9,092 permits. (https://www.elsalvador.com/dinero-y-negocios/transporte-autobuses-viceministerio-de-el-salvador/1277676/2026/ ; https://asamblea.gob.sv/node/11861)
- **Pyrotechnics monthly control register.** Real physical-plus-digital monthly control duty under the Ministry of Defence. Seasonal, tiny maker base, and a security regulator. (https://www.presidencia.gob.sv/intensificaran-los-controles-para-la-fabricacion-distribucion-y-venta-de-productos-pirotecnicos-ante-la-temporada/ ; http://www.registrodearmas.mil.sv/?page_id=2690)
- **Pawnshops.** Already AML-obligated parties under Decreto 426; covered by the country report's AML idea.
- **Artisanal fishing licences.** Licence every two years at a counter, fee of $11–16. No recurring report and no money. (https://www.transparencia.gob.sv/institutions/mag/services/9235.pdf)
- **Scrap dealers, tattoo studios, barbers, agroservicios.** No Salvadoran register or recurring-report obligation found in searches.

## 4. Method notes

What worked: queries naming the regulator in Spanish ("ASA", "juntas de agua inscripción", "pipas ASA") surfaced 2025–2026 lineamientos through local newspapers (El Mundo, El Salvador.com, Diario El Salvador) and the ASA's own service pages. The jurisprudencia.gob.sv and asamblea.gob.sv PDF vaults show up for decree queries.

What didn't: seed-list industries with no Salvadoran regulator (scrap, tattoo, agro-input shop registers) returned results from Argentina, Chile and Costa Rica. Counts of obliged operators are rarely published; the ASA has no public statistics on authorizations or wells. With the budget of 20 searches, the transport-subsidy documentation and the ASA file-format specification remain unverified.
