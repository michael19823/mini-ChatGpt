# Ethiopia: offline-industries pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. Budget: 20 WebSearch calls. I made 14 calls. The 14th was refused
("usage limit"), so I stopped there, as the instructions say. WebFetch was not used.
Everything below comes from search-result snippets. Anything not confirmed by a source is marked
**unverified** or **estimate**.

Context from the country report: a foreign solo founder can't bill Ethiopian SMEs by card. You
need a local partner or entity, and you bill in birr through Telebirr or bank transfer. In quiet
industries this matters even more, because buyers work in Amharic (or Afaan Oromo, Tigrinya and
others), deal face to face, and pay very little in USD terms. **Every idea below needs a local
co-founder or partner.**

## 2. Strongest opportunities

Neither idea clears the bar for building. They are written up because they are the best quiet
leads the regulator-first method surfaced.

### Opportunity: Edir ledger and Bureau-report book for Addis Ababa burial associations

**Industry:**
Edir (community burial and mutual-aid associations), Addis Ababa.

**Buyer:**
The Edir committee, meaning the chair, treasurer and secretary. These are volunteers, often
older members. A secondary buyer is the woreda/sub-city Edir council, or the Bureau itself as a
procurer.

**Trigger / Why now:**
In March 2024 the Addis Ababa City Administration approved a directive (signed by the head of the
City Women, Children & Social Affairs Bureau). It is the first state attempt to formalise Edirs.
Every Edir, existing or new, must register with the Bureau for certification. Edirs must also
audit their accounts, report assets and submit periodic audit reports. The directive gives Edirs
legal personality and makes them subject to the Commercial Code. Separately, Shega reports that
urbanisation and inflation strain Edir finances, which means more scrutiny of payouts.

**Current workflow:**
1. Members pay monthly contributions in cash to a collector or treasurer, who writes them in a
   paper ledger (general practice, **unverified** per Edir).
2. When a member dies, the committee pays the benefit in cash and records it, and members
   provide tent, chairs and labour.
3. Under the directive, the committee must now produce accounts, an asset list and an audit
   report for the Bureau at the woreda. This is presumably compiled by hand or by a hired
   bookkeeper (**unverified**).

**Pain:**
A new mandatory reporting duty falls on volunteer committees with no accounting training. Failing
to register or report presumably risks losing certification and legal status (**unverified**
enforcement). No evidence was found of fines or of committees complaining.

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper ledger books from stationers.
- Volunteer or hired bookkeepers and small audit firms.
- Possibly Telebirr/bank group-collection features and local "Edir apps" (**unverified**: the
  search for Edir apps was the refused call, so competitor diligence is incomplete).
- Generic Excel.

**Offline evidence:**
Edirs are neighbourhood bodies run by elected volunteers. The directive is the first
formalisation ever, so there was no prior reporting infrastructure. Their web presence is close to
zero.

**Offline channel:**
- The directive creates one Edir council per woreda, sub-city and city, which is a ready
  hierarchy to sell through.
- The Bureau's registration desks at woreda level.
- Kotebe Metropolitan University, which ran the 2024 census for the Bureau and so has the list.
- Telebirr agents and banks (CBE, Awash) that already chase Edir deposit accounts.

**Market count:**
6,796 Edirs in Addis Ababa (2024 Kotebe Metropolitan University survey commissioned by the
Bureau, via Shega). Other cities are not counted.

**The gap:**
No product found (**incomplete diligence**) that turns contribution collection, via Telebirr or
cash entry, into the Bureau's required accounts, asset register and audit pack in Amharic.

**Possible product:**
An Amharic mobile/USSD-friendly Edir ledger. It records member contributions (with Telebirr
reconciliation), payouts and assets, and prints the periodic report in the Bureau's format for
the woreda.

**MVP:**
A member list, a monthly contribution grid, a payout log, an asset list, and a one-click PDF
report in the directive's format. It would be sold via one woreda council as a pilot.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
ETB 100–300/month per Edir (about USD 1–2, **estimate**). Alternatively a bank or Telebirr
sponsors it in exchange for Edir deposits, which is a B2B2C model. Software alone has very low
willingness to pay. The realistic payers are a bank (deposit acquisition) or a done-for-you
bookkeeper.

**How to find first customers:**
Woreda Edir councils; the Bureau's registration list; CBE/Awash branch managers.

**Risks:**
- Volunteers resist paying.
- The Bureau may issue a free template or system.
- Banks or Telebirr may bundle this for free.
- Enforcement of the directive is unknown.
- Non-local founder access is close to zero: Amharic, community trust and Bureau relations are
  required.

**Kill condition:**
Any of these: (a) the Bureau does not actually enforce periodic audit reports; (b) a bank or
Telebirr already offers a free Edir module; (c) fewer than 5 of 20 interviewed Edir treasurers
would pay anything.

**Score:** 4/10
(Pain 4, Frequency 6, Mandatory 6, Fragmentation 2, Competition 6 unverified, Gap 5, Buyer
accessibility 6, WTP 2, MVP 8, Distribution 5. Founder access: needs a local.)

### Opportunity: Digital beekeeper-to-export lot traceability and residue-plan records for honey exporters

**Industry:**
Honey and beeswax exporters and their collector networks.

**Buyer:**
Quality or export manager at an Ethiopian honey exporter listed for the EU.

**Trigger / Why now:**
Ethiopia has been EU-listed for honey since 2008, which requires an approved residue monitoring
plan. Exporters prepare annual residue plans with fixed sampling levels and frequencies, and
samples come from members of the Ethiopian Honey and Beeswax Producers and Exporters Association.
In June–July 2024, UNIDO's GMAP validated a unified traceability system covering beekeeper
registration, harvest, transport, processing and sale. Its forms are currently **paper-based,
with digitisation planned**. EU honey rule changes (the AGRINFO notice on regulatory changes for
honey exports) add pressure. A mono-floral quality standards push followed in Oct 2025.

**Current workflow:**
1. Collectors buy crude honey from many small beekeepers and fill in paper registration and
   collection forms.
2. The exporter consolidates the paper into processing-batch records.
3. The exporter supports the annual residue-plan sampling and prepares the export documents and
   TRACES listing.

**Pain:**
If residue findings or traceability failures occur, the country's EU listing is at risk, and so
is every exporter's market. Linking many tiny beekeepers to a lot by hand is laborious.
Willingness to pay at exporter level is plausible but modest.

**Existing solutions:**
- The UNIDO/GMAP paper system, which donors are digitising, so a free donor-built tool is likely.
- Exporters' own in-house systems (UNIDO notes that EU exporters had implemented traceability
  individually).
- Generic agri-traceability vendors (e.g. TraceX, already active in Ethiopian coffee).

**Offline evidence:**
UNIDO states that the forms are paper. Sampling is coordinated through the association. There are
no software listings for Ethiopian honey traceability.

**Offline channel:**
- The Ethiopian Honey and Beeswax Producers and Exporters Association.
- UNIDO/GMAP project staff, who need an implementer for the digitisation step.
- The Ethiopian Apiculture Board (**unverified** name/status).
- The Addis ApiExpo trade fair (**unverified** whether it is still held).

**Market count:**
Not found. **Estimate: tens of exporters.** Ethiopia has millions of beekeepers, but they are not
buyers.

**The gap:**
Mobile capture of the paper GMAP forms at collector level, lot aggregation and a residue-plan
sample log, all in one place.

**Possible product:**
An offline-first Android app for collectors that mirrors the GMAP forms, syncs to an exporter
dashboard, and generates lot traceability and sampling records for EU buyers.

**MVP:**
Digitise the GMAP forms, aggregate them into lots, and export a PDF/CSV per lot.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
USD 100–300/month per exporter, or a donor-funded implementation contract (**estimate**). In
practice this is more likely a one-off donor tender than SaaS.

**How to find first customers:**
Association member list; UNIDO GMAP Ethiopia contacts; UNGM tenders.

**Risks:**
- Tiny market.
- A donor builds a free tool.
- Exporters keep in-house systems.
- Foreign founder access is possible only through a donor tender plus a local partner.

**Kill condition:**
UNIDO or the government commissions or releases a free digital version of the GMAP system, or
the count of active EU-listed exporters is under about 15.

**Score:** 3/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Livestock market traders and export suppliers.** The government ETLITS (MoA, built with
  USAID, EU, AU and World Bank support) is the traceability system. Proclamation 819/2014
  licensing is a one-off. A draft directive would remove brokers altogether. Traders pay in cash
  and have no software budget.
  ([Reporter](https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/39833/),
  [ETLITS](https://etlits.moa.gov.et/who-we-are),
  [LEAP/FAOLEX](https://leap.unep.org/en/countries/et/national-legislation/live-animals-marketing-proclamation-no-8192014))
- **Export abattoirs.** There are only 12, running at 10–30% utilisation. Their binding
  constraint is animal supply, not paperwork. Traceability is handled through government and
  donor channels.
  ([Dawan](https://www.dawan.africa/news/ethiopia-livestock-export-earnings-rise-35percent-to-dollar1283m),
  [Reporter](https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/article/traceability-prevents-meat-exporters-reaching-europe-north-american-markets))
- **Bajaj operators, Addis.** The city restricted bajajs in 2025 and is digitalising public
  transport, so the segment is shrinking.
  ([Addis Fortune](https://addisfortune.news/long-walk-to-destination),
  [Addis Fortune](https://addisfortune.net/articles/city-closes-door-on-new-bajajs/))
- **Overseas employment agencies.** There are few licensed agencies (an old figure of 20). The
  ministry's own systems are the substitute. Ethical and reputational risk is high.
  ([Reporter](https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/3093/),
  [ILO / Samuel Hall](https://www.samuelhall.org/publications/ilo-assessment-of-the-capacity-and-practices-of-overseas-private-employment-agencies-in-ethiopia/))
- **Artisanal gold.** The obligation is to sell to NBE, enforced through price premiums and
  licence revocation, not through a reporting workflow.
  ([Reporter](https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/45827/),
  [Shega](https://shega.co/news/governor-mamo-shakes-up-incentives-for-artisanal-gold-miners))
- **Scrap metal.** It is a state allocation channel (Ministry of Mines to steel mills). No private
  dealer register was found.
  ([Reporter](https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/26621/))
- **Equb.** No licensing or reporting directive was found.

## 4. Method notes

- What worked: English searches against the Ethiopian business press (The Reporter, Addis
  Fortune, Capital, Shega). These were the only sources of regulator actions and counts (Edir
  directive and census, bajaj association count, abattoir count). Donor documents (UNIDO, FAO,
  ILO) revealed paper workflows better than regulators did.
- What didn't work: regulator sites (EFDA, MoA, the city bureaus) gave no registers or counts
  through search. I made no Amharic-script queries because of the budget; that is the obvious next
  step (for example "የዕድር መመሪያ" for the Edir directive).
- Searches stopped at call 14 of 20 after a usage-limit refusal. The Edir competitor search was
  the refused call, so diligence for the top idea is incomplete.
- Overall: in Ethiopia, quiet industries are quiet mainly because the state or donors run the
  formal channel (ETLITS, NBE gold, eSW, ministry LMIS) and operators have little cash. There are
  no strong offline opportunities for a non-local solo founder.
