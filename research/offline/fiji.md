# Fiji: Offline (Quiet) Industries Pass

**Status:** 19 of 20 searches used. The first run was cut off by a usage-limit refusal after 3 searches. This report is the completed rerun after the limit reset. WebFetch was not used. Anything not backed by a search result is marked **unverified** or **estimate**.

**Context:** Fiji has about 0.9M people. The existing report (`research/countries/fiji.md`) found no standalone opportunity near the brief's benchmark of 5,000 buyers paying US$200–500/month. That ceiling is even lower for quiet industries. Two of them have a real 2025–2027 trigger:

- **Taxi base-and-stand reform:** every renewal needs a stand letter by 30 Sep 2027.
- **Liquor licensing reform:** the Law Reform Commission's final report is due 30 Oct 2026.

Most of the remaining quiet groups are regulated through paper registers and counter licensing, and very little money flows through them.

## 2. Opportunities

No quiet industry in Fiji reaches the bar. The two closest are written up below for completeness, with honest low scores.

### Opportunity: Taxi base/stand roster and LTA letter pack for base operators

**Industry:**
Taxi base and stand operators. These are private and municipal bases that dispatch affiliated taxis.

**Buyer:**
The owner or manager of a taxi base (private base company or municipal stand), who issues base or stand letters to affiliated permit holders.

**Trigger / Why now:**
LTA is moving from the open rank system (in place since April 2022) to a regulated Base and Stand system. Existing permit holders renewing must hold a stand letter from a gazetted stand before **30 September 2027**. Zone permit holders must submit base letters to LTA. In July 2026, LTA warned about applicants paying for stand letters before stands were gazetted.

**Current workflow:**
1. A permit holder asks a base or stand for a letter.
2. The base issues a paper letter, possibly charging a base fee (some municipal bases still charge one).
3. The permit holder carries the letter to the LTA counter for renewal.
4. The base tracks its affiliated taxis, fees and capacity in a notebook or spreadsheet (unverified).

**Pain:**
Operators struggle to get base letters (Minister's remarks in Parliament, May 2026). Stand capacity in urban areas is limited. Fraudulent pre-gazettal letter sales have been reported. Renewals fail without a letter.

**Existing solutions:**
Paper letters and LTA counter processing. Generic ride-hailing and dispatch apps exist (Fiji-specific ones were not verified). Spreadsheets. The taxi associations.

**Offline evidence:**
The base or stand letter is a paper artifact. Renewal happens at the LTA customer service centre. LTA tells operators to contact that centre for information. No base-management software turned up in any search.

**Offline channel:**
Taxi associations (one association was quoted on the 1,500 affected taxis). The LTA gazetted stand list, once published. Municipal councils that run stands.

**Market count:**
8,090 taxi permits (LTA figure via FBC; date unverified). The number of bases and stands, which is the real buyer pool, is unknown. Estimate: low hundreds.

**The gap:**
A verifiable record linking a base, its capacity and its affiliated permits. This could deter fake letters and speed up renewals. LTA might do this itself.

**Possible product:**
A simple base roster: affiliated permits, capacity, fees paid, and a generated letter with a verification code or QR that LTA counter staff can check.

**MVP:**
A web form plus PDF letter generator with QR verification, and a roster export.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
FJD 30–80/month per base (estimate). The ceiling is low.

**How to find first customers:**
The LTA gazette of taxi stands, taxi associations, and municipal councils (Suva, Lautoka, Nadi).

**Willingness to pay:**
Low. Base operators might pay a small fee. Drivers are capped at FJD 30,000 household income, so they won't pay. A done-for-you letter service would conflict with LTA's anti-fraud stance.

**Founder access:**
Needs a local founder. Without LTA buy-in the QR verification is worthless, and the market is relationship-driven.

**Risks:**
LTA builds its own register or rejects third-party letters. Policy reversal (permit freezes were lifted in the past). The buyer pool is tiny.

**Kill condition:**
LTA keeps the gazetted-stand allocation fully internal, or there are fewer than about 100 bases.

**Score:** 3/10

### Opportunity: FCCC price-control shelf-price compliance for small retailers

**Industry:**
Small retailers, canteens and supermarkets selling price-controlled goods.

**Buyer:**
The owner of an independent supermarket or canteen without a modern POS.

**Trigger / Why now:**
FCCC price control orders (fuel order 1 Feb 2025) and a review of the controlled-goods list. Recurring enforcement campaigns: more than 700 inspections before Easter 2025, which found 53 cases of non-compliance.

**Current workflow:**
1. FCCC publishes an order (PDF on PacLII or through the media).
2. The owner reads the maximum prices and hand-writes or prints shelf labels.
3. Inspectors check whether prices are displayed and stay under the maximum.

**Pain:**
Enforcement actions over missing price displays and prices above the maximum. The fines are not quantified in what I found.

**Existing solutions:**
POS vendors (Hike POS, Ideal POS and others, which also handle VMS fiscalization, so many shops already have a POS). FCCC public lists. Handwritten labels.

**Offline evidence:**
Price orders are published as PDFs. Compliance is checked by physical shelf inspection.

**Offline channel:**
Accredited VMS POS vendors as resellers, and wholesalers that supply canteens.

**Market count:**
Thousands of outlets (estimate; no register found).

**The gap:**
Pushing order changes automatically into shelf labels for shops without a POS price table. This gap is shrinking as VMS forces shops onto POS systems.

**Possible product:**
A price-order feed plus label printing, and a POS price-table import.

**MVP:**
Parse each FCCC order into a table and print labels.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
FJD 10–20/month (estimate). Shop owners are unlikely to pay.

**How to find first customers:**
Through wholesalers and POS vendors.

**Willingness to pay:**
Very low. Owners would only pay for a bundled POS feature.

**Founder access:**
A non-local founder could build it, but selling it needs POS partners.

**Risks:**
VMS POS adoption absorbs the feature. Orders change infrequently except for fuel.

**Kill condition:**
POS vendors already ship FCCC price tables.

**Score:** 2/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Digital second-hand/pawn register:** the law expects a paper book signed by police, and buyers are small. The substitute is a stationery register.
- **Scrap metal compliance:** a licence only (form, fee, police clearance), likely tens of dealers.
- **Cattle movement permits:** BAF authorises each movement itself under its SOPs. The users are smallholders, and there is nothing for third-party software to plug into.
- **Abattoir and meat compliance:** abattoirs are dominated by the statutory Fiji Meat Industry Board, and inspection is done by government meat inspectors.
- **Pesticide registers:** the obligation is product registration by a few importers at Koronivia.
- **Household employer FNPF:** households won't pay, coverage is voluntary, and payroll tools exist. The FNPF cut to 8% from 1 Aug 2026 is a payroll-vendor update, not an opening.
- **Cane harvesting gangs and lorry operators:** the pain is price and labour shortage. FSC controls the payment flow.
- **Inshore and commercial fishing licences:** licence-only obligation, low-income buyers.
- **Bus and minibus fare and QAMS submissions:** infrequent, with few mid-sized operators.
- **Liquor licensing:** licences are valid for 5 years. Put on watch until the Law Reform Commission final report (due 30 Oct 2026).

## 4. Method notes

- **What worked:** PacLII (statutes, prescribed forms, price orders, BAF declarations). Local news sites (fijivillage, fbcnews, fijitimes, fijionenews, maitvfiji), which carry the regulator's own figures and enforcement counts, such as LTA's 8,090 permits, FCCC's 700 inspections and Fisheries' roughly 900 licences.
- **What didn't:** counting buyers. Fiji publishes almost no licensee registers online, so base, liquor-licence and dealer counts are missing.
- **Language:** English works. Fijian and Hindi queries were not needed for regulator content.
- **Overall:** Fiji's quiet industries are licence-at-the-counter regimes run by the regulator itself. They rarely require recurring third-party filings, which is the pattern the brief rewards.
