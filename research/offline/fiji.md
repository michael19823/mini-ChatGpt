## 2. Strongest opportunities

**None.** No quiet industry in Fiji reached the evidence bar in this pass. The two verified obligations (scrap metal licensing and second-hand dealer registers) are real, and the second is literally a paper book. But neither has a recurring multi-authority filing, a 2024–2027 trigger, or enough buyers. Scoring them with the template would only produce numbers with nothing behind them. If forced, both would score about 2/10.

## 3. Rejected

- **Digital second-hand/pawn register (police-inspectable):** the Act expects a paper register that police sign on inspection, so the law does not require digital submission. The buyer pool is small and low-margin, and the substitute is a stationery register book. Any digital register would also need police acceptance (unverified).
- **Scrap metal dealer compliance:** the regime is licence-based (form, fee, police clearance), so there is nothing to file regularly. There are likely only tens of licensees (estimate), and enforcement runs through ministry and police warnings.
- **Household employer payroll:** households will not pay for software, and payroll tools already exist (DigiPayroll and others; see the existing report).
- **Market vendor licensing:** councils collect the fees themselves, and vendors can only afford very little.

## 4. Method notes

- PacLII (paclii.org) is the best source for Fiji statutes and prescribed forms. A search for "<industry> Act Fiji licence register" goes straight to the Act and its forms.
- Local news (maitvfiji.com, fijitimes) is useful for enforcement evidence.
- I could not test the other query patterns because the search quota ran out after 3 calls. A follow-up pass should start with LTA taxi and minibus permits, then MoA/BAF livestock movement and abattoir rules, then the Pesticides Act registers.
