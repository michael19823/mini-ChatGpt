# Gabon: offline-industries pass

**Market class:** small economy (about 2.4M people), French-speaking, CEMAC/XAF zone. Searches used: 20 of 20, all in French.
**Overall verdict:** thin. Gabon's quiet industries are mostly either very small in operator count, run as state-directed clean-ups (ministry census → commission → list), or squeezed by public payers that don't pay (CNAMGS, state school subsidies). None reaches the brief's bar. The best leads are small, service-plus-software plays that need a local partner. This report doesn't repeat the country report's opportunities (DGI e-invoicing, DTS/CNSS payroll, EUDR timber).

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: CNAMGS claims-and-rejection desk for pharmacies and small clinics

**Industry:**  
Pharmacies (officines, dépôts pharmaceutiques) and small private clinics accredited ("conventionnées") with CNAMGS.

**Buyer:**  
Owner-pharmacist (titulaire) of an independent officine, or the administrative manager of a small private clinic.

**Trigger / Why now:**  
In early 2026 the CNAMGS said it would reject invoices and coverage from any structure that had not deposited its 2026 administrative files, including the files of prescribing practitioners. The deadline was 15 Feb 2026, extended to the end of February, with a final cut-off of 15 Mar 2026. The CNAMGS is also moving to electronic care sheets (remote transmission) and a "zero paper" care pathway by end-2027. 122,000 beneficiaries were suspended provisionally, which creates eligibility exceptions at the counter.

**Current workflow:**
1. The patient presents a CNAMGS card and a paper prescription; the pharmacy dispenses and keeps the paper care sheet.
2. Staff batch the paper sheets and compile a claim to CNAMGS by period (exact format unverified).
3. CNAMGS rejects lines (suspended beneficiary, non-compliant prescriber, items not on the reimbursable list) and pays late or not at all.
4. The pharmacy tracks receivables by hand. The syndicate (Sypharga) negotiates the backlog collectively and sometimes suspends tiers payant (as on 1 Jan 2025).

**Pain:**  
Unpaid CNAMGS debt to pharmacies was about XAF 8bn in Oct 2025 (over XAF 4bn earlier). Pharmacies refused CNAMGS prescriptions, including for cancer patients. Pharmacies also complained about the new list of reimbursable medicines. The rejection risk under the 2026 file rule falls on the provider.

**Existing solutions:**  
- Pharmacy management software (French LGOs such as Winpharma, LGPI/id., LEO, VisioPharm), which handle stock and billing but not CNAMGS-specific rejections. Which ones are actually used in Gabon is unverified.  
- The CNAMGS's own planned electronic system (SEED / remote transmission), which may come with a provider portal.  
- Sypharga, which negotiates collectively.  
- Manual spreadsheets kept by pharmacy staff.

**Offline evidence:**  
Care sheets are on paper today, and CNAMGS digitalisation is still a project. Provider files are deposited physically. No Gabon-specific claims software turned up in searches.

**Offline channel:**  
Sypharga (Syndicat des pharmaciens du Gabon), which already organises officines around the CNAMGS dispute, and the Ordre des pharmaciens. Pharmacies cluster in Estuaire (Libreville/Owendo), so visiting them in person is feasible.

**Market count:**  
About 600 pharmaceutical establishments, including 122 officines, mostly in Estuaire (Gabonreview).

**The gap:**  
A per-claim view of what CNAMGS rejected and why, receivables ageing by period, and checking eligibility and prescriber status at the counter before dispensing. The general LGOs don't model CNAMGS rules.

**Possible product:**  
A lightweight claims register: scan or type each CNAMGS dispense, check it against the reimbursable list and prescriber list, produce the period claim file, then reconcile payments and rejections. Aggregated, anonymised receivables could also give Sypharga data for its negotiations.

**MVP:**  
A spreadsheet-import or tablet register plus a receivables and rejection dashboard for 5 officines, done-for-you onboarding.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
XAF 30,000–60,000/month (about USD 50–100) per officine (estimate). Buyers would probably pay only if it is bundled with a service (claims clerk).

**How to find first customers:**  
Sypharga leadership, the Ordre des pharmaciens roll, and walk-ins in Libreville.

**Risks:**  
The main cause of the pain is that the payer isn't paying, and software can't fix that. CNAMGS SEED may make its own tool mandatory. With 122 officines, revenue tops out around USD 100k/year. A local partner is needed.

**Kill condition:**  
CNAMGS ships a mandatory provider portal with rejection reasons by 2027, or interviews show pharmacies already know which claims were rejected and the only problem is cash.

**Founder access:**  
Needs a local partner (a pharmacist or ex-CNAMGS clerk). A non-local solo founder would struggle.

**Score:** 3/10

### Opportunity: Accreditation and compliance file keeper for private schools

**Industry:**  
Private lay and faith primary and secondary schools.

**Buyer:**  
The founder-director (fondateur) of a private lay school, or the education secretariat of a faith network.

**Trigger / Why now:**  
In 2025–2026 the Education Ministry reviewed private schools: 436 files examined, 380 kept, 221 fully habilitated, and more than 100 rejected. It announced a national "caravane" to track down more than 100 illegal schools and publish a blacklist. Criteria: regulatory acts (opening decree), infrastructure, approved programmes, and teacher qualifications. Separately, private schools were caught making irregular CEP exam registrations.

**Current workflow:**
1. The founder compiles a paper file: opening authorisation, premises documents, teacher diplomas and contracts, programme.
2. The file is deposited at the ministry and reviewed by a commission.
3. Every year, the school updates teacher lists, pupil numbers and exam candidate lists by hand.
4. Inspection visits check that the file matches what is on the ground.

**Pain:**  
Closure or a blacklisting for a missing document. Schools are also financially fragile because of state subsidy arrears (faith schools reportedly unsubsidised for 8 years).

**Existing solutions:**  
- Custom school-management builds by local agencies (Kolonell quotes XAF 5–16M for higher-education systems).  
- Xgest (Terza group), piloted in 74 public secondary schools and hosted by ANINF.  
- Generic international school SaaS.  
- Paper and the school secretary.

**Offline evidence:**  
Commission review of paper files, an inspection "caravane", a blacklist. No ministry portal was found.

**Offline channel:**  
Private-school founders' associations (lay and faith education secretariats; exact association names unverified), and the ministry's published list of habilitated and rejected schools (if published) as a call list.

**Market count:**  
1,938 private lay schools (ministry 2023 statistics via Gabonmediatime); 436 files in the 2025–26 review.

**The gap:**  
Nobody keeps an always-current "inspection-ready" file per school: teacher diplomas, contracts and CNSS status, opening decree, premises, pupil counts, CEP candidate eligibility.

**Possible product:**  
A simple file-and-roster app that produces the ministry file and annual returns, with expiry reminders and a CEP candidate list checked against enrolment records.

**MVP:**  
A checklist plus document vault plus teacher and pupil roster export for 10 schools, sold with a done-for-you file preparation service.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
XAF 10,000–25,000/month per school, or XAF 100,000–250,000 per file preparation (estimate). Schools would pay for the service more readily than for software.

**How to find first customers:**  
Lists of rejected and conditionally accepted schools, private-school associations, WhatsApp groups of school directors (unverified).

**Risks:**  
The review is a one-off campaign rather than a recurring filing. Schools are cash-poor. Better-off schools would buy full school-management software, which already exists. The ministry may build its own system (Xgest/ANINF precedent).

**Kill condition:**  
The ministry doesn't require annual re-filing, or interviews show directors handle the file once and forget it.

**Founder access:**  
Needs a local partner.

**Score:** 3/10

### Opportunity: Guard roster and licence-compliance kit for private security firms

**Industry:**  
Private security and guarding firms.

**Buyer:**  
Owner or operations manager of a guarding company (most are unarmed; 23 applied for non-lethal arms).

**Trigger / Why now:**  
A new ordinance regulating private security and transport of valuables (draft presented Jan 2025). Mandatory identification at DGAT, 17–31 Dec 2025. An authorisation commission reviewed 93 files in June 2026. An arrêté of 4 May 2026 sets conditions for selling arms, including non-lethal ones. The new framework is expected to regulate agent training and professional credentials (details unverified).

**Current workflow:**
1. Build a paper authorisation file: request to the minister, legalised ID, criminal record, proof of nationality.
2. Keep agent lists, posting schedules and (for armed firms) a weapons register on paper or Excel.
3. Run payroll and CNSS/CNAMGS separately.

**Pain:**  
Losing the right to operate. Clients (oil, mining, embassies) may start asking for proof of authorisation and agent credentials.

**Existing solutions:**  
Excel and paper; generic guard-tour and workforce apps (international); payroll in Sage.

**Offline evidence:**  
Counter filing at DGAT, room 308. A commission reviewed paper files in 48 hours.

**Offline channel:**  
The DGAT list of applicants and authorised firms (if it is published), and a direct call list of the 93.

**Market count:**  
93 applicants in June 2026 (Gabonmediatime).

**The gap:**  
Per-agent credential and training tracking matched to the new rules, plus a weapons register.

**Possible product / MVP:**  
Agent register with documents and expiry dates, an authorisation-file generator, and a monthly client compliance report.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
XAF 30,000–100,000/month per firm (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**  
The DGAT applicant list, and procurement contacts at oil and mining firms that buy guarding.

**Risks:**  
Only about 90 buyers. The detailed agent obligations have not been published. Generic workforce tools could cover most of the need.

**Kill condition:**  
The implementing texts set no per-agent registration or training-record duty.

**Founder access:**  
Needs a local partner. The sector requires Gabonese-national managers.

**Score:** 2/10

## 4. Method notes

- What worked: French queries naming the regulator plus "recensement", "régularisation", "sommées de se conformer" or "agrément". Gabonmediatime and Gabonreview report ministry communiqués and give counts (93 security firms, 436 school files, 600 pharmaceutical establishments).
- What didn't: Searching for official registers or forms. Gabon publishes almost no online registers or PDF forms. Domestic-worker and taxi-tax queries returned Moroccan or generic EOR content.
- Pattern: in Gabon, regulators formalise through a one-off census followed by a commission. That produces campaigns rather than recurring filings, and the state often builds the tool itself (eTax, SEED, COBAC platform, digital licence). Distribution needs a local partner in every case.
