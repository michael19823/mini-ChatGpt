## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Okada Union Rider Register (L.I. 2519 onboarding and renewal desk for motorcycle and tricycle unions)

**Industry:**
Commercial motorcycle (okada) and tricycle (pragya) transport.

**Buyer:**
The general secretary or branch chairman of a **recognised okada/pragya union** (district or station branch). The second buyer type is a **registered okada company**, which must run at least 50 bikes in each of at least 8 regions. Driving schools are a partner, not the buyer.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The Road Traffic (Amendment) Act 2025 legalised commercial motorcycles. **L.I. 2519 (Road Traffic Regulations 2026)** sets the rules.
- Reg. 164 bans commercial riding without a **DVLA commercial rider's licence**. Licence conditions: age 21+, a Ghana Card, **proof of membership of a licensed commercial transport organisation**, a medical exam, and theory, practical and in-traffic tests. The licence is renewed **every 2 years**.
- NRSA says the system **launches in Q4 2026**. **From 1 Jan 2027 NRSA registers only unions and companies.** Unions "undertake a formal registration process and update records with NRSA". NRSA passes member details to DVLA for number plates and licences.

**Current workflow:** *(presumed; must be validated with 5 union branches)*
1. A rider joins a station or branch and pays dues. The secretary writes the rider into a membership book or Excel sheet.
2. The secretary collects photocopies of the Ghana Card, the motorcycle papers and, later, the medical certificate and test results.
3. The union compiles a member list for NRSA, by NRSA template or upload (format unknown), and resubmits it whenever members join or leave.
4. DVLA issues plates and licences against the list. The secretary chases riders whose medical, test or licence documents are missing or expiring (2-year cycle).
5. Dues, levies and fines are tracked separately in cash books or by mobile money.

**Pain:**
- The union's legal standing (recognition) and every member's right to work now depend on an accurate, current member register.
- Thousands of first-time registrations will hit unions in a short window around 1 Jan 2027.
- Motorcycle deaths are rising and NRSA is "tightening regulation" (LRC coverage), so enforcement pressure is likely. *Unverified:* the exact sanctions on unions for bad records.

**Existing solutions:**
- **NRSA's TransportGhana platform.** It covers e-registration of unions, vehicles and drivers for trotro/bus. It is the most likely substitute if it extends to okada.
- DVLA's own licensing systems.
- Paper membership books, Excel and WhatsApp groups *(presumed)*.
- Driving schools (GHANADRIVE, "ready to provide professional training for okada riders") for the test component.
- Ride-hailing and okada-booking apps (e.g. OkadaX, which blogs about the new rules) serve passengers, not union compliance.

**Offline evidence:**
- The sector was illegal until 2025, so there is no installed software base.
- Unions are station-based. NRSA's own description is "unions update their records".
- No union-management SaaS for Ghana turned up in searches.

**Offline channel:**
- NRSA stakeholder engagements with unions (NRSA is running sensitisation ahead of the Q4 2026 launch).
- The **Okada Riders Association of Ghana** (ORAG, ghokada.com) and the motor-riders wings of GPRTU and PROTOA.
- Driving schools training riders for the tests (GHANADRIVE), and LRC (Legal Resources Centre) outreach.
- Station visits in Accra and Kumasi. Okada stations are physical and MMDAs are designating them.

**Market count:**
Unknown. The rider count was not found (*unverified*; likely tens to hundreds of thousands nationwide). Number of unions: not found. Companies must have at least 400 bikes each (≥8 regions × ≥50), so they will be few.

**The gap:**
NRSA's system is built for the regulator's view. Branch secretaries still have to do their own work:
- collect and track rider documents and expiries (medical, tests, licence, 2-year renewal);
- produce clean member uploads for NRSA;
- tie membership status to dues payment.

Whether NRSA will accept bulk uploads from a union tool is **unknown**.

**Possible product:**
An Android app for union branch secretaries. It works as a member register with a document checklist per rider (Ghana Card, bike papers, medical, test results, licence), expiry alerts, and an export in NRSA's upload format. Dues are collected by MoMo, with a rider's "good standing" tied to payment.

**MVP:**
- Member register.
- Document photos with status flags.
- CSV/PDF export matching the NRSA member template.
- MoMo dues ledger.

Pilot with 3 branches in Greater Accra.

**Pricing hypothesis:** *(estimate)*
GH₵1–3 per active rider per month, collected through dues: the union adds a small levy. Or GH₵200–500 per branch per month. A one-off onboarding fee per new rider (GH₵10–20) during the 2027 rush. The buyer pays for a **service plus software** (help with the first bulk registration), not for software alone.

**How to find first customers:**
ORAG and the union motor-riders wings, NRSA union-engagement meetings, driving schools, and walking the stations.

**Risks:**
- **Platform risk:** TransportGhana may let riders or unions self-register directly with no export path.
- Union politics and rival associations.
- Very low willingness to pay per rider.
- Policy delay: the date has already shifted to 2027.
- Founder access: **needs a local partner.** The sale happens at stations, in Twi, Ga and Hausa, and through union executives. A non-local solo founder could not realistically sell this.

**Kill condition:**
- NRSA's okada module takes per-rider self-registration with documents (no union intermediary data entry).
- Or 5 branch secretaries say they already manage with WhatsApp and will not collect a levy.

**Score:** 5/10
*(pain 6, frequency 6 (onboarding rush, then renewals and churn), mandatory 9, fragmentation 4, competition 5, incumbent gap 5, buyer access 5, WTP 3, MVP 8, distribution 5; discounted for regulator-platform risk and local-only sales.)*

### Opportunity: Scrap Depot Purchase Register (GIISDEC licensing and collector traceability)

**Industry:**
Scrap metal collection and aggregation.

**Buyer:**
Owner of a **scrap depot or aggregator yard** that buys from street collectors and sells to local steel makers or (legally or not) exporters.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- GIISDEC is leading a licensing regime with the Ministry of Local Government and the Attorney-General's Department. It covers collectors, aggregators, buyers, agents, brokers and manufacturers.
- It was announced as mandatory **"effective January 2026"**, with sanctions for unlicensed trading. Collectors must **operate under a depot to ensure traceability**.
- Its stated aims are to curb theft of national infrastructure (cables, rail), tax evasion and pollution.
- In parallel, the trade ministry considered (June 2025) forcing a share of non-ferrous scrap to local processors.

**Current workflow:** *(presumed)*
1. A collector brings a load. The depot weighs it on a platform scale and pays cash or MoMo.
2. Purchases are noted in an exercise book, if at all. Seller identity is rarely recorded.
3. Sales to mills or exporters are invoiced, and records are reconstructed at year-end for tax.
4. Under the new regime, the depot must presumably show registered collectors and a traceable source for each lot. *Details unpublished.*

**Pain:**
- Licence loss or sanctions.
- Police seizure of stolen-infrastructure scrap.
- Pressure from local processors (e.g. Recyclers Ghana) for traceable supply.
- *Unverified:* any actual 2026 enforcement.

**Existing solutions:**
- Exercise books and weighbridge tickets.
- GIISDEC's forthcoming system (format unknown).
- Generic POS and accounting apps.
- Association guidance (PROSDAG). No Ghana scrap-yard software was found.

**Offline evidence:**
- An informal cash trade.
- Associations lobby via press conferences, not online.
- The regime is still being drafted by a committee.

**Offline channel:**
- PROSDAG and the other scrap dealers' association.
- GIISDEC registration exercises.
- Steel mills that buy from depots, which want traceable supply and could mandate the tool.
- Physical yards in Accra and Kumasi.

**Market count:**
Not found (*unverified*).

**The gap:**
A per-purchase seller-ID and weight log that rolls up into whatever GIISDEC returns become. Its value depends entirely on the unpublished rules.

**Possible product:**
A mobile purchase register. Each line records collector ID (Ghana Card plus GIISDEC collector number), material, weight, photo and payment. It produces a lot-level traceability sheet for buyers or mills and a periodic GIISDEC return.

**MVP:**
Purchase log, collector roster with licence status, and a monthly PDF/CSV summary.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
GH₵150–400/month per depot *(estimate)*. Mill-sponsored seats are possible.

**How to find first customers:**
Associations, GIISDEC registration events, and mill supplier lists.

**Risks:**
- The regime may never be enforced. Ghana has had a nominal export ban since 1980 that is widely evaded.
- Dealers may resist traceability.
- GIISDEC may issue paper books.
- Founder access: **needs a local.**

**Kill condition:**
- GIISDEC regulations contain no per-purchase record-keeping or periodic return.
- Or licensing is still not live by Q1 2027.

**Score:** 4/10
*(pain 5, frequency 9, mandatory 6 (unconfirmed), fragmentation 3, competition 7, gap 6, buyer access 4, WTP 3, MVP 8, distribution 4; discounted for implementation uncertainty.)*

### Opportunity: 48-Hour Training Logbook for Driving Schools (L.I. 2519)

**Industry:**
Driver training.

**Buyer:**
Owner of a DVLA-certified driving school. GHANADRIVE or ASSODRIVE could act as a group buyer.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- L.I. 2519 reg. 39(1) requires **48 hours of certified basic training** before a learner's licence.
- The association has been publicly demanding enforcement (Sept 2026), with a public spat with the DVLA CEO over training quality (Aug 2026).
- Okada riders will need theory and practical tests from 2027.

**Current workflow:** *(presumed)*
- The school keeps a paper attendance sheet and issues a certificate.
- DVLA accepts the certificate when the test is booked.
- Fraud ("mushroom" schools, certificates without training) is a public complaint.

**Pain:**
- Certificate credibility. Schools want DVLA to enforce the rule so that informal teachers lose business.

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper logbooks and certificates.
- A **proposed DVLA national digital platform** tracking learners "from first payment to licence, including logged training hours". This is the main threat.

**Offline evidence:**
Paper certificates, and an advocacy fight carried out in the press.

**Offline channel:**
GHANADRIVE, ASSODRIVE and the Association of Certified Driving Instructors.

**Market count:**
230 registered schools (DVLA project) to 318 schools and 937 instructors (2019).

**The gap:**
A verifiable lesson log (GPS, timestamps, instructor sign-off) and a QR certificate DVLA could check. It is only useful if DVLA accepts it.

**Possible product:**
An instructor app that logs each lesson and issues a QR-verifiable 48-hour certificate, with a school dashboard.

**MVP:**
Lesson log, learner hours counter and a QR certificate page.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
GH₵5–10 per learner certificate, or GH₵200/month per school *(estimate)*.

**How to find first customers:**
Association executives. They want enforcement, so a tamper-proof log helps their case.

**Risks:**
- DVLA builds its own tracker.
- Only ~10% of drivers use schools, so volumes are low.
- A small market.

**Kill condition:**
DVLA announces its own learner-tracking platform, or refuses to recognise third-party certificates.

**Score:** 3/10
*(pain 4, frequency 8, mandatory 7, fragmentation 2, competition 6, gap 4, buyer access 8, WTP 3, MVP 8, distribution 6; tiny market and regulator-platform risk.)*

## 4. Method notes

**What worked:**
- English-language Ghanaian press (Graphic, GNA, MyJoyOnline, Citi, GBC, B&FT) queried as "<regulator> + <industry> + register/licence + 2026". Regulator names (NRSA, GIISDEC, MoFFA, WRC, Pharmacy Council, BoG) turned up triggers and counts quickly.
- L.I. numbers (2519, 2468, 2408, 1827) were the best anchors.

**What didn't:**
- Searches for counts of operators. Registers are rarely published.
- Searches for vendor or software names. There are none, which is expected.

**The recurring Ghana pattern:**
The regulator builds its own platform (BoG forex, NRSA TransportGhana, GoldBod traceability, GRA E-VAT). That kills most "software between operator and regulator" ideas. What remains is the intermediary's own back office: the union, the depot, the school.

The search budget was cut off at about 25 calls ("usage limit"). Planned follow-ups not run:
- okada rider count;
- details of the BoG susu digitisation guideline;
- GIISDEC regime status in 2026;
- MMDA business-operating-permit fragmentation.

Research model: Opus
