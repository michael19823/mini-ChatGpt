# Grenada: offline (quiet) industries pass

**Researched:** 2026-10-05. **Searches used:** 4 of 8 (stopped early: the market is a microstate of about 125k people, and the searches found no regulator register or trigger worth more spend).

## Verdict

No opportunity is strong enough to publish. Grenada has few operators in each quiet industry. The regulators publish no usable registers online. The obligations are old, with no 2024-2027 trigger found. A short honest report is the right output.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

None. The only candidate (an OECS payroll and filing add-on) is already in `research/countries/grenada.md`. This pass found nothing that raises it.

## 3. Rejected

- **Household payroll for domestic workers:** the buyer is a consumer. Willingness to pay is near zero, and the number of households is unknown.
- **Dealer registers (pawn, scrap, second-hand):** no Grenada register or statute was found. With a population of about 125k, the operator count is too small to matter.
- **Bus and taxi licence management:** about 1,400 buses (unverified). Owner-operators run on thin margins, and the Transport Board is the only receiving body.
- **Pesticide dealer records:** a Board licence regime exists, but the dealer count is tiny and there is no new trigger.

## 4. Method notes

- The regulator-first queries returned almost nothing for Grenada. Official registers and gazettes are not indexed, and the search engine fell back to other countries' laws.
- The search results mention expected 2026 NIS rates of 7.25% for the employer and 6.25% for the employee. The first-round report cites 7% for the employer. This is unverified and should be checked against nisgrenada.org if the payroll idea is pursued.
- A real pass would need the Grenada Parliament's S.R.O. archive (grenadaparliament.gd) or the Transport Board and Police licensing desks. The expected return is low.
- Founder access: an English-speaking common-law market, so a foreign founder can sell there. The blocker is market size, not access.
