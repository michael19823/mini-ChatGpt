# Guinea (Guinée, Conakry): offline / quiet-industries pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. Search budget: 20 WebSearch calls, all used; one was refused once for a usage limit and then retried. The queries were almost all in French. WebFetch was not used. Read this alongside `research/countries/guinea.md`. That report's mining-subcontractor, payroll-to-portal (eTax/E-CNSS) and e-Bilan ideas are not repeated here.

**Headline:** Guinea's quiet industries are regulated mostly through **bans, suspensions and licence purges**, not through recurring paperwork that software could carry. Artisanal gold mining has been suspended since June 2024 and banned outright since July 2025. Timber cutting stops every rainy season. Cross-border transhumance was banned for 2025–26. 41 quarry permits were withdrawn in March 2026. The one real 2026 paperwork trigger is gold: a July 2026 decree requires local refining and creates a **national gold-traceability register**. The state itself is building the platform for it, though, and the obliged operators are a few dozen comptoirs. No strong indie opportunity was found. This is a short, honest report.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

Only one idea survives, and it is weak.

### Opportunity: Gold comptoir traceability and export-dossier register (decree of July 2026)

**Industry:**
Gold buying and export houses (*comptoirs d'achat et d'exportation d'or*), their collectors, and semi-industrial gold miners.

**Buyer:**
The owner or compliance officer of a licensed comptoir in Conakry, Kankan or Siguiri. A secondary buyer is the newly approved local refinery, which must receive gold with traceable provenance and issue certified bars.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- Decree of 8 July 2026 (announced late June, published 4–8 July). Raw-gold exports are banned after a 90-day transition, which ends around early October 2026, i.e. now. During the transition, exports require "enhanced declarations" and a compliance plan filed with the Ministry of Mines and Geology.
- All gold must be refined at a state-approved refinery in Guinea, and only refined, certified, numbered bars may be exported.
- Each export needs a certificate of analysis, a certificate of origin and an export authorisation.
- A national gold-traceability register must record every operation, from production through collection, transport and refining to export, following the OECD due-diligence guidance for high-risk areas. It is backed by AML/CFT obligations and beneficial-ownership disclosure.
- A new export-authorisation procedure was reported on 3 Oct 2026.

**Current workflow (inferred; not confirmed by an operator):**
1. Collectors buy gold upcountry and record weight, seller and price in a paper or Excel ledger.
2. The comptoir consolidates the lots and has them assayed at the BNE/BCRG counter.
3. It now sends the gold to an approved refinery and receives bars with an analysis certificate.
4. It assembles the export dossier (origin, analysis, authorisation, customs, BCRG repatriation) and re-keys the lot data into whatever the ministry's traceability platform becomes.

**Pain:**
- The obligations are new and stack up: AML, KYC on suppliers, provenance at lot level, and refinery hand-off.
- Non-compliance means the export is blocked, which is the comptoir's entire revenue.
- There is no operator complaint in the sources; this is inferred from the decree.

**Existing solutions:**
1. The state's own "Plateforme nationale de traçabilité de l'or", mandated by the decree. It covers producer registration, production declarations, movement tracking and certificates of origin, and is the main substitute.
2. BNE/BCRG assay and buying counters (Kankan, Conakry).
3. International responsible-gold traceability tools used by refiners, e.g. LBMA-style due-diligence software and blockchain provenance pilots. Specific products active in Guinea are unverified.
4. Mining/business-law firms and consultants preparing compliance plans.
5. Paper and Excel ledgers.

**Offline evidence:**
- The decree speaks of registers and certificates, and the platform does not exist yet.
- Assay and counter work happens in person at the BNE/BCRG counters.
- No Guinean software vendor listing was found for comptoir management.

**Offline channel:**
- Ministry of Mines list of approved comptoirs (licences are granted by ministerial order).
- The Chamber of Mines (CMG).
- The approved refineries, which every comptoir must now pass through. A refinery is the natural single distribution partner.
- In-person visits to the comptoir cluster in Conakry/Kankan.

**Market count:**
Unknown. A few dozen licensed comptoirs is an estimate. A handful of refineries are approved or in the pipeline (unverified).

**The gap:**
A lot-level ledger that the comptoir controls: supplier KYC, purchase, assay, refinery receipt, bar number and export dossier. It would export into the state platform, once that exists, and produce the OECD due-diligence file that foreign bar buyers and refiners ask for.

**Possible product:**
A French-language, offline-first lot ledger for comptoirs. It captures supplier ID and purchases, tracks lots through assay and refining to bar numbers, and prints the export dossier and an OECD Annex II–style due-diligence report.

**MVP:**
A spreadsheet-like lot register with supplier KYC fields, plus a PDF generator for the compliance plan, the lot-provenance sheet and the export checklist.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
USD 200–500 per month per comptoir (estimate). It would be sold as software plus a set-up service, i.e. configuring the compliance plan. Comptoirs move large values, so they could pay, but buyers in this sector prefer discretion and relationships.

**Willingness to pay:**
The money is there. Buyers would more likely pay for a done-for-you compliance service (a consultant plus a tool) than for self-serve software.

**Founder access:**
Not realistic for a non-local solo founder. The sector is opaque, cash-heavy and politically supervised, AML exposure is high, and selling needs an in-country partner (law firm or refinery).

**How to find first customers:**
Through the approved refineries and the Ministry of Mines comptoir list, via a Conakry mining-law firm.

**Risks:**
- The state platform makes the private ledger redundant.
- Reputational and AML risk in serving gold traders.
- Very small buyer count.
- Policy reversals, e.g. artisanal mining reopening or refinery rules changing.
- Comptoirs may prefer *not* to have detailed records.

**Kill condition:**
- The ministry's traceability platform goes live with comptoir-side lot entry and certificate generation.
- Or fewer than about 20 licensed comptoirs exist.
- Or the refineries supply provenance tooling to comptoirs for free.

**Score:** 3/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Artisanal gold miners:** banned nationwide since July 2025 (https://guineenews.org/2025/07/14/siguiri-face-a-la-degradation-de-lenvironnement-le-gouvernement-interdit-lexploitation-aurifere-artisanale/).
- **Timber, charcoal and forest-transport permits:**
  - Cutting resumed on 1 Oct 2026 only for approved artisanal operators holding a professional card and an environmental specifications agreement, with transport documents (https://guineenews.org/2026/10/01/guinee-la-coupe-et-le-transport-du-bois-reprennent-des-ce-1er-octobre-sous-conditions/?amp=1).
  - That is real paperwork, but it is enforced through roadblocks and seasonal bans. The operators are informal and the permits are counter-issued, so a tool has nothing to file into.
- **Quarries:** 41 permits were withdrawn in March 2026 and the state is cleaning the cadastre (https://www.financialafrik.com/2026/03/11/guinee-letat-retire-41-permis-de-carrieres-pour-assainir-le-cadastre-minier-et-securiser-les-investissements). Titles are already covered by DAMANDA.
- **Money changers:** about 50 offices, and the street trade is outside the system (https://www.ecofinanceguinee.com/les-realites-du-metier-de-cambiste/).
- **Livestock trade:** transhumance was banned for 2025–26 (https://guineenews.org/2025/01/24/elevage-la-guinee-interdit-pour-trois-mois-la-transhumance-transfrontaliere-des-betails/).
- **Abattoirs:** two public abattoirs serve Conakry (https://guineenews.org/2023/06/12/boucheries-des-abattoirs-en-dessous-des-normes-de-labattage-et-du-traitement-de-la-viande/).
- **Moto-taxis:** the new A/A1 licence is a state, counter-issued, consumer-side process (https://afrique.le360.ma/societe/guinee-permis-impose-aux-mototaxis-la-mesure-inquiete-autant-quelle-rassure_VNK7O6QYLVGLVODQYXG726BNNY/).
- **Artisanal fishing:** pirogue registration is still unfinished (https://www.cffacape.org/s/Guinee_FR_Sep_2022_Full_Report_web.pdf).
- **Pesticide dealers:** old law and no trigger (https://leap.unep.org/en/countries/gn/national-legislation/loi-no-l92028ctrn-instituant-la-legislation-sur-les-pesticides).
- **Well drillers:** donor-tender driven (IsDB/SNAPE).
- **Domestic employers and scrap dealers:** no Guinea-specific evidence.

## 4. Method notes

- French queries on Guinéenews returned the most useful regulator-originated news: decrees, ministry communiqués and bans. Official registers and the Journal officiel did not show up in search, and no licensed-operator lists were found. Counts come from EITI, BCRG and news, and most are missing.
- In Guinea, "regulator first" mostly surfaces **prohibitions**, not filing obligations. The state also tends to build or announce its own platform (eTax, E-CNSS, DAMANDA, the gold traceability platform), which leaves little room for third-party filing tools.
- Generic French queries for domestic workers drifted to Moroccan results. Add "Guinée Conakry" plus an institution name (CNSS, BCRG, BNE).
