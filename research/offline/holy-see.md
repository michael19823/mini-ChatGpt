# Holy See (Vatican City State): offline-industries pass

**Research date:** 2026-10-05. **Searches used:** 2 of 8 (both returned results). Further searching was not worth it: the territory has no private small-business sector, so the answer is structural. Anything not in a cited source is marked unverified or estimate.

## Bottom line

No quiet-industry opportunity exists inside Vatican City. The seed-list industries need many small, independent, licensed operators. Vatican City has about 800 residents (estimate, unverified) and roughly 4,800 employees (per a third-party HR guide, rivermate.com). Over half work for the Holy See and the rest for the Governorate, as gardeners, shop staff, museum attendants and cleaners. Almost all are institutional employees, and most commute from Italy. Their payroll, social security and labour disputes go through the Labour Office of the Apostolic See (ULSA) and Vatican bodies, not private household or small-employer channels. Any quiet industry serving Vatican-related people really sits in Rome and Lazio and belongs in the Italy track.

None. No score is given, so that no market is invented.

## 3. Rejected

- Tour-guide accreditation tooling. Guides, not businesses, are the buyers. There is one 250 EUR renewal per year and nothing to automate.
- Vatican "official operator" ticket reseller. Online competition exists, and the access rules are unverified.
- Vatican labour and payroll compliance. About 4,800 staff under a handful of institutional employers is enterprise procurement.
- All seed-list groups above. Each has no population in the territory, so the verdict is structural, not competitive.

## 4. Method notes

- The regulator-first patterns worked only for the Vatican Museums (guide accreditation notices) and Vatican labour law (ULSA). The Vatican has no public licensing registers for trades.
- Local-language searches in Italian were not run, because the budget was spent on confirming that the population is institutional. A further angle would be the Italy-track "colf and badanti" and "tour guide" markets, where the real quiet-industry count exists.
