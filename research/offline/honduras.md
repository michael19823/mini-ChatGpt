# Honduras: Offline / Quiet-Industries Pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. Budget: 20 WebSearch calls, all used. WebFetch was not used (blocked). Every finding below comes from search-result summaries. I could not read any primary PDF in full.

Labels: **[V]** means verified from a search result in this session (URL given). **[U]** means unverified: background knowledge, inference or estimate.

> **Bottom line.** Honduras's quiet industries are regulated mostly on paper, but there is little money in them, and the regulators are digitising the same workflows themselves. Two candidates are worth an interview, and both are weak (3–4/10). Neither suits a non-local solo founder: both need a Spanish-speaking local who can visit counters, cooperatives and agro-shops in person. This complements the existing country report (`research/countries/honduras.md`), which already covers the APNFD/UIF compliance kit. That kit already includes pawn shops and car dealers, so I do not repeat them here.

## 2. Opportunities

### Opportunity: Regente-run compliance file for agroservicios (SENASA registration, renewal and product-control evidence)

**Industry:**
Agro-input retail (agroservicios) that sells phytosanitary products and fertiliser.

**Buyer:**
The **Category A regente agrícola**, a licensed agronomist who acts as technical lead for one or more agroservicios. The owner of a multi-branch agroservicio is the secondary buyer.

**Trigger / Why now:**
There is no strong 2026 trigger. The rules come from the plant-protection products regulation (Acuerdo 450-2023, *Reglamento para Registro, Uso y Control de Productos Fitosanitarios*) and the SENASA fee schedule (C.D. SENASA 005-2019) [V]. Registrations expire every 2 or 5 years, so a steady share of shops renew each year. SENASA has an electronic records system, but by Feb 2023 it had trained only 42 shop representatives on it [V].

**Current workflow:** (inferred) [U]
1. The owner gathers incorporation documents, the RTN, warehouse addresses and the regente's current credential.
2. The regente or a tramitador files the renewal at SENASA and pays fees (registration L 3,990 for 2 years or L 8,978 for 5 years; renewal L 2,415 or L 5,434; inspection L 1,500) [V].
3. The regente visits the shop periodically and checks shelves by hand against the SENASA product register: registered, not expired, not prohibited.
4. During a SENASA inspection, the shop must show it sells only registered products. Otherwise products are seized [V].

**Pain:**
Seizure of stock, and the illegal-import problem in the south (pesticides from Nicaragua) [V]. Fines and the frequency of seizures are **unverified**. There are no user complaints online, which is consistent with a quiet industry but is also not proof of pain.

**Existing solutions:**
- SENASA's own electronic records system and the government's trámites portal (tramites.diger.gob.hn) [V]. This is the main substitute and is free.
- Regentes and tramitadores who carry the paperwork by hand [U].
- Generic point-of-sale and inventory software that agroservicios already use for sales [U]. It does not check stock against the SENASA register.
- CropLife's Campo Limpio programme reaches the same shops for empty-container collection [V]. It is a channel, not a competitor.

**Offline evidence:**
Counter filing at SENASA, a regente who signs on paper, and almost no adoption of SENASA's e-system (42 trained) [V]. I found no software listing aimed at Honduran agroservicios.

**Offline channel:**
Regentes are the multiplier: one agronomist serves several shops. Reach them through the Colegio de Ingenieros Agrónomos de Honduras [U], and through **Campo Limpio / CropLife collection events**, which reach every participating agroservicio [V]. Distributor sales reps from the big input houses visit every shop monthly [U].

**Market count:**
Unverified. No public register count was found. Estimate: 1,000–3,000 registered agroservicios [U].

**The gap:**
No tool that (a) tracks each shop's registration and regente-credential expiry, (b) checks shelf stock against the current SENASA register (registered, prohibited, expired), and (c) produces an inspection-ready file.

**Possible product:**
A Spanish mobile app for regentes. They scan product labels in each shop they cover, and the app flags unregistered, prohibited or expired items against the SENASA register. It also tracks registration and credential expiry dates and prints a visit log that the shop keeps for SENASA.

**MVP:**
Import the SENASA product register by hand as CSV. Shelf checklist with search. Expiry alerts. PDF visit report per shop.

**Willingness to pay:**
Shops will pay for a done-for-you service (the regente plus paperwork), not for software. A regente might pay USD 10–25/month to cover several shops [U]. Score it as a service-plus-software model.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
USD 15/month per regente, or USD 5–10/month per shop billed through the regente. Estimate only.

**How to find first customers:**
Campo Limpio collection days, distributor sales-rep routes, the agronomists' professional college.

**Founder access:**
Needs a local. The buyers are agronomists in rural towns, reached in person and over WhatsApp.

**Risks:**
SENASA does not publish its product register in machine-readable form [U]. SENASA's own system improves. The market is tiny and willingness to pay is low.

**Kill condition:**
About 10 regente interviews show SENASA inspections of shops are rare (less than once a year), or that the SENASA register cannot be obtained in usable form.

**Score:** 4/10 (pain 4, frequency 4, mandatory 7, fragmentation 2, competition 6, incumbent gap 5, buyer access 4, WTP 3, MVP 7, distribution 4)

### Opportunity: Concession-renewal tracker for transport lawyers and taxi cooperatives (IHTT)

**Industry:**
Public land transport: taxis, mototaxis, rapiditos and buses.

**Buyer:**
The **apoderado legal**, a lawyer who files IHTT procedures on behalf of many concessionaires, and the administration office of taxi cooperatives and associations (*puntos*).

**Trigger / Why now:**
- In June 2026, IHTT moved to new anti-copy security paper for certificates and permits [V].
- More than 12,000 certificates were issued but never collected [V].
- IHTT hired 50 lawyers to clear the permit backlog [V].
- Transport and logistics are among the six priority areas in a 2026 government/IDB review of 365 procedures [V].
- Mototaxi renewals were made automatic [V].
- Transport groups asked in Feb 2026 to cancel concessions granted under the previous administration [V].

**Current workflow:** [V partly, U partly]
1. The concessionaire hands documents to a lawyer: the current certificate, an updated vehicle inspection slip, the driver's licence for the modality, and the RTN [V].
2. The lawyer books an appointment by email (citaenlineasps@transporte.gob.hn) and files up to 20 procedures per visit, or files some online [V].
3. The lawyer tracks each file's status by hand and returns to collect the certificate. The 12,000 uncollected certificates suggest this step often fails [V].
4. Deadline extensions are announced in the press, for example the 28 Feb registration deadline and permits extended to 31 Mar [V].

**Pain:**
A unit with an expired permit can be impounded. IHTT impounds vehicles [V]. Lawyers juggle dozens of files per client cooperative. The paperwork backlog is well documented [V].

**Existing solutions:**
- IHTT's own online procedure portal and appointment system [V].
- Lawyers' spreadsheets and paper folders [U].
- Generic legal case-management software (none found that is specific to Honduras) [U].

**Offline evidence:**
Appointments booked by email, security-paper certificates collected in person, a per-lawyer filing cap, and backlog news stories [V].

**Offline channel:**
Taxi associations and cooperatives (IHTT meets them directly [V]), and the lawyers queuing at the IHTT offices in Tegucigalpa and San Pedro Sula. Lawyers who work as apoderados are visible at the counter and appear in the press.

**Market count:**
5,000+ legal taxis in San Pedro Sula [V]. The national number of concessions is unverified; estimate tens of thousands of units [U]. The paying buyers are probably only a few hundred lawyers and cooperative offices [U].

**The gap:**
No tracker that knows each unit's permit and certificate expiry, the required document set per procedure type, and status and collection, and that sends reminders to concessionaires over WhatsApp.

**Possible product:**
A matter tracker for transport lawyers. It holds each unit's concession data and expiry dates, generates a document checklist per procedure, shows a status board, and sends client reminders.

**MVP:**
A spreadsheet-import tracker with expiry alerts and per-procedure checklists (renewal, vehicle change, transfer).

**Willingness to pay:**
Lawyers would pay a small amount for software (USD 20–40/month) [U]. Cooperatives would rather pay a lawyer than buy software.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
USD 25/month per lawyer, or USD 1 per unit tracked per year. Estimate only.

**How to find first customers:**
In-person at IHTT offices, through taxi association leaders, and through the Colegio de Abogados [U].

**Founder access:**
Needs a local. Transport is politically charged, and the sector suffers extortion pressure [V].

**Risks:**
IHTT reforms or a simplification programme remove the lawyer requirement. Politics and extortion in the sector. A generic tracker is close to the "generic CRM/document collection" trap in the brief.

**Kill condition:**
Lawyers each handle fewer than about 30 units, or IHTT's own portal starts sending expiry notices.

**Score:** 3/10 (pain 6, frequency 3, mandatory 8, fragmentation 2, competition 6, incumbent gap 4, buyer access 4, WTP 3, MVP 8, distribution 4)

**Sources:**

- IHTT appointments for concessionaires and apoderados: https://diarioroatan.com/ihtt-atiende-a-concesionarios-y-apoderados-legales-por-medio-de-citas-programadas/

## 3. Rejected

- **Juntas de agua:** about 5,000 boards exist (ERSAPS, 2006) [V], but they are volunteer and non-profit, and donors fund their tools. Nobody pays. https://www.ersaps.hn/documentos/Plan%20Fortalecimiento/Informe_RegWAS_Honduras_versión_ejecutiva_final.pdf
- **Domestic-worker payroll:** there is no specific minimum wage, C189 is not ratified and enforcement is weak, so there is no trigger [V]. https://www.tunota.com/honduras-hoy/cuanto-se-le-paga-a-una-empleada-domestica-en-honduras-y-cuales-son-sus-derechos2026-05-11
- **Cattle movement (GUIASA / Trazar-Agro):** the state system already exists, with 143k people registered, and the buyers are smallholders [V]. https://www.tsc.gob.hn/web/leyes/Acuerdo-SENASA-001-2020.pdf
- **Scrap dealers:** no register or obligation found in Honduras.
- **Casas de cambio:** too few licensed operators. Street changers are informal.
- **Municipal rastros:** the buyer is a municipality, so sales go through procurement.
- **Lobster divers / artisanal fishers:** donors and INFOP fill the gap, and the divers will not pay.
- **ARSA small-food licensing:** enforcement is weak (16 closures in 3 years in the capital), and ARSA has its own online channel [V]. https://www.elheraldo.hn/tegucigalpa/inspecciones-sanitarias-arsa-clausura-16-negocios-en-tres-anos-higuiene-DK30094412
- **Beekeepers:** no register and a tiny sector.

## 4. Method notes

- **Worked:** Spanish queries naming the regulator (SENASA, IHTT, ERSAPS, ARSA) together with the procedure ("renovación", "registro", "apoderado legal"). These surfaced FAOLEX copies of fee schedules, IAIP transparency PDFs and national press. Honduran press (La Prensa, El Heraldo, TNH) is the best source of enforcement and backlog evidence.
- **Didn't work:** queries for registers that do not exist (chatarreros, apicultores), which returned other countries' results. Queries for operator counts: Honduras publishes almost no counts of licensed establishments. "Trazar-Agro" queries also return Costa Rica first.
- **Data gap:** I found no public, downloadable list of licensed operators for any quiet industry. Prospect lists would have to be built in person.
