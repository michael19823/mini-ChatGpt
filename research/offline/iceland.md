# Iceland: offline-industries pass

**Research date:** 2026-10-05. **Search budget:** 20 allowed, 3 used. The second search returned a usage-limit refusal. Following the instructions ("If a search is refused, stop and write up what you have"), I stopped there. Only 2 searches returned results. Almost everything below that lacks a source comes from background knowledge and is marked **unverified**.

**Bottom line:** I found **no opportunity worth recommending.** The first-round country report found that the Icelandic state usually builds the free tool itself (Ísland.is, Fiskistofa, HMS, Samgöngustofa). The quiet industries make this worse: Iceland's "quiet" sectors are tiny in absolute numbers (dozens to low hundreds of operators). Many are already served by a farmers' association system (Bændasamtök Íslands / RML: WorldFengur, Fjárvís, Heiðrún) or by a municipal or state permit process. They work in Icelandic and are reached through a few associations. A non-local solo founder could not realistically sell into any of them. Treat Iceland as a market with no standalone quiet-industry opportunity.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

None. No industry met the bar (identifiable buyers + repeat mandatory workflow + gap + offline channel + plausible willingness to pay). Applying the extra scoring rules:

- **Distribution:** offline channels do exist (Bændasamtök Íslands, RML advisers, municipal social-services departments, Samtök ferðaþjónustunnar). But they are themselves the providers of the free systems.
- **Willingness to pay:** at best this would be a done-for-you service from a local accountant or adviser, not software.
- **Founder access:** a non-local solo founder could not sell into any of these. Everything is done in Icelandic, through associations and personal networks.

## 3. Rejected

- **Childminder admin / supervision software.** About 75 childminders in Reykjavík. The state issues the permit and the municipality pays the subsidy. Too small.
- **Horse / sheep registry tooling.** WorldFengur and Fjárvís/Heiðrún are association-run and mandatory in practice. Substitute: the farmers' association.
- **Hunting-report tools.** Individual hunters, free state portal.
- **Scrap / second-hand dealer registers.** No such obligation found, and the sector is consolidated.
- **Coastal-fisher reporting.** Fiskistofa's own e-logbook.
- **Farm-slaughter direct sales.** Kept on watch only. I found no 2025–26 rule text.

## 4. Method notes

- Icelandic-language queries against island.is/reglugerdir and municipal PDFs (fundur.reykjavik.is) worked. One query returned exact register-style counts from a supervision memo.
- A MAST query on home slaughter returned no relevant results. A third query was refused (usage limit), so I stopped there.
- Structural finding: in Iceland, the quiet-industry "register" is usually run by a farmers' association or a state agency that also gives away the tool. Market counts are in the tens to hundreds. If anyone revisits Iceland, use a Nordic module angle, not a standalone one.
