## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Agri-Input Shop "Monthly Return Autopilot" (pesticide e-register + seed/fertiliser stock reconciliation)

**Industry:**
Rural agri-input retail (pesticide, seed and fertiliser shops, "krishi seva kendras").

**Buyer:**
Owner of a licensed agri-input shop that holds an insecticide sale licence, usually alongside fertiliser and seed licences; tier-2/3 towns and villages. Secondary buyer: the 2–20-shop distributor who supplies them.

**Trigger / Why now:**
The Insecticides (Amendment) Rules 2026 were notified 17 Jun 2026 and came **into force 15 Sep 2026**. Dealers must keep **electronic** sales, distribution and stock registers for each insecticide, and file **monthly returns electronically within 15 days of month-end**. Licences to sell must also be applied for digitally (Form II). At the same time, Kharif 2026 inspection drives are suspending licences for e-PoS stock mismatches and record failures.

**Current workflow:**
1. Shopkeeper bills farmers in a paper bill book or a generic billing app (Vyapar, myBillBook, Busy, Tally).
2. Fertiliser sales are punched a second time into the iFMS e-PoS device (Aadhaar-authenticated). Incoming company stock must be "acknowledged" on the PoS.
3. Seed sales of QR-coded certified seed go through SATHI where integrated (24 states by mid-2025).
4. Until Sep 2026, insecticide stock registers were paper books checked by the agriculture inspector. Now they must be electronic, with a monthly return to the state licensing authority (e.g. Odisha's agri e-Licensing portal, which already demands monthly reports by the 15th from manufacturers).
5. Each month the owner, or a relative or local typist, compiles purchase and sale per product/batch and re-keys it into the portal.

**Pain:**
- Enforcement is real. Since 1 Apr 2026 one state drive suspended 90 fertiliser, 10 seed and 12 pesticide sale licences for e-PoS stock discrepancies, unmaintained statutory records and missing farmer signatures (Free Press Journal, Nashik). Haryana has suspended licences over "data mismatch" (Tribune).
- The new rules make paper registers non-compliant for every pesticide dealer at once.
- Maharashtra's agri-input sector shut down on 27 Apr in protest at "Inspector Raj", which shows how much inspection risk weighs on this trade.

**Existing solutions:**
- Generic billing/inventory apps with batch and expiry tracking: **Vyapar, myBillBook, Busy (agri edition), TallyPrime, Marg ERP, ClearOne (pesticide/fertiliser invoicing), GoFrugal (fertiliser vertical)**. None was found to generate the Insecticides Rules stock register or monthly return.
- Govt tools: **iFMS e-PoS** (fertiliser only), **SATHI** (seed only), state e-licensing portals (e.g. Odisha) for direct manual entry.
- Agritech retailer networks: **Gramophone GramKonnect** (5,000 stores, target 10,000), **DeHaat**, **AgroStar**. These serve their own franchise or partner stores, not the independent 2.5 lakh.
- Substitutes: paper register books sold by stationers, the local CA/typist, and the distributor's salesman.

**Offline evidence:** The rules themselves describe the old system as paper-based. Inspectors physically check registers and PoS stock. Dealers are DAESI-trained (MANAGE's diploma for input dealers), which suggests an older, non-graduate population. No SaaS review pages or forums for this compliance task were found.

**Offline channel:** (a) District and state dealer associations, e.g. the Maharashtra Fertilizers, Pesticides & Seeds Dealers Association, which organised the 2026 shutdown. (b) Pesticide company territory salesmen and distributors, who visit every shop monthly and want clean secondary-sales data. (c) DAESI batches run by KVKs/ATMA (classroom cohorts of dealers). (d) Public licence lists from state e-licensing portals → phone/WhatsApp outreach in Marathi/Hindi/Telugu.

**Market count:** ~2.82 lakh agri-input dealers nationally (DAESI guidelines, via ICAR journal); >2.5 lakh iFMS fertiliser retailers (OpenGov Asia); 85,000 dealers and distributors in Maharashtra (AgroSpectrum). How many of these hold an insecticide licence specifically was not found (estimate: a majority).

**The gap:**
No product found that turns one shop's sales and purchases into (i) the insecticide e-register and monthly return in each state portal's format, (ii) an iFMS-vs-physical-vs-books fertiliser mismatch alert before the inspector arrives, and (iii) SATHI seed-sale reconciliation. Billing apps stop at the GST invoice. Govt portals each cover only one input type.

**Possible product:**
A vernacular, Android-first "inspection-ready" register. The dealer photographs supplier invoices and bill-book pages (or imports a Vyapar/Tally export). The app keeps the statutory per-product stock register and produces the monthly return file or a portal-ready sheet for the dealer's state. It also flags fertiliser stock that disagrees with the iFMS PoS figure.

**MVP:**
One state (Maharashtra or MP), pesticides only. Invoice and bill-book capture → per-product/batch stock register → month-end return in that state's portal format. If there is no portal upload, a done-for-you filing service by a part-time operator. Add a "show inspector" register PDF.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
₹300–500/month or ₹3,000–5,000/year per shop (estimate). A done-for-you monthly filing at ₹500–1,000/month would likely sell better than self-serve software. Distributors might pay per shop to obtain clean secondary-sales data.

**How to find first customers:**
State e-licensing portal licence lists; the dealer associations above; one pesticide company's distributor in a single district; DAESI alumni batches at the local KVK.

**Founder access:** Needs a local partner who speaks Marathi, Hindi or Telugu and can visit district associations. A non-local solo founder could build the software but could not sell it alone.

**Willingness to pay:** Mostly for a **service plus software** (filing done for them), because the fear is licence suspension in season. Pure software WTP is low but non-zero. Shops already pay ₹2,000–6,000/yr for billing apps **(unverified)**.

**Risks:**
- The Centre or states may ship a free government app (as with iFMS e-PoS and SATHI) that does the register at the point of sale. That is the biggest risk.
- The portal format and whether retailers file centrally or to the state were not confirmed. Rules may be enforced on manufacturers and distributors first.
- Vyapar or myBillBook could add a "pesticide register" report quickly.
- Gramophone/DeHaat could bundle it for their networks.

**Kill condition:**
The dealer-facing return turns out to be a free government app or PoS that captures sales automatically. Or retail dealers are exempted or deferred from the monthly return. Or five dealer interviews show the distributor's salesman already files it for free.

**Score:** 7/10 (pain 8, frequency 8 monthly, mandatory 9, fragmentation 6, competition 6, incumbent gap 7, buyer access 8, WTP 5, MVP 7, distribution 7).

### Opportunity: Used-Vehicle Dealer "Deemed-Owner" Compliance Log (Form 29C / trip register / draft 29CA)

**Industry:**
Independent used car and two-wheeler dealers (roadside lots, tier-2/3 towns).

**Buyer:**
Owner of an independent used-vehicle lot that needs, or already holds, an RTO authorisation certificate.

**Trigger / Why now:**
Since the Dec 2022 CMVR amendment, the dealer is the "deemed owner" while a vehicle is in its custody. The dealer must file Form 29C on intake and keep an **electronic trip register on Vahan** (purpose, driver, time, mileage) for every test drive or workshop trip. The **draft of 21 Jul 2026** adds Form 29CA for each dealer-to-dealer handover, caps dealer custody at 6 months without a Form 30 transfer, and limits dealer hops to 2. ETV Bharat reports (Apr 2026) that UP now requires used vehicles to be bought and sold online **(details unverified)**.

**Current workflow:**
1. Buy a vehicle from the owner; both sign Form 29C, which is uploaded on Vahan/Parivahan.
2. Log each test drive or workshop trip in the Vahan trip register. In practice this is likely skipped **(unverified)**.
3. On sale, arrange Form 29/30 transfer, often through an RTO agent.
4. Under the draft, also file 29CA for inter-dealer moves and watch the 6-month clock per vehicle.

**Pain:**
Deemed-owner liability for accidents, challans and crimes during custody. A per-trip log is impractical on a busy lot. A 6-month cap creates a per-vehicle deadline. Consultants (e.g. Professional Utilities) sell the authorisation-certificate filing.

**Existing solutions:**
Vahan/Parivahan (govt, manual); RTO agents; consultants for authorisation; used-car DMS such as **MotorIQ** (lists "RTO compliance"); platform dealer apps from Cars24/CarDekho/Spinny for their partner dealers **(unverified)**.

**Offline evidence:** Filing happens through RTO agents. Dealer forums are absent, and dealers are small cash businesses.

**Offline channel:** RTO agents (who already file for these dealers), local used-car dealer associations **(unverified)**, financiers/NBFCs that fund dealer inventory, and walk-ins at auto markets (e.g. Karol Bagh-type clusters).

**Market count:** Not found. Estimate: tens of thousands of lots nationally. Needs a count of authorisation certificates from Vahan or RTI.

**The gap:**
A per-vehicle custody clock (29C date → 6-month limit → Form 30) plus a one-tap trip log that writes to Vahan, or produces evidence, with liability alerts. No tool was found that does this for small lots.

**Possible product:** A WhatsApp-first inventory list where each vehicle has a custody timer, trip-log entries and generated 29C/29CA paperwork, run as a service with an RTO agent.

**MVP:** One city. A per-vehicle custody tracker plus a trip-log sheet, with a weekly "vehicles nearing 6 months" alert.

**Pricing hypothesis:** ₹500–1,500/month per lot (estimate), or a per-vehicle fee bundled with transfer paperwork.

**How to find first customers:** Vahan authorisation lists if obtainable; RTO agent partnerships; inventory-finance NBFCs.

**Founder access:** Needs a local person, because the sale goes through RTO agents.

**Willingness to pay:** Mostly for done-for-you transfer service. Low for pure software.

**Risks:** The draft may not be finalised. Enforcement of the trip register appears weak. Vahan may add an app. Platforms bundle tools free for their dealers.

**Kill condition:** The 29CA draft lapses, or interviews show dealers ignore the trip register with no penalty.

**Score:** 5/10.

### Opportunity: Brick-Kiln Pellet Co-firing Monthly Report Service (Punjab / Haryana)

**Industry:** Brick kilns.

**Buyer:** Kiln owner (often a family business) in Punjab or non-NCR Haryana.

**Trigger / Why now:** The CAQM statutory direction mandates paddy-straw pellet/briquette co-firing: 20% from 1 Nov 2025, **30% from 1 Nov 2026**, 40% in 2027, 50% in 2028. HSPCB's portal requires registration, an upload of every pellet purchase immediately after buying, and monthly reports on production, total fuel, coal, biomass and pellets.

**Current workflow:** The owner or munshi keeps paper fuel and production books, buys pellets from local plants, and someone uploads purchases and the monthly report to the HSPCB portal. Food & Supplies and PCB officers verify fuel type and quantity jointly.

**Pain:** Licence suspension or penalty "without further notice". The target ratio rises each year, so the owner must prove the mix percentage every month.

**Existing solutions:** HSPCB portal (govt); Punjab mechanism unverified; local CAs/consultants **(unverified)**; pellet suppliers' invoices.

**Offline evidence:** Kiln owners are a classic paper and cash industry, inspected physically.

**Offline channel:** Brick kiln owners' associations at district level **(unverified names)**; pellet manufacturers, who need kilns to prove purchases; the PCB registration list.

**Market count:** 2,050 kilns in Punjab + 940 in Haryana (ThePrint, citing experts/CAQM context).

**The gap:** Turning pellet invoices and fuel books into a co-firing percentage, with the monthly upload done for the owner.

**Possible product / MVP:** A WhatsApp invoice-photo intake that produces a monthly co-firing calculation and portal upload, done for the owner.

**Pricing hypothesis:** ₹1,000–2,000/month in season (estimate). The total market is under ₹7 crore/year even at full capture.

**Founder access:** Needs a local Punjabi/Haryanvi operator.

**Willingness to pay:** Service only.

**Risks / Kill condition:** The market is small and seasonal (kilns run part of the year). Pellet suppliers may do the upload for free as a sales perk. The idea dies if pellet sellers already file for kilns.

**Score:** 4/10.

## 4. Method notes

- **Worked:** searching for "<Act> (Amendment) Rules 2026" plus "in force" found the strongest trigger (Insecticides Rules). Searching for licence-suspension news ("licences suspended" + kharif/mismatch) gave hard enforcement evidence. Hindi queries surfaced Hindi farm media (Kisan India, Amar Ujala) confirming the 15 Sep date, and the UP used-vehicle rule.
- **Did not work:** counts of operators for most police- and municipal-registered trades (pawnbrokers, mobile shops, rigs, used-car lots) are not online. They would need RTI or the state portals, which WebFetch could not open. Searches on cold-storage acts returned US statutes.
- **Next step:** confirm the dealer-level return portal and format for the Insecticides Rules 2026 in two states (Maharashtra, MP), then interview 5 shop owners and 2 distributors.
