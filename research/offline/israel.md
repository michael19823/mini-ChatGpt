## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Backflow-test router: one test, every receiving body (מז"ח)

**Industry:**  
Plumbing and water-system testers certified by MoH to install and test backflow preventers (מתקיני/בודקי מז"ח)

**Buyer:**  
The owner-operator of a small testing firm (1-5 testers). Many also clean and disinfect building water tanks. A second buyer is the property or facility manager of a building or business that must prove compliance each year.

**Trigger / Why now:**  
- The legal duty itself is old (Public Health Regulations, 1992).  
- Reporting has since split into two channels. Testers must now fill in a **MoH online reporting form** (gov.il service "reporting_test_fixture_to_prevent_backflow"), which only certified testers can use. The water supplier still wants the report and the tester's certificate by email under its own rules.  
- Water suppliers enforce this. If no report arrives, the supplier may install or test the device itself and bill the customer (Mei Carmel, Mey Sheva and other supplier pages).  
- No 2025-2026 regulatory change was found, so the "why now" is moderate (the date the online form launched is unverified).

**Current workflow:**  
1. The tester visits the site, tests the device and fills in a paper or Word test sheet.  
2. The tester types the same data into the MoH online form.  
3. The tester emails a PDF and a copy of the certificate to the specific water supplier (Mei Carmel, Mey Sheva, a rural water association and so on). Each supplier has its own address, its own form and sometimes its own portal.  
4. The tester sends the customer a certificate. The customer may need it for a business license or a building completion certificate (Form 4).  
5. The tester keeps a list in Excel or a diary to remind each customer next year.

**Pain:**  
- The obligation is mandatory every year, and failing it is costly: the water supplier does the test itself and bills for it.  
- The same data is entered at least 3 times: test sheet, MoH form, supplier email.  
- Rules are fragmented across 56 local authorities and the water corporations, and almost 1,000 rural water associations.  
- Each supplier publishes its own instructions page, which shows there is no common channel.  
- Hours lost per tester: unverified. Interview first.

**Existing solutions:**  
- The MoH online form (free, government).  
- Water suppliers' own email boxes and pages.  
- Generic field-service apps (none found that are specific to this work).  
- Excel and paper test sheets.  
- Some larger firms say they keep "documentation in management systems" and give clients online access to certificates (search snippet; product unnamed).  
- In the US, backflow tracking software (e.g. Tokay, BSI Online) is sold to water utilities, not testers. No Israeli version was found (unverified).

**Offline evidence:**  
- Test sheets are paper or Word.  
- Filing with suppliers is by email.  
- Testers are one-person firms with brochure websites (moshemazah.com, shomerltd, receptor, aqua-net).  
- No Hebrew software listing or review page exists for backflow testing.

**Offline channel:**  
- Phone or WhatsApp outreach to the MoH list of certified testers (a public PDF on gov.il).  
- The certification courses that train testers (course operators: unverified).  
- Valve distributors who sell backflow preventers and approved models (MoH list of approved devices).  
- Water suppliers' engineering departments, which could tell testers "send it through X".

**Market count:**  
- Testers: the MoH certified list, number unverified. Estimate: low hundreds.  
- Receiving bodies: 56 local authorities plus the water corporations, and about 1,000 water associations.  
- Devices: tens of thousands (estimate). One firm alone tests more than 1,500 a year.

**The gap:**  
Nothing found that takes one field test and produces the MoH form data (pre-filled to copy, since there is no API), the right package for the right water supplier with its address and format, the customer certificate and a renewal reminder 11 months later.

**Possible product:**  
A Hebrew mobile test sheet plus a routing table for every Israeli water supplier (address, required attachments, format). It generates the supplier email and PDF and a copy-ready MoH form summary, keeps the customer certificate archive and sends the annual reminder to the customer, which brings repeat revenue to the tester.

**MVP:**  
- A web form on the phone that produces the test sheet PDF.  
- A hand-built table of the 30 largest water suppliers.  
- One-click email to the supplier and the customer.  
- A renewal reminder list.  
- No MoH integration in v1: the data is shown in the same order as the MoH form, ready to copy.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
NIS 99-249/month per tester, or NIS 5-10 per test (estimate). This is software the tester would buy, because the annual reminders bring repeat jobs. A done-for-you filing option is possible later.

**How to find first customers:**  
Call testers on the MoH certified list, sorted by region. Start with testers who also disinfect water tanks, since they handle more documents.

**Risks:**  
- The market may be too small (low hundreds of testers).  
- MoH could forward reports to water suppliers itself, which removes the routing value.  
- Some water suppliers may already read the MoH data.  
- No API, so the MoH step stays manual.

**Kill condition:**  
- Water suppliers confirm that they receive the MoH online reports directly, so the second channel goes away.  
- Or fewer than about 200 testers are active.  
- Or 10 tester interviews show they spend less than 10 minutes a test on paperwork.

**Score:** 5/10  
Pain 6, frequency 7, mandatory 8, fragmentation 8, competition 7, incumbent gap 6, buyer access 8, willingness to pay 4, MVP 8, distribution 7. Weak why-now and a small market pull the total down.

### Opportunity: Compliance calendar and filing pack for rural water associations

**Industry:**  
Small drinking-water suppliers: water associations (אגודות מים) and the water committees of kibbutzim, moshavim and community settlements

**Buyer:**  
- The settlement's water manager or treasurer (often part-time, often older).  
- As a multi-client buyer: CPA firms and consulting engineers that serve many settlements.

**Trigger / Why now:**  
- New Water Rules on license conditions for engineering standards (2024) now apply to water suppliers (the exact duties for small suppliers are unverified; my search stopped there).  
- Each supplier must file an annual financial report to the Water Authority. For 2025 it is due by **31 August 2026**, with separate accounting for water income, expenses and assets.  
- The Water Authority published a 2026 cost and tariff update for water suppliers (December 2025) and a draft to create regional cooperative suppliers.  
- MoH drinking-water regulations (2013) require an annual sampling plan and an annual computerized report by **1 June**.  
- The State Comptroller has repeatedly criticised small suppliers (Tel Yosef, Ahihud).

**Current workflow:**  
1. The water manager agrees the annual sampling plan with the MoH district office and orders samples from a certified sampler and lab.  
2. Lab results arrive by email and PDF and are copied into the annual MoH report.  
3. The accountant builds the separate water accounts and the Water Authority financial report.  
4. Reservoir cleaning, sanitary surveys and the backflow preventers of agricultural and business consumers are tracked in diaries and Excel.

**Pain:**  
- Regulatory exposure: the State Comptroller named specific villages, and boil-water notices follow failures.  
- Two regulators with different calendars.  
- The work relies on volunteers or part-time staff.  
- CPA firms publish running "water economy in the settlement" updates, which shows clients need help.  
- Hours spent: unverified.

**Existing solutions:**  
- CPA firms (e.g. britcpa "water economy" practice).  
- Consulting water engineers.  
- Regional councils that support villages.  
- The labs' own result portals (unverified).  
- Municipal water billing systems: names unverified, not found in search.  
- Excel.

**Offline evidence:**  
- Filing goes through accountants and engineers.  
- Results arrive as email PDFs.  
- No SaaS was found for small Israeli water suppliers.  
- The buyers are villages, not tech firms.

**Offline channel:**  
- CPA firms that serve kibbutzim and moshavim, as resellers.  
- Regional councils' water departments.  
- The Kibbutz Movement and Moshav Movement economic units (unverified that they run water services).  
- Certified sampling labs, which already reach every supplier.

**Market count:**  
Almost 1,000 water associations (State Comptroller / Knesset research), plus 56 local authorities that supply water directly.

**The gap:**  
Nothing found that holds one supplier's full obligation calendar across MoH and the Water Authority, takes in lab result PDFs, flags breaches of drinking-water limits and builds the 1 June MoH report and the evidence file.

**Possible product:**  
A per-supplier obligations calendar, lab-result import with limit checks, a draft of the annual MoH report, and a register of consumers' backflow preventers. This last part links to opportunity 1, because the supplier is also a receiving body.

**MVP:**  
- A calendar built from the 2013 regulations.  
- Lab result entry or PDF upload with limit flags.  
- Export of the annual report table.  
- Sold first to one CPA firm serving 20+ settlements.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
- NIS 150-400/month per supplier (estimate).  
- CPA or engineer tier: NIS 1,500/month.  
- Likely a service plus software sold through the accountant, because the water managers themselves are unlikely to buy software alone.

**How to find first customers:**  
- The Water Authority list of licensed suppliers (public status unverified).  
- CPA firms with a kibbutz or moshav practice.  
- Regional councils' water engineers.

**Risks:**  
- The regional-supplier reform could merge small associations and shrink the market.  
- Labs or the regional councils could offer this for free.  
- Volunteer buyers have low willingness to pay.

**Kill condition:**  
- The Water Authority proceeds with consolidating into regional suppliers.  
- Or 5 CPA firms say the reports take them less than a day a year per client.

**Score:** 4.5/10

### Opportunity: Digital pest-control register (פנקס הדברה) with customer and inspector copies

**Industry:**  
Licensed pest-control operators (מדבירים)

**Buyer:**  
The owner-operator of a 1-10 person pest-control business, especially one serving food businesses, institutions and residential buildings.

**Trigger / Why now:**  
- The Sanitary Pest Control Law (2016) moved the trade to licensing. A license is renewed every 5 years, with two recognised trainings and an exam at each renewal (MoEP dataset notes).  
- The licensing regulations require a register entry **before every job**: site, date, international name of the pesticide, concentration, manufacturer and batch. The customer gets a copy, the register is kept 3 years and shown to the MoEP inspector.  
- No 2025-2026 change was found, so the why-now is weak.

**Current workflow:**  
1. Before the job, the operator writes the entry in a numbered paper book.  
2. The operator hands the customer a carbon copy or a handwritten certificate. Restaurants and food plants keep it for health and kashrut inspectors and for their food-safety (HACCP) audits.  
3. Customers phone again when inspectors ask for history. The operator searches the book.

**Pain:**  
- Mandatory for every job. For a busy operator that means several entries a day.  
- Food-business customers need records ready for audits.  
- An inspector can ask to see the register.  
- Enforcement intensity: unverified.

**Existing solutions:**  
- The paper register book.  
- Foreign pest-control field-service software (PestPac, GorillaDesk, FieldRoutes, Powered Now). None is in Hebrew or follows the Israeli register format (unverified).  
- Generic Israeli field-service and invoicing apps (Morning for invoices).  
- Excel.

**Offline evidence:**  
- A Hebrew search for pest-control software returned only foreign tools.  
- Operators' websites are brochure pages that advertise their license.  
- The register is defined as a numbered paper book.

**Offline channel:**  
- Phone or WhatsApp outreach to the public MoEP licensee dataset (2,133 records with license type and contacts).  
- Pesticide distributors and wholesalers that every operator buys from (names unverified).  
- The mandatory renewal trainings every 5 years, where course operators reach every licensee.

**Market count:**  
**2,133 licensed operators** (MoEP dataset): 1,129 buildings and open areas, 620 apartments, 308 fumigation. The number of firms is lower (estimate 1,000-1,500).

**The gap:**  
No Hebrew tool was found that produces a register entry in the legal format with pesticide product and batch lookup, sends the customer a signed digital copy, and keeps a 3-year archive per site that a food business can show its inspectors.

**Possible product:**  
A mobile register entry with a pesticide catalogue (the MoEP list of permitted products), a WhatsApp or email PDF to the customer, a per-customer history portal and a renewal-training tracker.

**MVP:**  
A phone form, a product list, a PDF to the customer and a searchable archive.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
NIS 49-149/month. This is software, but the price ceiling is low. One-person operators will compare it with a NIS 30 paper book.

**How to find first customers:**  
The MoEP dataset, filtered to "buildings and open areas" license holders, who serve businesses.

**Risks:**  
- Low willingness to pay.  
- It is unverified whether a digital register satisfies the "numbered pages" wording. The regulation may require a bound book, which would kill the product.  
- A generic field-service app could add a template.

**Kill condition:**  
MoEP says the register must stay on paper, or 10 operators say inspectors never ask for it.

**Score:** 4/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Household employers of foreign caregivers.** The obligations are real and monthly (payslip, BTL 3.6% from Jan 2025, a 12.5% deposit), but the substitute is a done-for-you service: licensed caregiver agencies, Kav LaMaasik, payroll bureaus and accountants (about NIS 1,200/yr to maintain a withholding file). BTL also has its own household-employer payment screen. Households would not buy software. The country report already rejected this area.
- **Scrap metal dealers.** A licensing and record-keeping bill was discussed in the Knesset in 2023-24, but it was not found enacted.
- **Second-hand dealers / police register.** Only a "show on request" register. Stationers' books are the substitute.
- **Precious-stone and gold dealers' AML.** Concentrated in the diamond bourse and served by compliance officers and lawyers.
- **Livestock marking and movement.** Dairy is served by the breeders' association. Other segments are small. Vet services take email reports.
- **Burial societies and cemeteries.** Served by Kanfey Sharet, UMP and the forum's GRAVEZ app.
- **Kashrut supervision.** The reform was cancelled by law (passed second and third reading), so the rules are politically volatile.
- **Driving instructors.** No new duty. The lesson reform is only a proposal.
- **Swimming pools.** 1994 rules with no trigger. Dosing controllers and labs already cover the log.
- **Domestic gas checks.** The large gas suppliers own the workflow.
- **Employers of Palestinian workers.** Market shrank in the war. Payroll bureaus already do it.

## 4. Method notes

- **What worked:**  
  - Hebrew regulator-side queries: the regulation name plus "דיווח"/"פנקס"/"טופס".  
  - gov.il service pages, nevo.co.il regulation texts, State Comptroller and Knesset research PDFs, and water suppliers' own instruction pages, which showed the fragmentation directly.  
  - The MoEP licensee dataset gave an exact count of pest-control operators.
- **What didn't:**  
  - Software-discovery queries in Hebrew returned foreign tools or noise.  
  - Queries for counts (number of backflow devices or testers, number of foreign caregivers) did not return figures.  
  - Scrap and second-hand queries pulled in foreign (US) law.
- The search hit its usage limit after about 25 calls. The 2024 engineering-standards Water Rules and the number of backflow testers remain unverified. Check them first in the next pass.
