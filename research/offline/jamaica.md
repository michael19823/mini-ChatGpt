# Jamaica: Offline (quiet) industries pass

**Date:** 2026-10-05. **Searches used:** 20 of 20. WebFetch was not available, so most facts come from search snippets only. Anything not seen in a snippet is marked unverified.

**Bottom line:** No quiet industry reached 6/10. Jamaica's quiet industries are mostly small in count, or regulated by laws that are stalled, or served by a government-sold paper tool. The best lead is the agricultural produce receipt-book system (traceability against praedial larceny), but the payer is weak and the state could digitise it itself.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Digital produce receipt and traceability book

**Industry:**
Agricultural produce trade (farm to retailer or vendor)

**Buyer:**
Farmers' groups, produce buyers and transport operators. The payer is uncertain; a likely payer is a government agency or JAS rather than the farmer.

**Trigger / Why now:**
The 2023 amendment of the Agricultural Produce Act raised fines from J$250,000 to as much as J$3M and jail from 3 months to 3 years. In September 2026 ministers were still urging buyers and farmers to disrupt the market for stolen produce and livestock. JACRA is also rolling out a traceability platform (details unverified).

**Current workflow:**
1. Farmer registers with RADA under ABIS.
2. Farmer buys a paper receipt book at a JAS office.
3. Farmer writes a receipt for each sale to a retailer or vendor.
4. The buyer carries the receipt in transit; police check it and may seize goods without one.

**Pain:**
Older Gleaner coverage (2015, "Farmers abusing receipt book system") shows misuse of the paper books. Penalties are large. I found no direct complaint evidence from farmers in 2026.

**Existing solutions:**
- JAS paper receipt book (about 100,000 books were issued in a past programme, JIS).
- RADA ABIS registration database.
- JACRA's planned blockchain traceability platform (government).

**The gap:**
Paper receipts cannot be verified quickly by police or buyers. Unverified: whether any digital receipt pilot exists.

**Possible product:**
A phone or SMS-based receipt issued against a farmer's ABIS number, with a verification link for police and buyers.

**MVP:**
SMS or WhatsApp receipt with a verification page, tested with one farmers' group.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
Likely a government or donor contract, or per-receipt fees of a few Jamaican cents. Individual farmers are unlikely to pay for software.

**Offline channel:**
JAS offices and parish branches, RADA extension officers, and farmers' and higglers' groups.

**Market count:**
167,278 registered farmers (JIS, "Farmers Urged to Register with RADA...").

**Offline evidence:**
The system is a paper book sold at counters; no software listing found.

**Willingness to pay:** Service-plus-software or a government contract only. Farmers would not pay software prices.

**Founder access:** Needs a local partner and government sponsorship. A non-local solo founder could not realistically sell this.

**Risks:**
- The state or JACRA builds it.
- Procurement is slow.
- Informal buyers resist traceability.

**Kill condition:**
JAS, RADA or JACRA announces its own digital receipt system, or no agency will fund a pilot.

**Score:** 4/10

### Opportunity: Security-guard payroll and statutory compliance for PSRA-registered companies

**Industry:**
Private security

**Buyer:**
Owner or office manager of one of about 331 registered security organisations.

**Trigger / Why now:**
The guard minimum wage rises under the 2026 Industrial Security Guards (Amendment) Order, with the national minimum wage at J$17,000 per week from 1 July 2026 (Parliament coverage, snippet only; the guard rate itself is unverified). The PSRA Act is under review (JIS, undated). NHT contribution disputes followed a September 2022 court ruling that guards are employees.

**Current workflow:**
1. Build guard rosters and shift pay by hand or in spreadsheets.
2. Compute guard minimum wage and allowances, then NHT, NIS, tax and other deductions.
3. Pay contributions and file returns.
4. Renew PSRA registrations.

**Pain:**
Gleaner (2022): over 60 per cent of security firms ignored NHT demands to pay the employer share. This is the strongest pain evidence, but it is dated. I did not find 2026 arrears data.

**Existing solutions:**
- General payroll packages (YaadBooks, HeadOffice, BizPay, iCoPAY; see the existing Jamaica report).
- Accountants.
- Global rostering tools (unverified for Jamaica).

**The gap:**
Roster-to-guard-wage rules (post and hours based pay, site billing) feeding statutory payments. Unverified that general payroll tools fail here.

**Possible product:**
Roster-driven payroll and NHT/NIS reconciliation for security firms, with a client billing export.

**MVP:**
Roster upload producing a pay run and statutory schedule for one company.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
J$ equivalent of US$50-150 per month per company (estimate).

**Offline channel:**
PSRA registered company list, security industry associations (names unverified), and guard training schools.

**Market count:**
331 organisations and 30,013 individuals registered in 2025 (Observer, 23 July 2026).

**Offline evidence:**
Weak. Only press reporting; no software listings were checked.

**Willingness to pay:** Would pay for payroll software if it avoids NHT arrears; evidence thin.

**Founder access:** A foreign solo founder could sell remotely but needs local statutory knowledge.

**Risks:**
- Competing payroll vendors.
- Small count.
- Thin margins in the sector.

**Kill condition:**
Interviews show firms already run rosters inside payroll software.

**Score:** 4/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Funeral homes:** about 1,000 establishments but about 100 certified, and the regulations have been stalled for years (JIS, Gleaner). No trigger and no recurring filing. Sources: https://jis.gov.jm/new-regulations-coming-for-funeral-homes/, https://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20190410/funeral-home-regulations-still-ice
- **Pesticide dealers and pest control operators:** triennial licences and unknown count. Source: https://jis.gov.jm/pca-reports-success-in-licensing-of-pest-control-operators/
- **Scrap metal:** only 13 valid exporters; JSWIFT already online. Source: https://jis.gov.jm/new-rules-for-scrap-metal-trade/
- **Butchers and abattoirs, fishers, route taxis, household employers, cannabis licensees, TPDCo operators:** no count, no payer or no trigger (see the table).

## 4. Method notes

- Worked: "Jamaica" plus the act name (Agricultural Produce Act, Pesticides Act) returned JIS and Ministry pages with counts and fines.
- Did not work: pawnbroker, second-hand dealer and money changer queries returned only US results. Jamaica scrap metal register queries also returned foreign results.
- Not covered: Gleaner and Observer articles are paywalled, so only snippets were read. No official licensing register lists were found for any industry. Counts for butchers, fishers, taxis, pest control and TPDCo are missing.
