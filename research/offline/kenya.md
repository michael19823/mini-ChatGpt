## 2. Opportunities

### Opportunity: Annual-Returns Rescue for Small Co-operatives and Non-Deposit-Taking SACCOs

**Industry:**
Co-operative societies: small non-deposit-taking SACCOs, dairy, coffee and farmer co-ops, transport and housing co-ops.

**Buyer:**
Secretary, treasurer or manager (often part-time and older) of a co-op with roughly 50–2,000 members. A second buyer is the small audit firm or co-op auditor that prepares their accounts.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- **May 2026:** CS Oparanya said 12,000+ SACCOs face deregistration. Only about 2,000 of more than 14,000 file returns. A gazette notice gives 21 days, then licences are revoked, and newspapers will publish the names of compliant SACCOs.
- Co-ops that have not filed for 3 years are being deregistered.
- **2026:** the new Cooperatives Bill was promised "within a month" (DP Kindiki, July 2026). It brings stronger oversight and pushes digital record-keeping.

**Current workflow:**
1. The treasurer keeps paper cash books and member ledgers, or Excel files.
2. At year-end the documents go to an auditor, who rebuilds the books.
3. The treasurer prepares the AGM and minutes, and updates the list of officials.
4. The annual return, audited statements and officials list are taken or posted to the county co-operative office or the Commissioner.
5. If the books are a mess, the audit slips, so the return slips; after 3 years the society is deregistered.

**Pain:**
- Under 5,000 of ~30,000 co-ops submitted audited results (16.6%). Audited accounts submitted fell to 4,062 in the year to June 2025.
- Deregistration threatens members' savings. The sector holds more than KSh 1.2 trillion across 7.4M members (Business Daily).

**Existing solutions:**
- Core-banking SACCO systems for deposit-taking SACCOs (SASRA-regulated; vendors such as SaccoWorks), which are too heavy for small societies.
- Dairy collection apps: Virtual City AgriManagr, Hamari Dairy and others.
- ERPNext and generic accounting software.
- The real substitute: a co-op auditor or accountant, the county co-operative officer, and paper ledgers.

**Offline evidence:**
- Regulators measure non-filing in the tens of thousands, and enforcement runs through newspaper lists and gazette notices.
- Officials are part-time volunteers.
- Filing goes physically through county co-operative offices (**unverified** whether an online filing portal exists in 2026).

**Offline channel:**
- County co-operative officers, who chase non-filers and could refer them.
- Small audit firms that audit co-ops: sell to them as a back-office tool.
- Co-op unions and county co-op federations.
- The gazette list of non-compliant SACCOs is a public, named prospect list, with phone outreach from it.

**Market count:**
- ~14,000 registered SACCOs and ~30,000 co-ops in all; ~2,000–5,000 currently file (The Star, May 2026; Business Daily, 2025).
- Many non-filers are dormant and worthless. The live, reachable non-filer pool is an **estimate** of 3,000–6,000.

**The gap:**
The software made for co-ops serves large SACCOs and dairy collection. Nothing found turns a small co-op's paper ledger into audit-ready statements plus a filing pack (return form, officials list, AGM minutes template, filing checklist and deadline tracking).

**Possible product:**
A simple member-ledger and cash-book app (mobile, M-Pesa statement import) that produces a year-end "audit pack" for the auditor and a "return pack" for the Commissioner. It would come with a done-for-you catch-up service for societies 1–3 years behind.

**MVP:**
- M-Pesa statement and Excel import into a contributions and loans ledger.
- Trial balance, a members' statement and the annual-return form pre-filled.
- An AGM minutes template.
- Sold first as a catch-up service through 2–3 partner auditors.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- Catch-up service: KSh 15,000–40,000 per society-year.
- Software: KSh 1,500–3,000 a month.
- Auditor seat: KSh 5,000 a month.
- Buyers would mostly pay for the service rather than the software. Treat it as service-plus-software.

**How to find first customers:**
- The gazette or newspaper list of non-compliant SACCOs.
- Two or three county co-operative offices (Nyeri, Kiambu, Meru: dense dairy and coffee co-ops).
- Co-op auditors listed with ICPAK.

**Risks:**
- Many non-filers are dormant shells with no money.
- The state may launch its own co-op management or filing system under the new Act.
- An ICPAK-registered auditor's sign-off is still required, so the product cannot replace the audit.
- A non-local founder needs a Kenyan partner: county offices and unions work in person and in Kiswahili.

**Kill condition:**
- Live non-filers will not pay KSh 15k or more for a catch-up; or
- the Commissioner rolls out a free mandatory digital ledger or filing platform under the new Act within 12 months.

**Score:** 6/10 (Pain 7, Frequency 4 (annual, with monthly bookkeeping), Mandatory 9, Fragmentation 4, Competition 6, Gap 6, Buyer access 8, WTP 4, MVP 7, Distribution 7). A non-local solo founder would struggle; this needs a local operator.

### Opportunity: Scrap Metal Dealer Digital Register and Council Returns

**Industry:**
Scrap metal dealers and collectors licensed by the Scrap Metal Council (Scrap Metal Act 2015).

**Buyer:**
Owner of a licensed scrap yard or dealer, including dealers holding the extra copper/aluminium licence.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The Scrap Metal Dealers Rules (LN 84/2022) require dealers to "automate their record keeping systems for real-time monitoring and submission of returns to the Council" within one year. That deadline has passed, and no dealer-side system was found.
- **Feb 2026:** the Council suspended export-licence issuance and renewal after a new wave of infrastructure vandalism. It warned licensed dealers to comply or face revocation.
- Vandalism of the SGR, power lines and county assets keeps the Council under political pressure.

**Current workflow:**
1. The dealer buys metal at the gate and weighs it on a platform scale.
2. The clerk writes into a paper register:
   - description, type, form, condition and weight of the metal, plus identifying marks;
   - date and time of receipt;
   - for a vehicle delivery: the registration and the driver's ID and phone number;
   - for a person: name, ID copy, phone number and address.
3. Copper and aluminium go into a separate register.
4. Records are kept for 7 years and shown to Council inspectors and police on demand.
5. The Council receives returns (format **unverified**); export permits go separately through KenTrade TFP.

**Pain:**
- Licence revocation, and with it the business, if inspectors find gaps after a vandalism incident.
- The dealer licence costs KSh 250,000, so the business at stake is substantial.
- Copying ID cards and handwriting entries at the gate is slow (**unverified** from operators).

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper registers. No Kenya-specific stationer product was found (in South Africa, Hortors sells one).
- Generic POS and scale software.
- KenTrade TFP, for export/import permits only.
- Foreign scrap-yard software (e.g. US ScrapRight-type systems; not verified for Kenya).
- No Kenyan dealer register software was found in search.

**Offline evidence:**
- No vendor listings.
- Operators are reached through association meetings and county officials.
- The Council talks to dealers through regional meetings reported by Kenya News Agency (KNA).

**Offline channel:**
- The **Scrap Metal Dealers Association**, membership of which is a licence condition, so every licensed dealer is a member.
- Scrap Metal Council regional sensitisation meetings.
- Scale suppliers who calibrate weighbridges and platform scales.

**Market count:**
- ~3,900 operators estimated in 2022 (press).
- Licensed dealers number in the low hundreds (**estimate**: 91 applicants nationally in 2022; 3 of 20 licensed in Murang'a by Dec 2025). The current Council licence list was not found.

**The gap:**
The rules demand a digital, real-time register and Council returns, yet nobody sells a cheap, Kenya-specific gate app. It would capture the ID photo, vehicle plate, weight and photo of the metal, flag copper and aluminium, and produce the return.

**Possible product:**
An Android gate app: photograph the ID and the metal, enter or connect the weight, log the vehicle, keep separate copper and aluminium books, give an inspector a view, and export the Council return. It should grow into a Council-facing dashboard if the Council wants to adopt it.

**MVP:**
An offline-first Android register with ID OCR, a printable daily register and a monthly return PDF. Pilot with 5 yards in one county.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
KSh 3,000–6,000 a month per yard (USD 25–45). Dealers would pay for software if the Council or association endorses it; otherwise only to satisfy an inspector.

**How to find first customers:**
- Scrap Metal Dealers Association branch chairs (e.g. Knight Scrap and Iron Metal Dealers Association).
- County trade officers in Nakuru and Murang'a, where dealers already meet the Council.

**Risks:**
- The market is small. The Council might mandate its own system or a single vendor; an endorsement from it is the real moat.
- The sector is politically volatile (bans in 2022, export suspension in 2026).
- The real number of licensed dealers is unknown.
- A non-local founder could not sell this: it needs association relationships.

**Kill condition:**
- Fewer than ~300 licensed dealers; or
- the Council will not recognise a third-party register as compliant with the "automate records" rule.

**Score:** 5.5/10 (Pain 6, Frequency 9 (per transaction), Mandatory 8, Fragmentation 2, Competition 8, Gap 8, Buyer access 7, WTP 5, MVP 8, Distribution 6). The small market and dependence on the regulator cap the score.

### Opportunity: PSRA Licence-Renewal Compliance Pack for Small Security Firms

**Industry:**
Private security guarding companies.

**Buyer:**
Owner or operations manager of a guarding firm with 20–500 guards.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The Private Security (General) Regulations 2025 require formal registration of every guard with a Guard Force Number (GFN). Firms must show proof of the KSh 30,000 minimum wage and of statutory remittances (NSSF, SHA, Housing Levy) to get or renew a licence. Clients must check that their provider is registered.
- **March 2026:** 27 illegal firms were shut.
- **September 2026:** a nationwide crackdown on unregistered firms and guards began. Employing an unregistered guard carries a KSh 500k fine.

**Current workflow:**
1. Guards are recruited.
2. Vetting documents (ID, good-conduct certificate, training) are collected on paper.
3. Each guard is registered with PSRA for a GFN.
4. Payroll is run (Excel, or Workpay at larger firms).
5. NSSF, SHA, Housing Levy and PAYE are remitted on separate portals.
6. At renewal, payslips and remittance receipts are assembled as proof.
7. Clients and tenders ask for the same evidence again.

**Pain:**
- Deregistration hits firms that do not pay the minimum wage.
- The fine per unregistered guard is high.
- Clients now carry a duty to check their provider, so the evidence is requested repeatedly.

**Existing solutions:**
- **Workpay**, with a dedicated security-company product: payroll, rostering, guard patrol.
- International guard software: SecurityTime, Guardhouse.
- Local payroll SaaS and accountants.
- PSRA's own registration process.

**Offline evidence:**
- Firm owners are often ex-uniformed staff.
- Enforcement is by field raids, and guard records are on paper.
- The industry's voice is unions (COTU, the guards' union) and associations, not forums.

**Offline channel:**
- Kenya Security Industry Association (KSIA) and Protective Security Industry Association (PSIA) members (**unverified** current names).
- PSRA's licensed-provider list.
- Training colleges that issue guard certificates.

**Market count:**
~2,000 firms (2023 press figure, **unverified**); 100,000+ registered guards (PSRA via KNA).

**The gap:**
Payroll tools pay guards. None found produce a per-guard "compliance passport" (GFN status, vetting expiry, wage ≥ 30k, remittance proof per month) and a client- or tender-facing certificate.

**Possible product:**
A compliance layer that reads payroll exports (Workpay, Excel) plus NSSF, SHA and KRA receipts. It reconciles them per guard and produces a renewal dossier and a shareable client certificate.

**MVP:**
Excel payroll upload plus receipt upload, a reconciliation of guard × month × statutory line, and a PDF dossier.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
KSh 50–100 per guard per month (a 100-guard firm pays KSh 5k–10k). Firms with Workpay already have most of this; small firms may want a done-for-you renewal service instead.

**How to find first customers:**
PSRA public list of registered firms, phone outreach, and security association meetings.

**Risks:**
- Workpay could add the feature cheaply.
- Firms that underpay guards want to hide the evidence, not produce it.
- PSRA may build its own portal checks.

**Kill condition:**
Workpay or PSRA already issues a per-guard compliance certificate; or fewer than 1 in 10 firms interviewed would pay.

**Score:** 4.5/10 (Pain 6, Frequency 6, Mandatory 8, Fragmentation 3, Competition 4, Gap 4, Buyer access 7, WTP 4, MVP 7, Distribution 5). A local founder is preferred.

## 3. Rejected

- **Agrovet multi-regulator compliance (PCPB, KEPHIS, VMD, county).**
  - The pattern is attractive: one shop answers several authorities, and the PCPB withdrawn/restricted list arrived in June 2025, followed by crackdowns in Aug–Sep 2026.
  - It was killed by very low willingness to pay and existing agrovet POS products (MTela, iPOS, agriPOS).
  - Enforcement targets counterfeit stock, not paperwork.
  - Possible later as a feature for a POS vendor.
- **Household employers of domestic workers.** There is no enforcement of NSSF, SHA or the Housing Levy for households, and no payer.
- **Livestock traders and abattoirs.** The state's ANITRAC (RFID plus national database, Bill 2026) is the system of record.
- **Borehole and water permits.** WRA's own app (Mar 2026) and smart-meter platform cover permits, payments and readings.
- **Matatu SACCOs.** Crowded field (SaccoWorks, MatatuOS, Simple Fare).
- **Boda boda SACCOs.** Cash-poor buyers and a contested bill.
- **Charcoal producers.** The draft 2025 regulations are not in force.
- **Fishers and BMUs.** Donor-funded state data systems already cover them.
- **Gold and mineral dealers.** Tiny base, and the state Mining Cadastre Portal already exists.
- **Exhauster and waste transporters.** NEMA plus county duplication is real, but no new 2025–26 manifest mandate was found. Re-check the Sustainable Waste Management Act regulations before discarding.

## 4. Method notes

- What worked:
  - Kenya News Agency (kenyanews.go.ke) and national dailies report regulator crackdowns with dates, which made enforcement easy to find.
  - kenyalaw.org Legal Notices gave exact register duties (scrap).
  - Searching "<regulator> crackdown 2026" surfaced triggers quickly.
- Operator counts are the weak point. Kenyan regulators rarely publish licence lists that search engines index (Scrap Metal Council, PSRA, PCPB agro-dealers), so counts come from ministers quoted in the press.
- English works fine; Kiswahili queries were not needed for regulator material.
- Not reached (budget): driving schools (NTSA records, curriculum Jan 2026), funeral homes and mortuaries, liquor licensees, weights-and-measures verification, NEA-licensed recruitment agencies for Gulf domestic workers.
