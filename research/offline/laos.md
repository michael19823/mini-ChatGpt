Laos was already marked low-value in the first round (`research/countries/laos.md`: a small, low-paying market that is hard to reach without speaking Lao). This pass did not change that. Claims without a listed source are marked **unverified**. Counts I could not source say "unknown". I did not invent any.

**Founder access (applies to every row):** A non-local solo founder cannot realistically sell in Laos. Several of the regulated trades below are legally reserved for Lao nationals: pawnshops by MOIC notification 2013 and the BOL licence conditions; money exchange is **unverified**. Paperwork is in Lao script, and local payment rails need a local partner (**unverified**, carried over from the first round).

## 2. Strongest opportunities

None reach the bar. The one lead with a real 2026 trigger is written up below for comparison. Its low score is deliberate.

### Opportunity: Pawnshop licence re-application and BOL reporting pack (Vientiane)

**Industry:**
Pawnshops (non-bank finance)

**Buyer:**
Owner-manager of a licensed Lao pawnshop, or the local accountant or law firm preparing its BOL filings

**Trigger / Why now:**
On 31 Jul 2026, BOL suspended new pawnshop and microfinance licences in Vientiane to "reorganise the licensing system" and "introduce stricter and more effective conditions". Applications are expected to reopen in Nov 2026 under a revised framework, with rules not yet published.

**Current workflow:**
1. The applicant assembles the paper dossier listed in the Lao Trade Portal procedure: capital proof of at least LAK 1bn, staff qualifications, criminal-record checks.
2. The applicant submits it at the BOL Financial Institution Supervision Department.
3. Once licensed, the shop keeps a pledge ledger and reports to BOL. The format and frequency are **unverified**.

**Pain:**
The re-licensing requirements are uncertain and will be stricter. Beyond that, I have no direct evidence of pain (no complaints were searchable).

**Existing solutions:**
Local law firms (Tilleke & Gibbins and VDB Loi both publish Lao licensing notes), accountants, paper ledgers, and generic pawn POS software from Thailand (**unverified**).

**Offline evidence:**
The licence procedure is a paper PDF on laotradeportal.gov.la. I found no Lao-language pawn software.

**Offline channel:**
The BOL list of licensed institutions (if published; **unverified**), and the law firms that advise applicants.

**Market count:**
Unknown. Probably tens, not thousands (estimate).

**The gap:**
A Lao-language pledge register that produces BOL report formats. That would only be worth anything if the November rules add recurring reporting.

**Possible product:**
A Lao-language pledge ledger with BOL export.

**MVP:**
A spreadsheet template plus a done-for-you filing service delivered through a local law firm.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
USD 20–50/month. Only a service would sell.

**How to find first customers:**
A local partner walking the shops in Vientiane.

**Risks:**
The sector is reserved for Lao nationals, the market is tiny, the rules are unknown, and the Kip is weak.

**Kill condition:**
The new BOL rules add no recurring report, or the licensed population is under 100. Both are likely.

**Score:** 2/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Money-exchange daily-limit log:** The Decision 449/BOL cap (daily FX buying no more than equity; individuals only) is real, but the rules keep changing, the buyers are Lao-only and there aren't many of them. Sources: https://vdb-loi.com/?p=3413, https://laotradeportal.gov.la/upload/files/6._License_to_Operate_a_Money_Exchange_Business.pdf
- **Export-cattle traceability (China protocol):** The GACC–MAF protocol requires licensed, certified export farms and 45 + 30 days in the Sing District zones. But only a few export companies are involved, FAO/donor programmes supply the tooling, and smallholders don't pay. Sources: https://laotradeportal.gov.la/en-gb/site/display/1962, https://www.fao.org/asiapacific/news/detail-events/ar/c/1630365/
- **Pesticide registration:** This happens once per product at MAF (Regulation 2860/MAF), and I found no per-sale register for retailers. Source: https://laotradeportal.gov.la/upload/files/29._Registration_of_Pesticides.pdf

## 4. Method notes

- **Worked:** English queries naming the regulator ("Bank of the Lao PDR", "MAF", "licence") reliably surface the **Lao Trade Portal** (laotradeportal.gov.la). It hosts unofficial English translations of the licence procedures and is the best register-of-obligations source for Laos. Law-firm notes (VDB Loi, Tilleke) and the Vientiane Times / The Star cover new BOL decisions.
- **Didn't work / not tried:** The budget was cut off after 5 searches, so I tried no Lao-language queries. I found no operator counts. A future pass should try Lao-script queries for "ໂຮງຊວດຈຳ" (pawnshop) and the MLSW recruitment-agency list, and check labour-export agencies first.
