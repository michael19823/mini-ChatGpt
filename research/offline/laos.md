# Laos (Lao PDR): offline-industries pass

**Status:** Mostly complete. 18 searches were attempted out of a budget of 20. Twelve returned results. Six were refused for usage or session limits (search 6 in the first run, then the last three of the second run). Three seed groups are still unresearched: household employers, tuk-tuk/taxi operators and pharmacies. They are marked below. Laos was already rated low-value in the first round (`research/countries/laos.md`: a small, low-paying market that is hard to reach without Lao), and this pass confirms that verdict. Claims without a listed source are marked **unverified**. Counts I could not source say "unknown". I did not invent any.

**Founder access (applies to every row):** A non-local solo founder cannot realistically sell in Laos. Pawnshops are legally reserved for Lao nationals (MOIC notification 2013; BOL licence conditions). Paperwork is in Lao script. Local payment rails need a local partner (**unverified**, carried over from the first round). Several of the most regulated activities are run by the state, not by small businesses. One example is seasonal labour to Korea, which the Ministry of Labour and Social Welfare (MLSW) runs through province-to-city MOUs.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

None reach the bar. Two weak leads are written up below for comparison.

### Opportunity: Per-worker migration dossier tracker for Lao recruitment agencies (Thailand MOU channel)

**Industry:**
Labour-export recruitment agencies

**Buyer:**
Owner or operations manager of an MLSW-licensed Lao recruitment agency

**Trigger / Why now:**
Thailand moved MOU work permits to the e-WorkPermit system on 3 Oct 2025. Employers struggled, and paper applications were temporarily allowed again. Lao authorities are pushing the legal channel: 73,000+ Lao workers were undocumented abroad in early 2025, and the government issued warnings about unauthorised brokers in Aug 2026.

**Current workflow:**
1. The agency recruits workers in the provinces and collects ID, passport or certificate of identity, medical checks and the contract.
2. It files the dossier with MLSW or the provincial labour office for approval to send the workers.
3. It coordinates with the Thai MOU agency or employer, who files on the e-WorkPermit system and pays THB 100 per application plus THB 900 per permit.
4. It tracks each worker's permit expiry and renewal through the Thai employer (**unverified** that Lao agencies do this).

**Pain:**
One worker's documents pass through two governments in two languages and scripts. The Thai portal changed in Oct 2025. I found no direct complaint from a Lao agency.

**Existing solutions:**
Paper files and spreadsheets (**unverified**); the Thai counterpart agency's systems; Thailand's own e-WorkPermit portal; broker networks.

**Offline evidence:**
Lao-side approvals happen at MLSW and provincial offices. I found no Lao-language agency software.

**Offline channel:**
MLSW's list of licensed agencies (not found online) and MLSW consultation meetings with agencies. The Thai counterpart agencies are a second route.

**Market count:**
33 agencies permitted to send workers abroad (ADB 2019; old figure).

**The gap:**
A bilingual Lao–Thai per-worker checklist that tracks expiries across both countries' documents.

**Possible product:**
A per-worker dossier and expiry tracker in Lao and Thai, sold to the Thai MOU agencies, which have more money, with Lao agencies as a secondary market.

**MVP:**
A shared checklist plus expiry alerts.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
USD 30–100/month per agency, or a per-worker fee.

**How to find first customers:**
Through the Thai side.

**Risks:**
Only 33 Lao buyers, a weak Kip, state-controlled channels, and the Thai side's own systems.

**Kill condition:**
Thai MOU agencies already do this tracking. This is likely.

**Score:** 3/10

### Opportunity: Pawnshop licence re-application and BOL reporting pack (Vientiane)

**Industry:**
Pawnshops (non-bank finance)

**Buyer:**
Owner-manager of a licensed Lao pawnshop, or the local accountant or law firm that does its BOL filings

**Trigger / Why now:**
BOL suspended new pawnshop and MFI licences in Vientiane on 31 Jul 2026 to "reorganise the licensing system" and add "stricter and more effective conditions". Applications are expected to reopen in Nov 2026 under a revised framework that has not yet been published.

**Current workflow:**
1. The applicant assembles the paper dossier: capital of at least LAK 1bn, staff qualifications, criminal-record checks.
2. The applicant submits it at the BOL Financial Institution Supervision Department.
3. Once licensed, the shop keeps a pledge ledger and reports to BOL. The format and frequency are **unverified**.

**Pain:**
The re-licensing rules are uncertain and will be stricter. Beyond that I found no direct evidence of pain.

**Existing solutions:**
Local law firms (VDB Loi, Tilleke & Gibbins), accountants, paper ledgers, and Thai pawn POS software (**unverified**).

**Offline evidence:**
The licence procedure is a paper PDF on laotradeportal.gov.la. I found no Lao pawn software.

**Offline channel:**
The BOL list of licensed institutions (**unverified** that it is published), and the law firms that advise applicants.

**Market count:**
Unknown. Probably tens (estimate).

**The gap:**
A Lao-language pledge register that produces BOL report formats. This only matters if the November rules add recurring reports.

**Possible product:**
A Lao-language pledge ledger with BOL export.

**MVP:**
A spreadsheet template plus a filing service delivered through a law firm.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
USD 20–50/month. Only a done-for-you service would sell.

**How to find first customers:**
A local partner visiting shops in Vientiane.

**Risks:**
The sector is reserved for Lao nationals, the market is tiny, the rules are unknown, and the Kip is weak.

**Kill condition:**
The new rules add no recurring report, or there are fewer than 100 licensees.

**Score:** 2/10

## 4. Method notes

- **Worked:** English queries that name the regulator (BOL, MAF, MLSW, MOIC) reliably surface the **Lao Trade Portal** (laotradeportal.gov.la). It hosts English procedure PDFs for licences and is the best obligations register for Laos. KPL, the Vientiane Times, The Star and the Laotian Times cover new decisions. VDB Loi and Tilleke publish legal summaries.
- **Didn't work:** queries about scrap (results drifted to Louisiana, "LA"). I found no operator counts beyond an old ADB figure for recruitment agencies. I tried no Lao-script queries.
- **Next time:** run Lao-script queries (for example ໂຮງຊວດຈຳ for pawnshop) and try to find the MLSW list of licensed agencies. The domestic-worker, tuk-tuk and pharmacy rows are still open.
