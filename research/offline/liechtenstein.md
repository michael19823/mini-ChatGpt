# Liechtenstein: Offline-Industries Pass

**Status:** Microstate (about 40,000 residents, 11 municipalities). Research date: 2026-10-05. I used 4 WebSearch calls (the budget was 8, and the run was resumed after an interruption). The market is fully accessible: it is in the EEA, uses the CHF, is in a customs union with Switzerland and is German-speaking. The constraint is size. Most quiet industries here have one to a few dozen operators.

**Bottom line:** No viable standalone opportunity. One candidate, household-employer payroll and registration, has documented pain on the record. It scores low because the buyer pool is small, the households are mostly informal and the likely substitute is an agency or a government voucher scheme. It is useful only as a Liechtenstein add-on to a Swiss or Austrian household-payroll service.

## 2. Opportunities

### Opportunity: Liechtenstein household-employer payroll and registration service

**Industry:**
Private households employing cleaners, nannies or carers (Hausangestellte)

**Buyer:**
A private household, often older residents or expat professionals in the finance sector. A secondary buyer is a cleaning or carer agency.

**Trigger / Why now:**
This is a long-standing problem with no confirmed 2025–2026 trigger. The LANV and the government have publicly said that bureaucracy drives undeclared work, and an Austrian-style Dienstleistungsscheck (service voucher) has been discussed. I could not confirm whether a voucher or simplified procedure was ever adopted (unverified). Such a scheme would be both the trigger and the kill condition.

**Current workflow:**
1. The household registers as an employer with the AHV-IV-FAK.
2. It arranges accident insurance (UVG) with a private insurer and pays half of the worker's mandatory health-insurance premium.
3. It calculates the social-insurance and withholding-tax deductions, issues payslips and files the annual wage declaration.
4. Alternatively it uses an agency (at an agency margin), or it doesn't declare the worker at all (the majority, according to the LANV).

**Pain:**
The LANV estimates that "well over 50%" of household cleaners are undeclared because of the bureaucracy, and that several authorities are involved even for a 20% part-time cleaner (Volksblatt 2017, archived at ahv.li). The pain is documented but dated (2017).

**Existing solutions:**
Agencies (Spitex and private carer agencies; names not verified), fiduciaries and accountants doing payroll for wealthy households, and the AHV office's own forms. Swiss analogues (Quitt!, the cantonal simplified procedures) and Austria's Dienstleistungsscheck do not cover Liechtenstein, which shows the model exists next door.

**Offline evidence:**
A union says most of the market is informal, registration goes through several separate authorities, and there is no LI-specific household payroll software (none was found in searches).

**Offline channel:**
Fiduciaries (Treuhänder) who already serve wealthy households, the AHV-IV-FAK counter (if it would refer clients, which is unverified), the LANV union and parish or community noticeboards. A Swiss household-payroll provider extending across the Rhine is the realistic route.

**Market count:**
Unknown. Liechtenstein has roughly 17,000 households (estimate), and formally declared household employers are likely a few hundred (unverified estimate).

**The gap:**
A single done-for-you service covering AHV registration, the UVG policy, the health-insurance share, payslips and the year-end wage statement for a Liechtenstein household.

**Possible product:**
A done-for-you household payroll service. The household enters hours monthly, and the service handles registration, contributions, insurance and documents.

**MVP:**
A form-plus-human service: onboarding checklist, a monthly payslip generator using Liechtenstein contribution rates, and AHV filing done by the operator.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
CHF 20–40 per month per household (estimate). The buyer would pay for a done-for-you service, not for software.

**How to find first customers:**
Referrals from fiduciaries, Liechtenstein expat and finance-sector employee networks, and LANV.

**Risks:**
The market is tiny, most households prefer staying informal, and a government voucher scheme would make the product redundant. Fiduciaries already do this for their wealthy clients. A non-local solo founder could only sell this as an extension of an existing Swiss service, and it needs a local partner for trust.

**Kill condition:**
Liechtenstein has adopted, or adopts, a voucher or simplified procedure run by the AHV, or there are fewer than about 500 declared household employers.

**Score:** 2/10 (standalone). It reaches about 3/10 as a Liechtenstein module for an existing Swiss household-payroll provider.

## 3. Rejected

- **Chimney-sweep inspection records.** Only a handful of district-appointed sweeps exist (Kaminfegerverordnung 1975, https://www.gesetze.li/chrono/pdf/1975063000), and Swiss trade software covers them.
- **Livestock movement and holding reports.** The free Swiss-shared TVD/Agate system is mandatory, and there are about 100 farms (https://www.statistikportal.li/statistikportal/publications/745-landwirtschaftsstatistik/2010/01/1/745.2010.01_01_landwirtschaftsstatistik-2010.pdf).
- **Beekeepers, abattoirs, dealers, taxis, tattoo studios, cemeteries, hunting.** Each group has single-digit to low-hundred operators, with no verified paper pain and no budget.

## 4. Method notes

German regulator-first queries ("anmelden AHV Arbeitgeber Privathaushalt", "Kaminfegerkreis Verordnung") found statutes on gesetze.li and the ahv.li press archive quickly. Results for Liechtenstein are often swamped by Swiss cantonal pages. Livestock queries returned only historical statistics. The quiet-industry lens confirms the earlier report's finding: in Liechtenstein, every quiet industry is too small, and each is already served by a Swiss system or a fiduciary.
