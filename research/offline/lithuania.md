# Lithuania: Offline (quiet) industries pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. About 16 WebSearch calls were used (4 more were refused by a session limit and later retried). WebFetch was not used, so everything comes from search snippets. Anything not confirmed on a source page is marked "unverified". Existing report (not repeated here): `research/countries/lithuania.md`.

**Bottom line:** Lithuania's quiet industries are mostly already served by free state portals (PPIS, GPAIS, VVAIS, the Livestock Register, CEMETY). Nothing reaches a "build" grade. The two best leads are a farm-inputs journal tool (agronomists and service providers, as a multi-farm product) and a vehicle-dismantler compliance tool. Both need customer interviews first, and both are capped by low willingness to pay.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Multi-farm input-journal tool for agronomists and farm-service providers (PPP + fertiliser + bee-spray notices)

**Industry:**
Crop farming (small and mid-size farms) and the agronomists, advisers and spraying contractors who serve them

**Buyer:**
An independent agronomist, adviser, or spraying/fertilising contractor who keeps journals for 20 to 200 farms. A secondary buyer is the farm owner. The farm itself rarely pays because PPIS is free.

**Trigger / Why now:**
The electronic PPP journal threshold fell in steps: above 150 ha from 2024-04-05, above 100 ha from 2025-04-05, and everyone from 1 ha from 2026-04-05 (ZUM/Mano ūkis snippets; the paper option still exists). The fertiliser journal became electronic-only from September 1 (year unverified in the snippet). So 2026 is the first season in which every small operator is inside the regime.

**Current workflow:**
1. The operator sprays or fertilises and notes it on paper, in a notebook, or by memory.
2. Within 30 days (PPP) or at set dates (fertiliser: Jul 1 and Dec 1, or 20 working days under agri-environment schemes) someone logs into paseliai.vic.lt and types each entry.
3. For bee-hazard products, a notice goes into PPIS two days before spraying.
4. Records (journals and purchase documents) are kept for 3 years for inspections by the State Crop Service.

**Pain:**
Mandatory with inspection by the State Crop Service. Pain evidence is thin: the agricultural press (manoukis.lt) wrote "many unanswered questions" and "will farmers need to connect to the internet right after spraying?", but no fine statistics or time-cost figures were found (unverified).

**Existing solutions:**
- PPIS journal on paseliai.vic.lt (free, state)
- Paper journals sold by stationers (biuroreikmenys.lt)
- Electronic or banking-portal filling, per the Ministry
- Farm-management software for Lithuania: not identified in searches (unverified; likely exists, so competitor diligence is incomplete)

**The gap:**
No identified multi-client tool for the person who enters data for many farms, with field photos or voice capture and reminders for the 30-day, Jul 1/Dec 1 and 2-day bee-notice deadlines. Whether PPIS has an API or bulk import is unverified.

**Possible product:**
A multi-farm logbook for advisers: capture applications in the field (offline mobile), check product and dose against the registered-products list, produce the exact entry sequence for PPIS (or push it if a file import exists), and track deadlines.

**MVP:**
One adviser, 20 farms, PPP and fertiliser entries, deadline alerts, and a printable audit-ready journal.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
EUR 2 to 5 per farm per month billed to the adviser; or EUR 15 to 30 a month per adviser (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**
Farm advisory service lists (unverified directory), accredited PPP training institutions (they hold the certified-user lists and issue certificates per the Ministry), agricultural cooperatives, farm-input retailers, and local farming press. A solo non-local founder could reach advisers online and by phone, but needs Lithuanian-language product and support.

**Risks:**
The state tool is free and could add a mobile app. Farmers pay nothing for compliance paperwork they do twice a year. A competitor may already exist.

**Kill condition:**
Interviews show advisers do not enter data for clients, or PPIS entry takes minutes per season, or a Lithuanian farm app already covers this at low cost.

**Score:** 4/10 (Distribution 5: named channels, but no verified list. Willingness to pay 3: likely service-only. Founder access: needs local language and trust.)

**Offline evidence:**
Paper journals sold at stationers; allowed paper filing; weak web chatter beyond farm-press articles.

**Offline channel:**
Training institutions and advisory services, farming cooperatives, input retailers.

**Market count:**
About 107.2k CAP applicants in 2026 (LRT/ZUDC snippet); the number of professional PPP users and advisers is unverified.

### Opportunity: Compliance autopilot for licensed vehicle dismantlers (ELV handlers)

**Industry:**
End-of-life vehicle dismantling and used-parts sale

**Buyer:**
Owner of a licensed dismantling yard (typically family-run, 2 to 10 staff).

**Trigger / Why now:**
No new 2026 regulation found. The trigger is enforcement and competition: the Environmental Protection Department ran the "Garažiukas" inspection campaign of ELV handlers (year unverified in snippet), published a one-stop checklist for handlers, and the ENTPTA association runs a "STOP illegal dismantlers" campaign. Licensed yards want proof of compliance to differentiate from the 100+ unlicensed advertisers.

**Current workflow:**
1. A vehicle arrives and is deregistered; a certificate of destruction is issued.
2. The yard dismantles, sorts parts and waste, and logs waste streams and handler transfers.
3. Waste accounting is kept in GPAIS; the annual report is confirmed by March 1.
4. Parts are listed for sale on classified sites. (Whether parts must be traced to the donor vehicle is unverified.)

**Pain:**
Enforced by inspections. Time and fine data were not found (unverified).

**Existing solutions:**
GPAIS (free), the AAD handler checklist, ERP modules with GPAIS integration (go-erp.eu, from the existing report), consultants (gpais.com), and the association's guidance. A dismantler-specific tool was not found (unverified).

**The gap:**
One vehicle record that generates the GPAIS waste entries, the parts inventory and the classified listing. Evidence that anyone wants this is weak.

**Possible product:**
A yard logbook: per-vehicle dismantling sheet, auto-allocated waste codes and weights, parts inventory with photos, annual GPAIS report checklist.

**MVP:**
Vehicle intake form, waste-code presets, quarterly and annual GPAIS checklists, parts list export to classified sites.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
EUR 30 to 60 a month per yard (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**
ENTPTA association member list and the competent authority's list of licensed waste handlers (access unverified). Contact the yards by phone.

**Risks:**
About 300 yards means a ceiling of roughly EUR 100 to 200k a year. The GPAIS API may not expose the needed journals. ERP vendors may already serve the larger yards.

**Kill condition:**
Yards report that ELV paperwork takes under an hour a month, or the association says members already use one common ERP.

**Score:** 3/10

**Offline evidence:**
Association-driven sector; enforcement news in trade and regional press; unlicensed competitors operate through classified ads, not software.

**Offline channel:**
ENTPTA association; licensed handler list; phone outreach.

**Market count:**
More than 300 licensed dismantlers (ENTPTA via eei.lt, secondary).

## 3. Rejected

- **Cemetery and funeral records:** CEMETY already serves 54 of 60 municipalities; buyers are municipalities (procurement) and funeral firms are tiny (229 firms, about 1,200 staff).
- **Beekeepers:** about 11,000 apiaries, but mostly hobbyists, one annual declaration, free Livestock Register, no spending power.
- **Scrap-metal dealers:** no register count or police-reporting regime found; only directory listings.
- **Animal transporters:** niche, licence-and-waybill regime, no count.
- **Beauty and tattoo studios:** one-off hygiene passport, no recurring filing.
- **Households as employers and seasonal foreign farm labour:** no household-specific data found; seasonal permits are one-off and handled by consultants.
- **Market and small food producers:** registration only.

## 4. Method notes

- Local-language queries combined with "registras", "žurnalas" and "privaloma" worked best; they surfaced the Ministry of Agriculture and VMVT pages and trade-press articles.
- The "register of licensed X dealers" pattern did not return official registers for scrap or second-hand dealers; counts for those groups stayed unverified.
- In Lithuania almost every quiet industry already has a free state portal (PPIS, GPAIS, VVAIS, Livestock Register), which caps willingness to pay. Ranking: farm journals and ELV yards are the only two with a mandatory recurring task and an offline channel. A service-plus-software model is more realistic than self-serve software.
- Not checked: second-hand/pawn/gold dealer regimes, driving instructors, taxi and minibus, hunting outfitters, chimney sweeps and boiler inspectors. Budget and an earlier session limit prevented this. Competitor diligence on Lithuanian farm-management software is incomplete.

## Opus review

**Verdict:** major issues for the lead opportunity. The trigger is confirmed. But the competitor diligence the report admits it left incomplete finds several Lithuanian farm-management tools, and the law puts the journal duty on the farmer, not the contractor.

**Claims checked (4 searches):**
- *PPP journal electronic thresholds: 200 ha (2024), 150 ha, 100 ha, then all users from 1 ha from 5 Apr 2026; paper still allowed; keep 3 years:* **confirmed** ([Mano ūkis](https://manoukis.lt/naujienos/ukis/pakeistos-augalu-apsaugos-produktu-naudojimo-taisykles); [NMA](https://nma.lrv.lt/lt/naujienos/pakeistos-augalu-apsaugos-produktu-naudojimo-taisykles/); [ŽŪR](https://www.zur.lt/augalu-apsaugos-produktu-naudojimo-apskaitos-zurnalui-pildyti-ketveriu-metu-pereinamasis-laikotarpis/)). The first threshold dates from 5 Apr 2024, which answers the report's "year not confirmed".
- *Entry deadline of 30 days:* **contradicted or outdated.** The current rules summarised by NMA and Mano ūkis say **15 calendar days**. The 30-day wording comes from the earlier version of the order.
- *Contractor or agronomist as the buyer:* **weakened.** The order says that when a spraying service uses products bought in the provider's name, the **service recipient (the farmer)** fills in the journal ([e-seimas](https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/rs/legalact/TAD/ee90cd10f2ba11ee97d7f4f65208a4ec/)). The contractor has no legal duty to sell to.
- *"Farm-management software for Lithuania: not identified":* **contradicted.** eAgronom has a Lithuanian site; Agro247, Geoface (a Lithuanian farm-management app) and FarmEasy are all live in Lithuania ([eAgronom LT](https://www.eagronom.com/lt); [Agro247](https://agro247.lt/); [Geoface](https://www.geoface.com/lt/2025/02/24/geoface-lietuviska-ukio-valdymo-programa/); [FarmEasy](https://farmeasy.lt/)). The state advisory service LŽŪKT also offers e-services ([LŽŪKT](https://www.lzukt.lt/elektronines-paslaugos/)). A search summary said its "e-GEBA" system generates PPP and fertiliser journals for PPIS; I could not confirm that on a source page (**unverified**). Either way, "no multi-client tool for advisers" no longer holds.
- *More than 300 licensed ELV dismantlers (ENTPTA via eei.lt):* **unverified.** A targeted search found no confirmation of the figure.

**Re-scores:**
- Multi-farm input-journal tool: **4 → 3.** Competition and incumbent gap drop: at least four local farm apps exist, and the state advisory service is the natural adviser channel. The contractor has no legal duty, which weakens the buyer.
- ELV dismantler tool: stays at 3/10. The market count is still secondary-source only.

**Treat as unreliable:** "within 30 days"; "no Lithuanian farm-management software"; the 300-dismantler count; "Garažiukas" campaign timing (no year given).
