# Mauritius: Offline-Industries Pass

**Date:** 2026-10-05 · **Searches used:** 2 of 20. The third search was refused with a usage-limit error, so I stopped as the instructions require. This is a short, partial report.
**Country context:** about 1.3M people. English is the legal language, French and Kreol are used in daily business. The existing report (`research/countries/mauritius.md`) covers the Tourist Fee, DNFBP AML, e-invoicing, PAYE/CSG returns and the beneficial-ownership register. None of those are repeated here.

**Bottom line:** This pass found no quiet-industry opportunity above 3/10. Its one documented finding is negative. Households that employ domestic workers already have a free government channel, the MRA's **MRAeasy** mobile app, for their monthly contribution returns. That removes the most obvious "household as employer" gap. Most seed-list groups below could not be screened because the search budget ran out. Their rows say "not screened" and make no claims.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

None reached the quality bar. The single candidate with evidence is documented below so that the rejection can be checked.

### Opportunity: Household-Employer Payroll Service (domestic workers)

**Industry:**
Households as employers (domestic workers, gardeners, live-in carers, drivers).

**Buyer:**
A household, often an expatriate or retiree, employing 1–3 domestic staff.

**Trigger / Why now:**
PRGF contributions (4.5% of monthly pay) became mandatory as monthly returns to the MRA from January 2022. That date is not new in 2026, and no 2025–2026 trigger for household employers was found.

**Current workflow:**
1. The household agrees a wage and pays cash or by bank transfer.
2. Each month it files the contribution return and pays through the MRA website or the MRAeasy app.
3. Payslips, leave and the end-of-year bonus are tracked by hand. The bonus is a statutory point in Mauritian labour law, but the details here are unverified.

**Pain:**
Low. The filing is a few fields for one or two workers, and the MRA gives a mobile app for exactly this case.

**Existing solutions:**
- MRA **MRAeasy** app (free; filing and payment for household employers)
- MRA e-services website
- Relocation firms, accountants and management companies that handle staff for expatriate clients (unverified, not researched)
- General payroll vendors (Sage VIP Mauritius and others), which are oversized for one worker

**Offline evidence:**
Weak. Household employers are probably not online communities, but the receiving authority has already gone mobile.

**Offline channel:**
Expatriate relocation agents and property-management conciergeries (which the country report names) could resell it. That is not verified.

**Market count:**
Unknown. No register or statistic for household employers was found.

**The gap:**
At most, there is no product that turns one employment record into a payslip, the monthly MRA figures, leave tracking and the year-end bonus together. That gap is too small to sell on its own.

**Possible product:**
A "one domestic worker" payroll assistant for expatriate households, sold through relocation agents.

**MVP:**
A worker profile, a monthly payslip PDF, the contribution figures to key into MRAeasy, and leave and bonus reminders.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
MUR 200–400 per household per month (estimate). It would most likely sell only as a done-for-you service bundled by a relocation firm.

**How to find first customers:**
Relocation agencies and expatriate property-management firms. No public list was found.

**Risks:**
The free MRA app, very low willingness to pay, an unknown market size, and the need for local sales partners.

**Kill condition:**
Already met in part: the free government app covers the filing. Abandon the idea unless relocation firms say they would pay to outsource staff admin.

**Score:** 2/10. Willingness to pay: households would pay for a done-for-you service, not for software. Founder access: a non-local founder could only reach buyers through local relocation firms.

## 3. Rejected

- **Household-employer contribution filing.** The substitute is the MRA's free MRAeasy app, which covers both filing and payment. The remaining payslip and leave work is too small to sell.
- **Pesticide dealer/user compliance (Use of Pesticides Act 2018).** This one is not rejected on its merits. It is parked for lack of evidence: I could not confirm a recurring register, licence return or 2025–2026 trigger. Sources: https://mauritius-chamber-of-agriculture.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Use-of-Pesticides-Act-2018.pdf, https://www.ecolex.org/details/legislation/use-of-pesticides-act-2018-no-8-of-2018-lex-faoc182259/

## 4. Method notes

- English queries for MRA obligations work well. The MRA publishes guides as PDFs at `mra.mu/download/...`.
- For Mauritian Acts, the Chamber of Agriculture's site and FAOLEX/Ecolex hold the texts. A follow-up pass should search the Government Gazette and the ministry pages (agriculture, fisheries, NLTA, municipalities) for licence lists. French queries are worth trying for the municipal and trade-licence groups.
- The pass was cut short after 2 searches by a usage-limit refusal. The seed groups marked "not screened" still need a proper pass, especially cane planters, fishermen, pleasure-craft operators and food producers.
