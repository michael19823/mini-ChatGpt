# Federated States of Micronesia (FSM): Offline-Industries Pass

**Date:** 2026-10-05
**Market class:** Microstate, about 105k people across four states (Yap, Chuuk, Pohnpei, Kosrae) spread over very long sea distances. The economy depends on Compact grants, fishing licence revenue and public employment. The private sector is small: retail and import wholesale, hospitality, construction and fisheries services.
**Search budget:** 8. The run was interrupted twice by API limits and resumed. 3 searches are logged in the final session. Searches made before the interruptions could not be recovered, but the total was kept within 8.
**Bottom line:** No quiet industry in FSM can support a standalone indie software product. Regulated quiet trades do exist: sakau (kava) bars in Pohnpei, copra buyers licensed under national law, betel-nut traders in Yap, and small retailers holding dual national and state licences. But each obligation is a one-off certificate or annual licence issued by a state agency at a counter. Buyer pools run from tens to low hundreds per state, and operators have little cash to spend on software. Section 2 is therefore empty on purpose.

This report does not repeat the items already covered in `research/countries/micronesia.md`: the VAT and Unified Revenue Authority reform, ASYCUDAWorld customs, fisheries reporting and electronic monitoring, payroll and social security, and multi-state sales tax.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

None. No candidate reached the brief's bar of identifiable buyers at scale, recurring mandatory reporting, willingness to pay and a concrete offline channel. I have not padded this section with template entries.

The nearest thing to an angle is a **Pacific kava/sakau premises-compliance tracker** sold to state EPAs and health departments across FSM, Kiribati, Vanuatu and Fiji, recording inspections and certificate status. That is a government sale, and it should be validated first in Fiji or Vanuatu, where the kava sector is far larger. Indicative standalone FSM score: 1/10.

## 3. Rejected

- **Sakau-bar compliance certificate tracker (Pohnpei).** The rule is real and enforced through EPA inspection. But it is one structural certificate with no periodic report. The substitute is the paper certificate on the wall, and the operators are informal.
- **Copra buyer licence and purchase records.** Licensing is required by statute, but there are only a few state-linked buyers. Digitisation would come through donor or government procurement.
- **Foreign-investment and state business-licence filing helper.** There is fragmentation (national plus four state regimes), but filings are one-off or annual. Local lawyers and accountants already handle the few hundred filers.
- **Dealer registers (scrap, second-hand, pawn).** No obligation was found in the FSM Code, and the trades barely exist.

## 4. Method notes

- fsmlaw.org (the FSM Legal Information System) and PacLII are the productive regulator-first sources. Searching "<industry> Pohnpei/FSM regulation" returns the statute or regulation page directly, as with the sakau-bar regulations and the Title 22 copra licence.
- No public licensing registers or counts exist online. Every market count above is an estimate.
- Local-language queries (Pohnpeian, Chuukese, Yapese, Kosraean) were not attempted. Legal text is in English and the budget was small.
