# Monaco: Offline-Industries Pass

**Date:** 2026-10-05
**Market class:** Microstate, about 38,000–39,000 residents. IMSEE counts 6,357 private-sector employers in total, of which 2,447 are households employing domestic staff (L'Observateur de Monaco, citing IMSEE).
**Search budget:** 8. The run was interrupted by an API limit and then resumed. 5 searches are logged in the resumed session. Any searches made before the interruption could not be recovered, but the total stayed within 8.
**Bottom line:** Monaco has no quiet industry that supports a standalone indie product. The only quiet group with a real count is **households employing domestic staff** (about 2,450 employers and about 2,750–3,230 staff). Even there, the receiving body (Caisses Sociales de Monaco) already gives household employers a free tele-service that calculates payslips, and agencies and payroll bureaus do the rest. Every other regulated quiet trade has a few dozen operators at most: antique and second-hand dealers, jewellers buying second-hand goods, taxis (90 licences) and market traders. Monaco's rules on these trades follow French models closely. So any product would be a French product with a Monaco option.

This report does not repeat the opportunities already covered in `research/countries/monaco.md`: the AML/CFT STRIX register for real-estate agents and DNFBPs, and Franco-Monegasque e-invoicing.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

Only one candidate is worth writing up, and it is weak. It is listed so the household-employer pool is on record, not as a recommendation.

### Opportunity: Monaco household-employer back office (domestic staff hire-to-payslip)

**Industry:**
Households as employers (gens de maison)

**Buyer:**
A wealthy resident household or family office employing 1–5 domestic staff (cleaner, nanny, butler, driver). Also the family's accountant or concierge who handles this work today.

**Trigger / Why now:**
There is no new regulatory trigger. The workflow repeats every month (CSM declaration and payslip), and every hire of a foreign employee needs a Service de l'Emploi hiring authorisation and work permit. Staff average 1.34 jobs each against 1.05 for the rest of the private sector, so many employees are shared across several households. That adds a coordination task for each employer.

**Current workflow:**
1. The household affiliates to the CSM as an employer of domestic staff and gets an employer number.
2. For a foreign hire, the household collects the hiring-authorisation form at the Service de l'Emploi, fills it in, signs it and returns it with a copy of the employee's ID. It then waits for the work permit.
3. The household registers the employee with the CSM.
4. Each month or quarter, the household files the single salary declaration (CCSS/CAR/CMRC/unemployment insurance), either on the paper form the CSM sends or through the CSM tele-service, and issues a payslip.
5. Leave, end-of-contract pay and changes to the contract are handled by hand, by an accountant or by a placement agency.

**Pain:**
Moderate. Households must follow Monaco labour law in full and pay monthly contributions. Placement agencies publish "how to hire domestic staff in Monaco" guides for 2024–2026 (Morgan & Mallet, Diamond Private, Platinum Nanny), which shows the steps confuse employers. No complaints or penalty evidence were found.

**Existing solutions:**
- The **CSM tele-service for employers of domestic staff** (free): online salary declaration, declaration history, contribution statements, and **payslip entry with automatic calculation**.
- Placement agencies (Morgan & Mallet, Diamond Private, Platinum Nanny) that guide or handle hiring.
- Local payroll bureaus and accountants (for example montecarlowork.com).
- Family offices and concierges for the wealthiest households.

**Offline evidence:**
The CSM still mails a declaration form every month. The work-permit request is a paper form collected at a counter. The know-how sits in agency blog posts, not in software listings.

**Offline channel:**
Placement agencies and Monaco accountants, as resellers. Or the concierge and family-office network. No public register of household employers exists, which is right for privacy reasons.

**Market count:**
2,447 household employers (IMSEE, cited by L'Observateur de Monaco).

**The gap:**
The receiving authority's own free tool already handles declarations and payslips, so a product could only add: (a) help with the Service de l'Emploi permit paperwork and keeping track of permits, (b) end-of-contract and leave calculations, and (c) coordination between the several households that share one employee. All three are small and mostly one-off or done once a year.

**Possible product:**
A done-for-you service from a bureau, supported by light software: hiring paperwork, the permit file, and monthly CSM filing on the household's behalf.

**MVP:**
A checklist and document pack for a foreign domestic hire, plus a monthly reminder and a register of permits and contracts.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
€30–60 per month per household as a service (estimate). Households would pay for a done-for-you service, not for software.

**How to find first customers:**
Partnerships with placement agencies and Monaco accountants. There is no public list of buyers.

**Risks:**
The free CSM tool sets the price at zero for the core task. Wealthy households hand this work to people they trust, not to an unknown SaaS. The market is capped at about 2,450 households. A non-local solo founder could not realistically sell this, because it needs a local presence and trust-based referrals.

**Kill condition:**
Interviews show households already use the CSM tele-service or an accountant without friction. Based on this pass, that is likely.

**Score:** 2/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Digital police register (livre de police) for antique and second-hand dealers and jewellers.** OS 4.365/1969 does require a daily register, but it must be a model approved by ministerial decree, kept in ink and initialled by the police commissioner, so a digital version may not be allowed. Only a few dozen dealers are obliged. The substitutes are French paper books (ARC Registres, ventes-pro) and Antikeo's online police book. Sources: https://legimonaco.mc/tnc/ordonnance/1969/11-20-4.365/ , https://monlivredepolice.antikeo.com/fonctionnement/
- **Taxi and foreign-taxi vignette compliance.** There are 90 licences, 28 foreign vignettes for 2026, filing is annual, and dispatch is centralised. Sources: https://journaldemonaco.gouv.mc/Journaux/2025/Journal-8774/Arrete-Ministeriel-n-2025-626-du-13-novembre-2025-fixant-pour-l-annee-2026-le-nombre-maximal-de-vignettes-accordees-aux-exploitants-de-taxis-etrangers-en-application-de-l-article-45-de-l-Ordonnance-Souveraine-n-1.720-du-4-juillet-2008-relative-a-la-r , https://monaco-hebdo.com/dossier/taxis-vtcla-discorde/
- **Household payroll SaaS on its own.** The CSM's free tele-service already calculates payslips and takes declarations.
- **Agriculture, animals, field trades, funeral.** These industries do not exist in Monaco, or have too few operators.

## 4. Method notes

- Searching in French on the receiving body worked best: caisses-sociales.mc, monservicepublic.gouv.mc, legimonaco.mc and journaldemonaco.gouv.mc each returned the exact obligation and form on the first query. Local news (L'Observateur de Monaco, Monaco Hebdo) relays IMSEE figures and was the only route to market counts.
- Searches for police registers mostly returned French stationery sellers. Monaco rules mirror French ones, so French substitutes apply directly.
- For a microstate, check the counts first. Most seed groups do not exist here or have fewer than 100 operators.
