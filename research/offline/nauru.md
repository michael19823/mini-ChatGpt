# Nauru: Offline-Industries Pass

**Date:** 2026-10-05
**Market class:** Microstate. About 12,000 people. The formal economy is government, the Regional Processing Centre (RPC), fishing-licence rents and, since January 2025, citizenship by investment (see `research/countries/nauru.md`).
**Search budget:** 8. The run was interrupted by an API limit and then resumed. 4 searches are logged in this session. Searches made before the interruption could not be recovered, but the total stayed within 8.
**Bottom line:** No quiet industry in Nauru can support a standalone indie software product. Nauru does regulate the right kinds of activity: a general business licence under the Business Licences Act 2017 (amended 2018), liquor licences, fishing licences under the new Fisheries Management Act 2024, and ministerial approval to export scrap metal. But each one has a buyer pool of a handful to a few dozen operators, and filing happens at a single government counter. A new Small Business Enterprises Act 2025 sets up a government fund and committee. It adds no reporting duty on businesses. Section 2 is therefore empty on purpose.

This report does not repeat the ideas already covered in `research/countries/nauru.md`: EST payroll withholding (Form A), ASYCUDA customs, PNA fisheries licensing, CBI agent due diligence and donor procurement.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

None. No candidate reached the brief's bar of identifiable buyers at scale, a recurring mandatory report, willingness to pay and an offline channel. A non-local solo founder could not realistically sell to Nauru operators: it would take an in-person relationship with a few government counters. If Nauru ever matters, it would be one jurisdiction inside a Pacific-wide product validated in Fiji or PNG, for example a small-fisher licence and catch-log app sold to fisheries authorities under donor programmes. That is a government or donor sale, not an indie SMB product. Indicative standalone score: 1/10.

## 3. Rejected

- **Business-licence renewal helper.** The Business Licences Act 2017 makes a licence mandatory for every business. But it is a yearly counter renewal with one Registrar and perhaps a few hundred licensees. The substitute is the Registrar's counter and the Gazette fee schedule.
- **Small-fisher compliance under the Fisheries Management Act 2024.** The trigger is real and recent. But the buyer is NFMRA or regional bodies (FFA, SPC), which already run their own tools, and the fishers would not pay.
- **Scrap-export approval paperwork.** It is a ministerial letter with an AUD 100 fee, needed for only a few consignments. The state's NRC dominates waste handling.
- **Small Business Enterprises Fund grant-application tool.** The Act is new (2025), but it creates a funding programme, not a recurring obligation. The buyer would be the government itself.

## 4. Method notes

- PacLII was again the best regulator-first source. Queries naming the Act and "Nauru" returned the Business Licences Act, the Liquor Control Act and the Gazette fee notices directly. The UNEP LEAP and FAOLEX databases summarise new Acts, such as the Small Business Enterprises Act 2025.
- SPREP's waste data profiles are the only source with counts of waste operators.
- Nauru publishes no public licence registers or counts. Every market count above is an estimate.
