# Netherlands: offline-industries pass (2026-10-05)

Method: regulator-first searching, mostly in Dutch. 29 WebSearch calls were made (no WebFetch). The search quota was exhausted mid-pass (the tattoo-studio follow-up was refused), so the write-up stops there. All evidence is at search-snippet level. Anything not shown in a cited source is marked "unverified" or "estimate".

This pass does not repeat the opportunities in `research/countries/netherlands.md`: LAVS asbestos, EU CATCH, DIWASS Annex VII, the spray register, Wtta and the others.

Headline: the Netherlands has few truly quiet industries. Most regulated niches have either a **free state tool** or a **vertical vendor that already integrated**. Examples of free state tools: the police-run Digitaal Opkopers Register (DOR), the Fauna Registration System (FRS), RVO's identification and registration (I&R) system, and the state track-and-trace system in the cannabis experiment. Examples of integrated vendors: HorseManager, EquineM and Anymal for horses, FYN for jewellers, and eight or more cemetery packages.

What remains are thin gaps at the very small end. These are dealers with no point-of-sale (POS) system, waste producers who still use a paper booklet, and fairground operators who fill in dozens of municipal tender forms. No lead clears the brief's "build" bar. One is worth a few interviews: the paper waste accompanying letter (afvalbegeleidingsbrief).

## 2. Opportunities

### Opportunity: Digital Waste Accompanying Letter (Begeleidingsbrief) for Micro Waste Producers

**Industry:**
Small firms that produce and haul their own commercial waste to recyclers and transfer stations: gardeners (hoveniers), small building and renovation contractors, demolition firms, glaziers and cleaners. Second buyer: small recyclers and transfer stations that receive their loads.

**Buyer:**
The owner-operator of a VIHB-registered micro firm (1–10 staff). Alternatively, the weighbridge or office manager of a small recycler who wants clean incoming letters.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- Recyclers are tightening acceptance. Van Werven announced that from 1 Jan 2026 loads without a fully completed and signed letter can be refused. The underlying legal duty is old; whether 2026 brings a statutory change is unverified.
- The SUTC electronic accompanying-letter standard (EBA) is being fully revised in 2026.
- Green-list waste moves into the EU DIWASS system from 1 Jan 2027, already covered in the main report.

**Current workflow:**
1. The waste producer phones or emails the recycler to get a waste-stream number (afvalstroomnummer).
2. At each trip, the driver fills in a carbon-copy paper letter from a bought booklet. It records producer, origin address, waste-stream number, the Eural waste code, estimated weight and VIHB number, and is signed.
3. At the gate the recycler checks and weighs the load and keys the data into its weighbridge system. The recycler then reports receipts to LMA (the AMICE system).
4. The producer files the copies for 5 years, and an accountant matches them to the recycler's invoices.

**Pain:**
- Recyclers say they may refuse service when the letter is incomplete, which costs a wasted trip.
- A TLN/SUTC trade article says smaller parties find EBA software "relatively expensive". It mentions suggestions for a free or low-cost portal.
- Digital use needs an LMA exemption.
- Hours lost per firm are not quantified (unverified).

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper booklets from Beurtvaartadres (the stationer substitute).
- LZP digital accompanying letter.
- The Wastedge app (LMA exemption since 2015).
- Afvalzorg's "rijden zonder begeleidingsbrief".
- Other vendors on LMA's public list of granted exemptions ("afgegeven toestemmingen").
- Weighbridge and waste ERP systems at recyclers.

**Offline evidence:**
- The statutory default is paper.
- Stationers sell preprinted booklets.
- Digital use requires case-by-case LMA permission.
- The trade body itself reports slow EBA uptake among small parties.

**Offline channel:**
- Recycler and transfer-station gates: partner with 2–3 regional recyclers and hand out QR cards at the weighbridge.
- The VHG (gardeners' association) and Bouwend Nederland regional chapters.
- Beurtvaartadres-style stationers as a reseller.
- Phone outreach to VIHB-list firms, which have public VIHB numbers.

**Market count:**
About 9,000 VIHB-registered Dutch firms (old NIWO figure, around 2007; current count unverified). Exempt producers who haul their own waste are not counted.

**The gap:**
A no-install, per-trip phone letter for micro producers is missing. It would store waste-stream numbers per recycler, pre-fill the Eural code and origin, collect a signature, and deliver EBA data to the recycler. The existing approved tools target recyclers and fleets, not a gardener with one van (inferred, unverified).

**Possible product:**
A phone web app that issues a compliant digital letter per trip. It works under the vendor's LMA exemption and sends an EBA message to the receiving recycler. It keeps the 5-year archive and exports a monthly tie-out against recycler invoices.

**MVP:**
- PWA (installable web app): saved waste-stream numbers, a trip letter with signature, and a PDF fallback that prints or shows at the gate.
- One partner recycler receives EBA or email.
- Apply for the LMA exemption.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- EUR 9–19 per month per van, or EUR 1 per letter.
- Recyclers pay EUR 100–300 per month for clean inbound data (estimate).
- Willingness to pay is for software at a very low price point. A done-for-you service is not needed.

**How to find first customers:**
- The public VIHB list (NIWO).
- VHG member directory.
- Partner recyclers' customer lists.

**Risks:**
- Paper costs almost nothing and is "good enough".
- Recyclers may simply offer their own app.
- The LMA exemption process takes time.
- Low price, so many customers are needed.
- Founder access: this needs Dutch and probably a local partner at recyclers. A non-local solo founder would struggle.

**Kill condition:**
Recyclers say paper refusals are rare. Or LZP/Wastedge already offer a free tier for micro producers. Or the 2026 EBA revision ships a free state or sector portal.

**Score:** 4.5/10. Pain 4, frequency 9, mandatory 8, fragmentation 4, competition 4, gap 5, buyer access 7, willingness to pay 3, MVP 8, distribution 5.

### Opportunity: DOR Connector for Small Second-Hand Dealers without an Integrated POS

**Industry:**
Second-hand and buy-in dealers ("opkopers"): phone, electronics and game shops, gold buyers outside the jeweller channel, antique and brocante shops, and resellers who sell through Marktplaats or their own webshop.

**Buyer:**
The owner of a 1–5 person shop.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- More municipalities have made DOR mandatory in their local bylaws, with announced start dates of 15 Jul 2025 and 1 Jan 2026.
- The Ministry of Justice and Security wants the dealer-notification portal (DOL) mandatory nationwide (2025/2026).
- A Penal Code amendment making digital DOR registration a national duty is pending, with its start date not yet known (Ondernemersplein).

**Current workflow:**
1. The dealer checks the seller's ID and photographs the item.
2. The dealer types item details (brand, serial or IMEI, description, photo) and seller data into the DOR web interface.
3. The dealer records the purchase again in their own stock, webshop or accounts.
4. On resale, the dealer marks the sale in DOR. The police may block an item on a Stop Heling match.

**Pain:**
- Double entry per item. CCV's own material sells integrations as "no need to enter twice".
- Fines or prosecution for failing to register.
- Volume per shop is unknown (unverified).

**Existing solutions:**
- The DOR web interface itself (free, police-run).
- The DOR API, with links approved via stopheling@politie.nl.
- 21south (21QUBZ) standard DOR integration.
- FYN Software, a jeweller POS free for members of the gold and silver trade federation FGZ.
- Wilmar and CycleSoftware for bike dealers.

**Offline evidence:**
- Dealers file at the police tool item by item.
- Registration is enforced locally by municipal enforcement officers (BOAs) and the police.
- No product aimed at phone or game shops was found.

**Offline channel:**
- Municipal DOL notification lists (dealers must notify the municipality).
- Phone outreach to dealers on municipal lists.
- Wholesale and parts suppliers to phone-repair shops.
- Partnering with municipal enforcement campaigns.

**Market count:**
6,182 dealers connected to DOR across 307 municipalities as of 3 Jan 2020 (CCV). Today's number is likely higher (unverified).

**The gap:**
A cheap capture app is missing. It would scan an ID, read the IMEI or serial, take a photo and push to DOR through the API. It would also mark sales in DOR automatically from WooCommerce, Shopify or Marktplaats orders. Big POS verticals are covered; the long tail is not (inferred).

**Possible product:**
A mobile intake app with DOR API sync and webshop sale sync. It would give a per-municipality reminder of local DOR and DOL rules.

**MVP:**
- Phone-shop intake (IMEI read, ID and photo) pushed to DOR.
- One webshop plug-in that marks items as sold.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
EUR 15–39 per month per shop. This is software only.

**How to find first customers:**
- Municipal DOL lists.
- Phone-repair supplier customer bases.
- Enforcement officers handing out information sheets.

**Risks:**
- The DOR web tool is free and may be "good enough".
- Police API approval is required.
- The police could improve DOR's own app.
- Founder access: this needs a Dutch local who can get police API approval.

**Kill condition:**
The DOR API is not open to new small vendors. Or most small dealers log fewer than about 20 items a month.

**Score:** 4/10. Pain 4, frequency 8, mandatory 8, fragmentation 4, competition 4, gap 5, buyer access 5, willingness to pay 3, MVP 6, distribution 4.

### Opportunity: Fair-Operator Tender Pack (One Attraction File, Many Municipal Applications)

**Industry:**
Travelling fairground operators (kermisexploitanten).

**Buyer:**
The owner of a family fairground business, often older and on the road 3–5 months a year.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The 2023 attractions decree (WAS 2023) tightened inspection and maintenance-file duties.
- Municipalities keep rewriting fair rules: 2026 conditions published for Roosendaal, Best, Oosterhout, Tynaarlo and others.
- Some municipalities now accept only digital applications with DigiD or eHerkenning, for example Etten-Leur.

**Current workflow:**
1. Each winter, the operator collects the application form of each municipality or private organiser. Every form differs.
2. The operator fills in the attraction's details and exact dimensions and makes a bid.
3. The operator attaches copies of the approved inspection body's certificate and the insurer's details with insured amounts.
4. The operator tracks allocations, payments and site rules, and repeats this for each fair attended. Every setup also needs an inspection.

**Pain:**
- Repeated re-keying and attachment of the same documents per municipality.
- An incomplete form means the application is excluded (conditions say forms must be "fully and legibly" completed).
- Not quantified (unverified).

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper, Word and PDF forms.
- Municipal e-forms.
- Private organisers such as De Kermisgroep, which run fairs for municipalities and take their own registrations.
- Associations BOVAK and NKB (protocols and information).
- The Flemish "Merke" platform shows a municipality-side tool exists; in the Netherlands no operator-side tool was found (unverified).

**Offline evidence:**
- Municipal bylaws require "the official application form".
- An operator association, not software, is the information channel.
- No SaaS listings were found.

**Offline channel:**
- BOVAK and NKB newsletters and annual meetings.
- Approved inspection bodies (which see every attraction yearly).
- Fairground insurers.

**Market count:**
About 950 operators established in the Netherlands (figure in municipal and VNG fair-policy documents; source year unverified).

**The gap:**
Nothing on the operator side keeps one attraction dossier and pre-fills each municipality's form. Such a tool would attach the current certificate and insurance, warn on expiry, and track bids and allocations.

**Possible product:**
An attraction dossier plus a form filler for municipal fair tenders, with a season calendar.

**MVP:**
- An attraction profile plus a PDF form-fill for the 20 largest fair municipalities.
- Expiry alerts.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- EUR 15–30 per month or EUR 150 per year per operator.
- A done-for-you "we file your winter applications" service at EUR 20–40 per application is more plausible than pure software.

**How to find first customers:**
- BOVAK and NKB.
- Fair calendars (kermis.nu) list who attends where.

**Risks:**
- A tiny market (about 950 operators, about EUR 150k ARR ceiling).
- An annual seasonal cycle.
- Organisers consolidating (De Kermisgroep) remove the fragmentation.
- Founder access: a Dutch speaker with trust in a close-knit community; needs a local.

**Kill condition:**
Most fairs are run through a few organisers with their own portals. Or operators apply to fewer than about 10 fairs a year.

**Score:** 3.5/10. Pain 4, frequency 4, mandatory 7, fragmentation 8, competition 6, gap 6, buyer access 6, willingness to pay 2, MVP 8, distribution 5; capped by market size.

## 3. Rejected

- **Tourist tax and night register for B&Bs and small campsites.** The substitutes are free Excel and Samsom paper registers from municipalities, and vacation-rental software that calculates the tax (Smoobu). The return is annual or quarterly and the value is low.
- **Short-term rental registration (EU 2024/1028).** Platforms carry the monthly data duty; the host's registration is one-off.
- **Household and PGB employers.** SVB runs PGB payroll, and private households are not enforced.
- **Horse locations (I&R).** HorseManager, EquineM and Anymal already report to RVO; hobby owners have no budget.
- **Beekeeper registration, hunters' FRS reporting, cat chipping.** Hobbyists using a free state tool.
- **EU dog and cat regulation.** Adopted in 2026 but phased: too early.
- **Cemeteries.** Eight or more vendors plus the KKA admin service; no new trigger.
- **Coffeeshop track-and-trace.** A state tool and about 80 shops.
- **Livestock transport documents and VKI.** Sector-led digitisation targeted for 2027.
- **Market traders.** Scarce-permit re-allocation is infrequent.
- **Migrant-worker housing.** Overlaps with Wtta and staffing tools.

## 4. Method notes

What worked:
- Dutch regulator vocabulary ("verplicht", "register bijhouden", "formulier", "aangifte", "ontheffing").
- Searching municipal regulation databases (lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl, officielebekendmakingen) for per-municipality forms.
- CCV, LMA and RVO pages, which state obligations and list approved vendors. LMA's list of granted exemptions is a ready-made competitor list.

What did not work:
- Counting operators. Registers rarely publish totals, and the counts found are old (VIHB 2007, DOR 2020).

Overall pattern: in the Netherlands the state usually provides a free tool (DOR, FRS, RVO I&R, the cannabis track-and-trace, the Omgevingsloket permit portal). This caps willingness to pay in quiet industries. The search quota ran out after 29 calls, so the tattoo studio row is unscreened.
