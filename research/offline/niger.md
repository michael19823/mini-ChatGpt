# Niger: Offline-Industries Pass

**Research date:** 2026-10-05 · **Search budget used:** 20 of 20. Three calls were refused because the search tool hit its usage limit, so 17 returned results. · **Languages:** French

## Bottom line

Niger is an inaccessible market for a non-local solo founder. Since the July 2023 coup it has had a military government with a record of tax claims against foreign firms. It has left ECOWAS for the AES. Connectivity outside Niamey is weak. See the country report at `research/countries/niger.md`.

The quiet industries are real and large in headcount: livestock trade, artisanal gold, moto-taxis and agro-input shops. The state regulates them through **export bans, prior authorisations, seizures and enforcement deadlines**, not through recurring registers or periodic filings that software could carry. Five Ministry of Commerce or government measures in 2024–2026 *removed* workflows rather than creating them:

- scrap export ban (Nov 2024);
- livestock export bans (May 2025 and 2 Mar 2026);
- suspension of exports of precious stones (Aug 2025);
- cancellation of the gold-refining conventions (Mar 2026).

**No quiet-industry opportunity reaches the build bar.** The three leads below score 2–3/10 and all need a local partner.

## Strongest opportunities (all below the build bar)

### Opportunity: Approved-product checker and sales register for agro-input distributors

**Industry:**
Agro-input and pesticide retail

**Buyer:**
Approved phytosanitary distributors (ADIPHYTO-Niger members) and their shop counters. A second buyer could be donor projects that fund input supply.

**Trigger / Why now:**
RECA surveys show widespread sale of unapproved products: 6 of 19 active ingredients approved, across 106 products. RECA flagged a "Chinese herbicide" issue in 2025. The plant-protection law (Law 2015-35) and the national pesticide-management committee provide the legal hook. **Unverified:** whether any 2025–2026 enforcement campaign targets distributors.

**Current workflow (estimate):**
1. The distributor imports or buys stock.
2. The distributor checks the CSP approval list (a PDF), if at all.
3. The distributor sells at the counter for cash.
4. No sales register is kept, or a paper notebook is used.

**Pain:**
Unapproved products harm farmers and the reputation of approved distributors. ADIPHYTO exists to separate its members from that informal market. Pain for the distributor is reputational and competitive, not regulatory (no fines found).

**Existing solutions:**
- CSP approved-product lists (PDF, Sahel-wide)
- RECA advisory notes and lists of distributors
- Paper notebooks
- Generic POS apps

**Offline evidence:**
RECA publishes paper and PDF notes. No distributor software was found. The association is the organising channel.

**Offline channel:**
ADIPHYTO-Niger, RECA and the regional chambers of agriculture, plus donor-funded input projects (World Bank projects appear in search results).

**Market count:**
Not found. ADIPHYTO's member count is unknown.

**The gap:**
A simple tool that checks a product against the CSP list at the counter and keeps a sales register a distributor could show an inspector or donor.

**Possible product:**
An offline Android app holding the CSP approved list, with a barcode or name lookup and a sales log. It would export a monthly sales report for the association or a donor programme.

**MVP:**
CSP list lookup plus a sales log, in French and Hausa.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
About 3,000–5,000 XOF per shop per month (estimate). It is more likely funded by a donor project or the association.

**How to find first customers:**
Through ADIPHYTO-Niger and the RECA distributor lists.

**Willingness to pay:**
Low for software. A donor or association sponsor is more plausible.

**Founder access:**
Needs a local partner, possibly RECA or a project. Not realistic for a non-local solo founder.

**Risks:**
- No enforcement obligation, so it is optional.
- Tiny revenue per shop.
- The CSP list is regional and changes.

**Kill condition:**
ADIPHYTO has fewer than about 50 members, or no donor or inspector would use the register.

**Score:** 3/10

### Opportunity: Livestock consignment paperwork folder (for when exports reopen)

**Industry:**
Livestock trading

**Buyer:**
Larger livestock traders and exporters

**Trigger / Why now:**
Export bans of May 2025 (Tabaski) and 2 Mar 2026, with seizures. Buyers at official markets pay a tax and get proof of purchase. Traders are protesting the bans.

**Current workflow (estimate):**
1. The trader buys at an official market and gets a paper tax receipt.
2. The trader obtains a veterinary certificate and a Commerce authorisation on paper.
3. The trader shows the originals at checkpoints and at the border.

**Pain:**
Seizure risk and paperwork at every checkpoint. The main pain is the ban itself.

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper receipts and certificates
- Donor-run market-information systems (status in 2026 unverified)
- Trader associations

**Offline evidence:**
Cash markets and paper receipts. No software was found.

**Offline channel:**
Trader associations and market committees, through a local field agent.

**Market count:**
Not found.

**The gap:**
Consolidated documents for each consignment. This has value only if exports resume.

**Possible product:**
A phone folder for each consignment that builds a PDF bundle.

**MVP:**
A WhatsApp-delivered PDF bundle builder.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
About 1,000–2,000 XOF per consignment (estimate). A done-for-you paperwork runner is more plausible than software.

**How to find first customers:**
Through livestock trader associations at the large markets, via a local agent.

**Willingness to pay:**
Low for software. A service is more plausible.

**Founder access:**
Requires a local.

**Risks:**
- The ban persists.
- Only paper originals are accepted.
- It drifts into generic document collection, which the brief flags as a trap.

**Kill condition:**
The export ban stays in place, or authorities accept only paper originals.

**Score:** 2/10

### Opportunity: Lot-level gold purchase register for comptoirs

**Industry:**
Artisanal gold trading

**Buyer:**
Licensed comptoirs and collectors. In practice the buyer would be the state or a refinery.

**Trigger / Why now:**
The Ministry of Mines intends to "structure" orpaillage (artisanal gold mining). The Royal Gold Niger SA refinery agreement was signed on 23 Apr 2025. The trigger has weakened since: in Mar 2026 the conventions of COMINI, AFRIOR and ECOMINE were cancelled for failing to pay taxes and file **annual technical and financial reports**. The state is enforcing reporting by cancellation, not through a usable register.

**Current workflow (estimate):**
1. The buyer purchases gold for cash at the site.
2. The purchase goes in a notebook, if recorded at all.
3. The gold is aggregated and leaves, often informally.

**Pain:**
State revenue loss. Operators have no incentive to record.

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper records
- Whatever intake system a refinery or SOPAMIN imposes (none found)

**Offline evidence:**
Clandestine, cash-based trade.

**Offline channel:**
Only through the Ministry of Mines, SOPAMIN or a refinery. Distribution score: 2.

**Market count:**
230+ sites and 800,000+ workers. Number of comptoirs not found.

**The gap:**
A per-lot origin register, which has value only if one is mandated.

**Possible product:**
An offline purchase register with lot export.

**MVP:**
An Android form plus a CSV/PDF lot export.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
Would be state- or refinery-funded (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**
None publicly available.

**Willingness to pay:**
Operators would not pay. Payment would come through procurement.

**Founder access:**
Not realistic. It needs a local partner with state access, and physical risk is extreme.

**Risks:**
- Armed groups operate around the sites.
- Nationalisations, such as SML in Aug 2025.
- Convention cancellations.

**Kill condition:**
No refinery is operating, or no per-lot origin rule exists.

**Score:** 1/10

## Rejected

- **Scrap metal dealers.** Scrap export has been banned since the Commerce order of 21 Nov 2024. Other metals need case-by-case prior authorisation, and there is no register. Sources: https://www.afrique-sur7.fr/niger-lexportation-de-ferraille-interdite, https://beninwebtv.bj/le-niger-interdit-lexportation-de-la-ferraille/
- **Moto-taxis and tricycles.** Regularisation by 1 Jan 2025 was a one-off, and undocumented bikes are seized and auctioned. Riders are consumers and the sector is described as illegal. Sources: https://fr.apanews.net/news/niamey-ultimatum-de-deux-mois-pour-les-motos-non-conformes/, https://afrique.le360.ma/societe/transport-a-niamey-les-kabou-kabou-ces-deux-roues-a-toute-epreuveen-toute-illegalite_W57GHPFKVZB7HNV55OS5QMMJ3A/
- **Domestic-worker social-security (CNSS) payroll.** No Niger CNSS e-platform or enforcement was found in two searches.
- **Well drillers.** The work comes through NGO and state tenders, so the obligation is a tender dossier, not a recurring filing. Source: https://www.lesopportunites.com/?p=14590
- **Butchers and abattoirs.** Approval and inspection sit with the state vet. Source: https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/Ner176645.pdf
- **Money changers.** There is a real monthly transaction report under the UEMOA rule, but formal bureaux in Niger are too few (estimate). Source: https://www.tresor.gouv.ci/tres/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/GUIDE-BCM.pdf
- **Onion and gum arabic exporters.** The only paperwork is a paper phytosanitary certificate, and there is no traceability trigger. Source: https://www.afrik.com/l-oignon-nigerien-veut-etre-certifie
- **Private schools.** The 2025-26 measure is a tuition-fee cut, which is price control, not paperwork. Source: https://yop.l-frii.com/niger-les-autorites-durcissent-le-ton-les-ecoles-privees-ont-une-semaine-pour-fermer-ou-appliquer-la/

## Method notes

- **What worked:** French regulator-first queries about bans and authorisations, such as "interdiction exportation", "arrêté ministère du Commerce", "agrément" and "conseil des ministres". They surface decrees through Le Sahel, ANP, Sahelien and Les Échos du Niger. The RECA (chambers of agriculture) PDFs are the best source of on-the-ground compliance evidence.
- **What failed:** searches for registers, lists and e-platforms (CNSS Niger, approved drilling firms, licensed comptoirs, bureaux de change) returned other countries or nothing. Niger publishes almost no licensing registers online, so market counts are mostly "not found".
- **Budget:** 3 of the 20 calls were refused when the search tool hit its usage limit.
