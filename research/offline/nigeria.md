# Nigeria: Offline (quiet) industries pass

*Research date: 2026-10-05. Track: Nigeria only. Builds on `research/countries/nigeria.md`, whose tax, expatriate-quota and HMO opportunities are not repeated here.*

> **Verification caveat.** WebFetch was blocked, so no primary page (gazette, regulator PDF or vendor page) was opened. Every fact below comes from **search-result titles and snippets** (37 WebSearch calls). Anything I could not confirm is marked **unverified** or **estimate**. All scores are provisional until someone reads the primary sources and does interviews.

**Main finding.** In Nigeria, regulation of quiet industries is mostly done by **state agencies and enforcement campaigns**: sealing offices, task forces, licence withdrawals. Recurring electronic filing is rare. Much of the "paperwork" is a periodic licence renewal, not a per-job report. The one quiet industry with a per-transaction, money-moving workflow that is still on paper is **Lagos PSP waste collection**. There, operators bill households door to door, and LAWMA is now demanding household data.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Household billing register and LAWMA performance pack for Lagos PSP waste operators

**Industry:**
Private Sector Participation (PSP) residential and commercial waste collection, Lagos State (licensed by LAWMA)

**Buyer:**
Owner or managing director of a LAWMA-licensed PSP operator, usually a small Nigerian company with a few compactor trucks, cash collectors and an office per route. Second buyer: AWAMN (Association of Waste Managers of Nigeria) as a channel or bulk buyer.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- LAWMA withdrew 27 "unserious" operators in 2025. It put 22 more on last warning, then withdrew 5 more licences in May 2026 and realigned routes.
- The LAWMA MD has asked PSPs for a "verifiable database" of households, so the state knows who pays and who doesn't. Government also requires "enumeration of all the houses" before any revenue restructuring.
- The Office of Environmental Services wants PSPs to "redesign templates" so their performance can be measured.
- Bills rose about 200% in a year (diesel costs). Residents are refusing to pay and switching to cart pushers. That makes collection rate and proof of service a survival issue.
- LAWMA's own lawmapay.com has been dormant for months.

**Current workflow:**
1. The operator is assigned a route (ward or street cluster) by LAWMA.
2. Collectors go door to door with paper bill books. They charge a flat fee per flat or shop (about ₦1,500–3,000 a month, as reported).
3. Residents pay cash or by bank transfer. Transfer payers still have to visit the operator's office to "validate" and get a paper slip.
4. Trucks pick up waste. Trips to dumpsites are logged by LAWMA at the dumpsite, but the operator has no household-level proof of service.
5. Arrears, disputes ("we paid but no pickup for weeks") and LAWMA data requests are handled by hand from exercise books and spreadsheets.

**Pain:**
- Revenue leaks: no individual digital payment platform at most PSPs, and paper validation slips.
- Licence-loss risk from performance reviews.
- Residents publicly accuse operators of "billing without pickup".
- The state demands household data that operators do not hold in structured form.

**Existing solutions:**
- **PSP Hub / "PSP Lagos: Track Waste Bills"** (Ashraaf Technologies). A billing app on Google Play and the App Store, built for LAWMA-licensed operators. Named users include Lab Cleanings Services, Nureni Saka Enterprises, Favoreno Global Services, Imperium Waste Services and Olamper Environmental Services. This is the direct competitor; its depth and pricing are unverified.
- **lawmapay.com**, LAWMA's official payment platform, dormant.
- Paper bill books from local printers, bank-transfer narration, and Excel.
- Generic payment links (Paystack, Flutterwave) and generic invoicing tools, with no route or household model.

**The gap:**
- Getting from **one collection round to the outputs LAWMA asks for**: an enumerated household register per route, paid/unpaid status, missed-pickup complaints matched to truck trips, and a monthly performance report in LAWMA's template.
- Reconciling transfers automatically to households (virtual account per household), so no office visit is needed.
- PSP Hub appears to cover billing. Whether it covers the enumeration and performance-evidence side is **unverified**, and only about 5 named operators out of 442 are listed.

**Offline evidence:**
- Payment validation in person at the operator's office.
- Paper slips.
- The state's own digital platform dormant.
- Operators coordinate through AWAMN meetings and LAWMA stakeholder sessions (about 350 stakeholders attended one), not online forums.
- No SaaS review pages exist for this category.

**Offline channel:**
- **AWAMN** (President David Oriyomi, per press) and its executive meetings.
- **LAWMA PSP stakeholder fora** and route-review meetings.
- Operator names and routes are published in LAWMA sanction notices, e.g. Kenny JPS (Igando-Ikotun), Clean Craft (Isheri-Oshun), Doveroost (Eti-Osa West). Use these for in-person visits and WhatsApp outreach.
- Office visits along Lagos routes.

**Market count:**
- **442 active PSP operators in Lagos** (LAWMA figure, NAN 2026).
- PSPs acquired 920 trucks in the early Sanwo-Olu years (ThisDay 2021).
- Similar PSP schemes in other states (Ogun, Oyo, FCT) are unverified.

**Possible product:**
A route-and-household register with virtual-account billing (one account number per household). It auto-reconciles transfers and sends SMS/WhatsApp receipts. Each month it outputs the LAWMA performance and enumeration report: households served, payment rate, trips, complaints closed.

**MVP:**
- Excel or bill-book import into a household register for one route.
- Virtual accounts through a Nigerian PSP/bank API, with auto-matched payments.
- A printable "paid" QR sticker per door.
- A monthly PDF/Excel pack in whatever format LAWMA's route officers ask for.
- Build time: weeks.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- 1–2% of collections, or a flat ₦50–100 per active household per month (estimate).
- Example: an operator with 5,000 billed households at about ₦2,500 bills about ₦12.5m a month. 1% is ₦125k a month (about $80), which is plausible against leakage.
- Willingness to pay is for **software plus onboarding service** (enumeration staff), not software alone.

**How to find first customers:**
- The LAWMA sanction lists and press.
- AWAMN executives.
- Visit the 22 "last warning" operators first: they have the strongest motive.

**Risks:**
- PSP Hub is already in market.
- LAWMA could revive lawmapay or mandate its own platform.
- Cash culture and household resistance.
- Political route allocation.
- **Founder access: needs a Lagos-based local.** Selling means office visits and AWAMN relationships, and a non-local solo founder could not do it realistically.

**Kill condition:**
- LAWMA mandates a single state platform (revived lawmapay) for all PSP billing, or
- PSP Hub already covers enumeration and performance reporting at a low price, or
- fewer than 3 of 10 interviewed operators would pay a share of collections.

**Score:** 6/10
(Pain 7, Frequency 9, Mandatory 6, Fragmentation 3, Competition 5, Gap 6, Buyer access 7, WTP 5, MVP 7, Distribution 6. Offline channel named: AWAMN and LAWMA fora.)

### Opportunity: Guard register and NSCDC licence-renewal file for private guard companies

**Industry:**
Private guard companies (PGCs) licensed by the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps under the Private Guard Companies Act

**Buyer:**
MD or operations/admin manager of a small-to-mid PGC, typically 50–500 guards placed at estates, banks, schools and factories.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- NSCDC is enforcing harder. It sealed 7 illegal PGCs in Abuja (May 2025). The FCT command will sanction companies with "fake documents… or an expired licence". It threatens withdrawal for Act violations.
- Renewal must be filed **3 months before expiry, and all staff must be registered**.
- NSCDC requires guards to be trained and certified at NSCDC colleges or accredited centres, plus "quarterly training and retraining".
- NSCDC wants PGCs deployed for election security. The 2027 election cycle is approaching; the exact dates are unverified.

**Current workflow:**
1. HR keeps guard files on paper or in Excel: ID, guarantor forms, training certificate, posting site.
2. Guards churn monthly, so the files go out of date.
3. Every renewal cycle, admin assembles the pack: CTCs, tax clearance, auditor letter, bank statement, staff register, training evidence. It is submitted through the NSCDC licensing portal and the state command.
4. Payroll for guards is run separately, often in cash or transfer, and client invoices are made per site.

**Pain:**
- Sealing of offices for an expired licence.
- Staff-registration requirement with high guard turnover.
- Fees (₦500k application, ₦100k per director PHS form) make a lapse costly.
- **Unverified**: hours spent per renewal, and whether NSCDC asks for periodic guard returns between renewals.

**Existing solutions:**
- NSCDC Private Guard Licensing Portal (government).
- Generic guard-tour and patrol apps (international, e.g. guard-management SaaS; not checked for Nigeria).
- Nigerian HR/payroll tools (SeamlessHR and others; Nigerian coverage known from the earlier report, guard-specific features unverified).
- Excel, and consultants or lawyers who assemble licensing documents.

**The gap:**
No product found that keeps a **churn-proof guard register tied to NSCDC training/certification status** and outputs the staff list and evidence pack NSCDC asks for at renewal. Ideally it also links each guard to site postings and client billing.

**Offline evidence:**
- Licensing is a paper-heavy pack (CTCs, PHS forms, passport photos).
- Licences are handed out at NSCDC HQ ceremonies.
- Enforcement is by office sealing.
- Operators gather through NSCDC command meetings, not online.

**Offline channel:**
- NSCDC licence presentation ceremonies and state-command PGC meetings (e.g. FCT command praising renewals).
- The Association of Licensed Private Security Practitioners of Nigeria (ALPSPN): known by name, membership size **unverified**.
- NSCDC-accredited guard training centres, which see every new guard.

**Market count:**
- **1,200+ approved PGCs** (NSCDC, July 2025).
- 1,364 licensed since 2003 (older NSCDC figure).
- Over 2 million guards claimed (NSCDC; treat as an unverified claim).

**Possible product:**
A guard register keyed to NSCDC training and certification status, with expiry alerts for the company licence and each guard's certificate. It produces a one-click renewal staff register and evidence pack, and adds a per-site roster that feeds payroll and client invoices.

**MVP:**
- A guard database with WhatsApp onboarding (photo, NIN, guarantor, training cert upload).
- A site posting roster.
- Licence and certificate expiry alerts.
- An exportable staff nominal roll in the NSCDC format.
- Build time: weeks.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
₦30k–100k a month per company, or about ₦300 per active guard per month (estimate). Willingness to pay is likelier for **payroll and site billing** (which cost money every month) than for compliance alone.

**How to find first customers:**
- NSCDC published lists of newly licensed PGCs (28 in July 2025, 20 in Sept 2022).
- State-command PGC registers.
- Estate associations that hire guards.

**Risks:**
- Compliance trigger is periodic (renewal), not monthly.
- NSCDC may not actually check staff registers.
- Payroll is a crowded space.
- Many PGCs are informal and pay guards in cash.
- **Founder access: needs a local**, ideally someone with ex-security-industry relationships.

**Kill condition:**
- NSCDC renewal turns out to need no staff-level data in practice, or
- interviewed PGCs say a spreadsheet plus an agent handles renewal for under ₦100k a cycle.

**Score:** 5/10
(Pain 5, Frequency 5, Mandatory 7, Fragmentation 3, Competition 6, Gap 5, Buyer access 6, WTP 5, MVP 7, Distribution 5)

### Opportunity: SCUML compliance kit for motor and jewellery dealers (KYC and cash-transaction register)

**Industry:**
Designated non-financial businesses supervised by SCUML (now inside EFCC): car dealers ("tokunbo" and new), jewellers, luxury-goods dealers

**Buyer:**
Owner of a car lot or jewellery shop. Realistically sold through the dealers' association, or bundled by a compliance consultant or law firm as a done-for-you service.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022 moved SCUML into EFCC. SCUML suspended and then resumed certificate issuance under the new regime (2025).
- EFCC met auto dealers on money laundering in April 2026.
- Earlier SCUML prosecutions: Sokoto car dealers docked, 5 dealers jailed.
- Naira depreciation has pushed ordinary car prices above the ₦5m individual cash-reporting threshold. The share of sales paid in cash is unverified.

**Current workflow:**
1. Register online with SCUML (free) and get a certificate.
2. Do KYC on buyers on paper: photocopied ID, address.
3. When a cash payment is at or above ₦5m (individual) or ₦10m (corporate), file a CTR. File STRs on suspicion. Reports go via SCUML to NFIU.
4. In practice most dealers do not file at all, judging from EFCC's repeated "sensitisation" visits.

**Pain:**
- Legal exposure (prosecution, fines, suspension of business), but enforcement is **sporadic and regional**.
- Pain is latent until EFCC visits.

**Existing solutions:**
- SCUML portal (government, free).
- NFIU goAML (government).
- Enterprise AML tools such as Youverify and Regfyl, aimed at banks and fintechs, not car lots.
- Lawyers and consultants who handle SCUML registration.

**The gap:**
A dealer-sized tool that captures buyer KYC at the point of sale (NIN/BVN check, photo), flags payments at or above the threshold, and prepares the CTR/STR for SCUML. Nothing aimed at dealers was found.

**Offline evidence:**
- EFCC reaches dealers by visiting association offices (e.g. Kwara Motor Dealers Association at EFCC Ilorin).
- Compliance is taught at in-person sensitisation sessions.
- No vendor targets this segment online.

**Offline channel:**
- State motor dealers' associations, which EFCC zonal commands already convene.
- EFCC/SCUML sensitisation events.
- Lawyers who do SCUML registrations.

**Market count:**
**Unverified.** No SCUML registration statistics surfaced. Dealer counts by state are unknown.

**Possible product:**
A point-of-sale KYC and cash register for dealers that turns each sale into a SCUML-ready record and a draft CTR/STR. It is sold as an annual "compliance kit" with registration help.

**MVP:**
- A WhatsApp or web form per sale (buyer ID, NIN lookup, amount, payment mode).
- A threshold flag.
- A monthly export formatted for SCUML/goAML submission.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
₦10k–25k a month, or ₦100k–200k a year bundled with SCUML registration (estimate). Willingness to pay is mostly for a **done-for-you service**.

**How to find first customers:**
Dealer associations named in EFCC press releases (Kwara, Sokoto, Gombe).

**Risks:**
- Dealers avoid recording cash precisely because of tax and AML exposure, so adopting the tool hurts them.
- Enforcement too thin to force purchase.
- Selling AML tooling to a sector EFCC watches carries reputational risk.
- **Needs a local**, preferably a lawyer partner.

**Kill condition:**
Dealers interviewed say EFCC has never asked for CTRs or KYC files at their lot.

**Score:** 4/10
(Pain 4, Frequency 7, Mandatory 7, Fragmentation 2, Competition 7, Gap 6, Buyer access 4, WTP 2, MVP 7, Distribution 4)

## 4. Method notes

- **What worked:**
  - English queries naming the **state agency plus an enforcement verb**: "LAWMA withdraws licences", "NSCDC seals", "EFCC car dealers SCUML", "LIRS shuts hotels".
  - Nigerian enforcement news (NAN, Guardian, Gazette, VON) is rich, and agency press releases give counts: 442 PSPs, 1,200+ PGCs, 82 BDCs.
  - Extended mode surfaced the PSP Hub competitor that standard mode missed.
- **What didn't work:**
