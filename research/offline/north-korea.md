# North Korea (DPRK): Offline-Industries Pass

**Date:** 2026-10-05
**Market class:** Inaccessible market. The country is under comprehensive sanctions, has no payment rails and no public internet. See `research/countries/north-korea.md` for the accessibility check: OFAC 31 CFR 510 / EO 13722, UNSCR 2397, EU Reg. 2017/1509, UK DPRK regulations 2019, SWIFT disconnection in 2017, and the Kwangmyong intranet only.
**Search budget:** 8. The run was interrupted by an API limit and then resumed. 1 search is logged in this resumed session. Any searches made before the interruption could not be recovered, but the total stayed within 8. I kept the search count low on purpose, because the verdict is settled by sanctions law and not by anything a search could find about the market.
**Bottom line:** North Korea does have quiet, regulated, paper-run trades. The clearest are the *jangmadang* market traders, who register stalls with a state commercial management office, pay daily stall fees, and must now register their supply source and selling price. But every buyer is either the DPRK state itself or a DPRK private trader. Selling software or services to either is prohibited for US persons and sanctioned under UN, EU and UK regimes. No lawful way exists to collect payment, and buyers have no internet access to deliver to. Section 2 is therefore empty on purpose.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

None. No industry passes the gate the method sets before scoring: a lawful sale, a payment and a delivery path. Indicative score for any DPRK quiet-industry idea: 0–1/10. I have not filled template entries for show.

## 3. Rejected

- **Market-stall registration and price-reporting helper for *jangmadang* traders.** On paper this is the textbook quiet-industry pattern: a new registration drive, a mandatory recurring report of supply source and price, many small owner-operators, and counter filing. It is killed by sanctions (OFAC comprehensive embargo; UN, EU and UK), by the lack of payment rails, by the lack of internet, and by the fact that the receiving body is a state control office. The substitute is cash paid to the market administrator and a paper ledger.
- **Border-trade documentation for Chinese counterparties of DPRK traders.** Rejected as sanctions evasion.
- **Any diaspora or South-Korea-based service "for" DPRK operators.** Still a prohibited service whose benefit lands in the DPRK.

## 4. Method notes

- Regulator-first search does not work here, because the DPRK publishes no licensing registers or gazettes in reachable form. The only usable evidence is defector-sourced reporting from Daily NK, Asiapress (Rimjin-gang) and NK Economy Watch. One search on market stall fees and registration found enough to describe the trader workflow.
- Further searching would not change the verdict: sanctions law decides it, not market evidence. The rest of the budget was deliberately left unspent.
