# Oman: offline-industries pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. I used 19 of the 20 allowed WebSearch calls and no WebFetch, so every fact below comes from search-result summaries. Anything I could not check is marked "unverified" or "estimate". The searches were in English only. Arabic queries would probably surface more of the regulator's own pages (MoL, MAFWR, the Ministry of Commerce).

**Overall verdict:** Oman's quiet industries are regulated mostly through national government portals (MoL, Bayan, MAFWR e-services). The paperwork is then done by about 900 **Sanad** service centres, which are government-service counters run by Omanis. This has two effects. The "integration layer" is a counter clerk, not the small operator. And the regulator usually owns the system of record, which leaves little space for third-party software. The one real 2025–2026 trigger that lands on a huge number of small, offline "employers" is the **domestic workers regulation (Ministerial Resolution 574/2025)**, which covers about 395,000 workers. Its buyer, though, is the household, and households rarely pay for software. Nothing here clears the brief's bar. The two best ideas score 4/10.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Domestic-worker compliance file for households, sold through recruitment offices

**Industry:**
Households as employers of domestic workers (Res. 574/2025), reached through licensed recruitment offices

**Buyer:**
Primary: the owner of a licensed domestic-worker recruitment office, who would bundle the service into the placement fee or sell it as an add-on. Secondary: Omani households with 1–3 domestic workers (maid, driver, gardener, home nurse, camel hand).

**Trigger / Why now:**
- Ministerial Resolution 574/2025 came into force on 12 Oct 2025. It requires a written Arabic contract registered electronically with MoL, a dedicated record per worker with wage documentation, and wage payment within 7 days by bank transfer or signed receipt.
- It sets leave entitlements (21 days annual, up to 30 days sick, weekly rest) and caps working hours at 12 per day.
- It adds an end-of-service gratuity of half a month's wage per year after 2 years, payable within 5 days of termination.
- Existing employment had to be regularised within 3 months.
- Fines of RO 50–500 per worker are reported by a secondary source (gccdomestic.com); I could not confirm them against the resolution text, so treat them as unverified.
- MoL inspected 166 recruitment offices in Muscat in Oct 2025 and recorded 11,172 labour violations in its latest reported period. Separately, 962 cases involved failure to repatriate or transfer a worker within 60 days of contract expiry.

**Current workflow:**
1. The household gets a worker through a recruitment office. The office or a Sanad centre registers the contract on the MoL portal.
2. Each month the household pays wages, usually in cash, and may or may not collect a signed receipt. Leave, sick days and deductions (capped at 25%) live in memory or a notebook.
3. At contract end or a dispute, the household and worker argue over unpaid wages, leave and gratuity with no records. The case goes to MoL or a labour complaint.
4. At expiry the sponsor must repatriate or transfer the worker within 60 days, and missing this is a recorded violation.

**Pain:**
- New legal duties to keep records and to prove payment.
- Gratuity and leave disputes at termination.
- Recorded MoL violations for missing the repatriation or transfer window.
- The pain is real but occasional for each household. It peaks at termination and disputes, not monthly. I found no evidence of household complaints, only official rule announcements.

**Existing solutions:**
- MoL portal (contract registration, the system of record)
- Sanad service centres (paid counter clerks)
- Recruitment offices (placement plus paperwork)
- gccdomestic.com (contract guides and a gratuity calculator for Oman; exact product scope unverified)
- Generic Oman payroll tools (Zoho Payroll, Keka), which are built for companies with a CR, not households
- Paper receipt books

**Offline evidence:**
- Wages are in cash, with a "signed receipt" written into law as an accepted proof.
- Contracts are filed through Sanad counters or recruitment offices.
- Household employers have no forum presence.
- No household-payroll SaaS for Oman turned up in searches.

**Offline channel:**
- Recruitment offices: 166 in Muscat were named by MoL, and Google Maps lists them. Offer them a co-branded "compliance file" handed to every household at placement.
- Sanad centres (~900): a referral fee per registration.
- WhatsApp outreach, since the household's existing relationship is with the recruitment office's WhatsApp.

**Market count:**
394,900 domestic workers (NCSI 2024, via GLMM). The number of employing households is unknown; an estimate is 200,000–300,000 households. The number of licensed recruitment offices nationally is unverified; Muscat alone had 166 inspected.

**The gap:**
Nobody keeps the household's per-worker record that the law now requires (monthly wage proof, leave ledger, deductions, running gratuity accrual), or produces the termination settlement and the 60-day expiry countdown. The MoL portal registers the contract but does not hold this record (unverified).

**Possible product:**
An Arabic-first WhatsApp and web "worker file". Each month the household confirms payment, and the worker signs a receipt on the phone or a photo of a paper receipt is uploaded. The tool tracks leave and sick days, accrues gratuity, warns 90/60/30 days before contract expiry, and produces a termination settlement sheet. Recruitment offices get a dashboard of all households they placed.

**MVP:**
- Recruitment-office dashboard: add a placement, and the household gets a WhatsApp link.
- Monthly payment confirmation with a receipt photo.
- Leave counter, gratuity calculator and an expiry alert.
- Arabic and English output, plus Urdu, Hindi, Bengali, Tagalog and Swahili receipts for the worker.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- Recruitment office: OMR 1–2 per active placement per month, or OMR 15–30 per month flat.
- Households directly: OMR 1 per month at most. **Willingness to pay:** households would not pay for software. Offices might pay if it cuts after-placement disputes and refund claims. This is a service-plus-software model with a thin margin.

**How to find first customers:**
Google Maps and Sanad listings of recruitment offices in Muscat (Al Khuwair, Ruwi, Seeb), then a walk-in visit or WhatsApp.

**Risks:**
- MoL adds wage-proof and leave tracking to its own portal or app.
- Households ignore the record-keeping rule because enforcement is complaint-driven.
- Recruitment offices may not care what happens after the guarantee period.
- **Founder access:** this needs an Arabic-speaking local, or at least a local partner. A non-local solo founder could not realistically sell it door to door.

**Kill condition:**
- Five recruitment-office interviews show they don't handle any post-placement disputes, or
- MoL's portal already holds monthly wage proof and leave for domestic workers.

**Score:** 4/10

### Opportunity: AML compliance kit for gold and jewellery shops

**Industry:**
Dealers in precious metals and stones (DPMS): gold souq shops, jewellers, and gold buyers

**Buyer:**
The owner, or the newly appointed compliance officer, at a small family-run gold or jewellery shop

**Trigger / Why now:**
- A 2025 MoCIIP directive requires every licensed precious-metals and stones establishment to appoint a dedicated AML/CFT compliance officer immediately and to email the officer's details to aml@tejarah.gov.om.
- The ministry warned of legal consequences for non-compliance.
- The directive implies ongoing duties: customer due diligence, record-keeping, and reporting suspicious transactions to the FIU. The specific cash thresholds in Oman are unverified.

**Current workflow:**
1. The owner names a family member as compliance officer and emails the ministry.
2. Customer IDs for large cash sales are photocopied or not recorded at all (estimate).
3. Suspicious transactions are rarely, if ever, reported. A consultant may write a one-off AML policy.

**Pain:**
The obligation is regulatory and the ministry has threatened enforcement. The pain will appear only when inspections or mutual-evaluation follow-ups start, which is unverified.

**Existing solutions:**
- AML consultants and law firms in Muscat, who sell policy documents
- UAE-focused DPMS AML/goAML software and consultancies (the UAE market is crowded; vendor names are unverified for Oman)
- Paper ID photocopies
- The FIU's own reporting channel

**Offline evidence:**
- Compliance officers are registered by email.
- Shops are souq-based family businesses.
- No Oman-specific DPMS AML tool was found.

**Offline channel:**
- Walk the gold souqs (Muttrah, Nizwa, Sohar, Salalah).
- OCCI jewellery and gold sector members.
- Gold wholesalers who supply the shops.

**Market count:**
Unverified. I found no public count of licensed DPMS establishments. A guess is several hundred to about 1,000 shops (estimate).

**The gap:**
A simple per-sale customer-ID capture at the counter, a threshold flag, a retained register, and a draft STR. Sized for a shop with no compliance staff and in Arabic.

**Possible product:**
A tablet or phone app at the till that scans the Omani ID or resident card and passport, logs high-value cash sales, keeps the register for the retention period, and prepares the annual risk assessment and training log the ministry expects.

**MVP:**
ID photo capture plus a sale log, a threshold alert, and an exportable register. A policy template is included.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
OMR 10–20 per month per shop, or a one-off OMR 150 setup and policy pack. Shops would mostly pay for a **done-for-you service** (policy plus training plus register), with software as the delivery tool.

**How to find first customers:**
The gold souq in person, and gold wholesalers' customer lists.

**Risks:**
- A small market.
- Enforcement may stay on paper.
- UAE DPMS AML vendors can add Oman cheaply.
- **Founder access:** this needs an Arabic-speaking local who is trusted in the souq.

**Kill condition:**
The ministry does not inspect DPMS shops beyond the compliance-officer email, or a UAE vendor already sells an Oman edition.

**Score:** 4/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Fishing logbooks / artisanal catch reporting.** About 28,000 boats is a large count. But the owners are individual fishermen, MAFWR runs its own data-collection system, and the new bound-logbook rule targets the larger IOTC-area vessels. Willingness to pay is very low.
- **Fish-transporter and boat licence renewals.** 1,840 licence violations in 2025 show the pain, but it is a once-a-year renewal reminder with no recurring workflow.
- **Well-drilling contractor registration.** Too few operators. The ministry's problem is illegal drilling, not contractor paperwork.
- **Scrap-metal dealer registers.** The export suspension (ferrous and non-ferrous scrap, batteries, tyres, used cooking oil) shrank the trade, and ROP and municipality inspections are physical.
- **Livestock import permits and camel numbering.** About 155 import permits per quarter, handled by clearing agents. Camel ID is a government service.
- **Beekeepers.** 6,231 of them, but no recurring obligation was found.
- **Street taxis.** The regulator already forced them onto licensed metering apps.
- **Home-based business licences.** 13,000+ permits, but it is a one-off RO 3 licence for 3 years.
- **Sanad centres as buyers.** They are the paid intermediary and run on government portals. They are a channel, not a software buyer.

## 4. Method notes

- Searching English Gulf press (Oman Observer, Times of Oman, Muscat Daily) for "<industry> violations" or "inspection campaign" worked best. These papers report the ministries' enforcement statistics, which gave counts and proof of enforcement.
- NCSI figures came through secondary sites (GLMM, IOTC country reports).
- Official registers and forms were not reachable through search.
- I did not search in Arabic, which is the main gap. Arabic queries on mol.gov.om, mafwr.gov.om and tejarah.gov.om (for example «سجل», «ترخيص», «مخالفات») would be the next step.
- In Oman the structural finding is that Sanad centres and recruitment or clearing offices are the paid "integration layer". Any quiet-industry product has to be sold through them, and it needs a local.
