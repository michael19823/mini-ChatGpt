# Papua New Guinea: offline-industries pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. **This is a truncated pass.** The search tool returned "You've hit your usage limit" on the 2nd of 3 parallel calls (3 calls made in total, 2 returned results). The instructions say to stop when a search is refused, so this report rests on 2 searches, on the existing country report (`research/countries/papua-new-guinea.md`) and on general background knowledge. Every claim that does not come from a cited search result is marked *unverified*. No WebFetch was used.

**Overall verdict:** PNG's quiet industries are very informal: alluvial gold panning, betel-nut and market trading, PMV (public motor vehicle) buses, village coffee and cocoa. Their regulators are commodity boards and the central bank, not municipalities, and the obliged formal businesses are few: tens, not thousands. I found **no quiet-industry opportunity strong enough to recommend** for PNG on its own. The one partial lead is gold-export dealers (licensed annually by the Bank of PNG, with a per-shipment MRA assay). But only a few dozen licensees exist, so it is at best an interview lead and a component of a regional gold-traceability product.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

Only one idea is written up, and it falls below the build threshold.

### Opportunity: Per-shipment gold export compliance pack for BPNG-licensed gold exporters

**Industry:**
Alluvial gold buying and export.

**Buyer:**
Owner or compliance/finance officer of a BPNG-licensed gold exporter. These companies buy alluvial gold from small miners and export it.

**Trigger / Why now:**
No new 2025–26 trigger was found. The licence is renewed every year, and the current requirements date from 2015 (BPNG PDFs). A possible trigger is the international AML/CFT pressure on gold trading (PNG's FATF/APG mutual evaluation follow-up) and buyer-side responsible-sourcing (OECD/LBMA) due diligence. Both are *unverified for PNG and not searched*.

**Current workflow:**
1. Buy gold over the counter from alluvial miners. Record the seller and weight *(how and whether this is recorded is unverified)*.
2. Send each export shipment for an assay certified by the MRA or another competent institution (BPNG requirement).
3. Assemble the shipment documents: assay report, export documentation, foreign-exchange reporting to BPNG *(unverified detail)*.
4. Each year, rebuild the licence pack: incorporation documents, paid-up capital evidence, performance bond, bank references, audited financial statements.

**Pain:**
Not evidenced. No complaints or enforcement actions were found (searches were cut off). The pain is inferred from the paper-based licence regime.

**Existing solutions:**
Accountants and audit firms, which prepare the annual pack; the MRA assay service; global responsible-sourcing tools used by refiners *(unverified)*; spreadsheets.

**Offline evidence:**
BPNG publishes its licence requirements and checklist only as scanned PDFs, and there is no online application portal. The licensee list is published as a document.

**Offline channel:**
The BPNG published list of licensed gold exporters, which gives company names that can be phoned or visited in Port Moresby. Also the MRA alluvial-mining SME programmes reported in The National.

**Market count:**
A few dozen licensees at most. Source: the BPNG "Licensed Gold Exporters" list for 2024; the exact count was not verified. Alluvial gold exports were reported at around K410 million a year (The National).

**The gap:**
A seller-level purchase register (miner ID, location, weight) linked to the shipment and its assay. This would yield an AML and responsible-sourcing file the refiner or bank accepts. *Hypothetical; not validated.*

**Possible product:**
A counter app that logs each gold purchase from a miner, links purchases to an export shipment, and produces the shipment pack plus a provenance report for the overseas refiner.

**MVP:**
A tablet form for purchases plus a PDF shipment-pack generator.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
K1,000–3,000 per month per exporter *(estimate)*. The value is high per buyer but the market is tiny.

**How to find first customers:**
The BPNG licensed gold exporters list, phoned directly.

**Willingness to pay:**
Probably only for a done-for-you service (a compliance consultant), not for self-serve software.

**Founder access:**
This needs a local presence. It is a cash-intensive, security-sensitive trade built on relationships, and a non-local solo founder could not realistically sell it.

**Risks:**
Too few buyers. Reputational and AML exposure from serving the sector. Exporters may prefer opacity.

**Kill condition:**
Fewer than 30 licensees, or the refiners and banks show they already supply their own KYC/provenance forms.

**Score:** 3/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Coffee/cocoa dealer licensing (CIC, Cocoa Board):** these are real licensed registers with paper-based quality control (CIC revised its green-bean standards and regulation guidelines with PHAMA Plus). But the exporter count is small, and EUDR traceability, the only frequent workflow, is already covered in the country report and by buyer-funded tools. Sources: [PHAMA Plus – New standards to improve PNG's coffee trade](https://phamaplus.com.au/?p=14358), [EMTV – Quality of export beans](https://emtv.com.pg/quality-of-export-beans-crucial-for-coffee-industry).
- **PMV operators, market and betel-nut vendors, household employers:** these are cash-based and informal, with no willingness to pay for software and no enforced recurring filing.
- **Forex dealers, abattoirs, liquor licensees, fishers:** the operator count is too small or the obligation recurs only once a year.
- **Scrap metal, second-hand dealers, security firms:** not assessed because the search budget was cut off. Security firms (SIA licensing plus guard rosters) are the most worth a follow-up search.

## 4. Method notes

- Regulator-first queries worked well for central-bank licensing ("Bank of PNG licensed gold exporters" returned the official list and requirement PDFs immediately). Commodity-board queries (CIC licence renewal) returned only press releases and NGO pages, not registers.
- PNG regulators publish scanned PDFs and press items. Local news (The National, Post-Courier, EMTV) is the main secondary source; local-language (Tok Pisin) queries are unlikely to help for regulation.
- The pass was ended by the search tool's usage limit after 3 calls (1 refused). A follow-up should spend about 5 searches on the Security Industries Authority, the scrap metal export rules, NAQIA meat premises, and the Second-hand Dealers legislation (if any).
