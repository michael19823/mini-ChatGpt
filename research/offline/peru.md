# Peru: Offline-Industries Pass

*Research date: 2026-10-05. Method: 36 WebSearch calls, mostly in Spanish and regulator-first (gob.pe, El Peruano, SBS, SUNAT, SERFOR, MTPE, SENASA). WebFetch was blocked, so every fact below comes from search-result summaries of the cited URLs. Anything not confirmed that way is marked **unverified** or **estimate**. This pass leaves out what the existing report (`research/countries/peru.md`) already covers: the Ley 32412 hydrocarbon, cyanide and mercury daily register, SIGERSOL waste manifests, mine-contractor accreditation, DIGEMID, SIRE and EUDR coffee/cocoa.*

**Headline:** Peru's quiet industries are heavily regulated on paper, but the state usually builds the receiving tool itself and often makes it free: MC-SNIFFS for timber, RECPO En Línea for gold, Formulario 1676 and the MTPE app for domestic workers. A real but smaller gap remains in turning one day's operations into a register, a report and evidence held for inspection. Nothing found here beats the existing report's #1 (the fuel-station register). The best quiet-industry lead is a **compliance kit for money changers and pawn/loan shops (SPLAFT)**, at about 5.5/10.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity A: SPLAFT-in-a-box for money changers and pawn/loan shops

**Industry:**
Casas de cambio (money changers, including registered street cambistas) and empresas de préstamo y/o empeño, all supervised by the SBS through UIF-Perú.

**Buyer:**
The owner-operator of a 1–3-location money changer or pawn shop. Also the outsourced "oficial de cumplimiento" (compliance officer) or small consultancy that serves 20–100 of these shops.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- Res. SBS 00650-2024 (in force 27 Feb 2024) re-did the register: registration comes before operating and before naming the compliance officer, and each location must be registered. Shops that don't register are ordered to stop operating and then closed by SBS resolution.
- The SBS publishes the list of closed shops and passes it to municipalities.
- The why-now is moderate rather than fresh: it is continuing enforcement (Gestión: "SBS endurece supervisión a 1,536 casas de cambio"), not a new 2026 duty.

**Current workflow:**
1. The owner registers with the SBS (Annex 1, a sworn statement, filed virtually or in person), then names a compliance officer to the UIF.
2. On every transaction above the threshold, the cashier collects ID and client details, usually on paper or in Excel (**estimate**), into the Registro de Operaciones.
3. The compliance officer, often outsourced, checks clients, writes ROS reports when needed, and keeps the RO to send to or show the UIF.
4. Once a year, the compliance officer writes the IAOC and keeps the manual and code of conduct up to date.

**Pain:**
- The penalty for operating unregistered is closure.
- UIF fines are graded by infraction (Regcheq publishes a Peru fine table).
- The obligation set (RO, ROS, IAOC, manual, KYC, audits) was designed for financial entities and lands on one-counter shops. The SBS itself notes the challenge of applying it without disproportionate burden on smaller firms.

**Existing solutions:**
- **MEP MiEmpresaPropia**: a done-for-you SBS/UIF registration plus compliance-officer service for these exact shops.
- **Regcheq** (Chilean RegTech with a Peru page on UIF fines): KYC and AML software, likely priced for fintechs (**unverified**).
- **Pirani**: risk/SPLAFT software for mid-size companies.
- Independent compliance-officer consultants and law firms (PRCP, Ferrere publish guides).
- The SBS/UIF's own forms.

**Offline evidence:**
- Registration can be filed in person.
- The market is sold by consultancies (MEP) rather than self-serve SaaS.
- No SaaS review pages or forums for Peruvian cambistas were found.
- Street cambistas work in cash at a physical spot.

**Offline channel:**
- The SBS public register of casas de cambio and préstamo/empeño lists names and addresses. Walk the clusters (Lima: Ocoña/Camaná, Miraflores; Tacna and Puno border points: **estimate**) and phone from the register.
- Better still, sell to the 10–20 outsourced compliance officers who each carry a portfolio of shops.

**Market count:**
1,536 casas de cambio (Gestión, Feb 2024, citing the SBS), plus an unverified number of pawn and loan shops.

**The gap:**
- Consultants deliver a manual and a name, but the daily RO capture at the counter, threshold flags, the ROS draft and the evidence pack for the IAOC stay manual.
- Fintech-grade KYC tools are oversized for a one-window shop.

**Possible product:**
- A phone/tablet counter app: scan the DNI or passport, log the operation, and flag automatically when it crosses the threshold or splits into structured amounts.
- The app produces the Registro de Operaciones in the UIF format, drafts the ROS, and builds the year's evidence for the IAOC.
- A multi-client console for outsourced compliance officers.

**MVP:**
DNI capture plus an operations log with threshold alerts, a monthly RO export, and an IAOC evidence pack. Sold first to 3 outsourced compliance officers.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- S/60–120 (about US$16–32) per shop per month.
- Or S/600–1,500 per month per compliance officer covering 20+ shops.
- Willingness to pay: shops pay today for a **done-for-you service**. Software alone sells best *through* the compliance officer, so this is a service-plus-software model.

**How to find first customers:**
The SBS register listing, MEP-type consultancies, and compliance officers listed in UIF training courses.

**Risks:**
- The exact RO threshold and filing format for this sector were not verified.
- Many cambistas may barely have any above-threshold transactions.
- The UIF could release its own form.
- Consultants may see the tool as a threat.
- Thin margins.

**Kill condition:**
Interviews show that fewer than 10% of shops log any above-threshold operations monthly, or that the UIF accepts a free template that consultants already fill in.

**Founder access:**
Needs a local, or a local partner who is a compliance officer. A non-local solo founder would struggle to sell to cash-counter businesses.

**Score:** 5.5/10. Pain 6, frequency 8, mandatory 9, fragmentation 3, competition 5, incumbent gap 6, buyer access 7, willingness to pay 4, MVP 8, distribution 6.

### Opportunity B (weak): Monthly Libro de Operaciones for sawmills and timber depots

**Industry:**
Forestry: primary transformation centres (sawmills, CTPs), timber depots, collection points and timber yards.

**Buyer:**
The owner or administrator of a small sawmill or depot in Ucayali, Loreto or Madre de Dios. Also the forestry engineer (regente) or accountant who prepares the book.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- RDE D000025-2023-MINAGRI-SERFOR-DE made the updated Libro de Operaciones formats mandatory from **15 Jan 2024**, with a physical inventory verified by the regional authority.
- New yield (rendimiento) percentages for sawn timber and flooring strips apply to CTPs from **May 2025** and are reported in the book. The book goes to the ARFFS **monthly**.
- SERFOR is linking the book to the MC-SNIFFS traceability module.

**Current workflow:**
1. Log inflows by transport guide (GTF) and species and volume; log outflows by product.
2. Compute yield against the official percentage.
3. Fill in the approved format (paper/Excel, or the electronic book in MC-SNIFFS) and send it monthly to the ARFFS.
4. Keep supporting documents for 4 years for SERFOR/OSINFOR inspections, where timber seizures happen.

**Pain:**
- A yield mismatch or an inflow without a GTF puts the load's legal origin in doubt, which leads to immobilisation and seizure (Andina reports SERFOR seizures).
- Regional governments run in-person coaching sessions for CTPs, which shows that operators struggle with the format.

**Existing solutions:**
- SERFOR **MC-SNIFFS** electronic Libro de Operaciones (free, state-run).
- Regional forest authority coaching.
- Forestry engineers and consultants.
- Generic sawmill ERPs (none Peru-specific found).

**Offline evidence:**
- Approved formats are published as resolutions in El Peruano.
- Coaching is face to face in Pucallpa and Iquitos.
- No local SaaS listings were found.

**Offline channel:**
Regional authority lists of authorised CTPs and depots (Gores Ucayali and Loreto), the Pucallpa timber associations (unverified names), and forestry regentes.

**Market count:**
Unverified. About 90 CTPs in Loreto, Ucayali and Madre de Dios per Inforegión (2023 data); the national number of depots and timber yards is probably larger (estimate).

**The gap:**
MC-SNIFFS receives the book but does not reconcile GTF inflows with outputs and yield before submission, or warn the operator ahead of an inspection.

**Possible product:**
A reconciliation layer: enter GTF inflows and daily output, check yield against the SERFOR percentage, and export the monthly book in the approved format. Optionally, an EUDR due-diligence pack for exporters.

**MVP:**
Excel-in, book-out with yield alerts.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
S/80–200 per month per mill. Software only is plausible for mid-size mills; small ones will want the regente to run it.

**How to find first customers:**
Gore authorisation resolutions (published on gob.pe), and regentes.

**Risks:**
- The free state tool may absorb the reconciliation.
- Small market.
- Illegal-logging reputational exposure.
- Amazon-region field sales.

**Kill condition:**
MC-SNIFFS already validates yield and GTF balance at entry, or there are fewer than about 300 obliged sites nationally.

**Founder access:**
Needs a local presence in Pucallpa or Iquitos.

**Score:** 4.5/10.

### Opportunity C (weak): Done-for-you domestic-worker employer compliance

**Industry:**
Households as employers.

**Buyer:**
Upper-middle-income households in Lima (Miraflores, San Isidro, Surco, La Molina) that employ a live-in or daily domestic worker. Also expatriate households.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- Ley 31047 (2020) and DS 009-2021-TR require a written contract registered on the MTPE app within 3 business days.
- Non-registration is a serious ("grave") SUNAFIL infraction, fined from 0.26 to 26.12 UIT in 2026 (UIT = S/5,500), per worker.
- Registrations are slowly rising: 964 contracts in Jan–Feb 2026.

**Current workflow:**
1. Write the contract.
2. Register the employer and worker on the MTPE app and upload the signed PDF.
3. Get a RUC and Clave SOL, then register the worker and dependants with SUNAT.
4. Pay Formulario 1676 monthly.
5. Compute and pay CTS (May/Nov), gratificación (Jul/Dec) and vacations, and issue payslips.

**Pain:**
- Fines, plus back-pay claims with interest.
- The rules are confusing: gratificación has no 9% EsSalud bonus for domestic workers, unlike the general regime.

**Existing solutions:**
- Free state apps (MTPE registration, SUNAT Form 1676).
- Free calculators (sueldojusto.pe).
- Family accountants.
- Placement agencies (unverified whether they offer ongoing payroll).

**Offline evidence:**
92–95% informal, about 1,100 registered contracts a year (MTPE 2024), and the work happens inside households with no B2B channel.

**Offline channel:**
Placement agencies, condominium and building administrators, international schools and embassies (for expats).

**Market count:**
About 441,000 workers. The addressable market is probably under 20,000 households (estimate).

**The gap:**
No single service runs contract registration, the monthly 1676, the semi-annual CTS/gratificación and the payslip for a household.

**Possible product / MVP:**
A concierge service at S/40–60 per month: the household gives Clave SOL access (a sensitive point) and the service files 1676, computes benefits and sends WhatsApp reminders.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
S/40–60 per month. Only a **done-for-you service** will sell; software alone won't.

**Risks:**
- Low enforcement against households.
- Handling Clave SOL credentials.
- Very low willingness to pay.

**Kill condition:**
SUNAFIL doesn't inspect households in practice, or fewer than 5 of 30 interviewed households would pay.

**Founder access:**
Needs a local.

**Score:** 4/10.

## 3. Rejected

- **Gold trader monthly purchase register (RECPO, DS 009-2025-EM).**
  - **Why it looked good:** 12,418 obliged traders and a monthly filing.
  - **Why it was killed:** Minem built **RECPO En Línea** with PNUD planetGOLD and the Artisanal Gold Council (piloted in Madre de Dios, Arequipa and Puno). The Ley MAPE proposes a state "real-time" mineral traceability system, though it is still stalled in Congress. The register count is called inflated, and the sector carries gold-laundering risk.
  - Sources: https://energiminas.com/2025/02/27/en-el-peru-existen-mas-de-12-mil-comercializadores-de-oro-cifra-excesiva-que-debe-transparentarse , https://lpderecho.pe/reglamento-ley-ampliar-plazo-proceso-formalizacion-minera-decreto-supremo-009-2025-em/ , https://www.artisanalminingchallenge.com/s/One-Pager-RECPO-En-Linea.pdf , https://gestion.pe/economia/ley-mape-propuesta-del-ejecutivo-plantea-trazabilidad-en-tiempo-real-ajustes-en-las-concesiones-mineras-y-otros-puntos-cuales-noticia/
- **Rice mill Registro IVAP.**
  - Mandatory, notary-legalised, and no more than 10 days late. Local vertical software already exists (Tecnocomp Sistema para Molinos) and there is no new trigger.
  - Sources: https://orientacion.sunat.gob.pe/06-registro-ivap-para-propietarios-del-molino-yo-que-presta-servicios-de-pilado , https://www.tecnocomp.com.pe/sistemas/molinos/
- **Scrap and second-hand dealers, used-phone sellers (RENTESEG), SUCAMEC explosives users.** No recurring small-operator filing could be verified within the budget.
- **Slaughterhouses, beekeepers, livestock fairs, pesticide shops, mototaxis, artisanal fishing, recicladores, JASS, cemeteries, hostal guest registers.** Too few buyers, no money, no recurring filing, or the state does the work (see table).

## 4. Method notes

- **What worked:**
  - Queries against gob.pe and El Peruano using the exact instrument name: "libro de operaciones", "Registro IVAP", "RECPO", "Res. SBS 650-2024".
  - Counts from news articles that cite the regulator (Gestión, Andina, Energiminas).
  - Consultancy pages (MEP) were the best way to find the real substitute.
- **What didn't work:**
  - Animal and dealer-register queries drifted to Argentina, because the SENASA name collides with Argentina's agency of the same name.
  - Generic "registro de chatarreros" turned up nothing for Peru.
  - Software and price queries for small Peruvian compliance niches returned almost nothing.
- **Recurring pattern:** Peru's regulators build their own free intake portals (MC-SNIFFS, RECPO En Línea, Form 1676, SIGERSOL). The remaining gap is pre-submission reconciliation and the counter-side capture of data, rather than the filing itself.
