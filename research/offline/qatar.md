# Qatar: Offline-Industries Pass

Researched 2026-10-05. Budget: 20 WebSearch calls. I used 16. Call 14 was refused with a usage-limit error. After the limit reset I ran 2 more searches to fill gaps (recruitment-office enforcement, falcon permits). WebFetch was not used. Everything below comes from search-result summaries, so treat any item marked "unverified" as such.

**Context.** Qatar has about 3M residents, most of them expatriates. Its "quiet" operators are mostly small expatriate-run shops (scrap, pest control, tanker trucks) working under Qatari licence holders, plus Qatari households and farm owners. The state digitises fast: Hukoomi, Ashghal, Ministry of Municipality (MoM) and MoL e-services. As a result, the work these operators do with a regulator is usually already an online form, and the remaining pain is low-frequency. The existing country report already covers food-import Watheq registration, DNFBP AML (gold/real estate) and the WPS SIF validator. I don't repeat them here.

**Overall verdict:** I found no quiet-industry opportunity in Qatar that clears the bar. The one real 2025–2026 trigger is the **extension of the Wage Protection System to all domestic workers** (phased between January 2025 and January 2026, then the payment deadline rules amended in September 2026). It creates a monthly obligation for a large group of households. But the buyer is a household, the substitute is the bank app, and the best channel (licensed recruitment offices) is a small market. It is worth at most a GCC-wide experiment.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Domestic-Worker Payroll & WPS Record for Qatari Households (sold through recruitment offices)

**Industry:**
Households as employers (domestic workers: maids, drivers, cooks, nannies)

**Buyer:**
The sponsoring head of household or family "PRO" (often a family member or the employer's office PRO who manages several domestic workers). Secondary buyer: the licensed recruitment office that placed the worker, offering this as an after-sales service.

**Trigger / Why now:**
MoL extended the Wage Protection System to domestic workers in phases: employers with more than 4 workers from 1 Jan 2025, 3 or more from 1 Jul 2025, 2 or more from 1 Oct 2025, and all domestic workers by 1 Jan 2026. In September 2026, the WPS rules were amended so that the due date is fixed at the 1st of each month and the transfer must be made within 7 days. Failing to pay through WPS can block the employer from recruiting further staff (per payroll-guide summaries; exact domestic sanctions unverified).

**Current workflow:**
1. The household opens or arranges a bank account for the worker. QIB, for example, has a "Domestic Workers Digital Account".
2. Each month, the employer transfers the wage from a personal bank app to the worker's account (IBAN/mobile/alias).
3. Overtime, leave, deductions, the annual ticket and end-of-service gratuity are tracked in the employer's head, on paper or not at all.
4. On a dispute, a contract end or a transfer to a new sponsor, the family reconstructs payment history from bank statements.

**Pain:**
The obligation is new and monthly, with a fixed deadline from Sept 2026, and it is backed by a recruitment block. Households with 2–5 workers face several transfers a month, with minimum-wage and allowance rules on top (QAR 1,000 + 500 + 300 if housing or food is not provided). The volume of disputes and penalties is **unverified**.

**Existing solutions:**
- Bank apps that handle the transfer itself (QIB Domestic Workers Account, QNB, CBQ, Dukhan, QIIB WPS services)
- Recruitment offices (262 listed) that advise informally
- General payroll SaaS (Zoho Payroll, greytHR, Bayzat), which is built for companies holding a CR, not households
- Lawyers or law firms when a dispute arises

**Offline evidence:**
Until 2025, domestic wages were paid largely in cash. Households have no CR and therefore no access to company payroll tools. Guidance is spread through recruitment-office and law-firm blog posts, not software listings.

**Offline channel:**
Recruitment offices: list the 262 offices from directories and call or WhatsApp them, offering a white-label "sponsor compliance pack" bundled with each placement. Bank branch relationship managers for domestic accounts are a second, harder channel.

**Market count:**
About 168,626 domestic workers (PSA Labour Force Survey 2022, via GLMM). Number of employing households: unknown, estimate 60–100k. 262 recruitment offices (GCC Domestic directory; not an official MoL count).

**The gap:**
The bank moves the money but keeps no employment ledger: wage components, leave balance, overtime, ticket entitlement, gratuity accrual, a signed monthly payslip and a WPS-transfer proof pack for disputes or sponsor transfer.

**Possible product:**
An Arabic-first mobile web app where the household enters each worker's contract once. Every month it reminds the household before the 1st, computes the wage due (allowances, deductions, leave), records the bank transfer reference, issues a bilingual payslip the worker can acknowledge, and keeps a running gratuity/leave/ticket balance. It is white-labelled for recruitment offices.

**MVP:**
Contract-to-monthly-schedule calculator + reminder + payslip PDF + an "end-of-contract settlement" calculator. No bank integration.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
QAR 15–30 per worker per month to households (estimate). Or recruitment offices pay QAR 100–300 per month to include it in their after-sales package. Willingness to pay software is likely low. Households would pay more for a done-for-you service (a PRO managing the paperwork), so expect service-plus-software.

**How to find first customers:**
Call 10 recruitment offices from the public directory listings. Pitch fewer disputes and replacement claims for their placements.

**Risks:**
- The bank adds a payslip feature or MoL adds a domestic e-contract ledger.
- Consumers rarely pay for compliance.
- WhatsApp/Arabic-first support would need a local.
- A non-local solo founder could build it but would struggle to sell it without an Arabic-speaking partner in Doha.

**Kill condition:**
Interviews with 5 recruitment offices show that sponsors never ask about WPS or settlement calculations. Or a major bank already issues domestic payslips and gratuity calculations.

**Score:** 3.5/10 (Pain 4, Frequency 8, Mandatory 8, Fragmentation 2, Competition 5, Incumbent gap 5, Buyer access 4, WTP 2, MVP 9, Distribution 4. Overall pulled down by consumer WTP and the small offline channel.)

### Opportunity: Placement & Guarantee-Period Tracker for Domestic Recruitment Offices

**Industry:**
Licensed domestic-worker recruitment offices

**Buyer:**
Owner or office manager of a 2–10 person recruitment office in Doha.

**Trigger / Why now:**
The domestic WPS rollout (2025–Jan 2026) and the Sept 2026 payment-deadline amendment raise the number of sponsor disputes. These run into the office's trial-period and replacement obligations (the link is unverified). There is no new rule aimed specifically at the offices that I could confirm.

**Current workflow:**
1. Collect the worker's CV and documents from the sending-country agent (WhatsApp/PDF).
2. Match the worker to a sponsor and sign the contract.
3. Track arrival, medical, QID and the trial/guarantee period.
4. Handle returns and replacements by hand, on paper and WhatsApp.

**Pain:**
Many placements per office, deadlines for the guarantee and replacement period, and refund disputes. Specific penalty data is **unverified**.

**Existing solutions:**
- GCC recruitment-office CRMs: Saudi vendors built around Musaned, plus some UAE Tadbeer-centre systems. Whether these are sold in Qatar is unverified.
- Excel + WhatsApp
- Generic CRMs

**Offline evidence:**
Offices market through low-quality SEO pages and phone numbers. I found no Qatar-specific vendor listings.

**Offline channel:**
Phone or WhatsApp outreach to the 262 listed offices, which cluster in a few Doha streets, so walk-ins work too. Sending-country agents in the Philippines, Kenya and India are a cross-referral channel.

**Market count:**
262 offices (GCC Domestic listing; the MoL register was not found).

**The gap:**
No Qatar-specific tracker for trial-period and replacement deadlines with a sponsor-facing payment/WPS checklist (unverified).

**Possible product:**
A lightweight case tracker per worker, from CV to arrival, guarantee expiry and replacement, with sponsor reminders. It bundles the household payroll pack above.

**MVP:**
A shared case board with deadline alerts and sponsor/worker WhatsApp templates.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
QAR 300–600 per office per month (estimate). They would pay for software if it is cheap, and pay more if Arabic support is included.

**How to find first customers:**
Directory scrape + walk-ins in Doha. This needs a local.

**Risks:**
- The market is small (262 offices give at most about $150k ARR).
- Saudi/UAE vendors could move in.
- The workflow is a generic CRM, which is a trap the brief warns against.

**Kill condition:**
Offices already use a GCC recruitment system, or the guarantee period is not a real cost driver. Light enforcement is an early warning sign: MoL issued 1 violation report in 730 visits in Q2 2025.

**Score:** 3/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Sewage tanker operators (Ashghal discharge permit).** This was the closest match to the Florida grease benchmark. But the permit is per tanker and the documents are static (registration, photos, volume certificate). I found no per-load manifest filed by the operator, and Hukoomi already hosts the service. Source: https://www.ashghal.gov.qa/ar/Services/Pages/ServiceDetailsPage.aspx?ServiceID=22&category=3&SecID=1 ; https://hukoomi.gov.qa/ar/services/request-tanker-permit-for-individuals-compounds
- **Fishermen and vessel licensing.** MoM digitised the fishermen, vessel, fish-transport and gear licences in April 2025. There are about 371 subsidised vessels, which is too small. Source: https://al-sharq.com/article/09/04/2025/ترخيص-الصيادين-والسفن-البلدية-تطلق-خدمات-إلكترونية-جديدة-لإدارة-الثروة-السمكية ; https://al-sharq.com/article/23/04/2026/فتح-باب-التقديم-لدعم-السفن-الخشبية-للصيادين
- **Farms.** Fewer than 1,000 producing farms. The state relationship is subsidy, not compliance. Source: https://www.raya.com/2026/06/29/وسط-تفاؤل-المزارعين-بوفرة-الإنتاج-بشائر-الرطب-المحلي-تبدأ-في-الظهور
- **Scrap / second-hand dealers.** I found no Qatari transaction register. The searchable obligation (the "Shumoos" register and radiation gates) is Saudi. Saudi Arabia is the market to research instead.
- **Livestock traders.** Markets are operated by Hassad under contract with MOCI. I found no trader-level reporting. Source: https://www.moci.gov.qa/news/455
- **Falcon passports / CITES.** MoECC's own platform already handles more than 24k applications (https://qna.org.qa/ar-QA/News-Area/News/2026-9/9/وزارة-البيية-والتغير-المناخي-تسجل-اكثر-من-24-الف-طلب-عبر-منصة-الصقور-وتصاريح-سايتس). Owners are hobbyists.
- **Pest control, salons, tutoring-centre teachers.** Licensing is one-off and inspections are passive. I found no recurring filing.

## 4. Method notes

- Arabic queries in the "إلزامي / تصريح / ترخيص + ministry name" pattern worked best. al-Sharq and Raya report MoM and MoL service launches and statistics. English queries mostly return payroll-vendor SEO.
- Queries for registers and counts ("عدد ... المرخصة") mostly failed. Qatar publishes few register-level counts outside data.gov.qa. The MOCI "active licences by municipality and business activity" dataset on data.gov.qa is the right next source for counting operators (https://www.data.gov.qa/explore/dataset/moci-active-certificates-by-municipality-and-business-activity/).
- I used 16 of 20 searches. A usage-limit refusal interrupted the run at call 14. I did not screen camel racing, beekeeping or labour-accommodation inspections. MoL's quarterly "sector statistics" releases (Gulf Times / Qatar Tribune) are the best enforcement source: they give inspection counts per licensed sector.
- Structural finding: Qatar's quiet industries are thin, and the state digitises their licences directly. A quiet-industry wedge here only makes sense as part of a GCC product, with Saudi Arabia as the anchor market (scrap registers, Musaned recruitment, domestic WPS).
