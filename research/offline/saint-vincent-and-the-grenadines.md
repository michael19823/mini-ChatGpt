# Saint Vincent and the Grenadines: offline (quiet) industries pass

**Date:** 2026-10-05. **Budget:** 8 WebSearch calls used of 8. WebFetch was not used.

**Bottom line:** SVG is a microstate (about 100k people). Every quiet industry I screened is
regulated mainly by one central body (police, Public Health Department, Fisheries Division,
Kingstown Town Board, NIS). That means there is **no local fragmentation**, and the obliged
populations are counted in tens to low thousands. None of these is a standalone software
opportunity. The two ideas below are written up only so that they can be pooled with OECS
neighbours, which have similar regimes. Neither one scores above 3/10 for SVG alone. The existing
country report's ideas (ASYCUDA broker tool, e-Tax PAYE + NIS) are not repeated here.

## 2. Opportunities

### Opportunity A: Household-employer payslip and NIS service (OECS-pooled)

**Industry:**
Households as employers (domestic workers, caregivers, nannies)

**Buyer:**
Households that employ a domestic worker or live-in caregiver (often expatriates, retirees and
professionals), or the small accounting firms that do their NIS filings.

**Trigger / Why now:**
Weak. The government has been considering a minimum wage increase (reported as about 20%,
date not verified). An increase would change the domestic-worker rate from the 2008 Order
schedule. The NIS contribution changes of 1 January 2026 are covered in the country report. I
found no new household-specific obligation.

**Current workflow:**
1. The household pays the worker in cash, often without a payslip.
2. If they comply, the employer registers with NIS and pays the employee and employer
   contributions at the NIS office, or through an accountant.
3. Minimum-wage changes come in by Order; the household rarely checks them.

**Pain:**
Low and mostly latent. The obligation exists, but I found no evidence of enforcement against
households.

**Existing solutions:**
- Doing it by hand at the NIS counter or through NIS eSubmit (see the country report).
- Local accountants.
- Global EOR/payroll guides (Rivermate, Playroll), which are aimed at companies, not households.

**Offline evidence:**
No household-payroll product is listed for SVG. Compliance is by cash and the counter.

**Offline channel:**
Employment agencies for domestic workers and caregivers, nursing homes (which the 2008 Order
also covers), and expatriate and retiree networks in the Grenadines.

**Market count:**
Unknown. There is no public count of household employers. The 2008 Order covers private
households, nursing homes, cooks, cleaners, child carers and adult carers.

**The gap:**
A one-worker payslip, minimum-wage check and NIS schedule, delivered as a done-for-you service.

**Possible product:**
A monthly household-payroll service across OECS states: payslip, minimum-wage check and NIS
filing, priced per worker.

**MVP:**
A form that produces a monthly payslip and NIS contribution schedule for one worker in SVG.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
US$10–15/month per worker, as a service only. Households will not pay for software alone.

**How to find first customers:**
Employment agencies and nursing homes.

**Risks:**
- Low enforcement means low urgency.
- It needs a local person to file at NIS.
- The market is tiny.

**Kill condition:**
NIS does not accept third-party filing for households, or fewer than 200 compliant household
employers exist.

**Willingness to pay:** Only for a done-for-you service. **Founder access:** It needs a local
partner, such as an accountant; a non-local solo founder could not sell this.

**Score:** 2/10

### Opportunity B: Cultivator record-keeping for medicinal-cannabis licensees (OECS-pooled)

**Industry:**
Licensed medicinal cannabis cultivation (traditional cultivators and farmer cooperatives)

**Buyer:**
Farmer cooperatives and small licensed cultivators that must keep cultivation and
traceability records for the Medicinal Cannabis Authority (MCA).

**Trigger / Why now:**
Caribbean MCAs are rolling out national seed-to-sale systems. GrowerIQ was selected by Barbados
(2023), St Lucia (2026) and St Kitts and Nevis (August 2026). SVG's own national system is
unverified. If SVG follows, small cultivators would need to get their data into it.

**Current workflow:**
1. Cultivators keep paper logs, as taught in the government's best-practice training.
2. The licence holder or co-op compiles the records for MCA inspection.

**Pain:**
Inferred, not evidenced: record-keeping is a licence condition, and many cultivators are
traditional hillside farmers.

**Existing solutions:**
- National traceability platforms bought by MCAs (GrowerIQ in neighbouring states).
- Paper logbooks.
- Co-op administrators.

**Offline evidence:**
The training was in person for about 1,200 traditional cultivators. Cultivation is in remote
hillside areas.

**Offline channel:**
The 8 licensed farmer co-ops, and MCA training sessions.

**Market count:**
About 1,200 traditional cultivators trained (gov.vc), and 8 co-ops among licensees. The current
number of active licensees is unverified.

**The gap:**
A mobile-first, offline-capable field log that exports to the national system. It is only
valuable if SVG has adopted, or adopts, a national platform that cultivators must feed.

**Possible product:**
A co-op-level field logging app, sold to co-ops and run as a white-label offering for MCAs.

**MVP:**
An offline Android form for plant and batch events, with a CSV export in the regulator's format.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
US$50–100/month per co-op. Cultivators themselves will not pay.

**How to find first customers:**
The MCA licensee list and the co-op leadership.

**Risks:**
- The national vendor (GrowerIQ-type) bundles a cultivator app.
- Political and regulatory change.
- Payment-processing limits on cannabis businesses.

**Kill condition:**
The SVG MCA adopts, or has adopted, a national platform that includes free cultivator data
entry. This is likely, given the regional pattern.

**Willingness to pay:** Low. A service sold to co-ops at most. **Founder access:** It needs a local
person and MCA goodwill.

**Score:** 3/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Scrap metal dealer register:** the licence is police-issued at a reported EC$40,000/year,
  and non-ferrous exports are suspended. That leaves a handful of dealers, and the rules date
  from 2012 (unverified for 2026). Source: https://www.searchlight.vc/news/2012/10/05/copper-exports-from-svg-banned
- **Butchers / meat inspection:** the inspection is booked by phone and the Environmental Health
  Officer issues the certificate. The butcher does no filing that software would replace.
  Sources: https://health.gov.vc/health/index.php/news/1212-public-health-department-meat-inspection ,
  https://onenewsstvincent.com/2022/12/21/butchers-must-contact-public-health-department-before-selling-meat
- **Fishers / catch reporting:** the Fisheries Division's Licensing and Registration System and
  its landing-site data collectors do the work (about 1,550 fishers and 600+ vessels). Fishers
  have no filing duty and no budget. Sources: https://www.crfm.int/~uwohxjxf/images/Agenda_Item_7_Ref_Doc_i_-_St._Vincent_National_Report.pdf ,
  https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/s3/2025-08/Saint-Vincent-and-the-Grenadines-final-2025-508.pdf
- **Pesticide dealers:** the 1973 Act has no modern recurring reporting duty that I could find.
  Sources: https://leap.unep.org/en/countries/vc/national-legislation/pesticides-control-act-1973 ,
  https://botanicalgarden.gov.vc/agriculture/images/stories/PDF_Documents/A-Pesticide-Directory-for-SVG.pdf
- **Minibus operators:** police licensing plus roadside enforcement; no recurring paperwork found.
  The association (VINTAS) exists but has no pain point to sell into. Source:
  https://searchlight.vc/news/2023/10/13/licenses-five-minibus-operators-suspended
- **Kingstown vendors:** EC$5/day paid in cash at the Town Board, about 90 permitted vendors,
  and a 2026 crackdown on illegal vending. There is no software buyer. Sources:
  https://www.searchlight.vc/front-page/2024/01/19/vendors-told-pay-due-fees-go ,
  https://onenewsstvincent.com/2026/02/17/government-signals-tougher-stance-on-street-vending-in-kingstown

## 4. Method notes

- Pointing English queries at local news sites (Searchlight, One News SVG, iWitness News) and at
  gov.vc / health.gov.vc gave the most concrete obligations. FAO/CRFM national reports gave the
  only usable operator counts (fishers and vessels).
- Generic register queries (pesticide dealers, minibus permits) returned other Caribbean
  states' laws. SVG publishes almost no online registers, so most market counts remain unknown.
- In a microstate, a single central regulator plus a small population leaves no fragmentation.
  A quiet-industry idea would need to be pooled across the OECS to be worth building.
