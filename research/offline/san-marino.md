# San Marino: Offline-Industries Pass

**Market:** Microstate (about 34k residents). Search budget: 8 WebSearch calls (all used). WebFetch not used.
**Bottom line:** No quiet industry in San Marino supports a standalone product. Every regulated small-operator group is counted in the tens or low hundreds, and the receiving authority is a single office (Ufficio del Lavoro, ISS, Ufficio Attività Economiche, ISS Veterinary Unit). The one group with a recurring paper-heavy workflow and a counted population is **households employing live-in caregivers (badanti)**: about 400–440 workers. That is too small for software. At most it is a done-for-you service run by a local, or a San Marino module inside an Italian household-payroll product. Score 2/10.

Most searches returned Italian rules (TULPS, Cas.Sa.Colf, the Italian anagrafe apistica). San Marino has its own law in each area, but very little of it is indexed. I have not assumed that Italian obligations apply.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

Only one candidate is worth writing up, and it is below the bar.

### Opportunity: Done-for-you payroll and permit service for San Marino families employing a badante

**Industry:**
Households as employers (live-in elderly and disability caregivers).

**Buyer:**
Families, usually adult children of an elderly resident, who directly employ a live-in caregiver, often a Ukrainian or Moldovan worker.

**Trigger / Why now:**
There is no strong 2026 trigger specific to badanti. Context:
- The 2026 family law creates a parallel "family caregiver" status, capped at 50 people. The law number is not verified.
- Tighter enforcement on unpaid contributions, with suspensions and revocations for defaulting employers (Libertas). Whether this applies to household employers is unverified.
- Unions publicly call for a review of the badanti rules (CDLS: "Badanti: necessario un confronto sulle regole").

**Current workflow:**
1. The family finds a worker, often through word of mouth or a predecessor's substitute.
2. The family or a union or patronato office registers the worker. This means the Registro Assistenza at the Ufficio del Lavoro, a work permit within the annual quota, and a declaration of family cover for the worker's off-hours.
3. Monthly payslip and ISS contributions, and holiday cover with substitutes. This is done by hand, by a union office, or by an accountant.

**Pain:**
Permit quota and substitute handling (unofficial estimates of 600–700 people against ~400 official suggest heavy churn and substitutes). The administrative work falls on elderly families. I found no published complaints about the paperwork itself.

**Existing solutions:**
- Union offices: CDLS and CSdL publish badanti rules and assist families. This is the real substitute and is cheap or included in membership.
- Local accountants and labour consultants (unnamed, estimate).
- Italian household-payroll services (WebColf, Assindatcolf, Domina-affiliated offices) are Italy/INPS only and do not handle ISS or SM permits (inferred from search results).
- The Ufficio del Lavoro counter itself.

**Offline evidence:**
Registration goes through the Ufficio del Lavoro register and quota permits. Advice is given in union offices. I found no SM-specific domestic-payroll software or app in searches.

**Offline channel:**
Union patronato desks (CDLS, CSdL), the ISS social services that assess the family's cover declaration, and parish and elderly-care networks. In practice this needs a local person at a desk.

**Market count:**
About 440 domestic workers in 2022 (Domina / Osservatorio Lavoro Domestico), about 400 badanti officially (GiornaleSM).

**The gap:**
One SM-specific monthly payslip, ISS contribution and permit-renewal calendar per family. The unions already cover most of this.

**Possible product:**
A local service that runs payslips, ISS contributions, permit renewals and substitute paperwork for a monthly fee, with a simple internal tool. Software alone would not sell.

**MVP:**
A spreadsheet-backed payslip and contribution calculator for the SM domestic contract, plus a renewal reminder by phone or WhatsApp.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
€15–30 per family per month as a service (estimate). Even at 100% of 440 families, that is under €160k per year. Realistically it is 10–20% of that.

**How to find first customers:**
Partnership with a union patronato or a local labour consultant. There is no public list of household employers.

**Risks:**
Unions provide the service cheaply. The market is tiny. It needs Italian/SM labour-law expertise and a local presence. A non-local solo founder could not realistically sell this.

**Kill condition:**
Unions or patronati already run payroll for most badanti employers. This is likely.

**Score:** 2/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Family-caregiver application helper (2026 family law):** capped at 50 caregivers. The state pays, and ISS has already issued circulars.
- **Dealer registers (gold, second-hand, scrap):** no San Marino rule found (Italian TULPS does not apply), and operators number single to low double digits.
- **Livestock, beekeeper and canine registers:** the ISS Veterinary Unit already runs electronic registers. Farms number in the tens.
- **Ambulanti and personal-care licences (barbers, beauticians, tattoo):** one-off licensing at the Ufficio Attività Economiche, with no recurring report. The register is computerised and searchable for a fee.
- **Organic and small food producers:** the certifier, Italian HACCP consultants and generic apps cover this.
- The country report's e-invoicing (HUB-SM) idea was not re-reported.

## 4. Method notes

- Plain Italian queries return Italian rules (INPS, TULPS, Italian anagrafe apistica), because San Marino shares the language. **Restricting to .sm domains** (consigliograndeegenerale.sm, iss.sm, gov.sm, cdls.sm, libertas.sm, sanmarinortv.sm) was the only pattern that surfaced SM law and counts.
- Union sites (CDLS) and the Domina / Osservatorio reports gave the only usable market count.
- I found no public operator counts for dealers, ambulanti or personal-care trades. They would need the fee-based Registro delle Attività Economiche.
- With about 34k residents, every quiet industry is too small. San Marino should be dropped from this pass.
