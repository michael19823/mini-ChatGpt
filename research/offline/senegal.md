# Senegal: Offline (Quiet) Industries Pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. Language of queries: French. Budget: 20 WebSearch calls, all 20 used. The 17th was refused for a usage limit, and after the reset the last 3 went to filling gaps: the ANSD domestic-worker count, the list of licensed changers, and the Highway Code status. None of the three answered its question. WebFetch was not used (blocked by the method).

> **Evidence limits.** Every claim comes from search-result summaries, not from regulator PDFs read in full. Counts marked **(estimate)** are mine, and anything marked **(unverified)** could not be confirmed. Overall, Senegal's quiet industries turned out to be mostly **informal and unregistered**, or **handled by free state campaigns** (registration drives, biometric cards, fleet-renewal programs). Few of them file recurring reports that a small vendor could automate. This is a short report, and the honest conclusion is that no strong lead was found.

This pass does not repeat the opportunities in `research/countries/senegal.md` (CNSCL local content, ARCOP APPEL bids, DGID e-invoicing, Ndamli IPRES/CSS).

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Monthly BCEAO reporting and AML register for licensed manual money changers (UEMOA-wide)

**Industry:**
Manual foreign-exchange bureaus (agréés de change manuel), including changers attached to hotels and travel agencies.

**Buyer:**
The owner-manager of a licensed bureau de change. Secondary buyers are hotels and travel agencies holding a change-manuel licence, and accountants who prepare the monthly report for them.

**Trigger / Why now:**
In July–August 2025 the BCEAO published a new set of foreign-exchange instructions under the revised UEMOA external financial relations regulation (R09):
- **Instruction 07-07-2025 RFE** sets new conditions of operation for licensed manual changers.
- **Instruction 15-07-2025 RFE** governs periodic reports to the authorities. It came into force on **1 Aug 2025**. Licensed changers must send their periodic reports and supporting documents within **10 working days after each month end**. Annex 2 is the declaration of currency buy and sell operations. Submission can be physical or electronic.

In Senegal, the Ministry of Finance's Direction de la Monnaie et du Crédit asked licensed changers to identify themselves or risk being struck off, which signals that the ministry is cleaning up its register.

**Current workflow (inferred, to be confirmed in interviews):**
1. Each transaction is written in a counter ledger or Excel sheet: currency, amount, rate, client ID.
2. At month end, the operator or an accountant totals buys and sells by currency into the Annex 2 format.
3. The report is printed and deposited at the DMC or BCEAO branch, or emailed.
4. Separately, the operator handles KYC and AML: client ID copies, cash thresholds, and suspicious-transaction reports to CENTIF.

**Pain:**
- The obligation is monthly, with a short deadline, and the licence can be withdrawn.
- New formats came in 2025.
- CENTIF data shows 33 exchange-regulation infringements in 2023 STRs. However, changers are not a visible STR source (banks provide 86% of reports), which suggests weak AML compliance by changers.
- The number of hours spent is unverified.

**Existing solutions:**
- Excel templates and a paper ledger (most likely substitute).
- The bureau's accountant.
- Generic forex-bureau POS software sold internationally. Whether any version handles the BCEAO Annex 2 format is **unverified**.
- Bank core systems, which cover banks only, not changers.

**Offline evidence:**
- Paper or physical submission is explicitly allowed.
- The DMC tracks its own licensees through newspaper notices and a phone or email contact.
- No Senegal or UEMOA changer software appeared in any search.

**Offline channel:**
- The DMC's annual published list of licensed changers (a public register), followed by phone or WhatsApp outreach and visits to bureaus at Dakar airport, Sandaga and the hotel districts.
- Accounting firms that already prepare the reports.
- Possibly a professional association of changers (none was identified; unverified).

**Market count:**
Unknown for Senegal. The Ministry of Finance publishes the list yearly, but no 2025 Senegal list was found online. The DMC must be asked for it (dmc@minfinances.sn, as given in the DMC notice). Niger publishes its list online. Estimate: tens to low hundreds of licensed changers per country, times 8 UEMOA countries **(estimate)**.

**The gap:**
A tool that turns daily counter transactions into the exact BCEAO Annex 2 monthly report, with a KYC register and cash-threshold alerts, in the UEMOA format.

**Possible product:**
A tablet or phone counter app where each FX transaction captures a client ID photo, and the app generates the monthly Annex 2 report and keeps an AML register. It works across all 8 UEMOA countries because the rules are uniform.

**MVP:**
An Excel-compatible daily ledger template plus a converter that produces the Annex 2 report, sold together with a done-for-you monthly filing service to 5 Dakar bureaus.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
15,000–30,000 FCFA/month (about USD 25–50) per bureau. A done-for-you filing service would cost more.

**How to find first customers:**
The Ministry of Finance (DMC) list of licensed changers, and in-person visits.

**Risks:**
- The market is tiny, and many changers operate informally without a licence, so they won't buy compliance tools.
- The BCEAO may introduce its own e-reporting portal.
- Annex 2 may be trivial to fill in Excel.
- AML features attract liability.
- A non-local founder would need a local partner for visits and trust in a cash business.

**Kill condition:**
Kill the idea if Senegal has fewer than about 50 licensed changers, if the monthly report takes under an hour in Excel, or if the BCEAO announces a mandatory portal with its own data entry.

**Founder access:**
Hard for a non-local solo founder. It needs a Dakar partner.

**Willingness to pay:**
Likely only for a done-for-you service. Paying for software alone is doubtful.

**Score:** 4/10

### Opportunity: Done-for-you domestic-worker employment pack (contract, hire declaration, IPRES/CSS) for Dakar households

**Industry:**
Households as employers of domestic workers (aides-ménagères, nannies, drivers, guards).

**Buyer:**
Middle-class, diaspora and expatriate households in Dakar. Secondary buyers are embassies and NGOs that require their staff's domestic employees to be declared (unverified).

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The 2026 parliamentary examination of the new Labour Code and Social Security Code bills, with deputies specifically raising domestic workers' lack of cover.
- The ADDAD and SYNTRAD campaign to ratify ILO C189 (June 2026).
- IPRES and CSS conventions to extend coverage to the informal economy (May 2026).

None of these has yet created a new enforced obligation, so the why-now is weak.

**Current workflow:**
1. In most cases there is none: no contract and no declaration.
2. Compliant employers fill in the paper "déclaration de mouvement de travailleur" for the labour inspector, then affiliate the worker to IPRES (gens de maison branch) and CSS.
3. They declare and pay contributions on Ndamli (free) or at the counter.

**Pain:**
The exposure is legal (labour disputes, work accidents) rather than procedural. About 90% of domestic workers have no social cover.

**Existing solutions:**
- Ndamli, the free IPRES/CSS platform.
- Labour inspectorate counters.
- Placement agencies (bureaux de placement) and accountants.
- The afrotools.com and africarrieres.com salary calculators.

**Offline evidence:**
- Paper declarations dating from a 1963 arrêté.
- Informal hiring by word of mouth.
- No household-payroll product was found.

**Offline channel:**
- Domestic-worker placement agencies in Dakar.
- HR departments of embassies, international NGOs and multinationals, which could offer it as a benefit for expatriate staff.
- The ADDAD association.

**Market count:**
Unknown. ANSD employment surveys do not isolate domestic workers in search results (vie-publique.sn ANSD reports).

**The gap:**
No one handles the end-to-end paperwork for a household: contract, hire declaration, IPRES/CSS registration, monthly payslip and contribution payment.

**Possible product:**
A subscription service where the household enters the worker once, and the service produces the contract and payslips and handles Ndamli filing each month.

**MVP:**
A WhatsApp intake, a manual Ndamli filing by an operator, and a PDF payslip.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
5,000–10,000 FCFA/month per worker. Expatriate households could pay more.

**How to find first customers:**
Placement agencies and expatriate HR departments.

**Risks:**
- Very low willingness to pay and no enforcement.
- Ndamli is free.
- This is a service business, not software.

**Kill condition:**
Kill the idea if the new Labour or Social Security Code adds no enforced household obligation, or if fewer than 5 of 20 expatriate households interviewed would pay.

**Founder access:**
Needs a local operator.

**Score:** 3/10

## 4. Method notes

- **What worked:** French queries naming the instrument ("instruction BCEAO", "arrêté", "agrément", "immatriculation") and the regional institution (BCEAO, UEMOA) rather than Senegal alone. Ministry press notices that ran in Seneweb, Le Soleil and AllAfrica were the main way to see a register.
- **What didn't:** queries on "register" and "count" for trades (security companies, veterinary wholesalers, changers) returned no Senegalese lists, and counts are rarely published online.
- **Structural finding:** in Senegal, enforcement of quiet trades takes the form of one-off **state campaigns** (registration drives, biometric cards, fleet renewal) rather than recurring operator filings. That leaves little room for workflow software. The more promising angle is **UEMOA-wide BCEAO rules**, which apply identically in 8 countries.
