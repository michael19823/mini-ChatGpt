# Solomon Islands: Offline-Industries Pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. Search budget: 8 WebSearch calls, 6 used, all after the earlier usage-limit interruption. This is a short report on a microstate.

## Bottom line

Solomon Islands has about 0.75m people. Its formal private sector is about 2,071 registered businesses, 85% of them in Honiara (DFAT figure cited in the country report). The quiet industries do exist and are regulated: Honiara City Council (HCC) bus and taxi operators, market vendors, CEMA-licensed commodity buyers and exporters, mill licensees, and households that employ staff and owe SINPF contributions. But each group is either tiny, informal and cash-based, or already handled by a counter clerk or an existing portal. **No quiet industry clears the bar for an indie software product.** The best-supported one is still weak: a done-for-you "licence and compliance book" service for HCC public-transport operators, at **2/10**. It needs a local person on the ground and earns little money.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: HCC public-transport operator compliance book (service plus light software)

**Industry:**
Informal urban transport: Honiara minibus, taxi and small truck operators.

**Buyer:**
Owner-operator, or owner of 2–10 buses or taxis in Honiara. The Honiara bus association is a possible group buyer.

**Trigger / Why now:**
In 2024 HCC published conditions on licensed operators under the Regulation of Business Licences Ordinance 2011. Operators must give the council the names, driver's licence details and contact details of every driver and conductor, show destination signs and complete the full route, charge the standard fares, and must not have tinted glass. HCC has since run roadside crackdowns, and drivers and conductors have been fined for overcharging (SIBC). The 2025 Fees, Rates and Charges Ordinance reset the fees.

**Current workflow:**
1. The owner renews the HCC business licence for each vehicle at the HCC counter and pays cash or by bank deposit.
2. The owner informs HCC, on paper or in person, of the drivers and conductors engaged. Turnover among drivers and conductors is high.
3. Vehicle registration and roadworthiness are handled separately with the transport authority (not verified in this pass).
4. On a roadside check, the driver has to show the licence and conditions. Fines are paid when issued.

**Pain:**
Enforcement is real and recurring: crackdowns on short-route running and on tint, and fines for overcharging. Keeping the driver and conductor list current with HCC is a recurring duty, because drivers change often. There is no evidence of operators complaining about the paperwork itself; their complaints are about fares and enforcement.

**Existing solutions:**
HCC counter (paper), the operator's own exercise book, the bus association (informal coordination), and WhatsApp/Facebook groups (unverified). No software found.

**Offline evidence:**
The conditions exist only as a Gazette PDF on PacLII. The licence is issued at the HCC counter. No online HCC licensing portal was found.

**Offline channel:**
The Honiara bus association (it has publicly called for clean vehicles and standard fares). Bus terminals and the Central Market bus stand. Approaching operators in person at HCC licensing windows during the renewal period.

**Market count:**
Unverified. No public register of licensed public vehicles was found. Estimate: low hundreds of buses plus a few hundred taxis in Honiara.

**The gap:**
There is no simple way for an owner to keep an up-to-date driver and conductor list, licence expiry dates and the conditions sheet in one place, and to hand HCC a clean list. This is a small, low-value gap.

**Possible product:**
A done-for-you renewal and driver-list service sold through the bus association. Behind it sits a tiny register (an SMS or WhatsApp reminder plus a printable driver-list form for HCC).

**MVP:**
A spreadsheet-backed register for each fleet. It prints the HCC driver and conductor notification and sends an SMS reminder 30 days before licence expiry.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
SBD 50–100 (about USD 6–12) per vehicle per year, collected through the association (estimate). Total market value is well under USD 10k a year.

**How to find first customers:**
Bus association meetings; the bus stand at Central Market; HCC licensing counter queues.

**Risks:**
Cash economy; HCC may change forms without notice; enforcement may be political and on/off; operators may simply pay the fines; revenue is too small for a non-local founder.

**Kill condition:**
HCC does not actually require the written driver list at renewal, or the association will not endorse the service.

**Willingness to pay:**
Operators would pay, if at all, only for a done-for-you service (someone queues at HCC for them). They would not pay for software.

**Founder access:**
A non-local solo founder could not sell this. It needs a Honiara-based person or the association itself.

**Score:** 2/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Domestic-worker SINPF payroll for households:** the obligation is real (7.5% plus 5%, monthly, with a 2% per-month late surcharge), and SINPF does crack down on employers. But households that actually register staff are a few hundred expatriate or elite households, covered by an employer's HR team or an accountant. The rest are informal. Sources: https://sinpf.org.sb/index.php/employers-services/22-about-contribution-rates/51-surchage-penalty.html, https://theislandsun.com/sinpf-cracks-down-on-non-paying-employers
- **Market-vendor fee management:** HCC collects cash daily at the market. Vendors have no record-keeping duty and no money to pay. Source: https://www.sibconline.com.sb/?p=10286
- **CEMA commodity export licences:** annual, tens of exporters, one form. The substitute is the Chamber of Commerce tree-crop export brokerage plus ASYCUDA. Sources: https://solomonchamber.com.sb/tree-crop-export-brokerage/regulatory-requirements-for-export/export-license-for-tree-crops, https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/sol65080.pdf
- **Mill licence and timber levy:** 3-year licence. Community mills are donor-supplied (67 portable mills in 2024). The only plausible buyer is the Ministry or a donor, through procurement. Source: https://paclii.org/sb/legis/sub_leg/fratuatamlr819
- **Scrap metal / second-hand / pawn dealers:** no dealer-register or police-reporting obligation was found.

## 4. Method notes

- What worked: searching for "HCC" and "Gazette" brought up Honiara City Council ordinances on PacLII/AustLII, and SIBC news reports on enforcement.
- What didn't work: there are no public registers of licensees anywhere (HCC, CEMA, Forestry), so every market count is an estimate. Searches on dealer registers (scrap, pawn) returned other countries' regimes.
- Local-language queries (Pijin) are not useful for regulator content, which is in English.
- In this market the regulator-first approach finds obligations, but not paying buyers.
