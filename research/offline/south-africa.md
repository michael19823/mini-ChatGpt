## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: SHGA Yard Book (scrap-dealer transaction register, waste-picker register and inspection pack)

**Industry:**
Scrap metal dealers and recyclers (small yards and "bucket shops") registered under the Second-Hand Goods Act.

**Buyer:**
Owner-manager of a small or medium scrap yard that buys from walk-in sellers and waste pickers. Secondary buyer: the MRA as an accredited association that wants its members to stay compliant.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The **Second-Hand Goods Amendment Bill, 2026** was approved by Cabinet in Aug 2026, with public comment closing 2 Nov 2026. It bans cash for controlled metal (electronic payment only), requires a waste-picker register "in the prescribed format", extends registration to sellers, and covers copper, gold and chrome. It made the news on 5 Oct 2026 (BusinessDay, Sowetan, TimesLive).
- Enforcement is already running. Cape Town's Metal Theft Unit carried out 1,257 scrapyard compliance inspections and issued 3,095 fines in about 10 months, and closed 42 dealers and 94 bucket shops for non-compliance or failing to register.
- The SAHRC (30 Sep 2026) has called for cashless copper trading. Copper theft costs about R45bn a year.
- The 2024 draft dealer and recycler regulations also proposed monthly electronic reports to ITAC (purchase date, metal type and grade, tonnage, cost, seller ID). Whether that was finalised is *unverified*.

**Current workflow:**
1. A seller arrives with metal. The yard weighs it and writes the ticket by hand or in basic weighbridge software.
2. The clerk copies or photocopies the seller's ID and writes particulars (name, address, phone, ID number, how identity was verified) into the SHGA register book.
3. The seller is paid, often in cash today.
4. Copies are kept in files for 5 years. When SAPS or a metro unit inspects, the owner digs out books and ID copies.
5. A yard that exports does separate ITAC Price Preference System paperwork per consignment.

**Pain:**
The penalties are closure and fines (Cape Town figures above). The proposed cash ban forces a payment workflow change for sellers who are often unbanked. A new waste-picker register format is coming. Five-year retention of ID copies on paper is also a POPIA exposure (*inference*).

**Existing solutions:**
- Global scrap-yard SaaS: ScrapRight, ReMatter, BuyScrap Software (from $99/month, listed on Capterra ZA), ScrapIT, RecyclerGuard, Scraplytics (claims African presence), Waste Logics weighbridge app. All are built around US and UK scrap laws. None was found modelling the SHGA register, SAPS inspection or the proposed waste-picker register.
- Paper register books and in-house weighbridge PCs.
- Bank and fintech payouts (instant EFT, cash-send vouchers) for the cashless rule, which are not tied to the register.

**Offline evidence:**
SAPS application forms come from the Designated Police Officer. The legal register is a "prescribed form" book. Enforcement happens through on-site inspections, not portal filings. No SA-specific SHGA software surfaced in Capterra ZA or in searches.

**Offline channel:**
- MRA (77 members, 150 facilities) and SEIFSA, both active in the Bill's comment process.
- Weighbridge and scale suppliers and technicians who already visit every yard.
- Walk-ins in industrial areas of Cape Town, Ekurhuleni and Durban, prioritised by press coverage of inspection campaigns.
- The SAPS Designated Firearms/SHG officers' compliance checklist as a sales script.

**Market count:**
SAPS says "in excess of 3,000" scrap dealers (unverified recency). Cape Town alone has >600 dealers and bucket shops. MRA has 77 members and 150 facilities.

**The gap:**
A cheap, phone-first register that captures ID, seller photo, load photo, weight and payment reference in one step. It would produce the SHGA register and waste-picker register in the prescribed format, pay electronically and record the payment reference, and print an "inspection pack" on demand. Today, global tools don't know the SA registers, and local yards use paper.

**Possible product:**
An Android tablet or phone app at the scale, built on ID-barcode scan (SA smart-ID and green-book barcode), photos and weight entry. It writes an SHGA-compliant register and pushes an electronic payout through a partner rail. It exports the 5-year archive, an inspection PDF and, if finalised, the ITAC monthly report.

**MVP:**
Transaction capture (ID scan, photos, weight, metal type), an SHGA s.21 register PDF and CSV, and a one-tap inspection pack. Payment-rail integration comes after the Bill's final text.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
R600–R1,500 per month per yard. Optionally R1–R3 per transaction once electronic payouts are included. Onboarding as a done-for-you service (loading existing customer IDs) for about R2,000.

**How to find first customers:**
The MRA member list, SAPS-certified facility lists (if obtainable), Google Maps scrapyard listings, weighbridge suppliers and the metro metal-theft-unit inspection areas.

**Willingness to pay:**
Software, if it prevents closure. Small yards will want it bundled with setup (service plus software). Yards that buy from waste pickers are cash-heavy and may resist transparency, the same "deliberate non-compliance" risk seen with security firms.

**Founder access:**
Needs a local, or at least a local partner. It means yard visits, Afrikaans, isiXhosa and isiZulu sellers, and SAPS relationships. A non-local solo founder would struggle.

**Risks:**
The Bill could stall or change, and the prescribed formats are not yet published. Informal yards may prefer cash. Weighbridge vendors could add an SHGA register cheaply. POPIA duties apply to ID images.

**Kill condition:**
If 10 yard owners say SAPS never asks for the register, or a local weighbridge vendor already ships SHGA registers and e-payouts, drop it.

**Score:** 6/10 (strong why-now and live enforcement. The Bill is not final, buyers resist transparency, and a local is needed)

### Opportunity: Borehole Completion Router (one drilling job → NGA geosite + municipal registration + client handover)

**Industry:**
Borehole drilling contractors, and the pump installers they work with.

**Buyer:**
Owner of a small drilling company (1–3 rigs) serving homeowners, farms and estates.

**Trigger / Why now:**
DWS draft groundwater regulations (gazetted Dec 2025 / Jan 2026, comments closed 1 Mar 2026) would require:
- a geosite identifier from the National Groundwater Archive (NGA) before drilling;
- drilling data submitted within 2 months of completion;
- only NGA-registered drillers managing a drilling operation;
- all borehole owners captured on the NGA.

DWS is reviewing the comments. In Jul 2026 DWS confirmed (via OUTA) that ordinary domestic users don't need to register their *water use*. That does not remove the driller data duty in the draft (*the interaction is unverified*). The NGA platform has already been extended to register drilling companies.

**Current workflow:**
1. Driller sites and drills, and fills in a paper driller's log (depths, strata, yield test).
2. In Cape Town, the owner or driller emails water@capetown.gov.za with erf number, account number and type, and the City sends a street sign. Other metros (e.g. Johannesburg) have their own steps.
3. Under the draft rules: request an NGA geosite ID before drilling, then upload drilling and geosite data to the NGA within 2 months.
4. The client gets an informal handover (yield, pump spec), and estate agents later want borehole documents at property transfer.

**Pain:**
80,000–100,000 boreholes a year against an archive of about 293,000 geosites shows how much goes unreported. New duties would land on drillers per job. Transfer attorneys ask for borehole compliance at sale (Van Deventers article).

**Existing solutions:**
- The DWS NGA platform itself (the receiving authority's portal).
- Paper or Excel driller logs.
- Hydrogeology consultants (siting, yield tests) who produce reports for large jobs.
- General field-service apps (Jobber-type tools, *not checked locally*). No driller-specific SA product was found.

**Offline evidence:**
No licensing regime for drillers today. Municipal registration is by email. Driller logs are on paper. The trade body (BWA) argues publicly for legal drilling, which suggests a non-compliant fringe.

**Offline channel:**
- The Borehole Water Association of Southern Africa (BWA, since 1980), its members and its training.
- Rig and pump suppliers.
- SADC-GMI driller training, which DWS ties to NGA registration.

**Market count:**
Drillers: unknown (*estimate*: low thousands nationally). Jobs: 80,000–100,000 boreholes a year (DWS, via press).

**The gap:**
No tool turns the driller's log once into: (a) the NGA geosite submission, (b) the right municipal registration (email or form per metro), and (c) a client and property-transfer "borehole file" PDF.

**Possible product:**
A mobile driller log (strata, depths, casing, yield test, GPS, photos). It requests and records the NGA geosite ID, produces the NGA data upload, routes the municipal notice by metro and issues a branded borehole certificate to the client.

**MVP:**
Driller log, client borehole certificate PDF and a Cape Town email template, plus an NGA-format export once the final regulations specify the fields.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
R50–R150 per borehole, or R400–R900 per month per rig.

**How to find first customers:**
BWA member directory, Google Maps "borehole drilling" listings per metro, and rig and pump suppliers' customer days.

**Willingness to pay:**
Software, if the final regulations make NGA submission mandatory and enforced. Otherwise only the client certificate has value (a marketing tool, weak).

**Founder access:**
Mostly remote-sellable through BWA, but a local helps with suppliers and Afrikaans-speaking farm drillers.

**Risks:**
The regulations stay draft or are softened. DWS may add a mobile capture app itself. Enforcement capacity at DWS is weak.

**Kill condition:**
Final regulations drop the driller submission duty, or are not gazetted by mid-2027.

**Score:** 5/10 (good per-job, multi-authority pattern. The trigger is still draft and the market size is unverified)

### Opportunity: Speculator Movement Pack (FMD-era paperwork for livestock agents and speculators)

**Industry:**
Livestock agents, speculators, backgrounders and small auction houses (cattle, sheep, goats).

**Buyer:**
An owner-operator speculator or agent who buys from many small and communal farmers and sells to feedlots, auctions and abattoirs.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- FMD was declared a national disaster in 2026.
- New national FMD control measures were gazetted on 8 Jul 2026 (GG 54972, Animal Diseases Act s.9). They consolidate earlier directives and require identification and traceability of vaccinated animals, health declarations before every movement and 28-day isolation.
- FMD protocols require feedlots, speculators and backgrounders to keep *auditable stock sheets* (arrivals, departures, mortalities). Movement permits must be applied for 5 working days ahead, with documents by category.
- KZN agents are "racing to have trading restrictions lifted".

**Current workflow:**
1. At each purchase, the speculator writes a Stock Theft Act s.6 document of identification (seller, buyer, animal particulars, date, ownership certification) and keeps it for 1 year or more.
2. The seller signs a paper health declaration.
3. Inside a disease management area: a permit application goes to the state vet with attachments.
4. Animals are isolated for 28 days, and a stock sheet is kept by hand or in Excel.
5. Optionally the movement is entered in the RMIS app or a herd system.

**Pain:**
Several paper documents per load, many small sellers (communal farmers without GLNs), audit exposure and lost trade when documents are missing. AAM Livestock Agents in KZN cut operations by 50%.

**Existing solutions:**
RMIS Traceability Platform and its free RMIS Movement App (GLN-based), Studbook SA IDTrax (May 2026), AIMS (aims.farm), AgriWebb (pushes movements to RMIS) and eight or more herd-management programmes linked to RMIS. Paper document-of-identification books (*unverified*: sold through farmers' co-ops and stationers).

**Offline evidence:**
RMIS says its free app exists because producers "are not yet using digital livestock management systems". Declarations and ID documents are paper. Permits are applied for at state vet offices.

**Offline channel:**
RPO (Red Meat Producers' Organisation) district meetings, auction-day presence, the agents' association, and state vet offices that publish protocols.

**Market count:**
Unknown for agents and speculators (*estimate*: 1,000–5,000). The vaccination target is about 14M cattle.

**The gap:**
Existing tools are farm-centric: one producer, one GLN. A speculator's day involves 10–40 small sellers, many without GLNs. Nobody generates the s.6 ID document, the health declaration and the stock sheet together from one load capture and then pushes to RMIS.

**Possible product:**
A load-capture app: sellers, ear tags or brands, photos and signatures. It prints or WhatsApps the s.6 document and health declaration, keeps the 28-day isolation stock sheet, assembles the permit application pack and posts movements to RMIS through its API.

**MVP:**
Offline-first Android app producing the s.6 document, health declaration and stock sheet. RMIS API integration in v2.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
R300–R800 per month per speculator, or R20 per load.

**How to find first customers:**
Auction-day visits, RPO structures, and agents listed by provincial veterinary services where permits are processed.

**Willingness to pay:**
Low to medium. Speculators are cash traders. The free RMIS app anchors price expectations, so the product may need to be sold as a done-for-you paperwork service at auctions.

**Founder access:**
Needs a local (Afrikaans and isiZulu, auction networks).

**Risks:**
RMIS or Studbook adds multi-seller load capture. Government mandates its own LITS system (Parliament briefings, 2026). FMD restrictions ease and urgency drops.

**Kill condition:**
The RMIS Movement App already supports multi-seller loads and prints s.6 documents, or speculators say the state vet only accepts its own forms.

**Score:** 4/10 (real 2026 trigger and per-load frequency. Free incumbent, low WTP, and the buyer count is unverified)

### Opportunity (weak): Hunt Paperwork Pack for outfitters

**Industry:**
Hunting outfitters and professional hunters (PHs).

**Buyer:**
Owner-outfitter running 10–60 foreign clients a season.

**Trigger / Why now:**
None found for 2025–26. This is the weakest part of the case.

**Current workflow:**
1. Before the hunt: the outfitter checks the farm's provincial permits and applies for provincial hunting permits and TOPS/CITES permits.
2. During the hunt: a paper PH Register is signed by the client, the PH and the outfitter.
3. After the hunt: the PH Register plus permits support the export-permit application. The outfitter files provincial hunt returns, which count toward PH and outfitter permit renewal (minimum returns differ by province).

**Pain:**
Errors on registers delay trophy exports, and "PH register must always be signed by all relevant parties" is a recurring warning from PHASA.

**Existing solutions:**
Paper PH Register books, trophy shippers, taxidermists and permit agents, and US outfitter booking software (*not verified* for SA permits).

**Offline evidence:**
Signed paper registers and provincial returns. DFFE statistics are compiled from provincial submissions.

**Offline channel:**
PHASA conventions, provincial PH exam and training schools, taxidermists and dip-and-pack firms.

**Market count:**
2,786 registered PHs (2022). 7,756 foreign clients (2024). Outfitter count unknown.

**The gap:**
One hunt record that produces the PH Register, each province's return format and the export-permit bundle.

**Possible product:**
A per-hunt digital register with e-signatures that outputs the provincial return and the export dossier.

**MVP:**
A digital PH Register and an export dossier PDF for one province (Limpopo).

**Pricing hypothesis:**
R150–R300 per hunt, or R6,000–R12,000 per season.

**How to find first customers:**
The PHASA member directory and provincial outfitter permit lists.

**Willingness to pay:**
Software or service. Outfitters earn foreign currency and spend per client, but volume is low and falling.

**Founder access:**
Remote-sellable through PHASA, but a provincial-form specialist is needed.

**Risks:**
Provinces may refuse non-original registers. Volumes are shrinking. There is reputational risk around trophy hunting.

**Kill condition:**
Provinces require original paper registers with wet signatures and won't accept printed or e-signed ones.

**Score:** 3/10

## 4. Method notes

- What worked: searching the **legislation text** (acts.co.za section pages for SHGA s.21 and Stock Theft s.6), **municipal enforcement press releases** (Cape Town Metal Theft Unit), **Cabinet and gazette news** (SHGA Bill 2026, FMD GG 54972, DWS draft groundwater regulations) and **parliamentary replies** (PMG) for counts.
- English was enough. Afrikaans queries weren't needed for regulator material, but Afrikaans would matter for interviews (livestock, drilling).
- What didn't work: registry counts. SAPS publishes no dealer register, and driller, speculator and outfitter totals were not findable without WebFetch. Capterra ZA listings mix global tools with local ones, so "listed in ZA" does not mean SA-compliant.
- Follow-up worth doing: get the final text of the SHGA Amendment Bill (prescribed register format), the status of the ITAC monthly scrap report, and the final DWS groundwater regulations.
