# South Sudan: offline / quiet-industries pass

Date: 2026-10-05. Search budget: 8 WebSearch calls, all used. WebFetch was not used.
Existing country report: `research/countries/south-sudan.md`. Its opportunities, the NGO RRC/county compliance pack and SSRA PAYE filing, are not repeated here.

**Bottom line:** South Sudan is a mostly inaccessible market for this pass. Most quiet industries are regulated, but enforcement is done in person by police and county officials, payment is in cash, and money is often collected informally. Operators (boda-boda riders, butchers, cattle traders, forex dealers) cannot pay for foreign software. The only *new, recurring, written* obligation with a credible trigger is the **National Social Insurance Fund (NSIF) rollout**. Employer registration opened on **1 October 2026**, with a three-month grace period, and monthly remittance is due by the 15th. Even that is a weak opportunity, because the paying buyers are mostly the same INGOs and foreign firms covered in the existing report.

## 2. Opportunities

### Opportunity: NSIF Contribution Schedule Builder (employer onboarding + monthly schedule)

**Industry:**
Formal employers: INGOs and national NGOs, oil-sector service contractors, construction contractors, hotels, private schools and clinics, faith-based employers.

**Buyer:**
HR/Admin Manager or Finance Officer at an employer with roughly 20–500 staff in South Sudan. Their outsourced accountant is the secondary buyer.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The National Social Insurance Fund Act 2023 created the NSIF.
- **June 2026:** the Ministry of Labour revoked a 2010 directive and ordered employers to remit directly to the NSIF. The order applies to private employers, NGOs, UN agencies and diplomatic missions.
- **1 October 2026:** the NSIF ordered employers to start registering workers. A three-month grace period runs to about January 2027, after which late-registration penalties apply under the Act.
- Ministry of Labour Circular No. 5/2026 is contested in an opinion piece. Unions, including oil workers, have asked for suspension or delay. MPs have warned that workers could lose benefits.
- The contribution split is reported inconsistently: one source says 17% employer / 8% employee, another says 8% employer / 17% employee. This confusion is itself evidence that the rules are not settled.

**Current workflow:** *[inferred; the NSIF form format was not verified]*
1. HR exports the staff list and gross pay from the payroll tool (Excel, Sage, or the HQ ERP).
2. HR collects employee identity details for registration and registers each employee with the NSIF within 30 days of hiring *(unofficial site)*.
3. Each month, finance computes the employee and employer contributions and fills in a contribution schedule, which can be "digital or physical" *(unofficial site)*.
4. Finance pays by the 15th of the following month. Separately, it files PAYE on SSRA eTax by the same day (existing report).
5. The previous pension/provident arrangement under the 2010 directive has to be wound down or reconciled, so there is a transition reconciliation.

**Pain:**
- New monthly deadline with stated penalties: 5% per month on unpaid amounts, interest at 2% above bank rate, and possible revocation of the certificate of good standing *(source is an NSIF-branded site on netlify.app, unverified as official)*.
- Rates and coverage are contested, so employers face rule churn.
- Hiring and turnover create continuous registration work, because INGO staffing is project-based.

**Existing solutions:**
- HQ payroll/ERP systems used by INGOs and oil firms, which can add a deduction line.
- Local accounting and audit firms in Juba, which will add NSIF filing to their PAYE service.
- Excel templates.
- Any NSIF portal or form (status unverified; no official portal found).

**Offline evidence:** The fund is new and has no confirmed e-portal. Its own guidance allows physical schedules. Enforcement and disputes play out through radio and union statements, not online forums. No software vendor lists NSIF support (none found).

**Offline channel:** The South Sudan NGO Forum (member mailing and HR working groups). The South Sudan Chamber of Commerce. Juba accounting firms, who would act as resellers. NSIF's own employer sensitisation sessions during the grace period. WhatsApp outreach to INGO HR managers whose job ads are public.

**Market count:** About 150 INGOs (existing country report), plus an unknown number of national NGOs, oil contractors and formal SMEs. **No register count was found (estimate: low hundreds of employers able to pay foreign SaaS).**

**The gap:**
- Payroll tools compute deductions but will not produce the NSIF-specific registration and monthly schedule format.
- Payroll tools will not track the 30-day registration window per new hire.
- Payroll tools will not reconcile the old 2010-directive arrangement against NSIF.
- The existing report's PAYE gap sits on the same payroll run, so one payroll export could produce both the SSRA PAYE return and the NSIF schedule.

**Possible product:**
A tool that takes one payroll export each month and produces three things: the SSRA PAYE return (with local-employee exemption logic) and the NSIF contribution schedule, a new-hire registration checklist with 30-day deadline alerts, and a penalty calculator.

**MVP:**
Excel upload → NSIF schedule (format copied from a real form obtained in Juba) + PAYE workings PDF + deadline email/WhatsApp reminders. Configurable rates, because the split is disputed.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
USD 50–150 per month per employer. Alternatively, a white-label licence to accounting firms at USD 200–400 per month. INGOs pay from HQ cards or invoices. Local SMEs would only pay for a done-for-you accountant service, not for software.

**How to find first customers:**
NGO Forum member list, INGO job postings (HR roles), Chamber of Commerce, Juba audit firms.

**Risks:**
- The NSIF rollout may be suspended or delayed after union pressure.
- The NSIF may publish its own portal and template, which would remove the gap.
- Rates and rules may change.
- Buyers are few, and INGO funding is shrinking.
- HQ payroll vendors can add a line item.
- Sanctions screening is needed, because some officials are designated (existing report).

**Founder access:** A non-local solo founder could sell to INGO HR remotely through the NGO Forum, but would need a Juba contact to get the actual NSIF form and keep up with circulars. Local SMEs need a local partner, in practice an accounting firm.

**Kill condition:**
Kill the idea if any of these turns out to be true:
- The NSIF publishes a free online schedule upload that accepts payroll CSVs.
- The rollout is suspended past mid-2027.
- Fewer than 5 of 20 INGO HR managers say they lack a way to produce the schedule.

**Score:** 4/10
(Pain 5, Frequency 7, Mandatory 7, Fragmentation 3, Competition 5, Incumbent gap 5, Buyer access 5, WTP 4, MVP 8, Distribution 4. The score is pulled down by the tiny paying market and the unstable rollout.)

### Opportunity (tracked, not recommended): Licensed Forex Dealer Rate & Transaction Log

**Industry:**
Informal and newly licensed foreign-exchange dealers and bureaus, mainly in Juba.

**Buyer:**
The dealer association's secretary, or the bureau owner.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- The BoSS ban on unlicensed FX trade took effect on 1 April 2024, after a 45-day grace period.
- BoSS renewed its directive to license informal dealers in February 2026.
- Dealers must form umbrella associations, operate from fixed premises, register with BoSS and pay taxes.
- Dealers must display buying and selling rates and keep a tight spread against the BoSS reference rate.
- The reform is part of the World Bank-funded financial sector modernisation plan, which includes AML aims.

**Current workflow:** *[inferred]*
1. The dealer checks the BoSS reference rate by phone or radio.
2. The dealer writes the rates on a board.
3. Cash trades are done with no record, or noted in an exercise book.
4. Association dues and licence fees are paid in person.

**Pain:**
- Police and BoSS crackdowns on unlicensed dealers.
- Spread limits.
- AML expectations under the 2012 AML Act, but no specific reporting format was found.

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper notebooks and rate boards.
- Licensed bank FX desks.
- Mobile money agents (unverified).

**Offline evidence:** Dealers literally operate "under trees". Licensing and enforcement happen in person through BoSS and the police.

**Offline channel:** The dealer associations BoSS ordered dealers to form, and the BoSS licensing desk.

**Market count:** Unknown. No public BoSS list of licensed bureaus was retrieved.

**The gap:**
- A simple rate-spread compliance and transaction log, if BoSS starts demanding periodic returns. This is unconfirmed.

**Possible product:** A mobile app that logs each trade and the BoSS reference rate, checks that the spread is within the limit, and exports a monthly return.

**MVP:** An Android app with an offline ledger and a PDF export.

**Pricing hypothesis:** USD 5–15 per month per dealer, collected through the association. This is doubtful.

**How to find first customers:** The dealer associations.

**Risks:**
- No confirmed reporting duty.
- Very low willingness to pay.
- The work needs a local seller.
- Regulatory reversals.
- An AML tool for unbanked cash dealers carries reputational and sanctions risk.

**Founder access:** Needs a local seller.

**Kill condition:** BoSS imposes no written return requirement on dealers. That is the current state, so the idea is effectively killed unless that changes.

**Score:** 2/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Boda-boda licensing compliance:** the enforcement is roadside impoundment and cash fines (SSP 1,500 for a missing number plate, according to the Minister of Interior's order). Riders cannot pay, and county associations are being dissolved. Sources: [Sudans Post: NBeG impounds 100+ motorcycles](https://www.sudanspost.com/traffic-police-impound-over-100-motorcycles-in-northern-bahr-el-ghazal/), [Radio Yei: Morobo crackdown](https://radioyei.org/news/business/autos/police-launch-crackdown-on-unlicensed-motorcycles-in-morobo-county/), [Sudans Post: Juba County suspends illegal boda associations](https://www.sudanspost.com/juba-county-orders-suspension-of-illegal-boda-boda-associations/), [Sudans Post: Twic licensing drive](https://www.sudanspost.com/twic-motorists-on-notice-as-traffic-police-enforce-licensing-drive/).
- **Cross-border livestock permits:** the government vet issues a paper health certificate and permit per 5 head (USD 120), with a 14-day quarantine. There is no third-party layer and the buyers pay in cash. Source: [Eye Radio](https://www.eyeradio.org/?p=82467).
- **Butchers and market-trader charge ledgers:** the pain is overlapping and extra-legal collection by several bodies. That is a political problem, not a software one. Sources: [Eye Radio: Butchers Union threatens strike](https://www.eyeradio.org/butchers-union-threatens-to-strike-as-govt-unwilling-to-address-land-taxation-concerns/), [Eye Radio: Juba traders blame multiple charges](https://www.eyeradio.org/juba-traders-blame-multiple-charges-for-price-hikes-chamber-of-commerce-foresees-relief/), [VOA: Illegal checkpoints](https://voaafrica.com/a/6972628.html).
- **Scrap metal and charcoal export warrants:** rare, per-shipment ministerial warrants, with a local scrap ban in Torit. Sources: [Environmental Peacebuilding](https://www.environmentalpeacebuilding.org/news/show/ba84b5308b23), [Africa-Press: Torit mayor bans scrap business](https://www.africa-press.net/south-sudan/?p=42045).
- **Household employers / domestic workers under NSIF:** households will not register or pay for software.

## 4. Method notes

- **What worked:** local radio and news sites (Eye Radio, Radio Tamazuj, Sudans Post, Radio Yei) are effectively South Sudan's official gazette. Directives, circulars and crackdowns surface there, not on ministry websites.
- **What didn't:** registers and official forms. There is no public register of licensed dealers, butchers or bureaus, and no NSIF form PDF was found. English-language queries were enough; Arabic and local-language queries were not tried within the budget.
- **Market counts:** none could be sourced from a register.
- **The NSIF "employers" page** is on netlify.app, so its official status is unverified. The penalty and schedule details from it need confirmation from someone in Juba.
