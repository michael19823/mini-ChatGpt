# Spain: offline-industries pass (2026-10-05)

Scope: quiet, regulator-facing industries only. The opportunities in `research/countries/spain.md` (tourist tax, funeral expediente router, phytosanitary contractors, beneficial owner, pay transparency) are not repeated here. I ran about 31 WebSearch calls and stopped early because the search tool hit its usage limit. Some competitor checks therefore could not be finished, and the affected claims are marked "unverified".

Bottom line: most quiet industries in Spain are already covered. Either the receiving authority gives away a free app (INSST Prevención10, regional digital hunting tags, RETO, PescaREC, the Andalusian beekeeping Excel book), or a small vertical vendor already exists (ORONET for gold buyers; piscinae, SenPool and SILOE Autocontrol for pools; a3 XAGROS for RETO; Familia Fácil at EUR 8/month for domestic payslips). One strong lead remains: **electronic water-volume reporting under Orden TED/1191/2024**. Its second-category deadline is 1 Nov 2026 and its first-category deadline is 1 Nov 2027. Reporting is split across about a dozen basin authorities, and the buyers are farmers and irrigation communities, who are about as offline as buyers get.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Basin-authority volume reporter for small water-right holders (Orden TED/1191/2024)

**Industry:**  
Agriculture and other private water users: farm wells, livestock farms, golf courses, rural hotels, small municipal or private supplies, and the irrigation communities that hold concessions for their members.

**Buyer:**  
Owners of second-category rights (20,000 to 500,000 m³/year authorised), which in practice means medium farms and agro-businesses. Also secretaries of small irrigation communities. Secondary buyers: the service firms that run irrigation communities, and farmers' union offices (ASAJA, COAG, UPA) that already file for members.

**Trigger / Why now:**  
Orden TED/1191/2024 (in force since 1 Nov 2024) replaces ARM/1312/2009. Holders must record meter readings electronically, at least daily, and report to the basin authority. Deadlines: **second category by 1 Nov 2026**, first category (<20,000 m³) by 1 Nov 2027. Second-category holders must report daily volumes and the quarterly total within the month after each quarter. A separate deadline required pre-2013 meters to be replaced by 8 Oct 2025. Sanctions are under political discussion: in Aug 2026 the CHD president promised ASAJA Ávila to review groundwater sanctions, some of them above EUR 60,000.

**Current workflow:**  
1. The farmer reads the meter by hand, or the meter's datalogger stores readings.  
2. Readings are written into the paper control book, or a spreadsheet kept by the farmer, a cooperative or the union office.  
3. Once a year (old regime; e.g. CHD by 31 Jan), and quarterly for cat. 2 under the new regime, someone logs into the basin authority's e-office with a digital certificate. They type in volumes or upload the authority's own exchange file (Júcar requires "ficheros electrónicos de intercambio normalizados").  
4. Each basin authority has its own technical spec and channel: CHS SICA, CHJ resolution of 9 Nov 2023, CHD website, plus the intra-regional authorities (ACA Catalonia, Andalusia, Augas de Galicia, URA). Exceptions such as meter swaps, faults, gaps or volumes over the concession are handled by phone or letter.

**Pain:**  
Fines for wrong or missing data (ASAJA Ávila complaint, 2025-2026). FENACORE says 85% of irrigation communities cannot meet electronic-administration duties. ASAJA Palencia had to publish reminders that filing is "exclusively through the CHD website". The volume of filings rises from one per year (or none) to four per year, with daily granularity.

**Existing solutions:**  
- The basin authorities' own e-offices and exchange-file formats (free, but each one differs and all need a digital certificate).  
- Meter and telemetry vendors (Contazara, Hidroconta, Regaber and others): hardware and loggers. Whether their clouds file directly with each basin authority is **unverified**.  
- Hydrology data platforms (KISTERS WISKI, promoted on iagua for TED/1191/2024) aimed at large irrigation communities and utilities.  
- Telemetry and service firms (CoReSat blog on volume reporting; Eurofins environment notes), plus consultants, union offices and community service firms that do the filing by hand.  
- Irrigation-community management software (billing, member roll) exists in the market, but I could not name or check vendors before the search budget ran out (**unverified**).

**Offline evidence:**  
Paper control books are the legacy legal format. Farmers' unions publish filing reminders as print-style notices. FENACORE reports widespread inability to deal with the authorities electronically. No SaaS review listings for "volume reporting to the basin authority" turned up in my searches.

**Offline channel:**  
ASAJA, COAG and UPA provincial offices, which already hold members' certificates and file the PAC (CAP subsidy) and water returns. FENACORE and regional irrigation federations (congresses, newsletters). Meter installers and approved verification bodies, which visit every site when meters are replaced (deadline Oct 2025, then new loggers for 2026-2027). Agricultural cooperatives' technical offices.

**Market count:**  
FENACORE: >400 member communities, >700,000 irrigators. Registered rights: >80,000 in the Duero basin alone, plus 20,000 registered and 40,000 pending wells in the Ebro (agronewscastillayleon/iagua). The share in category 2 is unknown. A guess of 30,000 to 80,000 obliged second-category holders nationally is an **estimate**.

**The gap:**  
Meters and loggers capture the data, and the basin authorities accept it, but nothing in between turns a generic logger export or a manual photo reading into each authority's exact quarterly file. Nothing flags gaps, meter changes or over-concession volumes before filing, or keeps an evidence trail for inspection, at a price a farmer or union office will pay. This is the router pattern of the Florida grease benchmark: one reading, a dozen receiving bodies.

**Possible product:**  
"Contador → Confederación": a filing layer for union offices and community secretaries. Readings come in from a logger CSV, a vendor API or a WhatsApp photo of the meter dial. The tool validates them against the concession volume, then produces the authority-specific exchange file or a pre-filled e-office submission, plus a digital control book.

**MVP:**  
One basin authority (Júcar or Segura, which publish detailed technical specs and have intense water scarcity) and one buyer type (an ASAJA/COAG provincial office filing for 50 to 200 members). Features: CSV or photo import, daily series, quarterly file generation, deadline calendar, anomaly flags.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
EUR 5-10 per holder per month billed to the union office or community, which bundles it into membership. Or EUR 60-120 per holder per year direct. Union offices already charge members for paperwork, so done-for-you service (they file) plus software (we prepare) is the realistic model. Pure self-serve to farmers would sell poorly.

**How to find first customers:**  
Provincial ASAJA, COAG and UPA offices in Valencia, Murcia, Albacete and the Duero provinces. Members of FENACORE's regional federations. Meter installers listed by the basin authorities and MITECO's approved-meter pages, as resellers.

**Risks:**  
A basin authority may build free direct logger ingestion, or MITECO may standardise on one national file format. Meter vendors may bundle filing into their cloud. Each authority's spec changes. Certificate and representation (apoderamiento) handling is needed for filing on behalf of others. Deadlines tend to slip in Spain (watch for an extension order). Selling to farmers is seasonal and relationship-driven.

**Kill condition:**  
Two or three major meter or telemetry vendors already file automatically with each authority at no extra cost. Or the basin authorities extend the cat. 2 deadline indefinitely. Or union offices say they file with a simple web form in minutes.

**Willingness to pay / founder access:**  
Union offices and communities pay for service. Farmers pay little for software. A non-local solo founder would struggle: this needs Spanish, a presence at agricultural fairs (FIMA, Agroexpo) and visits to union offices. Realistic only with a local partner, such as an agronomist or a community service firm.

**Score:** 6/10 (pain 7, frequency 6 (quarterly), mandatory 9, fragmentation 8, competition 6 (partly unverified), incumbent gap 6, buyer access 7, WTP 5, MVP 7, distribution 6)

### Opportunity: Breeder and núcleo zoológico register book for the Ley 7/2023 Central Registry (watch)

**Industry:**  
Dog and cat breeders, kennels and boarding, pet shops, shelters.

**Buyer:**  
Owner-operators of registered núcleos zoológicos and registered breeders, including breed-club breeders affiliated with RSCE (the Spanish canine society).

**Trigger / Why now:**  
Ley 7/2023 creates a Central Registry System for Animal Protection with minimum data for breeders, núcleos zoológicos and behaviour professionals. Draft royal decrees were notified to the EU TRIS system in 2025. The ministry answered RSCE's objections in Oct 2025. Press in Jul 2026 says the government is speeding up the four implementing RDs to finish before 2027. Nothing is enforceable yet.

**Current workflow:**  
1. The breeder registers with the regional núcleo-zoológico register (paper or regional e-office).  
2. Births, sales, transfers and deaths go into a paper entry/exit book. Microchip transfers go through the vet and the regional pet registry.  
3. Inspections check the book. Under the draft rules, data will also have to reach the interoperable central registry.

**Pain:**  
Expected rather than proven: rules differ by region, and the national layer adds a second registry. Pain evidence today is limited to RSCE's formal objections.

**Existing solutions:**  
Regional pet registries (accessed by vets), vet clinic software (QVET and others) for microchip records, breed-club pedigree systems, paper books from the regional authority. No breeder-specific Spanish compliance tool found (my search was shallow).

**Offline evidence:**  
Paper entry/exit books. A hobbyist and small-business population. Vets act as the gatekeepers to the registries.

**Offline channel:**  
RSCE and breed clubs, dog shows, veterinary clinics (they chip every litter), pet-feed distributors.

**Market count:**  
Unknown. Regional núcleo-zoológico registers are public in some regions. No national count found (**unverified**).

**The gap:**  
Possibly a single litter and transfer book that produces regional and central-registry data without re-keying. This depends entirely on what the final RD requires.

**Possible product:**  
A digital litter and transfer register with vet hand-off and a pre-filled export for the regional or central registry.

**MVP:**  
Wait until the RD text is final. Then build a digital entry/exit book for one region plus an inspection-ready PDF.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
EUR 10-20/month. Hobby breeders will pay little.

**How to find first customers:**  
RSCE breed clubs, dog-show exhibitor lists, regional núcleo-zoológico registers.

**Risks:**  
The RD may slip past the 2027 election. The government registry may be vet-operated only, leaving breeders nothing to file. Low willingness to pay. The topic is politically contested.

**Kill condition:**  
The final RD puts all reporting through vets and the regional pet registries.

**Willingness to pay / founder access:**  
Software only, low. A non-local founder could sell through clubs, but Spanish is required.

**Score:** 3/10 (watch only)

## 3. Rejected

- **Domestic-worker employers (RD 893/2024 risk assessment):** INSST Prevención10 is free and official. Payslip services cost EUR 8-15. Not enough money left.
- **Gold buyers and jewellers (police book, 115B, AML):** ORONET by DigiNet is an entrenched vertical vendor.
- **Second-hand and phone-repair shops (Ertzaintza 24 h reporting):** one region, with a government e-portal. Elsewhere the police book is paper with no electronic filing.
- **Scrap dealers:** waste traceability is covered by e-SIR tools. The police book has no digital receiving end.
- **Beekeepers:** free regional books, mostly hobbyists.
- **RETO phytosanitary sales:** a3ERP XAGROS-RETO, a3fitosanitarios and Agroinventario already automate it.
- **Hunting estates:** regions give away digital-tag apps (Andalusia, CLM, CyL, Aragón). The catch report is annual.
- **Pools (SILOE):** piscinae, SenPool, SILOE Autocontrol and GOPool exist. The upload is annual.
- **Small-scale fishing e-logbook:** state apps, plus cofradía and fish-market vendors (GrupoCIE, Sinerxia).
- **Live-animal transport:** the new EU regulation is not adopted. TRACES and movement guides are run by vets.

## 4. Method notes

What worked: searching Spanish regulator vocabulary such as "libro registro", "comunicación", "plazo", the order or RD number, "sede electrónica" and "confederación". Farm-press sites (agronewscastillayleon, iagua) were the best "insider pain" source. Searching "software + <obligation>" in Spanish found the incumbents quickly (ORONET, XAGROS-RETO, piscinae). What didn't work: English queries; generic searches for software that integrates with police books; searches for protests in the fishing sector. The session hit the search usage limit at about 31 queries, so competitor diligence on irrigation-community software and on meter vendors' filing features is still open. That should be the first check before any customer interviews.
