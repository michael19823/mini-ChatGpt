# Sudan: Offline-Industries Pass

**Status: inaccessible market. This is a short report and recommends no opportunity.**
Researched 2026-10-05. Searches used: 4 of 8. I stopped early on purpose: the country report's accessibility verdict applies to quiet industries even more strongly than to digital ones (see `research/countries/sudan.md`).

The country report found that payment rails, internet access and buyer stability are broken for a foreign solo founder. I found the same gate here. Quiet industries in Sudan are cash-based, work through brokers and are often controlled by the armed parties. The regulators that would create a filing obligation (the Ministry of Animal Resources, the Sudanese Mineral Resources Company, the Central Bank) operate from Port Sudan. They are tightening controls through committees and decrees, not through registers that a third party could plug into. One further point is specific to this pass. In several of these sectors, gold and gum arabic in particular, the trade is entangled with war financing. That adds a sanctions and reputational risk on top of the access problem.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

**None.** No quiet industry in Sudan clears the brief's final decision rule. Three items fail it for any foreign solo founder: identifiable buyers (no public registers), plausible willingness to pay (no working way to collect SaaS fees), and a clear distribution channel (associations and chambers have been displaced to Port Sudan or abroad). I'm leaving the Opportunity template empty rather than scoring an idea I wouldn't recommend.

The nearest candidate, kept on a watch-list only, is a **livestock export shipment file**. It would hold the per-consignment veterinary certificate, quarantine record and importer protocol documents for live sheep going from Suakin to Saudi Arabia. If scored now it would get about 2/10. The trigger exists (exports resumed in 2026 after the Saudi protocol review, after earlier rejections that killed 3,000 sheep). But the workflow sits inside the ministry, the exporters are unlisted, and a foreign founder couldn't reach or bill them. Revisit after a ceasefire, possibly as an add-on to a Horn of Africa or Gulf-importer product rather than something sold to Sudanese exporters.

## 3. Rejected

- **Livestock export documentation:** the state runs the protocol and quarantine; there is no exporter register; payment rails don't work. The substitute is the ministry plus brokers.
- **Gold trader documentation and transit paperwork:** state enforcement (an SMRC and Central Bank joint committee, a planned criminal law). The commodity finances the war, so sanctions and reputational risk are high.
- **Gum arabic traceability:** the chain is opaque because armed groups control it, not because the paperwork is badly tooled. A software fix doesn't apply.
- **Exchange bureau daily reporting:** goes to the regulator's own e-form; the parallel market dominates; regulated finance needs a local licensed partner.
- **Households as employers, scrap and second-hand dealers, transport, street trade:** no enforced recurring obligation, and the base is displaced or cash-only.

## 4. Method notes

- English news queries about the regulator ("Ministry of Animal Resources", "SMRC", "Central Bank") worked. Sudan Tribune and Dabanga carry most regulatory news, now issued from Port Sudan.
- The Arabic CBoS query returned the 2022 exchange-company regulation from cbos.gov.sd. It also returned PDFs from a `cbos01jo2022.dev2.dot.jo` domain that appears to be a Jordanian development site, not Sudan's central bank. The "daily e-form within two hours of close" rule came from that mixed result set, so its attribution to Sudan is unverified.
- Eye Radio results on gold and gum arabic rules are South Sudanese. I excluded them.
- I didn't spend the remaining 4 searches. More searching wouldn't change the accessibility verdict.
