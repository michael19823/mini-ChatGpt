# Tanzania: Offline-Industries Pass

Date: 2026-10-05. Budget: 20 WebSearch calls, all used. The existing report (`research/countries/tanzania.md`) already covers mining local content, safari permits, input VAT/IDRAS, NHIF claims and Tier-2 microfinance, so none of those are repeated here.

**Summary:** Tanzania's quiet industries are mostly reached by **government-built systems**, not by software vendors. AMCOS cooperatives use MUVU, fertiliser agro-dealers use TFRA's subsidy system, livestock uses the state ear-tagging campaign (NLITS), honey uses the MNRT traceability system, and clearing agents use TANCIS. The only regulated, recurring, paper-heavy register that has a countable operator base and no state system yet covering it is the one kept by **mineral brokers and dealers**. Even there, the 44 state mineral markets already record each transaction, so the opportunity is narrow.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Mineral Broker and Dealer Register and Returns Kit (monthly / quarterly Mining Commission returns plus 20% gold set-aside ledger)

**Industry:**
Licensed mineral trading: gold and gemstone brokers at buying stations, and dealers at Mineral and Gem Houses (Geita, Chunya, Kahama, Mererani/Arusha and others).

**Buyer:**
Owner-operator of a licensed dealer business, plus mid-sized brokers. Dealers who export, or who supply refineries under the 20% rule, are the best payers.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- Since 1 Oct 2024, under the Mining (Minerals and Mineral Concentrates Trading) (Amendment) Regulations, dealers must reserve at least 20% of the gold they obtain for domestic refineries or BoT. That creates a new ledger and a new reconciliation.
- Recent amendments moved brokers from "submit on licence expiry" to **quarterly** reports.
- BoT targets 6 t/year of domestic gold purchases.
- A May 2026 Mining Commission digital tax system covers withholding tax and PAYE, which shows the Commission is digitising, but no dealer-returns e-service was found.

**Current workflow (inferred from the regulations and legal guides; confirm in interviews):**
1. The broker buys from PML miners at a buying station, pays cash, and writes the purchase into a register book with seller and licence number, weight, purity and price.
2. The broker sells to a dealer at the mineral market. Royalty (6%) and inspection fee (1%) are assessed there, and the market records the transaction.
3. The dealer keeps a precious-minerals register per mineral and compiles monthly records for the Mining Commission **by the 3rd of each month**. Brokers compile quarterly.
4. The dealer tracks how much gold is set aside for refineries or BoT against the 20% minimum, and keeps export permit and valuation papers.
5. The dealer's accountant then separately reconciles against TRA (withholding tax, VAT/EFD) and the licence renewal every 12 months.

**Pain:**
- The register and its returns are mandatory, with penalties from TZS 200,000 to TZS 50m depending on the breach.
- Licences are annual (12-month validity), so the register history affects renewal.
- The 20% set-aside adds a quota that must be proven.
- No direct complaint evidence was found. Pain is inferred from the regulation, not observed.

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper register books and Excel.
- Mineral-market (Masoko ya Madini) records kept by the Commission's regional officers.
- OMCTP mining cadastre portal (licensing and payments, not transaction returns).
- Generic accounting software (QuickBooks/Tally) run by accountants.
- Law and consulting firms (Rive & Co, CM Advocates, Zatra, Eden Law) that handle licensing, not monthly returns.
- No dealer-specific software was found in one competitor search.

**Offline evidence:**
- The register format is fixed in the 1999 regulations.
- Trade happens in cash at physical markets.
- The Geita gold exchange was reported as "initially conducted manually".
- No software listings or forums were found. The only Swahili discussion is JamiiForums "how to start a minerals business" threads.

**Offline channel:**
- Visit one mineral market in person (Geita or Chunya). Dealers sit in the same building, and the Commission's resident mining officer is there.
- Work through FEMATA (Federation of Miners' Associations of Tanzania) and the regional miners' associations (REMAs). They exist, but their reach to dealers is unverified.
- Use accountants who serve dealers in Geita and Mwanza.
- Do WhatsApp outreach from the Commission's published licence lists, if they are available (unverified).

**Market count:**
- 548 dealer licences and 1,290 broker licences were issued Jul 2024–Mar 2025 (Mining Commission figures via TanzaniaInvest).
- Licences last 12 months, so the active base is plausibly about 700 dealers and 1,700 brokers (estimate).
- There are 44 mineral markets and 120 buying centres.

**The gap:**
Nothing turns the per-transaction register into the Commission's monthly or quarterly return and proves the 20% set-aside, while also giving the accountant TRA-ready numbers. Today the market records, the dealer's book and the accountant's ledger are three separate copies.

**Possible product:**
A Swahili/English mobile-first register. The broker or dealer logs each purchase and sale (photo of the market receipt, weight, purity, counterparty licence). The app produces the monthly/quarterly return in the Commission's format, a 20% set-aside tracker, and an accountant export.

**MVP:**
- A WhatsApp or Android form for the register.
- A monthly PDF/Excel return in the regulation's format.
- A set-aside dashboard.
- Sold together with a **done-for-you monthly filing service** delivered through a local accountant.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- Dealers: TZS 100–250k/month (about US$40–100) including filing.
- Brokers: TZS 20–40k/month.
- Willingness to pay is likely for the **service**, not for software alone. Unverified.

**How to find first customers:**
- Walk the Geita and Chunya mineral markets.
- Get introductions through FEMATA or REMAs and through the resident mining officers.
- Contact accountants serving dealers.

**Risks:**
- The Mining Commission could add a returns module to its markets system or OMCTP, which would remove the gap. Its digital push in 2026 makes this plausible.
- Dealers in a cash business may not want a digital trail.
- AML / reputational exposure.
- **Founder access:** a non-local solo founder cannot sell this. It needs a Swahili-speaking local partner on the ground in Geita or Mwanza, plus trust.

**Kill condition:**
- Interviews show the mineral market's own records already satisfy the monthly return, so dealers file nothing extra.
- Or the Commission announces an online dealer-returns e-service.

**Score:** 4.5/10. The obligation is real and recurring and the base is countable, but the gap may already be closed by the state markets, and willingness to pay and founder access are poor.

### Opportunity: Borehole Driller Completion-Report and Permit Pack (basin water boards)

**Industry:**
Water-well drilling contractors.

**Buyer:**
Owner of a small drilling firm (1–3 rigs) doing private, NGO and LGA boreholes.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- No 2025–26 regulatory trigger was found.
- Demand is steady from NGO and LGA tenders (for example a Plan International Lake Zone FY26 tender, and an EACOP EOI for drilling, Jun 2026), and these buyers want documented completion reports.

**Current workflow:**
1. Hydrogeological survey.
2. Drilling permit from the basin water board.
3. Drilling, keeping a log and a pumping test.
4. Water-quality test.
5. Completion report and borehole registration (TZS 100,000 per borehole under one rules document; the attribution to Tanzania is not fully verified).
6. A separate report to the client or NGO.

**Pain:**
The same well data goes to the basin board, the client (an NGO or LGA with its own format) and the water-use permit file. No complaint evidence was found.

**Existing solutions:**
- Word/Excel templates.
- Hydrogeology consultants.
- Generic borehole-log software (not checked in this pass).

**Offline evidence:**
Paper permits and reports at the basin offices. No software listings for Tanzania.

**Offline channel:**
- Basin water board offices: the list of registered drillers is held there.
- NGO WASH tender lists.
- The Drilling and Dam Construction Agency (DDCA) ecosystem. Unverified as a channel.

**Market count:**
Not found (estimate: low hundreds of firms).

**The gap:**
Nothing takes one drilling record and produces both the regulator's completion report and the NGO's or client's report.

**Possible product / MVP:**
A field form on a phone that produces the basin completion report and the client report as PDFs.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
TZS 30–60k per borehole report, or a done-for-you report service.

**How to find first customers:**
Basin board registers; WASH tender award lists.

**Risks:**
- Low volume per firm.
- Unknown count.
- Basin forms may differ by basin, which adds fragmentation and cost.
- A local is needed.

**Kill condition:**
Fewer than about 200 active drillers, or basin boards that accept a one-page form only.

**Score:** 3/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Household employers of domestic workers:** C189 ratification and the new regulations and inspection form are not yet enforced. Households won't pay. Revisit if NSSF launches a mandatory household-employer scheme. (https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/tanzania-brink-historic-recognition-domestic-workers, https://webapps.ilo.org/globalcare/south-4-care/8)
- **Livestock identification:** the government's e-tagging campaign (from Jun 2025) and the NLITS registry are the system. (https://dailynews.co.tz/tanzania-injects-69-2bn-for-livestock-vaccination-campaign/, https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/2010/12/eng@2010-05-28)
- **Livestock markets and traders:** an LGA and district veterinary permit workflow from 2001. Cash traders; no willingness to pay. (https://tanzlii.org/en/akn/tz/act/gn/2001/131)
- **Fertiliser agro-dealers:** TFRA's own agro-dealer tool and the subsidy system. (https://dailynews.co.tz/tfra-issues-strong-warning-to-traders-who-sell-fertilizer-outside-the-subsidy-system/)
- **AMCOS cooperatives:** MUVU, plus the TCDC–Vodacom digitisation push (2026). (https://ippmedia.co.tz/the-guardian/business/read/vodacom-tanzania-tcdc-to-foster-cooperatives-digital-transformation-2026-06-08-123213)
- **Honey and beekeepers:** the MNRT honey Digital Traceability System. Small export volume.
- **Scrap metal:** no register obligation was found.
- **PML artisanal miners:** royalty is collected at the market, and there is no willingness to pay.
- **Boda-boda operators and street traders:** government registration desks; consumer-like buyers.
- **Agrovets and game-meat dealers:** no trigger, or a tiny market.
- **ADDO drug shops:** a plausible quiet industry (paper dispensing registers, Pharmacy Council reforms, inspection possibly moving to TMDA), but it was not diligenced. Count and competitors (pharmacy POS) were not checked. A follow-up lead if more budget is available. (https://medicopress.media/pharmacy-council-overwhelmed-government-eyes-tmda-for-inspections/, https://thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/news/national/one-year-pharmacy-graduates-in-tanzania-to-lose-recognition-from-december-5470852)

## 4. Method notes

- **What worked:** English queries naming the regulation ("Mining (Mineral Trading) Regulations register monthly") and regulator statistics reported in TanzaniaInvest, The Citizen, Daily News and IPP Media.
- **What didn't work:** Swahili queries returned junk. "kibali cha kusafirisha mifugo" returned electronics pages, and the domestic-worker Swahili query returned Kenyan NSSF results. The search engine is US-biased, and Swahili regulator pages (tumemadini.go.tz) surface only for precise terms.
- **Recurring pattern:** in Tanzania the state itself digitises quiet sectors (MUVU, the TFRA tool, NLITS, honey DTS, TANCIS, OMCTP). The usual substitute is a government system, not paper. That is why most of these industries were rejected.
- **Founder access:** a non-local solo founder would struggle with every row. Each needs a Swahili-speaking local partner.
