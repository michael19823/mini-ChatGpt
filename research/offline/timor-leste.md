# Timor-Leste: offline-industries pass

**Researcher:** offline-industries agent (Timor-Leste) | **Date:** 2026-10-05 | **Search budget used:** 8 of 8

## Summary verdict

**No quiet industry in Timor-Leste supports an indie software product.** The quiet industries do exist: livestock and pig traders, microlet (minibus) operators, small-scale fishers, kiosks and street traders, and household employers of domestic workers. But they fail on one of three counts. Some have no recurring obligation that falls on the operator (for example, domestic workers are outside the Labour Code). Some have obligations that are run by the state through mobile teams or new government portals: MCI launched an online licensing system in March 2026, and Fisheries runs mobile boat licensing. And in every case the operators are subsistence-scale, with no realistic willingness to pay for software.

The few real 2025–2026 regulatory triggers point to **government or donor procurement, not SME SaaS**: the animal health framework (Decree-Law 41/2023 and Ministerial Decree 41/2025), the MCI licensing system (Mar 2026), and fisheries MCS for IUU, which was presented at SEAFDEC in Feb 2026. Two items are kept below as watchlist entries only. Both score low. A non-local solo founder could not sell either of them.

## Opportunities (watchlist only; none recommended)

### Opportunity: Animal movement and health-certificate log for livestock traders and municipal vets

**Industry:**
Livestock (pigs, cattle, buffalo, goats) trading and transport between municipalities

**Buyer:**
Realistically the Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock, Fisheries and Forestry (veterinary services) or a donor programme (ACIAR, FAO). The trader is not a credible buyer.

**Trigger / Why now:**
Decree-Law 41/2023 created the animal health and welfare framework, which covers movement control, veterinary products and **animal identification systems**. Ministerial Decree 41/2025 regulates import and transit of animals and animal products (import authorisation, sanitary certificate, pre-export inspection). ASF movement bans (e.g., pigs out of Dili) continue to be enforced.

**Current workflow:**
1. The trader buys animals in villages and transports them by truck between municipalities.
2. Where controls apply, the trader obtains a veterinary certificate or movement permit from a municipal vet office (paper; unverified detail).
3. At checkpoints, police or vets inspect the paperwork. Importers additionally obtain import authorisation and certificates for each consignment.

**Pain:**
Disease losses (ASF) are large for households. The paperwork pain falls on the regulator, which has to trace movements, not on traders. No evidence found of traders being fined for missing records.

**Existing solutions:**
Paper permits; donor-funded animal-health information systems (unverified which one is in use); FAO and ACIAR projects (ACIAR LS-2019-158 is on pig/ASF work in TL).

**Offline evidence:**
No trader associations, software listings or online forms found. Controls are enforced physically at checkpoints.

**Offline channel:**
Municipal veterinary officers and the ministry's livestock directorate; ACIAR/FAO project teams.

**Market count:**
Unknown. No register of livestock traders found. Effectively one buyer (the ministry or a donor).

**The gap:**
Digital movement permits and animal ID, which is a government system build.

**Possible product:**
An offline-first movement-permit and certificate app for municipal vets, sold as a donor-funded project.

**MVP:**
Android form for issuing movement certificates with a QR code that checkpoint staff can verify offline.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
Project contract, USD 20–80k (estimate), with no recurring SaaS revenue.

**How to find first customers:**
FAO TL office, ACIAR project leads, the MAPPF livestock directorate.

**Willingness to pay:**
Software only through donor or government procurement. Traders would pay nothing.

**Founder access:**
Needs local presence, Tetum/Portuguese and donor networks. Not realistic for a non-local solo founder.

**Risks:**
Procurement cycles. Donors favour open-source tools (e.g., DHIS2-type systems, unverified for TL). One-off project.

**Kill condition:**
A donor-funded animal-ID system is already being built for MAPPF. This is likely.

**Score:** 2/10

### Opportunity: Done-for-you MCI commercial licence filing and renewal for kiosks and small traders

**Industry:**
Small retail (kiosks, lojas), small workshops and industrial units needing a commercial or industrial licence

**Buyer:**
Owner of a Dili retail or wholesale business; more realistically a local accountant or licence agent ("despachante") serving many owners

**Trigger / Why now:**
In March 2026 MCI launched the Commercial and Industrial Licensing Information System (lic.mci.gov.tl) together with CCI-TL, to make licensing and inspection "faster and more transparent". Licensing and renewal are moving online for owners who have mostly used the counter.

**Current workflow:**
1. The owner registers the company at SERVE.
2. The owner then applies at MCI for the commercial or industrial licence, now through the online system (unverified how much remains on paper).
3. MCI inspects premises and the licence is renewed periodically (renewal period unverified).

**Pain:**
Expected friction for low-literacy owners moving to an online portal. No complaints or penalties were found yet.

**Existing solutions:**
MCI's own free portal; SERVE one-stop shop; CCI-TL help desks (assumed); local accountants and law firms (Miranda, VdA) for larger firms.

**Offline evidence:**
The system launched only in Mar 2026. Licensing was previously done at the counter. No private software for this exists.

**Offline channel:**
CCI-TL (co-launched the system); accountants in Dili; door-to-door in Dili's commercial districts.

**Market count:**
About 29.5k sole traders, 18.7k single-shareholder and 6.2k joint-shareholder companies in SERVE (from the country report). How many need an MCI licence is unknown.

**The gap:**
Hand-holding: filling the portal for owners who can't. That is a service, not software.

**Possible product:**
A licence-agent service with a simple tracker for renewals and inspection dates.

**MVP:**
A WhatsApp intake plus a spreadsheet tracker of client licences and renewal dates.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
USD 10–30 per filing (estimate). Micro-scale.

**How to find first customers:**
CCI-TL members; the SERVE registry; accountants.

**Willingness to pay:**
For a done-for-you service only, at low prices. Owners would not pay for software.

**Founder access:**
Requires a local, Tetum-speaking operator. A non-local founder cannot sell this.

**Risks:**
The free state portal plus CCI-TL support covers it. Renewal frequency is possibly multi-year.

**Kill condition:**
The licence is multi-year or one-off, or CCI-TL provides free filing help. Both are plausible.

**Score:** 2/10

## Rejected

- **Domestic-worker payroll and social security for households.** There is no obligation: domestic workers fall outside the Labour Code, and the special law was only re-tabled (2024 commitment, not enacted per the latest source). Only 4,479 employers of any kind were registered with INSS in 2022. Revisit only if the law passes and mandates INSS registration.
- **Microlet and taxi operator compliance.** Licences are one-off or annual from DNTT, and the owner-drivers have no WTP. The visible conflict is about licence quotas (bus strikes), not paperwork.
- **Fisher licensing and catch reporting.** The State licenses boats through mobile teams (FAO RFLP), and 99.5% of fishing is small-scale wooden-boat work. MCS/IUU systems are donor and government projects (SEAFDEC 2026).
- **Scrap metal dealers.** The trade was banned in 2006 "until further notice". No current licensed register was found.
- **Money changers.** The economy is dollarised, so the trade is negligible.
- **Pharmacy and other import licences.** Too few operators. SAMES and the customs single window already cover the flow.
- **Animal importers (MD 41/2025).** Real per-consignment work, but only a few dozen importers (estimate), and the permits sit alongside the TileSW single window.

## Method notes

- Searches worked best when they targeted **legislation databases** (FAOLEX via leap.unep.org, ADB LPR, WTO import-licensing notifications) and the state news agency **Tatoli**. Together these gave the real triggers (DL 41/2023, MD 41/2025, MCI licensing system).
- Searches for registers and counts (traders, microlets, fishers) returned almost nothing current. Counts here are old or estimates.
- One fisheries result (Notification 2024/27 on small-scale fisheries) was **Türkiye's**, not Timor-Leste's, and was discarded.
- Searches in Portuguese ("trabalhadores domésticos", "licença actividade comercial") returned only English sources. Tetum queries were not tried within the budget.
