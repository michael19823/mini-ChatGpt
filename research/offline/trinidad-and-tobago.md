# Trinidad and Tobago: Offline (Quiet) Industries Pass

Researched 2026-10-05. I made 20 WebSearch calls. Two were refused for usage limits partway through, so 18 returned results. WebFetch was not used, so all facts come from search-result snippets. The population is about 1.4M. The existing country report (`research/countries/trinidad-and-tobago.md`) already covers NIS/Empower for general employers (4/10) and FIUTT DNFBP compliance (4.5/10). I don't repeat those here.

**Bottom line:** No quiet industry in T&T clears the brief's bar. Two weak leads are worth noting. The first is household employers of domestic workers, who now face NIS contribution rises in 2026 and 2027. The second is quarry operators, with licence hold-overs and disputed self-assessed royalties. Both are small and both lean towards a done-for-you service. Most other quiet-industry regimes in T&T are either Bills that were never proclaimed or have lapsed (private security, fisheries, the Scrap Metal Act dealer regime), or 1980s-era licensing that is barely enforced (pesticides, pawnbrokers).

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Household-employer NIS and pay-record service for domestic workers

**Industry:** Households as employers (domestic workers, caregivers, gardeners)

**Buyer:** Middle- and upper-income households employing one or more domestic workers. A secondary buyer is the domestic-staff placement agency that places them.

**Trigger / Why now:** The NIS contribution rate rose from 13.2% to 16.2% of insurable earnings on 5 Jan 2026, with a further rise to 19.2% scheduled for Jan 2027 (per a payroll-guide snippet; confirm against NIBTT). NIBTT is also migrating employers to its Empower portal (see the country report).

**Current workflow:**
1. The household registers as an employer with NIBTT (in person or via Empower) within 14 days of hiring.
2. Each month it works out the contribution class from wages, splits the employer and employee share, and pays at a Service Centre or through bank bill-pay before month-end.
3. It keeps a pay record per worker: name, NI number, wages, weekly contributions.
4. In practice many households skip all of this. Arrears surface years later when the worker claims a benefit.

**Pain:** Failing to keep pay records carries a fine of TT$4,000 and 6 months' imprisonment, and failing to pay contributions is an offence. The domestic workers' union cites an employer told she owed 6 years of arrears, who then fired the worker. Rate changes in two consecutive years make household calculations error-prone.

**Existing solutions:** Bank bill-pay to NIBTT (payment only). The Empower portal (free, but self-service). Local payroll packages (Paymaster, TTPay, Payroll123), which are priced and designed for businesses. Accountants. Global EOR providers (Rivermate, Multiplier and others), which are irrelevant for households. I found no household-specific product.

**Offline evidence:** Household employers aren't a "business" segment. NIBTT contribution guidance is static HTML pages. Workers' rights work happens through a union and ILO cooperative projects, not online tools.

**Offline channel:** Domestic-staff placement agencies (bundle the service at placement). Neighbourhood accountants. The National Union of Domestic Employees, as an advocate rather than a buyer, which can push employers to comply.

**Market count:** ~30,000 domestic workers (ITUC/union estimate, unverified and dated). Households paying formally are an unknown, likely small, fraction (estimate).

**The gap:** A tool built for a household with one worker. It would produce the monthly contribution figure at the current rate, a payslip and a pay-record ledger, plus a reminder and a bill-pay reference.

**Possible product:** A WhatsApp or SMS monthly reminder plus a web ledger. It would compute NIS at the current class table, generate the payslip and pay record, and keep an arrears-proof history.

**MVP:** One-page calculator plus a PDF payslip and annual pay record, updated for the 2026 and 2027 rates.

**Pricing hypothesis:** TT$30–60 per month per household (~US$5–9), or bundled into agency placement fees. Households would probably pay only for a done-for-you service, not for software.

**How to find first customers:** Placement agencies (directory listings), accountants serving high-income households, union referral.

**Risks:** Low compliance culture means households may not want a paper trail. Very low price ceiling. NIBTT's Empower could add a household flow. A foreign founder would struggle without a local partner agency.

**Kill condition:** Agencies won't bundle it, or interviews show fewer than ~2,000 households pay NIS formally.

**Score:** 3.5/10. It is mandatory with a real 2026/2027 trigger, but willingness to pay is low and the market is tiny. It needs a local partner. It is better as a CARICOM household-employer product (Barbados, Jamaica and others have similar regimes, unverified).

### Opportunity: Quarry production log and royalty-return pack

**Industry:** Quarrying / aggregates

**Buyer:** Owner-operators of sand and gravel quarries, many family-run and represented by the T&T Aggregate Producers Association (TTAPA).

**Trigger / Why now:** In May 2025, MEEI issued one-year "hold-over" letters to 25 operators whose licences had expired, pending renewal. TTAPA is publicly asking how outstanding royalties will be settled. TTEITI 2024 flags outstanding royalties and illegal quarrying. MEEI ran an illegal-quarrying campaign with a 2025 flyer.

**Current workflow:**
1. The operator estimates extracted volume. Most quarries have no weighbridge.
2. The operator self-calculates the royalty due under the Minerals Act and Regulations, and pays MEEI.
3. On renewal, the operator assembles licence documents. Disputes over arrears are negotiated case by case.

**Pain:** Licences go unrenewed, royalty arrears accumulate, operators strike (24 quarries in 2024), and enforcement is selective.

**Existing solutions:** Accountants and consultants; MEEI's own forms; generic weighbridge or ERP software for the few large operators (ANSA McAL, Mineral Mines of T&T, EMBD, which are state or conglomerate owned).

**Offline evidence:** Volumes are self-reported without weighbridges. Licensing happens by letter and media release.

**Offline channel:** TTAPA. Phone calls to the 25 hold-over operators named in press coverage.

**Market count:** ~90 operators, 8 fully licensed (Guardian/Express). 25 on hold-over letters (MEEI).

**The gap:** An auditable record of production volume and royalties that an operator could show MEEI during renewal or an arrears settlement.

**Possible product:** A truck-load ticket log (phone app at the gate) that rolls up into a monthly volume and royalty statement and a renewal document pack.

**MVP:** Load-ticket app plus a monthly PDF statement.

**Pricing hypothesis:** US$100–200 per month per quarry. More plausible as a consultancy engagement during settlement.

**How to find first customers:** TTAPA, plus the hold-over operator list from press coverage.

**Risks:** Operators may prefer not to have auditable volumes, since self-reporting currently benefits them. Fewer than 100 buyers. It is politically sensitive. A local presence is needed.

**Kill condition:** MEEI doesn't require or accept operator-produced volume records, or TTAPA members show no interest.

**Score:** 2.5/10. The pain is real but it is a governance problem. Fewer than 100 buyers, and incentives run against transparency.

## 3. Rejected

- **Scrap metal register:** 31 licensed sites. The dealer regime of the 2022 Act is not operating, and licences are extended year to year (https://tradeind.gov.tt/extension-of-time-scrap-metal-dealers-licence/).
- **Fisher catch and licensing:** the Fisheries Management (No. 2) Bill 2020 lapsed on 18 Mar 2025. A 2023 draft is still under review, so there is no duty to serve (https://www.ttparliament.org/publication/the-fisheries-management-no-2-bill-2020/, https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/s3/2025-08/Trinidad-and-Tobago-final-2025-508.pdf).
- **Private security firm compliance:** the Private Security Industry Bill is not proclaimed, and TTPS licensing is a one-off (https://ttconnect.gov.tt/operate-a-security-firm-license). Watch for proclamation.
- **Maxi-taxi permits:** ~5,000 vehicles, but the obligation is a one-off counter permit with no recurring filing (https://mowt.gov.tt/Divisions/Transport-Division/Services/Vehicle-Registration-Maxi-Taxi/Maxi-Taxi-Operator-s-Permit).
- **Pesticide outlets:** ~107 outlets (2005). The board is under-resourced and enforcement is weak (https://leap.unep.org/en/countries/tt/national-legislation/pesticides-licensing-premises-regulations-1987-l-n-no227-1987, https://ttconnect.gov.tt/?p=2118).
- **Pawnbrokers:** the 1889 Act's police book inspection is the only duty found, with no count and no trigger.
- **Funeral homes:** the informant registers deaths in person, and there is no funeral-home licensing yet. The Professional Funeral Association is lobbying for oversight (https://ttconnect.gov.tt/?p=1622, https://www.ttt.live/?p=150664).

## 4. Method notes

- What worked: ministry press releases (tradeind.gov.tt, energy.gov.tt) and local press (Guardian, Express) gave hard operator counts. laws.gov.tt and ttparliament.org showed quickly whether a regime is actually in force.
- The main finding about T&T: many modernising Bills (fisheries, private security, scrap metal) lapse or sit unproclaimed for years. A "why now" based on a Bill is unreliable here. Check proclamation status first.
- What didn't work: abattoir and meat searches returned other countries' regulations. No official registers of pawnbrokers or pesticide outlets are online.
