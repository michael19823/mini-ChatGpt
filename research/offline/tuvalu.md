# Tuvalu: Offline-Industries Pass

**Classification:** Microstate (population about 9,500). Search budget: 8 searches, 7 used.

**Bottom line:** No quiet-industry opportunity meets the bar. Tuvalu does have the regulatory scaffolding: a business licence from the Ministry of Finance plus an annual Kaupule (island council) operating fee, food-premises registration under the Food Safety Act 2006, monthly TNPF provident-fund returns, and a Traffic Act with commercial and bus licences. But each obliged population is tens of operators at most, and the work is done at a counter in Funafuti, where the clerk is often a phone call away. The fees involved are AUD 20–200 a year. Software has nothing to save here that a founder could charge for. **No opportunities are scored.** The rows below are a screen, kept short.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

None. Every obliged population I found is in the single digits to low hundreds. The work happens at one or two counters on Funafuti and costs AUD 20–200 a year in fees. The brief's benchmark is about 5,000 reachable buyers paying $200–500 a month. Tuvalu misses it by two to three orders of magnitude in every row.

**Possible add-on only (not scored):** a Pacific "small-employer provident-fund return" helper built for Fiji (FNPF), Samoa (SNPF) or Kiribati (KPF). TNPF returns could be added cheaply as one more template. The diligence belongs to those larger markets, not to Tuvalu.

---

## 3. Rejected

- **TNPF return helper for Tuvalu alone:** a real monthly mandatory form, but there are too few employers. The substitute is the TNPF counter and spreadsheets. The household-employer angle doesn't apply because domestic workers are exempt.
- **Business licence / Kaupule fee renewals:** annual and cheap, done in person.
- **Scrap/recyclables export compliance:** one operator, supported by donors (EIF).
- **Labour-mobility worker-processing tool:** the buyer is government or Australian approved employers, and the volumes are tiny (290 PALM workers).
- **Food-premises inspection records:** one regulator and no recurring logbook burden was found.

---

## 4. Method notes

- English works; Tuvaluan-language queries are pointless online. The useful sources were **PacLII** (statute text and forms), the **Tuvalu trade portal** (step-by-step counter procedures with fees and phone numbers), and **DevPolicy/PALM** data.
- No public register counts were found for businesses, food premises or employers. CEIC lists a "businesses registered" series, but the figure was not visible. The 2022 census (ILO catalogue) has a business-legal-form variable, but I could not see a count.
- For microstates, two or three searches to confirm scale are enough. The answer is driven by population, not by digging.
