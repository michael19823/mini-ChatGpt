# Uganda: offline / quiet-industries pass

**Status: partial, low-to-medium confidence.** 20 WebSearch calls were made in total. The 19th was refused
("You've hit your usage limit"); after the limit reset, the 20th repeated it. WebFetch was
not used, so every claim rests on search-result snippets. A claim marked *unverified* or *estimate*
was not confirmed by a source. The first-round report (`research/countries/uganda.md`) covered EFRIS
reconciliation and coffee EUDR. Neither is repeated here.

**Overall finding:** Uganda's quiet industries are heavily regulated on paper, but most obligations sit
on the *state's* side of the counter: a district officer issues the permit, or a government portal
does the licensing. Few of them create a recurring filing that the operator does itself and would pay
to automate. Two exceptions came out of this pass: **mineral (gold) dealers**, who face new 2026
register and returns rules, and **SACCOs**, which file quarterly returns to UMRA. Neither is strong.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: Mineral-dealer purchase register and DGSM returns for small gold buyers

**Industry:**
Gold and mineral dealing: small licensed mineral dealers and aggregators who buy from artisanal miners
in Busia, Mubende, Kassanda, Buhweju, Karamoja and similar areas.

**Buyer:**
Owner-operator of a small Mineral Dealer's Licence (MDL) holder, or the compliance officer of a mid-size
aggregator or exporter that buys from many small dealers.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- **Feb 2026:** the Ministry of Energy and Mineral Development issued guidelines saying that every gold
  transaction must be between holders of valid dealer, mining or refining licences. Buyers must verify the seller's licence and keep proof of purchase.
- **May 2026:** consultation on the Mining and Minerals (Mineral Markets and Buying Centres)
  Regulations 2026, under which trade moves into licensed buying centres.
- **May 2026:** reports say gold buyers will undergo biometric registration.
- **2024–2026:** the MCRS e-portal rollout for licences was completed.

**Current workflow:**
1. The dealer buys gold from a miner or another dealer, weighs and assays it, and pays in cash or mobile money.
2. The dealer writes the transaction into the register required by the Act: seller name and address,
   the seller's right to possess the mineral, nature and weight, price, date, place of origin, and the
   buyer or consignee on resale.
3. Since Feb 2026 the dealer also has to check and keep evidence that the seller holds a valid licence.
4. Periodically, the dealer compiles returns to DGSM. Sources disagree on frequency: one says monthly,
   another quarterly. The exporter then needs the chain-of-custody documents.

**Pain:**
- **Regulatory pressure:** "Govt tightens the paper trail on gold" (Monitor, 2026), and the ministry
  has run enforcement trips to eastern Uganda (Red Pepper). Dealing without a licence is a criminal
  offence.
- **Exporter demand:** exporters need provenance evidence to sell to refiners (*inferred*).
- **No sourced complaint:** no complaint about the register work itself was found (*unverified*).

**Existing solutions:**
- DGSM's MCRS portal (licensing; it is not confirmed that it takes transaction returns).
- Paper register books (*unverified*: normal practice).
- Law firms and consultants selling MDL setup and compliance (Global Law Experts guides; Novalex;
  dealer blogs such as Nile Precious Metals and Mansa Minerals).
- Government buying centres, which may become the place where the first-sale record is made.
- International traceability or ASGM due-diligence platforms (*unverified* whether any are active in Uganda).

**Offline evidence:**
- **Recent move off paper:** licensing moved off paper only in 2024–26.
- **Ledger rule:** the purchase register is set out in the Act as a ledger.
- **Cash trade:** trading is in person and in cash at mine sites.
- **No software listing:** no Ugandan "gold dealer register" software listing appeared in searches.

**Offline channel:**
- **Licence list:** the DGSM/MCRS licence register (the cadastre is public in many countries; the
  Uganda listing is *unverified*), used for phone or WhatsApp outreach.
- **Gatherings:** the ministry's consultation workshops, such as the Munyonyo session in May 2026.
- **Exporters:** gold exporters and refiners who need clean records from their suppliers and could
  push their dealer network onto the tool.

**Market count:**
Unknown. No count of MDL holders was found. Context: about 500,000 artisanal miners are targeted for
formalisation (Monitor), and gold exports reached a record Shs 21.1 trillion (allAfrica, Jan 2026).
A guess of a few hundred active dealers is an *estimate*.

**The gap:**
A phone-first transaction register would capture each purchase with a seller-licence check, a photo,
a scale reading and a mobile-money reference. From that it would produce the DGSM return and a
per-lot chain-of-custody pack for the exporter. Nothing like this surfaced for Uganda.

**Possible product:**
An Android app plus a web back office that serves as the dealer's statutory register and generates
DGSM returns and exporter provenance packs.

**MVP:**
- Offline-capable Android form for each purchase.
- Seller-licence number field with a lookup against a manually maintained list.
- A monthly return as PDF or Excel in the DGSM format, which must be obtained first.
- A lot export for the buyer.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
USD 30–100 per month per dealer, or per-lot fees paid by the exporter (*estimate*). It is more likely
sold as software plus a done-for-you returns service.

**How to find first customers:**
The DGSM licence list, mining-district associations (*unverified* names), and two or three exporters
willing to require it of their suppliers.

**Risks:**
- **Not final:** the regulations are still in consultation and the return format is unknown.
- **Buying centres:** they could become the only legal point of first sale, which would centralise
  record-keeping on government systems.
- **AML and fraud exposure:** gold fraud is endemic and a foreign founder takes on reputational risk.
- **Data honesty:** dealers may not want accurate records.

**Kill condition:**
- The final 2026 regulations require that purchases be recorded in the buying centre's or the MCRS's
  own system.
- Or dealers say returns are a one-page summary their lawyer files for free.

**Founder access:**
A non-local solo founder could not realistically sell this. It needs a local partner with mining
contacts, and it carries AML and reputational exposure.

**Willingness to pay:**
Dealers handle high-value goods, so they can pay. They would probably pay for "keep my licence safe"
as a service rather than for software.

**Score:** 5/10

### Opportunity: UMRA quarterly-returns pack for small Tier 4 SACCOs

**Industry:**
Tier 4 microfinance: savings and credit cooperatives (SACCOs) and non-deposit-taking MFIs licensed by
UMRA since 2021.

**Buyer:**
Manager or bookkeeper of a small rural SACCO, or the accountant or cooperative union that serves
several of them.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- UMRA began licensing SACCOs in 2021 and plans quarterly compliance monitoring of all licensed
  institutions in its FY2025/26 plans.
- Licences must be renewed each calendar year.
- UMRA has announced enforcement operations against non-compliant institutions.
- No 2026-specific rule change was found.

**Current workflow:**
1. The SACCO keeps member savings and loans in a ledger, Excel or a core-banking package.
2. Each quarter, someone prepares the prescribed-format financial returns, including asset
   classification and provisioning.
3. The principal officer and finance manager endorse the returns, which are then submitted to UMRA.
   The submission channel is *unverified*: no online system was found.

**Pain:**
- **Mandatory:** quarterly returns are required.
- **Provisioning is hard:** loan-ageing and provisioning tables are hard to produce from paper
  ledgers (*inferred*).
- **No complaints found:** no sourced complaints.

**Existing solutions:**
- SACCO core-banking and MIS packages sold in Uganda. Names were not verified in this pass, and
  several are known to exist (*unverified*).
- Accountants and auditors.
- Cooperative unions and NGO capacity programmes.
- UMRA's own templates.

**Offline evidence:**
- Returns must be signed by two officers.
- UMRA publishes its licence lists as PDFs.
- No submission portal surfaced in the search.

**Offline channel:**
- UMRA's published list of licensed SACCOs.
- District commercial officers.
- Cooperative unions such as UCSCU (*unverified* in this pass).
- Accountants who serve several SACCOs.

**Market count:**
UMRA's budget documents describe on-site and off-site supervision of 750 institutions each quarter.
The full licensed population is larger but was not found. The March 2022 licence list exists as a PDF.

**The gap:**
A tool that reads loan and savings ledgers in Excel or CSV and produces the UMRA quarterly return,
including loan ageing and provisioning. *Unverified*: incumbent core-banking packages may already do this.

**Possible product:**
"UMRA return in 10 minutes": upload the loan book and trial balance, get the endorsed-format return
plus the exceptions (missing collateral data, overdue buckets).

**MVP:**
- Excel templates.
- A loan-ageing and provisioning engine.
- PDF and Excel export in UMRA's format.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
USD 10–30 per SACCO per quarter, or USD 50–150 per month for an accountant with 10+ SACCOs (*estimate*).

**How to find first customers:**
The UMRA licensed list and accountants who audit SACCOs.

**Risks:**
- Incumbent SACCO software may already include the report.
- UMRA may launch its own portal.
- Many SACCOs have very little cash.

**Kill condition:**
- Interviews show that the major SACCO MIS vendors already print the UMRA return.
- Or that UMRA's offline supervision makes the return trivial.

**Founder access:**
Partly remote-able (it is Excel-in, PDF-out), but selling to rural SACCOs needs a local partner or
an accountant channel.

**Willingness to pay:**
Low. More plausible as an accountant tool than one sold directly to SACCOs.

**Score:** 4/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Fisheries licensing helper (boats, fish traders, fish maw):** the trigger is real. The
  species-specific licence deadline was 15 Sep 2026, and on Lake Albert only 1,625 of 5,075 registered
  boats were licensed. But licensing goes through a government portal integrated with eCitizen, the
  licence is annual, and fishers have very low WTP. The substitutes are the portal and the landing-site
  fisheries officer.
- **Agro-input dealer compliance:** 46% of dealers are unregistered and the regulator's functions were
  restructured in 2024. But the obligation is an annual registration through a MAAIF form, with no
  recurring filing found. At most a service.
- **Livestock movement permits:** paper AMPs are issued by the district veterinary officer, not
  filed by traders. Traceability is being built by MAAIF and Makerere.
- **Timber and charcoal movement permits:** a five-copy paper book, but the DFO writes it. The dealer
  has no workflow to automate.
- **Boda-boda registration:** state-run (KCCA, MoWT digital plates). Riders don't pay.
- **NDA drug shops and vet outlets:** annual licensing run by NDA and DVOs. Enforcement is visible
  (closure campaigns) but annual only. Dispensing-register software was not researched.
- **Scrap metal, domestic employers:** no Ugandan obligation or trigger surfaced. The 2025 Employment Amendment's new Part IXA regulates migrant (non-citizen) workers, an expat/HR-lawyer market, not households (Ortus Advocates, KTA Advocates alerts 2026).

## 4. Method notes

- **What worked:** English queries naming the regulator (UMRA, NDA, DGSM/MCRS, MAAIF, DFO) plus
  "licence / register / 2026". Ugandan news sites (Monitor, New Vision, Eagle Online, Independent)
  carry enforcement campaigns and new rules well. Uganda's regulatory language is English, so
  local-language queries were not needed.
- **What didn't:** generic seed-list queries (scrap metal, animal traceability act, domestic
  workers). They returned Kenya, Zambia or UK material.
- **Uganda pattern:** the paper form is usually filled in *by the official*, so many quiet
  industries offer nothing for operators to automate.
- **Budget used up:** all 20 searches. Next steps: the DGSM return format and frequency, a count of
  MDL holders, SACCO MIS vendors, and pharmacy dispensing registers.
