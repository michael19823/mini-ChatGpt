# United Kingdom: offline-industries pass

Date: 2026-10-05. 42 WebSearch calls; WebFetch was blocked, so every finding comes from search-result summaries. Anything marked "unverified" or "estimate" needs a direct read of the source before anyone acts on it. This pass does not repeat the opportunities in `research/countries/united-kingdom.md`: highway licences, DoLS, CMA vets, vape duty, DWT and EV street works.

**Bottom line:** most UK quiet industries are covered by one of three things:
- a dominant niche package. Guntrader already emails Form 11 firearm transfer notices to police forces, and AMCS serves scrap yards.
- a free government tool, such as MMO eSales or AHDB Medicine Hub.
- a known payroll service, such as Nannytax.

One gap matches the "one job, many receiving authorities" pattern: **memorial (headstone) permits**. Masons file them job by job with roughly 2,500 burial authorities and Church of England dioceses, mostly on paper or PDF. A second, smaller lead is new and Scotland-only: **muirburn, grouse and trap licences** under the Wildlife Management and Muirburn (Scotland) Act 2024.

## 2. Opportunities

### Opportunity: Memorial Permit Router for monumental masons

**Industry:**
Monumental / memorial masonry (headstones, additional inscriptions, renovations)

**Buyer:**
Owner or office manager of an independent memorial mason business (typically 1–10 staff, often family-run, often a showroom attached to a workshop). Secondary buyer: funeral directors who sell memorials and subcontract the fixing.

**Trigger / Why now:**
There is no single national trigger. That is the weakness. There is a steady churn of local rule changes:
- councils are reviewing memorialisation and headstone policies (Newcastle-under-Lyme 2025);
- new and revised forms keep appearing: Spelthorne Dec 2025, Burnham-on-Sea 2026, St Albans diocese CR2 2025, Birmingham diocese churchyard regulations 2025;
- some councils are moving to separate portals (PlotBox Memorial Mason Portal, Byond, Torfaen online form), which adds one login per authority;
- in Scotland, the Burial (Management) Regs 2025 mandate scheduled headstone inspections, which raises the bar on fixing standards and paperwork (the link to mason permits is inferred).

**Current workflow:**
1. The family picks a design in the showroom. The mason identifies the cemetery or churchyard and the grave owner (holder of the exclusive right of burial / lair).
2. The mason downloads that authority's form (PDF/Word), or finds its portal. Each authority has its own size, material and foundation rules. For churchyards, the diocesan regulations apply and the incumbent signs; a faculty is needed if the memorial falls outside the regulations.
3. The mason gets the grave owner's signature (often a wet signature) and the grave deed number, and attaches a drawing and inscription proof.
4. The mason posts, emails or hand-delivers the form with the fee, at least 14 days before fixing. They then chase the permit, book a fixing appointment, and keep the authority's registration (NAMM/BRAMM card, £5m public-liability certificate) current. Many authorities run separate annual registration schemes.
5. If they fix without a permit, the mason can be suspended from that authority's cemeteries, and the memorial can be removed at cost.

**Pain:**
- Forms differ per authority (council, town or parish council, diocese).
- The owner's signature and the deed have to be chased.
- Annual mason registration is per authority.
- Sanctions are explicit: suspension from all the council's cemeteries (Chorley, Hull rules), and removal with costs recovered (town council regulations).
- One case shows the pressure on masons: an application form was "posted under the door of the town council offices just hours before the stone was erected".
- No complaint threads were found online, which is consistent with a quiet industry but means pain intensity is **unverified**. Interviews are needed.

**Existing solutions:**
- **SAMM Software** (UK): mason-side quotes, supplier pricing, "permits and location tracking", artwork approvals, scheduling. It tracks permits; whether it fills or submits authority forms is unverified.
- **PlotBox Memorial Mason Portal** and **Byond**: authority-side portals, used only where the authority has bought them.
- **BACAS** and other bereavement back-office systems: authority-side; staff re-key the paper form.
- **MonuDesk**: monument-dealer order software that handles "cemetery paperwork". It appears US-focused (unverified for UK).
- Substitute: the mason's own folder of downloaded PDFs, plus phone calls to cemetery offices.

**Offline evidence:**
- Current paper/PDF forms from district, town and parish councils and dioceses (2025–26 versions).
- Wyre's form carries an "entered on BACAS" office box, so authority staff re-key the data.
- Torfaen introduced an online form specifically to cut postage and travel.
- Trade news lives in print-led media (Stone Specialist, NAMM News PDF). There are no SaaS review pages for mason software.

**Offline channel:**
- NAMM, by phone 01788 542264 and through NAMM News advertising.
- BRAMM.
- Stone wholesalers and importers who supply every mason, such as Granart (mentioned by Stone Specialist). They are a natural reseller.
- ICCM (Institute of Cemetery and Crematorium Management) events, where cemetery managers would welcome cleaner applications.
- Phone outreach from the public "registered masons" lists that councils publish (Forest of Dean, Tavistock, and others).

**Market count:**
- NAMM RQMF register: 1,166 memorial companies + 400 fixers. BRAMM: ~500 fixers (overlapping).
- Estimate: 1,200–1,500 mason businesses in the UK.
- Burial authorities: ~2,500 (Stone Specialist, older figure), plus Church of England churchyards.
- UK deaths: 568,613 in 2024 (ONS, via Stone Specialist). Burials are a minority of these; memorial volume is an estimate, roughly 100k+ permits a year including additional inscriptions (unverified).

**The gap:**
Nobody keeps a maintained library of each authority's form, rules (dimensions, materials, waiting period) and submission route, or pre-fills the form from the mason's order. Mason software tracks that a permit exists; the authority's portal is only that authority's.

**Possible product:**
"Order once, permit everywhere." The mason enters the order once (deceased, plot, owner, design, inscription). The tool:
- picks the right authority's form and rules;
- validates dimensions;
- pre-fills the PDF or portal and collects the grave owner's e-signature (where the authority accepts it);
- tracks fees, permit status and expiry of each authority's registration and insurance.

**MVP:**
Authority form library and pre-fill for one region, about 60 authorities, for example one county plus its town and parish councils and one diocese. It adds a remote signature link for the grave owner and a permit/registration tracker. Submission stays as email, done by the mason.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
£30–60/month per mason, or £3–5 per permit. Payment is likely only if the product saves office time. Many masons may prefer a done-for-you service ("we file it"), which would make this a service-plus-software business with a low ceiling.

**How to find first customers:**
Council and diocese "registered memorial masons" lists, the NAMM member search, and the wholesaler's customer list.

**Risks:**
- Authorities may accept only wet signatures.
- Pain per job may be small (a 20-minute form), so willingness to pay is weak.
- The market is ~1,200 firms with older owners.
- SAMM may add filing.
- PlotBox may spread portals across councils.
- Low frequency per mason: a small mason does perhaps 50–200 jobs a year (estimate).

**Kill condition:**
The idea dies if interviews with 10 masons show permits take under 15 minutes each and cause no lost jobs or suspensions, or if SAMM already pre-fills authority forms.

**Founder access:**
A non-local solo founder could sell this by phone and through NAMM, but it is much easier with a UK presence: trade-fair attendance and visits to masons' yards.

**Score:** 5/10

### Opportunity: Scottish moorland licence pack (muirburn, grouse and trap returns)

**Industry:**
Scottish sporting estates, hill farms and crofts that burn heather; gamekeepers

**Buyer:**
Estate factor or head keeper; the land agents who manage estates (law firms and land agents already advise on the regime, e.g. Gillespie Macandrew, Turcan Connell).

**Trigger / Why now:**
The Wildlife Management and Muirburn (Scotland) Act 2024 created three licences:
- **Muirburn licence:** required for any muirburn from 15 Sep 2026. Applications opened July 2026. Licences last up to 5 years. Every burn must be recorded, with an annual return by 31 May.
- **Grouse (s16AA) licence:** in place since 2024, with a returns form.
- **Wildlife trap licence:** delayed from 2025.

**Current workflow:**
1. The applicant maps the burn areas and classifies peatland vs non-peatland using NatureScot's interactive map and peat-depth survey data.
2. They attach evidence of Lantra-approved training and a Muirburn Code declaration, then apply online.
3. During the season, keepers record each burn (date, location, size), on paper or in GWCT's Epicollect5 app.
4. Before 31 May, they compile the annual return. Grouse returns go on a separate NatureScot .docx form; trap licence numbers and records are kept separately.

**Pain:**
- The obligation is new, layered and enforced (licences can be suspended).
- Records and maps must reconcile with the licensed areas and peat depth.
- Pain is expected rather than observed: the first return is due May 2027.

**Existing solutions:**
- GWCT Data Management Services (Epicollect5 recording of ignition points, peat depth, predator control).
- NatureScot's own online application and mapping tool.
- Land agents and solicitors.
- General estate/shoot management apps (not verified for muirburn returns).

**Offline evidence:**
- Returns are on .docx forms.
- The workforce is gamekeepers working on paper or with a free data-collection app.
- Advice comes through GWCT, Scottish Land & Estates and the shooting press, not SaaS listings.

**Offline channel:**
- Scottish Land & Estates.
- Scottish Gamekeepers Association.
- Lantra muirburn course providers, who train every applicant.
- GWCT Scottish advisory (as a partner or competitor).
- Land agents' rural offices.

**Market count:**
Not found. Estimate: a few hundred grouse moors/estates plus a larger, unknown number of farms and crofts doing muirburn. NatureScot has not yet published application counts.

**The gap:**
A single per-estate record that ties licensed areas, peat-depth points, each burn, trap records and grouse bag data together, and produces the NatureScot annual returns. GWCT collects the field data, but the return assembly and the cross-licence view are unconfirmed.

**Possible product:**
A field app that records burns against the licensed polygons and warns before a burn on peat or outside the licensed area. It generates the 31 May muirburn return and the grouse return, and keeps the trap register.

**MVP:**
Import licensed areas as a shapefile or a drawn polygon. Log a burn on the phone with GPS. Export the return in NatureScot's format.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
£300–1,000 per estate per year. Estates pay for keepers' kit and agents' fees; farms and crofts would pay little.

**How to find first customers:**
Scottish Land & Estates members, Lantra course cohorts, and the licence holders NatureScot publishes (if it publishes them; unverified).

**Risks:**
- A tiny market.
- GWCT already offers the recording, and is trusted.
- Political risk: a future change of government or grouse policy.
- Seasonal, annual return.
- It may be a GWCT feature rather than a product.

**Kill condition:**
The idea dies if GWCT's service already produces NatureScot returns, or if fewer than ~300 estates hold muirburn licences after the first season.

**Founder access:**
This needs a Scottish/rural local. Trust in this community is very tribal.

**Score:** 4/10

## 3. Rejected

- **RFD transfer notifications:** looked like the perfect "one sale, ~45 police forces" router, with 2,619 dealers, per-force PDF Form 11s, and post, fax or email. It is killed by Guntrader's Gun Register, which already produces Form 11s and emails the right force, and is used by most RFDs. Also covered by Epos Link Gun Tracker. (gov.uk firearms statistics 2025–26; guntrader.uk/gun-register)
- **Scrap metal dealers:** the duty is to keep records, not to file them. AMCS and yard systems cover the sites, and mobile collectors can't pay.
- **Nanny / household employer payroll:** Nannytax (£276–£474/yr) and similar services.
- **Shellfish registration documents:** paper triplicate forms and an FSA 2025 audit, but the buyer is the LA and the gatherers are poor.
- **First-sale fish sales notes:** free MMO eSales/ERS.
- **Gangmasters (GLAA):** no recurring filing; moving into the Fair Work Agency.
- **Livestock journey logs:** volumes collapsed after the live-export ban.
- **Wales special procedures licensing:** a 3-year licence; consent-record apps exist.
- **Animal activity licensing:** inspections, not filings.
- **Scottish burial authority duties:** public-sector buyer; PlotBox and Byond cover it.
- **Watch list:** knife bulk-sale reporting and seller licensing (Crime and Policing Act 2026, licensing consultation closed Feb 2026); England non-surgical cosmetics licensing (no date yet); national minimum standards for taxis and private hire (sets of standards not yet published).

## 4. Method notes

- **Worked:**
  - "<industry> application form" plus council or diocese names, which surfaces current PDF/DOCX forms (strong offline evidence).
  - Home Office official statistics for dealer counts.
  - Trade press (Stone Specialist, Shooting UK, GWCT) for counts and substitutes.
  - "<industry> software UK", run early to kill ideas fast (Guntrader, AMCS).
- **Didn't work:**
  - Searches for national counts of LA-licensed trades (scrap, animal licences). These only return district FOIs.
  - Queries without UK domain filters drifted to US/Australian results.
  - Allowed-domain filtering (police.uk, gov.uk) fixed that.
