# Vanuatu: offline (quiet) industries pass

Researched 2026-10-05. 6 of 8 WebSearch calls used; WebFetch not used. Facts come from search-result summaries only. Vanuatu has about 330k people (estimate) and about 3,000 VAT-registered businesses (see the existing country report). Regulator registers are thin and mostly not online, so counts are mostly unverified. A short honest report is the right output.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

None. I found no quiet industry in Vanuatu that meets the bar of a mandatory recurring obligation, a few thousand reachable operators, and willingness to pay for software.

Closest candidate, scored 2/10: **Public transport permit and licence renewal assistant (Efate minibuses and taxis)**.
- Buyer: owner-operators of "B" plate vans (up to 1,400 permits).
- Trigger: 2026 renewals and a fuel subsidy tied to a valid permit.
- Substitute: DCIR counter visit. No software gap found.
- Offline channel: the operators' union or the PLTA (name unverified).
- Market count: 1,400 permit ceiling (Daily Post).
- Willingness to pay: only for a done-for-you service. Software value is negligible, and a non-local founder could not sell this.

## 3. Rejected

All rows marked Reject above. The nakamal levy, household payroll and bus permits are annual, single-authority tasks with no software demand. The meat sector has two operators. Second-hand dealers and pawnbrokers have no regime I could find.

## 4. Method notes

- Regulator-first queries returned legislation PDFs on PacLII and some DCIR/VBR/Daily Post news. Counts of operators almost never appear.
- Dealer-register queries returned only Australian registers, suggesting the regime does not exist in Vanuatu.
- Not searched (budget left unused): domestic workers' work permits, cemetery and church bodies, pesticide sales. These are unlikely to change the conclusion for a market this small.
- Vanuatu should be judged only as an add-on to a Fiji/Pacific product, as the existing report concludes.
