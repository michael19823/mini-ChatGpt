# Vietnam: Offline-Industries Pass

*Research date: 2026-10-05. Country report this builds on: `research/countries/vietnam.md`, which covers gold shops, seafood, durian, household tax, EPR, PCCC, CBAM, EUDR and clinics. None of those are repeated here.*

## Limitations (read first)

- **Searches:** 14 of the 40-search budget were used. The session hit usage limits twice, and the coordinator asked for the report to be written from the evidence in hand.
- **WebFetch:** blocked. All evidence comes from search-result snippets. No primary legal text was read in full.
- **Evidence tags:**
  - **[S]** means sourced this session. The URL is listed.
  - **[B]** means background knowledge that was not re-verified. Treat it as unverified.
- **URLs and numbers:** no URL was invented. Every number is either sourced or marked "estimate".
- **Overall finding:** this is an honest, thin report. Vietnam's quiet industries are heavily state-digitised through **free government tools**: VNeID for residence notification, the MAE livestock database built by VNPT, and TE-FOOD for pork traceability in HCMC. Where the state has not digitised a task, cheap POS vendors have already taken the stock ledger: KiotViet, Sapo and MISA eShop. No opportunity in this pass scores above 4.5/10.

## 2. Strongest opportunities (all weak)

### Opportunity 1: ANTT compliance kit for small conditional premises (pawn, karaoke, lodging, gas)

**Industry:**
Small businesses in trades that need a security-and-order (ANTT) certificate: pawnshops, karaoke bars, guesthouses, LPG shops and massage premises.

**Buyer:**
The owner-operator, who is the person named as responsible for security and order on the certificate.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- Decree 282/2025 sets new residence-notification fines for lodging businesses: 2–12M VND per breach, doubled for organisations [S].
- Karaoke licence fines rise from 15/5/2026 [S].
- Provinces publish quarterly police inspection lists, for example Lâm Đồng's list for Q2/2026 [S].

**Current workflow:**
1. The owner keeps the paper register in the police-prescribed format, bought from a stationer or copied from a law-firm template.
2. The owner fills in the periodic report to district or commune police by hand.
3. Separately, the owner notifies guest stays on VNeID or ASM software (lodging only).
4. Separately, the owner keeps PCCC (fire-safety) dossiers.
5. Inspection day means collecting all of it by hand.

**Pain:**
- Police reports note late reporting by pawnshops [S].
- Inspections are scheduled and published [S].
- Fines are material for a micro-business.
- The pain is episodic, around inspection days, rather than daily.

**Existing solutions:**
- Free VNeID for lodging notifications [S].
- Pawn software: LendMe, MISA eShop and Pawnmate [S].
- Hotel property-management systems with lodging-notification connectors [B].
- Paper register books from stationers and law firms such as Luật Minh Khuê and ACC Group [S].
- Law firms that prepare the certificate dossier [S].

**Offline evidence:**
- Registers are prescribed paper books.
- Reports are filed at the counter or by template.
- Police checklists are published as scanned PDFs.

**Offline channel:**
- Commune and district police hold the list of ANTT premises: one district lists 184 by type [S].
- LPG distributors' dealer networks.
- Hiring a local agent to visit premises would also work.
- A non-local solo founder **cannot** sell this. It needs a Vietnamese founder with police-liaison comfort.

**Market count:**
- One sample district had 184 premises [S].
- National total: unknown. Estimate: tens of thousands of premises, excluding household landlords.

**The gap:**
- No single tool assembles the ANTT register, periodic report, PCCC file and inspection pack across trades.
- Each trade's vertical tool covers only its own core ledger.

**Possible product:**
A mobile "inspection-ready" folder. It produces the police-format register and periodic report from simple daily entries, and reminds the owner of certificate, PCCC and report deadlines.

**MVP:**
- Pawnshops and karaoke in one province.
- Register in the official layout, printable periodic report, deadline reminders.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
100–200k VND/month (about US$4–8), or a done-for-you dossier service at 1–3M VND a year. Owners are more likely to pay for the service than for software.

**How to find first customers:**
- Provincial police inspection lists, such as Lâm Đồng's for Q2/2026 [S].
- Business-registration lookups by industry code.
- Door-to-door visits.

**Risks:**
- Police may add these trades to VNeID or a national ANTT portal for free.
- ARPU is very low.
- Selling to police-regulated trades is trust-sensitive.

**Kill condition:**
- The Ministry of Public Security announces an online ANTT reporting portal, or
- owners say the paper register costs them less than an hour a month.

**Score:** 3.5/10

### Opportunity 2: Regulator-format ledger and licence kit for pesticide and fertiliser retailers

**Industry:**
Agricultural-input retail: pesticide (thuốc BVTV) and fertiliser shops.

**Buyer:**
The owner of a rural đại lý (agent shop), often a family household business.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- **Decree 33/2026/NĐ-CP** (21/01/2026) rewrites the conditions for trading pesticide and fertiliser and for certificates [S].
- **Decree 214/2026/NĐ-CP**, effective 05/08/2026, sets fines of:
  - 2–3M VND for not maintaining trading conditions;
  - 2–4M VND for selling pesticide together with other goods;
  - 3–5M VND for trading without a certificate [S].
- Household businesses also move to tax self-declaration from 1/1/2026 (see the country report).

**Current workflow:**
1. The shop keeps a paper stock-in/out/balance register (sổ nhập–xuất–tồn), which is mandatory [S].
2. It checks products against the permitted and banned lists by hand.
3. It keeps the seller's training certificate and the shop certificate current.
4. Market-surveillance and agriculture inspectors check conditions, labels and the register [S].

**Pain:**
- Lâm Đồng market surveillance ran 61 inspections in the first 8 months of 2026, found 59 violations and fined over 602M VND [S].
- An Giang inspectors seized 3,690 pesticide units for label violations [S].
- Most of this is label and condition enforcement rather than register failure, so the register pain itself is unproven.

**Existing solutions:**
- KiotViet, Sapo and MISA eShop all handle inventory for agri-input shops. FPT Shop lists 5+ dedicated "vật tư nông nghiệp" POS products [S].
- Paper registers.
- Law firms and training centres for the certificate (mnec.edu.vn [S]).

**Offline evidence:**
- Paper register.
- Commune-level shops.
- Inspections are reported as in-person market-surveillance campaigns [S].

**Offline channel:**
- Pesticide and fertiliser distributors: each manufacturer runs a dealer network of level-1 and level-2 đại lý [B].
- Provincial plant-protection sub-departments run the mandatory seller training courses. That is a captive room of new shop owners.
- A local founder is needed.

**Market count:**
Unverified. Estimate: about 30,000+ pesticide retail points [B]. Fertiliser shops are additional. No official count was found.

**The gap:**
- POS tools track stock for business purposes.
- They do not reliably check products against the current MAE permitted and banned list.
- They do not print the register in the inspector's format.
- They do not track certificate and training expiry.

**Possible product:**
An add-on to an agri-input POS, or a standalone app, that:
- flags banned or deregistered active ingredients when stock is received;
- prints the register in the inspection format;
- keeps an inspection pack with the certificate, training certificate and invoices.

**MVP:**
Barcode or name lookup against the published MAE pesticide list, plus a printable stock register, for one province.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- 100–150k VND/month per shop, or
- distributor-paid at about 50k VND per dealer per month, as a dealer-loyalty tool. The distributor-paid model is more realistic.

**How to find first customers:**
Distributor dealer lists, provincial training classes, and the lists of certified shops that provincial agriculture departments publish [B].

**Risks:**
- KiotViet or MISA adds the banned-list check cheaply.
- Willingness to pay is very low.
- The pain is dominated by labels and counterfeits, which the shop can't fix.

**Kill condition:**
- Inspectors don't actually check the register, or
- a major POS already ships the MAE list.

**Score:** 4/10

### Opportunity 3: Print-job register and report for small printing shops

**Industry:**
Printing and post-press establishments (cơ sở in).

**Buyer:**
The owner or manager of a small offset or digital print shop.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- In May 2026 the HCMC culture department and city police inspected printing establishments [S]. Breaches found:
  - unlicensed post-press work;
  - licences not updated after changes;
  - **incomplete files for print jobs**;
  - **missing periodic reports** [S].
- The government is also proposing policies for the printing industry in 2026 [S].

**Current workflow:**
1. For each order, the shop collects the customer's documents and a sample.
2. It writes the job into the print-job register (sổ quản lý ấn phẩm nhận in) by hand.
3. It archives the files.
4. It compiles the periodic report to the provincial culture department from the register.

**Pain:**
- The violations cited in the HCMC inspection are exactly the record-keeping and reporting tasks [S].
- The scale of fines was not verified this session.

**Existing solutions:**
- Print-shop MIS and quoting tools [B, not verified].
- Paper registers.
- The provincial online public-service portal for licence procedures [B].

**Offline evidence:**
- Inspections are done in person.
- Breaches concern physical record files [S].

**Offline channel:**
- The Vietnam Printing Association and its provincial associations [B].
- The VietnamPrintPack and HanoiPrintPack trade fairs, which drew 350 exhibitors in 2025 [S].
- Equipment and consumables dealers.

**Market count:**
- 2,402 licensed printing establishments as of 14/02/2023 [S].
- Registered (non-licence) shops are additional and uncounted.

**The gap:**
- Print MIS handles quoting and production.
- No evidence was found that it generates the legal job register and the periodic report.

**Possible product:**
A job-intake form (customer, ID or permit, quantity, sample upload) that writes the legal register automatically and produces the periodic report.

**MVP:**
A web form plus register export plus report template, for HCMC.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
200–400k VND/month.

**How to find first customers:**
Licence lists from provincial culture departments [B] and trade-fair exhibitor lists.

**Risks:**
- The market is small, at around 2,400 licensed shops.
- The pain is episodic.
- Print MIS vendors could add the feature.

**Kill condition:**
- Fewer than about 1,000 shops do work that falls under the register requirement, or
- the culture departments move reporting into their own portal with job-level entry.

**Score:** 3/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Boarding-house and guest residence notification.** The obligation is real and fined (Decree 282/2025 [S]). It was killed by **free VNeID**, whose channels Circular 116/2026/TT-BCA extended [S]. Hotels' property-management connectors cover the paying segment [B].
- **Pawnshop register software.** Already served by LendMe, MISA eShop and Pawnmate [S]. It survives only inside the cross-trade ANTT kit (Opportunity 1).
- **Livestock activity declarations.** Covered by the free MAE/VNPT national database and app [S]. The buyers are smallholders.
- **Pork and poultry traceability in HCMC.** TE-FOOD runs the city programme [S].
- **Domestic-worker employers.** No recurring filing exists. The employer only pays an insurance-equivalent amount to the worker [S].
- **Scrap dealers.** Not an ANTT-conditional trade. There is no register or recurring report to serve [S].
- **Driving schools.** DAT and telematics hardware vendors serve them [B].

## 4. Method notes

- **What worked:** Vietnamese queries built from the regulator's own vocabulary. Examples: "sổ theo dõi", "báo cáo định kỳ", "điều kiện an ninh trật tự", "Giấy chứng nhận đủ điều kiện", plus a decree number. They surfaced:
  - new 2026 decrees (33/2026, 214/2026, 282/2025, 283/2026);
  - provincial police and department PDFs, including inspection lists and district counts of ANTT premises.
- **What didn't work:**
  - National operator counts are rarely published. Only the printing count (2,402) was found.
  - Search snippets often give district-level samples instead.
- **Structural finding:** in Vietnam the state is unusually quick to put quiet-industry obligations into free apps (VNeID, the livestock database, eCDT, TE-FOOD in HCMC). Software-vendor-led markets are the exception. The paper-to-portal gap the brief looks for is mostly already closed, or closed for free.
- **Follow-up:** with more budget, the next targets would be LPG retailers and veterinary-drug shops, and the actual fine scale for the print-job register.
