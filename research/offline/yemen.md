# Yemen: Offline (quiet) industries pass

**Verdict: no opportunity. Yemen stays inaccessible for a non-local solo founder.** The existing report (`research/countries/yemen.md`) already found FTO and OFAC exposure in the north, a split central bank, and no card or subscription rails. A quiet-industry lens does not change those blockers. It adds one more: the quiet industries are mostly informal and have no machine-readable register.

Search budget used: 6 of 8. Most queries returned Gulf, Iraqi or Omani material rather than Yemen-specific regulator pages. "Not found" below means not found in these searches, not "does not exist".

## 2. Strongest opportunities

None. No quiet industry cleared the minimum bar of an identified obligation, a countable register, a buyer who can pay, and a seller who can legally and practically reach them.

The only partially live thread is the exchange-house sector. CBY-Aden's 2025 campaign (suspensions, revocations and closures citing AML/CFT law) shows real enforcement. A possible product would be an AML/KYC and licence-readiness register for small exchange houses in the government-controlled south. It was not scored as an opportunity because:
- UNMONEY is regulator-mandated infrastructure;
- no payment rail exists for a foreign vendor to collect fees;
- the total number of licensed operators is unknown;
- a sanctions screen of every customer would be needed.

## 3. Rejected

- Domestic-worker payroll: no Yemen regime found.
- Scrap and gold dealer registers: nothing found.
- Honey export traceability: poor buyers, northern sanctions exposure, better served from Saudi importers.
- Well-driller reporting: no register or count.
- Exchange-house AML compliance: regulator-provided network and no rails (see section 2).
- Taxi and moto-taxi licensing: bans, informality, tiny ticket size.

## 4. Method notes

- Arabic queries about domestic workers and dealer registers returned Saudi and Gulf regulator pages. Yemeni regulators barely publish searchable registers, which is itself the finding.
- The only productive Yemeni regulator source was the Central Bank of Yemen-Aden news feed and Yemen Monitor, for exchange-house enforcement.
- Search counts for other sectors were not obtainable. Statements about their size would be invention.
- Any Yemen angle is best treated as an add-on to a Gulf trade-compliance product, as in the country report.
