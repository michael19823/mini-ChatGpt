# Zambia: Offline (quiet) industries pass

Research date: 2026-10-05. Budget: 20 WebSearch calls, all used. 18 returned results. Two calls failed on a temporary usage limit, and I resumed after it reset. No WebFetch was used. Every fact below comes from search-result summaries. Anything not confirmed by a source is marked **unverified** or **estimate**.

The existing country report (`research/countries/zambia.md`) covers mining local content (SI 68 / LOCAS), Smart Invoice input-VAT checks, payroll, customs agents, ZAMRA traceability and ZEMA licensing. None of those is repeated here.

**Bottom line:** Zambia's quiet industries are regulated mostly through **one-off or multi-year licences** (PACRA, RTSA, WARMA, the Minerals Regulation Commission). Few of them carry **recurring per-job filings**, which is what produces software pain. The best candidates are:

- **agro-dealers** under the new national e-voucher input programme, the first season run fully on e-vouchers;
- **borehole drillers**, who make per-borehole filings to WARMA.

Both are weak to moderate. The livestock traceability regime is the trigger to watch, but its Act has not commenced yet.

## 2. Strongest opportunities

### Opportunity: E-voucher redemption and payment reconciliation for agro-dealers (CATSP)

**Industry:**
Agricultural input retail: agro-dealers selling seed, fertiliser and agrochemicals, from rural single-shop dealers to provincial distributors.

**Buyer:**
The owner-manager of an agro-dealer registered for the e-voucher programme. Secondary buyer: seed and fertiliser companies that supply many agro-dealers and carry the credit risk.

**Trigger / Why now:**
2025/26 is the first season in which **all 116 districts use e-vouchers exclusively** for the Comprehensive Agriculture Transformation Support Programme, which replaces FISP. It targets 1,024,434 farmers ([Zambia Monitor](https://www.zambiamonitor.com/?p=67834)). Agro-dealers must declare their provincial, district and constituency coverage. The government says arrears are cleared and that payments will now be **fortnightly**.

**Current workflow (inferred; not observed):**
1. The dealer gets stock on credit from seed and fertiliser companies, ahead of the season.
2. Farmers redeem e-vouchers at the shop through the programme's system (POS or phone). The platform operator is **unverified**.
3. The dealer keeps a paper or Excel log of redemptions per product to cross-check the system.
4. The dealer waits for the fortnightly payment and matches the payment amounts to redemptions by hand.
5. The dealer chases discrepancies with the district agricultural office and the supplier.

**Pain:**
- 2024/25 reports mention system downtime and queues at redemption points ([Zambia Monitor](https://www.zambiamonitor.com/?p=70348)).
- Earlier seasons had payment arrears to agro-dealers. The ministry's statement that "all outstanding payments had been cleared" implies the backlog existed.
- Every unreconciled redemption is unpaid inventory that was bought on supplier credit.
- The pain is seasonal, concentrated from about October to January.

**Existing solutions:**
- The ministry's e-voucher platform itself, which presumably provides some dealer statement (**unverified**).
- Generic POS and stock software (Pastel/Sage, QuickBooks, local POS vendors).
- Paper exercise books and Excel.
- Supplier account managers at the seed companies (Zamseed, SeedCo and others named in the trade; their involvement here is **unverified**), who reconcile on dealers' behalf.

**Offline evidence:**
- The dealers are rural shops.
- The programme's documented problems are physical: queues, downtime, travel costs.
- No agro-dealer software listings were found for Zambia.

**Offline channel:**
- Seed and fertiliser companies' dealer networks and annual dealer meetings.
- District Agricultural Coordinator offices, which hold the dealer list.
- The Zambia National Farmers Union (ZNFU), whose agro-dealer involvement is **unverified**.
- WhatsApp outreach to the dealer list if the ministry publishes it (**unverified**).

**Market count:**
More than 650 registered agro-dealers ([AgriFocus Africa, Feb 2026](https://agrifocusafrica.com/2026/02/27/a-smarter-way-to-support-farmers-in-zambia/)). Farmers served: 1,024,434 (ministry target, 2025/26). Historically, earlier e-voucher attempts were undermined by unpaid supplier arrears ([LSE case study](https://www.lse.ac.uk/africa/assets/Documents/AICP-Working-Papers/Zambia-evoucher-case-study.pdf), [Freshplaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/north-america/article/2148887/zambia-s-agriculture-e-voucher-failed/)). Because the universe is about 650 dealers, a dealer-paid tool is small. The supplier-paid angle matters more.

**The gap:**
The programme's system is built for the government (farmer eligibility, fraud control), not for the dealer's cash flow. No tool was found that ties redemptions and stock to fortnightly payments and supplier credit lines.

**Possible product:**
A phone-first dealer ledger. It captures each redemption (or imports the dealer statement if one exists), matches fortnightly payments, flags unpaid or disputed redemptions, and produces a dispute pack for the district office. It also produces a statement for the supplier who extended credit.

**MVP:**
- An Android or WhatsApp form to log redemptions and stock received;
- a "paid vs owed" report each fortnight;
- PDF export for the district office and the supplier.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
- Dealers: K150–400 per month in season (about $6–16, an estimate).
- More realistic: the supplier pays $2–5 per dealer per month to see dealer-level exposure. Rural dealers will likely pay only for a done-for-you service, if at all.

**How to find first customers:**
- Through 1–2 seed or fertiliser companies' dealer networks (B2B2B).
- District agricultural offices.
- Agro-dealer lists in provincial tender and coverage declarations.

**Founder access:**
A non-local founder could not realistically sell this alone. It needs a local partner with agro-input sector relationships.

**Risks:**
- The ministry's platform may already give dealers a full statement.
- The programme design changes almost every season (FISP, then e-voucher, then CATSP).
- The pain is seasonal.
- Dealers have low willingness to pay.
- Political risk around who controls redemption data.

**Kill condition:**
Kill the idea if any one of these is true:
- the platform already shows each dealer a reconciled redemption and payment statement;
- payments really do arrive fortnightly with few disputes (ask 10 dealers);
- suppliers won't pay for dealer-level visibility.

**Score:** 4/10

### Opportunity: WARMA borehole job pack for licensed drillers

**Industry:**
Borehole drilling contractors: domestic, farm and institutional boreholes, plus NGO and government rural water contracts.

**Buyer:**
The owner or operations manager of a WARMA-licensed drilling company.

**Trigger / Why now:**
- Three 2018 statutory instruments set the rules: Groundwater and Boreholes; Licensing of Drillers; Charges and Fees ([rural-water-supply.net](https://rural-water-supply.net/en/resources/803-)). They require a **notice of intention to drill**, **borehole registration** and **defect reporting**, and only licensed drillers may drill, with **GPS on rigs**.
- Enforcement attention is rising. WARMA has publicly warned that boreholes in Lusaka West, Makeni and Chongwe may dry up ([Zambia Monitor](https://www.zambiamonitor.com/water-authority-raises-the-alarm-says-boreholes-in-lusaka-west-makeni-chongwe-others-may-dry-up/)).
- No 2025–26 rule change was found, so the "why now" is weak.

**Current workflow (inferred):**
1. The client (household or farm) contracts the driller.
2. The driller files a notice of intention to drill with WARMA, using a paper form or the counter (format **unverified**).
3. On site, the driller records the drilling log: lithology, depth, water strikes, yield test.
4. The driller writes a completion report for the client, and for NGO jobs a separate donor format.
5. The driller or client registers the borehole with WARMA and pays the registration fee.
6. The driller files a defect report if needed.

**Pain:**
- Every job needs a regulator filing plus a client report plus, often, a donor or NGO report in a different format. This is the "one job, many receivers" pattern.
- Unlicensed or unregistered drilling is targeted by enforcement.
- There are no penalty examples in my sources.

**Existing solutions:**
- Paper drilling logbooks.
- Excel templates.
- Hydrogeological consultants who write completion reports.
- Global borehole-log software (e.g. general geotechnical logging tools; none found that is specific to Zambia).
- NGO M&E systems (mWater and others; their use in Zambia is **unverified**) on the donor side.

**Offline evidence:**
- The regulations specify paper notices and registration.
- Drillers are small contractors with no web presence of their own beyond Facebook pages (**unverified**).

**Offline channel:**
- WARMA's licensed-driller list.
- The Zambia drillers' association, whose existence is **unverified**.
- Rig and consumables suppliers in Lusaka.
- NGO WASH tender lists, which name the drilling contractors.

**Market count:**
**Unverified.** Estimate: 100–300 licensed firms. One academic source notes that most licensed drillers are class-B international firms ([UNZA thesis](https://dspace.unza.zm/handle/123456789/9073)).

**The gap:**
No tool was found that turns one field drilling log into the WARMA notice and registration data, the client completion certificate and the NGO report format.

**Possible product:**
A mobile drilling log that generates WARMA forms, a client completion report and donor-format reports, and keeps a register of each driller's boreholes for licence renewal.

**MVP:**
A form-based field log and three PDF outputs.

**Pricing hypothesis:**
$20–60 per month per rig, or $3–5 per borehole report (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**
- WARMA's licensed-driller list (request it or find it on the website, **unverified**).
- NGO tender award notices.

**Founder access:**
A non-local founder could build this from abroad. Selling it needs one local driller as a design partner.

**Risks:**
- The market is tiny.
- WARMA could digitise the filings itself.
- Drillers may simply skip the filings.
- The product overlaps with generic field-form apps (KoBo, ODK).

**Kill condition:**
Kill the idea if fewer than about 150 licensed drillers exist, or if WARMA notices are a one-page form that takes a few minutes.

**Score:** 3/10

### Watch item (not scored as an opportunity): livestock traceability under the AIT Act 2024

The Animal Identification and Traceability Act 2024 creates:
- an Animal Identification and Traceability Unit;
- registration of identification marks **and of agents**;
- traceability of animals and of animal products and by-products.

It repeals the Animal Identification Act 2010. It **has not commenced**: it waits for a ministerial SI ([ZambiaLII](https://zambialii.org/akn/zm/act/2024/2/eng@2024-04-18), [Parliament](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/node/11699)). The Animal Health (Amendment) Bill 2025 moves all traceability matters into the AIT Act ([Parliament committee report](https://www.parliament.gov.zm/sites/default/files/documents/committee_reports/REPORT%20OF%20THE%20COMMITTEE%20ON%20AGRICULTURE%2C%20LANDS%20%26%20NATURAL%20RESOURCES%20ON%20THE%20ANIMAL%20HEALTH%20%28AMENDMENT%29%20BILL%2C%20N.A.B%20NO.%204%20OF%202025%20FOR%20THE%20FOURTH%20SESSION%20OF%20THE%20THIRTEENTH%20NATIONAL%20ASSEMBLY.pdf)). The 2026 FMD outbreak in seven southern districts was linked to illegal cattle movement, which is the political case for commencement.

If the commencement SI appears, registered agents, cattle traders and abattoirs will have per-movement record duties. That would fit this pass well. Revisit it then, and find out whether the ministry builds the system itself (the most likely outcome) or leaves room for agent-side tools.

## 3. Rejected

- **Domestic-worker payroll and NAPSA for households.** The obligation exists, but there is no enforcement evidence, and contributions are about K65 a month per side, so no household will pay. The substitute is cash and nothing.
- **Scrap metal dealer registers.** The 1960 Act requires a K750 PACRA licence. No trigger and no police-register enforcement were found.
- **Charcoal permits.** Permits are being banned district by district. The trade is informal, and corruption in permit issuance is documented by TI Zambia.
- **Minibus and bus Road Service Licences.** The workflow happens once every 3 years. Operators are cash-poor. RTSA's e-ZAMTIS is the substitute.
- **Honey export traceability.** Too few exporters. Government and donors run the traceability work.
- **Mineral trading permits and artisanal returns.** Return frequency is unverified. The market is partly illicit. The Minerals Regulation Commission cadastre is already digital.
- **Liquor licences.** Annual council renewal only, and no trigger found.
- **Fishing licences and fish traders.** Non-compliance is mass, the licence is annual, and buyers are cash-poor.
- **Funeral and burial permits.** Each burial needs a permit, but there is only one receiving body (the council), and no 2025–26 trigger was found.

## 4. Method notes

What worked:
- Searching ZambiaLII, parliament.gov.zm and policyvault.africa for "<industry> Act" was the best source of obligations and commencement status.
- Zambia Monitor and Lusaka Times gave enforcement news.

What did not work:
- Queries asking for counts ("number of licensed drillers", "registered agro-dealers") returned nothing. Zambian regulators rarely publish registers online, so every market count here is unverified.
- English is the right search language. Local languages (Bemba, Nyanja) were not tried, since official texts are in English.

Market traders were not assessed because the budget ran out.
