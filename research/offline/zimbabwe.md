# Zimbabwe: offline-industries pass

**Budget used:** 20 of 20 WebSearch calls. WebFetch was not used. Every claim below comes from the
search-result snippets of those 20 searches. Anything not backed by a snippet is marked
**unverified** or **estimate**.

**Context carried over from the country report:** the market is accessible but has friction (USD/ZiG,
EcoCash, card collection is hard). The country report covered POTRAZ data-protection licensing,
TIMB tobacco contractors and ZIMRA fiscalisation, and none of those is repeated here.

**Headline:** Zimbabwe's quiet industries are heavily regulated *on paper*, and 2026 brought real
enforcement: the butchery and stock-theft blitz, the mining safety blitz, and digitisation of the
mining cadastre. But willingness to pay for software is low, operators are mostly informal, and the
state is building its own free tools in two places (the DVS Digital Livestock Stock Card and the
Mining Cadastre). Nothing scores above 5. The best shape is a **done-for-you compliance service with
light software**, sold through a local partner.

## 2. Opportunities

### Opportunity: Small-scale miner title-compliance service (claims, cadastre, returns, EIA)

**Industry:**  
Artisanal and small-scale gold (and chrome) mining claim holders

**Buyer:**  
The registered holder of a block of claims (often an individual or a small syndicate). The second buyer is a mining-services consultant or a district miners' association that manages compliance for many claim holders.

**Trigger / Why now:**  
- General Notice 01 of 2025 moves all titles to the Computerised Mining Cadastre Information Management System (CMCIMS). From **1 July 2025** every title holder must submit updated coordinates to the new geospatial standard. More than 60,000 licences have been digitised, and full rollout is being finalised in 2026.
- The Mines and Minerals Bill 2025 has been gazetted.
- A 2025 "Responsible Mining Audit" checked whether production returns were being submitted.
- A National Safety Enforcement Blitz in 2026 targets ASM (ground collapses caused 54% of Q1 2026 fatalities).

**Current workflow:**  
1. The holder pays annual inspection fees to the Provincial Mining Director and gets an inspection certificate. Without one, the claim can be forfeited.
2. The holder hires a surveyor or consultant to re-capture coordinates for the cadastre.
3. The holder hires an EIA consultant for EMA (historically US$1,500–3,000; EMA's streamlined ASM route is about US$253).
4. The holder submits production returns and delivers gold to FGR buying centres, keeping FGR receipts.
5. All of this is tracked in paper files at the claim and at the provincial mining office.

**Pain:**  
Losing the title through forfeiture is the most expensive possible failure. Historic mass forfeitures happened (the Sunday News 2020 headline "Govt forfeits 1 000 mining claims"). Enforcement blitzes are under way, and EIA costs have pushed miners to operate without certificates.

**Existing solutions:**  
- Government CMCIMS (registry only, run by the regulator);
- local mining consultants, surveyors and EIA practitioners (paid per job);
- FGR one-stop service centres (a buyer-side service, not compliance);
- associations: Zimbabwe Miners Federation (ZMF) and district associations (exact services unverified).

I found no SaaS aimed at ASM title compliance in Zimbabwe (an absence, not verified exhaustively).

**Offline evidence:**  
Title administration was paper-based until the cadastre. About 85% of ASM are unregistered. Miners work in remote areas with poor connectivity and rely on consultants and provincial offices.

**Offline channel:**  
District ASM associations and ZMF meetings; FGR buying centres and one-stop service centres (miners physically visit to sell gold); Provincial Mining Director offices; the mining consultants and surveyors who already serve the miners (resell through them).

**Market count:**  
More than 1 million ASM operators (ZMF estimate). More than 60,000 mining titles digitised in the cadastre (Ministry, via press). Titled claim holders are the real buyers, so ~60,000 is the ceiling. That number covers *all* titles, including large mines.

**The gap:**  
No one gives a claim holder one calendar and evidence file covering all of these: inspection-fee due date, cadastre coordinate status, production returns, EIA/EMA renewal, and FGR delivery records. Consultants handle each piece as a separate one-off job.

**Possible product:**  
A compliance tracker sold through consultants. A consultant enters a client's claims once and gets due-date alerts (by SMS or WhatsApp) for inspection fees, returns and EMA renewals, plus a printable evidence pack for inspectors. A done-for-you tier files on the client's behalf.

**MVP:**  
A multi-client dashboard for one consultant firm or one district association: claim register, due dates, document vault, and SMS reminders to miners.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
US$5–10 per claim per month through consultants, or US$100–300/month per consultant firm (estimate). Miners pay for done-for-you filing, not for software.

**How to find first customers:**  
Approach 2–3 mining consultancies and surveyors in Kwekwe, Gweru, Bindura and Shurugwi (all unverified as hubs, though these are known gold districts). Pitch district association executives and ZMF.

**Risks:**  
- The cadastre may add its own reminders and online payments.
- Politically connected operators ignore the rules.
- Cash-based economy.
- Selling it **needs a local partner**; a non-local solo founder cannot realistically sell it.
- Sanctions screening of mining-linked customers.

**Kill condition:**  
CMCIMS offers online renewal with automatic notices, or consultants say clients won't pay a recurring fee.

**Score:** 5/10 (Pain 7, Frequency 5, Mandatory 8, Fragmentation 5, Competition 6, Gap 5, Buyer access 4, WTP 4, MVP 7, Distribution 4. Distribution goes through a named offline channel, but only with a local partner.)

### Opportunity: Butchery meat-provenance file ("Nyama Yabvepi-ready")

**Industry:**  
Butcheries and small abattoirs

**Buyer:**  
Butchery owner-operator, small abattoir operator, or a chain of 2–10 butcheries

**Trigger / Why now:**  
In May 2026 a nationwide police blitz against stock theft and illegal meat led to about 3,000 arrests for operating without butchery licences, failing to produce stock movement permits, and breaching public-health and veterinary rules. 205 butcheries and 125 food outlets were closed. Earlier "Operation Nyama Yabvepi" ("where did the meat come from?") rounds closed 111 butcheries.

**Current workflow:**  
1. The butcher buys a carcass from a registered abattoir, or buys live animals and gets them slaughtered there.
2. The butcher keeps paper copies of the DVS movement permit, the police stock clearance certificate and the abattoir slaughter/inspection documentation.
3. When police or health inspectors raid, the butcher must produce matching documents for the meat on the hooks.
4. Abattoirs must also keep throughput, condemnation, water-test and food-handler medical records for 3 years.

**Pain:**  
A missing permit means closure and arrest, and the 2026 blitz shows this is enforced. The paper trail matching stock to documents is the weak point.

**Existing solutions:**  
- Paper files and receipt books;
- DVS Digital Livestock Stock Card (government, free; covers ownership, movement and transactions, but is aimed at farmers);
- E-Livestock Global (Mastercard-backed blockchain traceability, aimed at farmers and feedlots);
- large processors (e.g. Colcom) have their own systems;
- Zimbabwe Association of Abattoirs (guidance).

**Offline evidence:**  
Permits are paper documents from police posts and vet offices. Enforcement is by physical raid. There are no butchery software listings for Zimbabwe (absence not exhaustively verified).

**Offline channel:**  
Zimbabwe Association of Abattoirs (members supply butcheries), local-authority health departments that license butcheries, and butchery equipment and packaging suppliers. Owners could also be called directly via councils' licensed-premises lists (if obtainable; unverified).

**Market count:**  
Unknown. 205 butcheries were closed in one blitz, which suggests several thousand nationally (**estimate**).

**The gap:**  
Nothing links each carcass to its permit, its clearance and its abattoir record, or produces a one-page "show the inspector" file per batch. The DVS stock card stops at the farmer.

**Possible product:**  
A phone app: photograph the permit, clearance and abattoir slip, tag them to a purchase batch, and produce an inspection-ready provenance file plus a stock reconciliation.

**MVP:**  
WhatsApp or SMS intake of document photos into a per-butchery dated ledger, with a printable monthly register.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
US$5–15/month per butchery (estimate). WTP is low. Abattoirs might pay more (US$30–50) for the 3-year record set.

**How to find first customers:**  
Through the abattoir association and local health departments in Harare and Bulawayo.

**Risks:**  
- DVS may extend the Digital Stock Card to butchers and abattoirs, which would be free and authoritative.
- Owners whose meat comes from stolen stock won't want a record.
- Very low WTP.
- **Needs a local seller.**

**Kill condition:**  
DVS or police accept the Digital Stock Card as proof at butchery level, or butchers say a plastic sleeve file is enough.

**Score:** 4/10

### Opportunity: Kombi fleet-owner compliance calendar (weak)

**Industry:**  
Commuter omnibus (kombi) and taxi fleet owners

**Buyer:**  
An owner of 2–20 kombis, or a kombi association

**Trigger / Why now:**  
- SI 6 and SI 10 of 2026 (Jan 2026) reset operator licences to US$125/vehicle and route permits to US$20.
- The 60 km radius rule for new route permits applies from Sept 2024.
- In ZIMRA Public Notice 69 of 2025, tax clearance (ITF263) was to be valid for **one month** in 2026. ZIMRA was reconsidering this in Dec 2025, and the outcome is unverified.
- Presumptive tax is US$50/month per kombi, collected by ZINARA.

**Current workflow:**  
1. The owner pays presumptive tax and the ZINARA licence at the counter.
2. The owner renews the operator licence and route permit at the Ministry of Transport.
3. The owner keeps ITF263, insurance and permits in each vehicle for police roadblocks.
4. Seizures happen when anything has lapsed.

**Pain:**  
Kombis get impounded when papers lapse (theanchor.co.zw, "Govt steps up kombi seizures"), and fleets have many expiry dates per vehicle.

**Existing solutions:**  
The ZINARA counter (one-stop collection), runners and agents who renew for owners (unverified), paper.

**Offline evidence:**  
Everything is paid and renewed at counters, and documents are physical papers carried in the vehicle.

**Offline channel:**  
Kombi rank marshals and associations (e.g. GPTOA, unverified for 2026), insurance brokers who sell motor cover to kombis, and ZINARA offices.

**Market count:**  
Unknown (estimate: tens of thousands of vehicles).

**The gap:**  
Expiry tracking across several documents per vehicle, which is generic fleet-reminder territory.

**Possible product:**  
An SMS/WhatsApp expiry tracker per vehicle, plus a done-for-you renewal runner service.

**MVP:**  
Spreadsheet import of the fleet, then SMS reminders.

**Pricing hypothesis:**  
US$2–5 per vehicle per month (estimate).

**How to find first customers:**  
Owners at ranks, and kombi associations.

**Risks:**  
- This is a generic reminder app, a trap the brief warns about.
- Payment already collapses at ZINARA.
- Policy churn (ZUPCO franchise, bans).
- Needs a local.

**Kill condition:**  
ZIMRA keeps annual ITF263, or owners say runners already handle it.

**Score:** 3/10

## 3. Rejected

- **Livestock traders and auctions:** the government's free DVS Digital Livestock Stock Card (2025–26) covers ownership, movement clearance and transactions. E-Livestock Global (Mastercard) also exists.
- **Domestic-worker payroll:** the new minimum wage is US$90–117/month (June 2026) and households are not enforced against, so there is no budget and no trigger.
- **Scrap metal dealers:** Ch. 409 requires a register and police reporting, but I found no 2025–26 enforcement or reform, so the obligation looks dormant (unverified).
- **Second-hand goods dealers:** the 1898 Act requires a police book. The trade sits in informal markets, raids target stolen goods, and dealers won't pay to keep the book.
- **Gold buyers:** FGR is the sole buyer, so there is no market.
- **Borehole drillers:** the GW1/GW4 permits are one-time per borehole, and drillers or property agents can file them as a service.
- **Burial societies and funeral assurers:** burial societies are informal, and only 8 IPEC-regulated funeral assurers exist.
- **Council business licences:** the 2025–26 consolidation (11 licences abolished, fees cut) reduces the burden.
- **EMA effluent compliance:** the buyers are industrial firms, not quiet micro-operators, and consultants handle it.
- **Tobacco growers:** TIMB and contractors own the systems (see the country report).

## 4. Method notes

What worked:
- Searching for **enforcement news** ("crackdown", "blitz", "closed", "arrested") in the Herald, NewsDay, allAfrica and Business Times. Zimbabwe's state press reports enforcement in detail.
- Searching for **statutory-instrument numbers and fee changes** (SI 6/10 of 2026, General Notice 01 of 2025).
- ZimLII for old dealer Acts.

What didn't work:
- No public licensing registers turned up in search, so every operator count except mining titles and tobacco growers is unknown.
- Association websites (ZMF, ZAA, kombi associations) were mostly invisible.
- English was sufficient. Shona and Ndebele were not tried; operation names like "Nyama Yabvepi" are the only local-language hooks.

Next steps:
- Phone-level validation with a mining consultant and the abattoir association.
- Obtain the CMCIMS fee and renewal rules.
