# Competitor check: law firms (Algeria)

Dropped idea: C0013, Law 25-11 data-protection compliance. No URL known; type: consultant. 5 of 6 WebSearch calls used.

## Verdict: weak

Law firms are the default way to get Law 25-11 compliance, but I found no firm-specific productised offer, no published prices and no complaints. Hourly or retainer advice is a service, not a software competitor, and it leaves room for a tool.

## Evidence
- Law 25-11 of 24 July 2025 amended Law 18-07. It adds a mandatory DPO, records of processing, DPIAs, prior consultation and breach notification within 5 days. Source: https://algeriatech.news/?p=22415 and https://cookie-script.com/fr/privacy-laws/algeria-data-protection-law-18-07-and-amendments
- algeriatech.news says there is no SME exemption for the DPO, and that the ANPDP is expanding inspections to the private sector (secondary source, unverified). Source: https://algeriatech.news/?p=22415
- Outsourced DPO: the same coverage says small and mid-size organisations often find an external DPO, "typically a lawyer or privacy consultant on retainer", easier than a full-time hire. Source: https://algeriatech.news/?p=29209
- A large firm, Gide, has published client alerts on the ANPDP and Algerian data-protection rules. That is thought leadership aimed at corporate clients, not a priced SME service. Sources: https://www.gide.com/en/news-insights/protection-of-personal-data-focus-on-algerian-regulations/ and https://www.gide.com/sites/default/files/2023-07-31-gide_alerteclient_anpdp_en_5.pdf
- Local legal work is described as "lawyers billing by the hour, and spreadsheets", with no standardised digital workflow. This is an opinion piece from a tech outlet. Source: https://algeriatech.news/?p=25216
- Searches in Arabic and French (6 results pages in total) returned only academic papers on Law 18-07 and the general legal explainers above. I found no small-firm websites, service pages or reviews for data-protection compliance.
- **Complaints: none found.** I found no reviews, forum threads or news about law-firm data-protection work in Algeria. This is not evidence of satisfaction. Review coverage of Algerian legal services is likely thin.

## Pricing
- No law-firm fees found (unverified; none published in the results).
- Reference point only, from algeriatech.news (secondary source, unverified): a full compliance setup (DPO, ROPA, DPIA, breach protocol) costs a startup roughly 500,000 to 1,000,000 DZD per year. An internal DPO in Algiers is put at 120,000 to 180,000 DZD per month. Sources: https://algeriatech.news/?p=22415 and https://algeriatech.news/?p=26964 (the salary figure came from the search summary of the second page and is unverified).

## Fit gaps
- Bespoke, hour-based advice does not scale to the many small firms that now need a DPO, a record of processing and a 5-day breach workflow.
- No sign of Arabic/French templates, a self-serve ROPA/DPIA tool or an ANPDP filing workflow from firms (absence in my searches, so unverified).
- Large firms such as Gide target corporate clients, so small and mid-size businesses look underserved.

## Opening
A cheap self-serve toolkit (ROPA, DPIA, breach-notification templates in Arabic and French, plus an ANPDP filing guide), possibly sold as a bundle with partner lawyers who act as the external DPO, fits the gap between hourly legal advice and a 500k to 1M DZD per year compliance programme.
