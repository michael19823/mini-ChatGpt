# Brevity (Australia) - competitor check

Idea killed: C0045 Support at Home claiming and evidence.

## Verdict: strong

Brevity (Brevity Care Software, Sydney, founded 2017 per CB Insights snippet) is a real, active vendor with a dedicated Support at Home product page that covers claiming. 4 of 6 searches used; none found complaints.

## Evidence
- Brevity markets "Industry-Leading Support at Home Software": client budgets, rostering, itemised claiming, claim reconciliation, client contributions, monthly participant statements, accounting integration (https://www.brevity.com.au/?p=7581).
- ISO/IEC 27001 certified (same page).
- Brevity states it started as an NDIS digital partner and now also serves aged care (https://www.brevity.com.au/ndis-software/; CB Insights listing).
- Momentum: active, current content on Support at Home. No acquisition or shutdown found. A "Brevity Lite" tier exists (https://brevity.com.au/meet-brevity-lite).
- Complaints: none found. The Slashdot comparison page showed no user reviews. A Capterra listing exists (https://www.capterra.com/p/266848/Brevity/) but I did not see review text. No app-store, Reddit or forum complaints surfaced. Not exhaustive; app stores were not searched directly.

## Pricing
- Capterra: starts at $120 per user per month (as listed; unverified currency and plan).
- Brevity site snippet (NDIS plan): Business $64.90/month plus $6.49 per client per month; Enterprise price on request. Support at Home pricing not found (unverified).
- ShiftCare's comparison page notes Brevity charges per client (https://shiftcare.com/compare/brevity); this is a competitor's claim.

## Fit gaps
- Positioned as a full platform (rostering, billing, care planning), likely heavy for a small provider wanting only claiming and evidence. Inference, not verified.
- Origin is NDIS; depth of Support at Home claiming and evidence versus dedicated tools is unverified.

## Opening
Narrow: a lightweight claiming and evidence tool for small providers or those not wanting a full platform; no complaint evidence supports a gap.
