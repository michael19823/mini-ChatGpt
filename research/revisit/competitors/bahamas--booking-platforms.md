# bahamas -- booking platforms (Airbnb, VRBO etc.; generic, no URL)

Dropped idea: C0076 Short-term rental tax compliance.

## Verdict: weak (low confidence; research cut short)
Only 1 of 6 searches completed. The second returned "usage limit", so no further searches were run.

## Evidence
- A third-party guide (bnbcalc.com, a rental-regulation blog) says Bahamas short-term rentals owe VAT plus a guest/occupancy tax. It gives 12% VAT and 10% guest tax, but also says Nassau owners charge 10% VAT as of 2023. The rates conflict, so they are unverified. https://www.bnbcalc.com/blog/short-term-rental-regulation/Dunmore-Town-Bahamas-Guide
- The same source says owners must report and pay VAT to the Department of Inland Revenue and collect and remit the guest tax. For foreign owners, platforms such as Airbnb or VRBO "can be authorized" to collect VAT. Unverified: no official source was checked, and nothing confirms the platforms actually do this in the Bahamas.
- The same source says the government is increasing enforcement. Unverified.
- Complaints: none found. Reviews, forums and local-language sources were not searched. This is not evidence that no complaints exist.

## Pricing
Not researched. Platforms charge hosts commission or fees, not a tax-compliance fee. Specific figures are unverified.

## Fit gaps (inferred, unverified)
- Platforms collect tax only where an arrangement exists, and only on bookings made through them. Direct bookings and local owners' VAT registration, filing and guest-tax remittance would stay with the owner.
- Platforms are not a tax-filing tool and do not file Inland Revenue returns for the host. This is an inference and was not verified.

## Opening
Platforms look like partial collectors, not compliance tools, so a host-side filing and reconciliation tool may stay open. This is unconfirmed until the platform tax-collection arrangement and owner complaints are verified.
