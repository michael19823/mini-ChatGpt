# Chile: Unnamed privacy SaaS (Ley 21.719 compliance)

Idea killed: C0190, Chile Ley 21.719 data-protection compliance. The competitor is a category, not one named product. 5 WebSearch calls used.

## Verdict: beatable

The category is real but aimed at enterprises and law-firm-led projects. No small-business self-serve product at a published price turned up. No customer complaints were found.

## Evidence
- The law takes full effect 1 December 2026. It does not exempt micro or small firms. Source: https://www.diarioconstitucional.cl/2026/06/12/la-ley-21-719-entra-en-vigor-el-1-de-diciembre-y-expone-vacios-en-regulacion-de-pequenas-empresas/
- Vendors found that mention Ley 21.719:
  - OneTrust, delivered in Chile via AlayIAtrust (https://alayiatrust.com/en/enterprise). It is a consulting-plus-platform model; its stated timeline is 3-6 months for enterprises and 1-4 months for SMEs.
  - Lawwwing (https://lawwwing.com/en/legal-compliance-chile/). It generates privacy notices and sets up consent.
  - BigID, Cassie and Kiteworks. These are global enterprise tools.
  - Confidata, Prey and Global Suite Solutions. These appeared in the results; I did not verify what they sell.
- Pymes are described as underserved. One article says complex compliance models need hours of work, trained staff and tools, and costs that "often exceed their structure." The same piece cites a survey that 72% of firms are not ready (secondary figures, unverified). Source: Diario Constitucional (link above); also https://www.emol.com/noticias/Economia/2026/09/23/1212212/pymes-ley-proteccion-datos-personales.html (appeared in results; I did not read it).
- Complaints: none found. The Trustpilot search returned nothing on these vendors. There were no app-store, Reddit or forum complaints. Review coverage in the results is thin.

## Pricing
No prices were published in the results. AlayIAtrust sells free assessments, which implies quote-based pricing. OneTrust is generally enterprise-priced (unverified here). Lawwwing's price is unverified.

## Fit gaps
- Enterprise or consultant-led tools. Nothing found was aimed at self-serve use by a micro or small firm.
- Likely gaps (unverified): Spanish-first and Chile-specific guided workflows, such as the 72-hour breach notice to the Agency, a records-of-processing register, and templates for typical pyme practices (WhatsApp marketing, biometric attendance, factoring).
- The Agency's rules and the pyme regime are still developing, so tools may lag the law.

## Opening
A cheap, Spanish-language, self-serve Ley 21.719 kit for Chilean pymes (register, consent, breach notices, ARCO rights handling), launched before 1 December 2026. The window is short and Lawwwing-type and consultant offerings could move down-market.
