# HMLR (Dominican Republic)

Idea killed: C0253, Dominican Republic ARS claims plus e-CF for clinics.
Searches used: 3 of 6 (English and Spanish). All results were directory listings; there is no known vendor URL.

## Verdict: beatable

HMLR matches the idea feature for feature and is cheap. But it has no visible customer base: 0 reviews and no complaints found. Any opening depends on execution and distribution, not on documented customer dissatisfaction.

## Evidence
- GetApp and Capterra list HMLR as a modular cloud/SaaS ERP for small and medium healthcare organizations in the Dominican Republic. Listed targets are clinics, medical centers, hospitals, laboratories, pharmacies and diagnostic centers. Listed features are DGII e-CF invoicing, ARS billing, POS, CRM, work orders, inventory, accounting, payroll and reports. Spanish support. Sources: https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/hmlr/ and https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/1107530/HMLR
- The GetApp listing shows 0 user ratings (per the search summary).
- No complaints, app-store reviews, forum threads or outage news were found. The search for opiniones and quejas returned only the directory listings and unrelated UK HM Land Registry material.
- Momentum: unverified. There is no vendor site, no changelog and no funding or acquisition news in the results. Directory presence suggests it is live, but nothing shows how actively it is developed.

## Pricing
- Starting price US$60.00 with per-feature pricing and a free trial, per the Capterra and GetApp listings. The billing period and the actual module prices are unverified.
- A $60 entry point is not overpriced for small clinics. Price is not a gap.

## Fit gaps
None verified. The listed scope covers the dropped idea. Unverified, and worth checking, are:
- the depth of ARS claim formats and rejection handling, since "ARS billing" may mean only invoicing;
- whether it supports multiple ARS plans, glosas (claim rejections) and reconciliation;
- offline or mobile use;
- e-CF certification status with the DGII.

## Opening
A narrower, claims-first product (ARS submission, glosa follow-up and reconciliation) for small clinics could still fit, because HMLR is a broad ERP of unproven depth. This needs validation with real clinics.
