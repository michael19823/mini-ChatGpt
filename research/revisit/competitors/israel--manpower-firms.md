# Israel: manpower firms (authorized foreign-worker bureaus)

Idea it killed: C0460, foreign-worker employer compliance (deposits, insurance, PIBA notices).
Searches used: 3 of 6 (2 Hebrew, 1 English). No URL known; this is a category of licensed agencies, not one product.

## Verdict: beatable

The firms are a done-for-you service and not a software competitor. In home care (caregivers) the law requires employers to go through them, so they are a channel more than a rival. They have no known self-serve tool for small employers.

## Evidence
- Recruiting a foreign caregiver for home care must go through licensed bureaus, and employer and worker register with one. Source: https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/גיוס_וליווי_עובד_זר_בסיעוד_על_ידי_הלשכות_המורשות
- A person receiving home care who employs a caregiver is exempt from the monthly deposit. They pay the worker the amounts set in the regulations at the end of employment. The 12.5% deposit (6% severance, 6.5% pension) applies to other employers, including labor contractors, and is paid through the Population and Immigration Authority deposits unit. Source: https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/הפקדת_פיקדון_חודשי_עבור_עובדים_זרים. This means the deposit-filing pain falls mostly on non-caregiving employers (construction, agriculture, industry, manpower contractors), not on household employers.
- Reported complaints are mostly about illegal placement fees charged to workers, not about compliance tooling:
  - Caregivers reportedly paid $4,500 to $9,000, average about $7,350. Source: https://humantraffickingsearch.org/?p=32514
  - About 150 manpower companies serve caregivers. Source: the same search summary. I did not open it; unverified.
  - Agencies reportedly give weak attention to monitoring labor standards. Sources: https://hotline.org.il/en/caregivers/ and https://www.972mag.com/privatizing-israels-migrant-care-puts-profits-before-people/ (search summaries only; unverified in detail).
- No employer-side complaints (outages, filing errors, app reviews) were found. I found no Google Play, App Store, Trustpilot or Reddit reviews for these firms. Finding none is a valid result here.
- Momentum: licensed, long-established and legally entrenched. No evidence of software development or recent change found.

## Pricing
- Reported legal ceiling: NIS 2,000 from the employer on hiring plus NIS 70 per month. The old structure allowed a total of NIS 3,400 charged to the worker. Source: search summary of themedialine/972mag pieces; unverified. That monthly fee reportedly covers service and monitoring, not necessarily compliance filing.
- Actual fees vary by firm; I found no published price list.

## Fit gaps
- It is a human service, not a tool. It offers no self-serve dashboard for deposits, insurance renewals or PIBA notices that I could verify.
- Caregiving employers are tied to the bureau, so software would sit alongside it or be sold through it.
- Non-caregiving small employers are not served by this channel, since bureaus focus on caregiving and licensed recruitment.
- The bureaus have a conflict of interest: reports allege they profit from illegal fees, which reduces employer trust.

## Opening
A cheap self-serve compliance tracker for small non-caregiving employers (deposits, insurance, PIBA deadlines) is not blocked by these firms. The caveat is that the deposit duty may not apply to household caregiver employers.
