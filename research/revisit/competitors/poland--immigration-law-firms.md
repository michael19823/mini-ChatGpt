# Poland: immigration law firms (C0777, foreign-worker legalisation agencies)

## Verdict: beatable

A fragmented set of boutique firms and agencies, with no dominant scaled product. The state is pushing applications into a free portal (MOS 2.0), which erodes the paperwork part of their business.

## Evidence
- Many individual firms offer legalisation of residence and work: RPMS (https://rpms.pl/prawo-imigracyjne/), Stachurski & Grzybowska (Wrocław, employer audits and training), E-Prawnik online (https://e-prawnik.pl/uslugi-prawne/cudzoziemcy.html), Dudkowiak & Putyra (https://www.dudkowiak.com/our-experience/immigration-law.html). They work mostly by power of attorney, forms and office contact. No single scaled platform found (6-search limit, so not exhaustive).
- Customer complaints about these firms: none found. No reviews on Trustpilot, Google or forums surfaced. Absence is not proof.
- The main pain is state delay, not the firms. The Ombudsman (RPO) has recorded years of complaints about slow voivode proceedings. In Lower Silesia, 2022 decisions took over 7 months on average. NIK found 189 to 500 cases per employee, and 1,325 in Wielkopolska in 2022. Lower Silesia paid over PLN 12 million in costs for delay between 2022 and 2024 (https://bip.brpo.gov.pl). A firm cannot fix this, though it is the reason customers hire one.
- Fraud shadow: the Border Guard and PAP reported prosecutions of agencies selling fictitious work declarations or fake legalisation (for example, 35 accused over at least 800 people; a firm that issued 265 fictitious documents; sources on strazgraniczna.pl and pap.pl). This is a trust problem for the segment, and the cases involved criminal agencies, not law firms. It leaves room for a transparent, compliant service.
- Momentum: MOS 2.0 became mandatory for residence applications from 27 April 2026. It is free, saves drafts, and needs no booking (EY, Envoy Global, migrant.poznan.uw.gov.pl). This shrinks the pure form-filling work of firms. Law firms are likely to pivot to digital and employer packages.

## Pricing
- Firm fees: unverified. No published price lists found.
- State fees (verified via search summaries): work permit types A and B are PLN 200 (up to 3 months) or PLN 400 (over 3 months) from 1 Dec 2025. Types C, D and E are PLN 800. The statement of entrusting work is PLN 400. Power of attorney stamp duty is PLN 17.

## Fit gaps
- Firms are bespoke and per-case. Nothing found suggests cheap, self-serve, multilingual tooling for small employers or individual workers (unverified).
- Their value is tied to paper filing, which MOS 2.0 now digitises.
- Delay tracking, case status and deadline reminders for employers are not shown as offered (unverified).

## Opening
A low-cost, multilingual, MOS-aware guided-filing and case-tracking service for small employers and workers. Pair it with transparent pricing and compliance to counter fraud-agency distrust. The risk is that MOS is free and the idea is a service business, not software.
