# Singapore: generic payroll SaaS (Talenox, Payboy, Swingvy, etc.)

Idea killed: C0884, Singapore security agency rostering and PWM payroll.
Searches used: 5 of 6 (English and Chinese).

## Verdict: beatable

Generic payroll tools handle CPF/SDL/tax well and are cheap. None of the search results showed one built for security-agency rostering with Progressive Wage Model (PWM) wage-band logic. Dedicated guard-rostering tools exist (mostly foreign), so the gap is narrower than it looks.

## Evidence
- Talenox: rated 4.8 from 17 reviews on the Xero App Store. Praise is for affordability, ease of use and support. One reviewer reported a Xero integration that kept failing. Source: https://apps.xero.com/sg/industry/professional-services/app/talenox/reviews (as summarised by search; not opened).
- Payboy: G2 reviews praise support and ease of use for standard SG payroll. Its attendance, overtime and leave data syncs into payroll. Source: https://briohr.com/blog/best-hr-software-singapore/ (secondary, vendor-written blog). Not security-specific.
- Swingvy: described as locally built with CPF and Employment Act compliance. No complaints found in the results.
- Rostering gap: results for Singapore security-guard rostering showed mainly foreign or generic tools (SecurityTime, TrackTik, Deputy, OctopusPro). They list GPS clock-in, payroll export and compliance tracking. A custom-app vendor page claims PLRD compliance and MOM rest-day rules for Singapore (rationalgo.ai, unverified marketing claim). SecurityTime and TrackTik are not confirmed to model PWM wage grades.
- No PWM-specific payroll or rostering product appeared in the English or Chinese searches. That is absence of evidence, not proof none exists.
- PWM context: security officer minimum basic wage is S$2,960-3,530 depending on licence grade, with a schedule of rises to 2028. In-house security officers were added from 2022, with a new schedule from 2026 (MOM). The pressure to comply is real.
- Complaints: no substantial complaints found beyond the single Talenox Xero integration report. Finding almost none is a valid result, but it was a thin search.

## Pricing
- Talenox Singapore (incl. GST): Payroll app S$27.25/month for the first 5 employees, then S$5.45 per full-time employee per month. Suite (payroll, leave, profiles) S$43.60 for the first 5, then S$8.72 each. Part-timers S$4.36 each. Annual billing is about 15% cheaper. Source: https://www.talenox.com/pricing/ (via search summary).
- Payboy, Swingvy: pricing not verified.
- A 100-guard agency would pay roughly S$550-900/month on Talenox. That is cheap and hard to undercut on payroll alone.

## Fit gaps
- No evidence of PWM wage-grade validation, allowance and overtime rules specific to security, or licence and training tracking tied to PWM (unverified for each vendor).
- Likely no site, post and contract-based rostering with payroll feeding (unverified).
- Foreign guard-rostering tools may lack CPF, SDL and MOM specifics (unverified).
- Generic tools serve generic SMEs, so they may not map shift patterns, 12-hour shifts and rest-day rules to pay.

## Opening
Combine rostering with PWM-aware payroll for guard agencies, which neither generic payroll tools nor foreign rostering tools evidently do. Payroll alone is a poor wedge, because Talenox-class pricing is already low.
