# digitFMS (South Africa)

Dropped ideas: C0915 (telematics-style diesel logbooks), C0917 (diesel-refund telematics and pump automation).

## Verdict: beatable

## Evidence
- 4 WebSearch calls (English and Afrikaans). digitFMS is a real but small South African fleet and fuel-management vendor. Search snippets describe it as founded in 2007, with 21-50 employees, offering bulk fuel bowser management, truck fuel monitoring, vehicle tracking, and CCTV, access control and alarms. The only sources for this were aggregator snippets (e.g. https://prospeo.io/c/digitfms-email-format); I found no official site in the results (unverified).
- No mention of SARS diesel-rebate logbooks or refund filing for digitFMS in any result. Its fuel-monitoring product could be used for rebate record-keeping, but I found no evidence it is marketed that way (unverified).
- No app-store reviews, Reddit posts, forum threads or news items. No complaints found.
- Market context: SARS moved diesel rebate claims to a standalone digital platform. Grain SA is piloting an automated diesel logbook with private technology providers (https://www.grainsa.co.za/news-headlines/latest-news/lastest-developents-regarding-the-diesel-rebate-system). Cartrack publishes a logbook-audit guide (https://cartrack.co.za/blog/sars-diesel-refund-how-to-pass-a-logbook-audit-and-get-your-100-percent-refund). Larger vendors (Banlaw, Gilbarco) also sell fuel management in South Africa.

## Fit gaps
- Hardware-led (bowser and tank monitoring, tracking), so it requires installed hardware and a sales-led engagement; likely not suited to small farms wanting a cheap logbook (inferred from the product description, unverified).
- No evidence of a SARS-format rebate logbook or refund-claim workflow.

## Momentum
Unknown. Appears to be a small established firm; no recent news or product launches found.

## Opening
digitFMS sells hardware-based fuel control, not rebate paperwork, so a software-first, low-cost SARS-format diesel logbook and refund-claim tool for small farmers and miners is still open; the larger threats are Cartrack and the Grain SA pilot.
