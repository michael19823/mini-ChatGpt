# South Africa: intermediaries (consultant, no URL)

Ideas killed: C0913 (South Africa COIDA injury-on-duty medical claims).
Searches used: 5 of 6 (English x4, Afrikaans x1). The intermediary class is TPAs/claims administrators and COIDA consultants, led by CompSol. No single URL.

## Verdict: beatable

Intermediaries (TPAs that take cession of medical invoices and pay providers early for a fee) exist because the Compensation Fund is slow. They are real, but they are a financing/factoring model, they are under regulatory pressure, and they are not a software product. A software-only tool could beat them on fee, but the main demand is cash-flow, which software does not supply.

## Evidence
- Intermediaries exist because of Fund inefficiency; providers cede claims to TPAs for immediate payment at a fee. Source: https://pmg.org.za/committee-meeting/32718/
- Cited average Fund payment time is 347 days, against a court-ordered 75-day standard that is not being met (as stated in the hearing summary; not verified at primary source). Source: https://pmg.org.za/committee-meeting/32718/
- CompSol is the largest named intermediary: it represents over 2000 providers, handles about 43% of COIDA cases, administers about 405,000 medical accounts a year, and was reported owed over R716 million by the Fund (all from submissions, unverified). Sources: https://pmg.org.za/committee-meeting/32718/ , https://pmg.org.za/files/210422cOMPSOL.pdf
- Fund system: SAP-based CompEasy replaced the old IT system in Oct 2019 at R285m and was reported to continue delays. Source: https://pmg.org.za/committee-meeting/32718/
- Regulatory risk to intermediaries: Clause 43 of the COIDA Amendment Bill sought to ban cession of medical invoices to TPAs and financial institutions. The Portfolio Committee reportedly amended it to permit TPAs. The Amendment Act 10 of 2022 was reportedly brought into effect on 23 January 2026 (single search snippet, verify). Draft regulations of 10 May 2024 cover registration of third parties. Sources: https://pmg.org.za/committee-meeting/34575/ , https://www.wcawca.co.za/?p=13779
- Fraud alert: the Fund warns that unscrupulous firms pose as Fund representatives or licensed COID service companies, and that Fund services are free to employers. This hurts trust in paid claim-help offers. Source: https://www.acts.co.za/news/blog/2025/11/coida-scam-alert-service-offerings
- Fund delays are widely reported (providers cutting services, widows waiting). Sources: https://www.fin24.com/Economy/South-Africa/healthcare-providers-retrench-halt-services-as-compensation-fund-payouts-delayed-20200223 , https://groundup.news/article/the-widows-who-wait-the-compensation-funds-endless-delays/
- Complaints about the intermediaries themselves (reviews, forums, app stores): none found. The complaints found are about the Fund, not the TPAs. This is a search gap, not proof of satisfaction. The Afrikaans search returned only English news.

## Pricing
- TPA fee: not found. The sources say only "at a fee" (Source: https://pmg.org.za/committee-meeting/32718/). No percentages or published prices found. Unverified.
- Fund services are free to employers (see fraud alert above).

## Fit gaps
- Intermediaries sell speed of payment (factoring), which software cannot replicate without capital.
- Small or solo providers and employers are probably served less well, as inferred from the large-scale players; not confirmed by any source.
- Possible gaps for a software tool: claim-completeness checking before submission (the Fund urges providers to submit correct documents: https://labourguide.co.za/coid/news-coid/employment-and-labours-compensation-fund-urges-medical-service-providers-to-submit-relevant-and-correct-documents-to-speed-up-medical-payments), status tracking, and employer-side compliance. Unverified as unmet.

## Momentum
CompSol and other TPAs are established but face regulatory change (Amendment Act, third-party regulations) and Fund payment arrears. Direction after January 2026 is unverified.

## Opening
A cheap claim-prep, tracking and chasing tool for small providers and employers, with no cession or financing, could undercut TPA fees. It cannot supply early cash, and its value depends on how the Amendment Act and third-party regulations were finalized.
