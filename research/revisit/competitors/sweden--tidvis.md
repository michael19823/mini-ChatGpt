# Tidvis (Sweden) - competitor check

Dropped idea: C0971, Sweden personal-assistance time reporting ELT.

## Verdict: strong

Tidvis is a purpose-built, established product for exactly this buyer (Swedish personal-assistance providers). It already covers scheduling, time reporting, payroll and documentation.

## Evidence
- Tidvis describes itself as a business platform for personal assistance and LSS that combines scheduling, time reporting, payroll and documentation. Source: https://tidvis.se/en/sectors/personal-assistance
- Self-reported scale: 3,600 customers and 27,000 daily users (vendor claim, unverified independently). Source: https://tidvis.se/en
- Says it is approved by Försäkringskassan and supports BankID signing to Försäkringskassan, collective agreements, working-time rules, hour-based vacation calculations and municipal forms (vendor claim). Source: https://tidvis.se/en/sectors/personal-assistance
- Automatic checks of daily rest (11 h), weekly rest and the EU 48-hour maximum when a shift is scheduled (vendor claim). Source: https://tidvis.se/en/sectors/personal-assistance
- Momentum: the site is live and actively marketed; no sign of shutdown or acquisition found. Release cadence unverified.

## Complaints
None found. Searches (Swedish and English) for reviews, Trustpilot, Reddit, Flashback and Facebook returned nothing about Tidvis. This does not show the product is well liked, only that no public reviews are indexed. App-store reviews were not checked, so that is unverified.

## Pricing
Not found in the searches. Unverified. One pricing search failed on a usage limit. Check tidvis.se directly.

## Fit gaps
No gaps confirmed. Possible ones, all unverified:
- It may be priced for mid-sized and large providers (not confirmed).
- It may not suit very small providers or individual assistance users who employ their own assistants (not confirmed).
- Offline use and the mobile app were not checked.

## Opening
Little visible opening against Tidvis; only a niche (for example, a very cheap or simple tool for tiny providers or self-employers) is plausible, and it would need pricing and review evidence first.
