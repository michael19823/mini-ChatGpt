# Apsiyon (Turkey) - competitor check for C1067 (apartment/site management)

## Verdict: strong

Market-leading Turkish property-management SaaS, about 750,000 units, funded, and acquired by Aareon in 2025.

## Evidence
- Founded 2012, Istanbul; claims 750,000+ units serviced. Source: https://tech.eu/2020/03/18/apsiyon-5-million/
- Raised USD 5M (Earlybird Digital East Fund, angel Jak Baruh), about USD 10.6M total per aggregators. Sources: https://tech.eu/2020/03/18/apsiyon-5-million/ ; https://www.apsiyon.com/blog/apsiyon-team-raises-5-million-usd-to-fuel-international-expansion
- Bought rival Siyonet in 2017 (consolidator). Source: https://webrazzi.com/2017/05/29/apsiyon-siyonet-site-yonetimi/
- Momentum: Nov 2025 share purchase agreement to sell 100% to Aareon (German, TPG/CDPQ/Advent backed), per search summary; see https://iflr1000.com/NewsAndAnalysis/turkiye-paksoy-advises-founders-on-their-sale-of-apsiyon/Index/15792
- Product: manager web app plus resident mobile app (Apsiyon Manager on App Store: https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/apsiyon-manager/id1362003256); dues collection, accounting, meter reading/invoicing, complaints, package tracking, announcements, surveys, site board.
- Complaints: none found. Searches (Turkish and English, including sikayetvar/Ekşi Sözlük angle) returned no specific negative reviews; Ekşi Sözlük snippets were positive. Store ratings and Google Play reviews were not checked/visible. Absence of complaints is not proof of satisfaction.

## Pricing
- Old reports (2011/2017 era, search summary only, unverified for today) describe per-unit monthly fees of about 1 and 2 TL with no setup fee and a 2-month free trial. Current pricing not verified; likely quote-based. Per-unit pricing is cheap for small buildings, so price is not an obvious weakness.

## Fit gaps
- None confirmed. Possible (unverified) gaps: small self-managed apartment buildings with a volunteer manager, and non-Turkish markets (it is pursuing international expansion).

## Opening
Little: a generic Turkish apartment-management tool would face an entrenched, well-funded, corporate-backed leader; only a narrow niche (tiny self-managed buildings, or a specific vertical add-on) might remain.
