# 120Water, United States

Idea killed: C1123 Lead service line inventory software

## Verdict
strong

120Water is a well-funded, widely adopted, actively developed specialist in exactly this niche, with state-level contracts. Four searches ran (4 of 6 used; some were vendor/press results, not independent reviews).

## Evidence
- Scope: end-to-end lead service line inventory and replacement platform plus a machine-learning "Probability Finder" for building inventories from existing data. Source: https://platform.softwareone.com/product/lead-service-line-inventory-replacement/PCP-2836-3938 and https://www.120water.com/news/120water-launches-new-lead-service-line-probability-finder
- Adoption (vendor press, unverified independently): PWS Portal launched Sept 2022, licensed in over 41 states, nearly 500 public water system customers. Source: https://www.120water.com/news/120water-launches-service-line-inventory-management (as summarised in search results; date of the figures unclear).
- State contracts: selected to manage the Indiana lead service line database (https://www.120water.com/news/120water-platform-selected-to-manage-indiana-lead-service-line-database); Rhode Island partnership reported (https://www.silicon.co.uk/press-release/rhode-island-partners-with-120water-to-support-digital-lead-service-line-database).
- Momentum: $43M growth investment led by Edison Partners, announced January 2024 (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240110301036/en/120Water-Secures-%2443M-Growth-Investment-Led-by-Edison-Partners). Vendor claims over 60% annual revenue growth (vendor claim, unverified). No evidence found of shutdown or acquisition; nothing newer than 2024 surfaced.
- Complaints: none found. A search for complaints, Reddit and forum posts returned nothing about 120Water. G2, Capterra, Trustpilot and app-store reviews were not surfaced by any search, so the absence is not proof of satisfaction.

## Pricing
No published price found. Unverified. For context, free alternatives exist: Esri's Lead Service Line Inventory solution (no cost per search summary, but small utilities may need GIS skills and licences; the cited licence cost of about $5,000-10,000 per year came from a search summary and is unverified), BlueConduit Starter (free), and a Duke/NC Rural Water Association tool for North Carolina (https://nicholasinstitute.duke.edu/publications/development-and-deployment-lead-service-line-inventory-application-north-carolina).

## Fit gaps
- Not confirmed by any source. Hypotheses to test: the full platform may be heavy or costly for very small systems (unverified); the vendor also sells testing and consulting, so it targets mid-size and large programmes.
- Competition from free tools (Esri, BlueConduit Starter) narrows the cheap-tool space too.

## Opening
Little: only a very cheap, no-GIS tool for tiny water systems might fit, and that gap is unconfirmed and already partly served by free tools.
