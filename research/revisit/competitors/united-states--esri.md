# Esri (United States) - competitor to C1123: Lead service line inventory software

## Verdict: strong

Esri ships a purpose-built, preconfigured Lead Service Line Inventory solution on ArcGIS, with many utility case studies. It is a strong competitor for utilities that already run GIS. It is weakest for very small utilities without GIS staff.

## Evidence
- Esri documents a "Lead Service Line Inventory" solution: preconfigured maps, apps and dashboards to inventory lines, validate materials in the field, track replacements and compliance, and share with the public. https://doc.arcgis.com/en/arcgis-solutions/reference/introduction-to-lead-service-line-inventory.htm
- It includes a public Survey123 form for customers to report their service line material (with photos). Auburn embedded it in a hub site. https://doc.arcgis.com/en/arcgis-solutions/reference/introduction-to-lead-service-line-inventory.htm
- Esri says the design aligns with the US EPA service line inventory template. Case studies on esri.com: City of Plano TX (81,000+ lines), Greater Johnstown Water. https://www.esri.com/en-us/industries/blog/articles/city-of-plano-texas-inventories-81000-plus-water-service-lines-to-meet-lcrr-requirements-and-inform-residents
- Momentum: actively developed and marketed, with partner and engineering-firm content (Frontier Precision, Stantec, Burns & McDonnell) pushing it.
- Complaints: none found. A search for complaints turned up no reviews or forum posts. Only a third-party page (liveinthefuture.org, unverified, appears to be an idea-list site) says small utilities cannot justify the cost or the GIS analyst.

## Pricing
- ArcGIS Online Creator named user: $550/year (from search summary of Esri's pricing page; Field Maps is included with Mobile Worker/Creator/GIS Professional). https://www.esri.com/en-us/arcgis/products/arcgis-online/pricing
- The liveinthefuture.org page claims about $5,000-10,000 per year for the relevant tier. This is unverified and not from Esri.
- The solution itself comes with an ArcGIS subscription. Total cost depends on user count and staff time to configure.

## Fit gaps
- Needs a GIS analyst or consultant to deploy and configure (an assumption supported only by the unverified third-party claim above).
- It is a GIS toolkit, not turnkey compliance software. Records, EPA/state submission formats and customer notification letters probably need extra work (unverified).
- Small systems (a few hundred connections) without GIS or IT staff are poorly served.

## Opening
A turnkey, no-GIS, low-cost tool for very small water systems (inventory, EPA/state submission, customer notices) is still open, but this is not a gap in the larger-utility segment.
