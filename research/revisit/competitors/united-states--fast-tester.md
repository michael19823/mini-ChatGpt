# FAST Tester (United States) - competitor check

Killed idea: C1104 Backflow tester multi-utility hub.

## Verdict: not-a-real-competitor (in the US)

FAST Tester appears to be a Canadian product (MTS Inc., Vernon, BC), used by BC municipalities. I found no US utility using it.

## Evidence
- FAST Tester is a web app by MTS Inc. (Vernon BC) for submitting backflow test reports to utilities that use the FAST Cross Connection Control Program. Sources: https://pemberton.ca/public/download/files/235239 , https://rdno.ca/sites/default/files/2025-01/2025_Info_Sheet_Test_Reports_Fast.pdf , https://www.kamloops.ca/node/10492
- All utilities found using it were in British Columbia (Pemberton, Whistler, RDNO, Kamloops). No US utility found in 4 searches. One search summary claimed "multiple North American utilities", but I could not verify that from any US source.
- Complaints: none found for FAST Tester. No app-store, Reddit or forum reviews surfaced.
- Related, US-relevant: a Bothell, WA page reports poor reception of SwiftComply among Western Washington cross-connection professionals (clumsy, default form not meeting state DOH requirements). This is about SwiftComply, not FAST. Source: https://projects.bothellwa.gov/news/19-swift-comply-gets-poor-reviews
- US utilities are adopting SwiftComply (e.g. Longmont CO requires it from 2026-02-02; Fullerton CA). The real US competitors for C1104 are SwiftComply and Syncta (Watts), which should be checked separately.
- Momentum: apparently active (Kamloops fee for email/paper reports from July 2025). Unverified beyond that.

## Pricing
- Tester annual registration of $49.99 plus tax, or $25 first year then $50 per year in some areas. Unverified currency, presumably CAD. Sources: Pemberton and RDNO pages above.

## Fit gaps
- Not a US-market product: no known US utility coverage, and BC-oriented forms.
- Per-tester annual fee covers all FAST utilities, so it is cheap for testers.

## Opening
FAST does not block C1104 in the US. The real incumbents are SwiftComply and Syncta, and the multi-utility hub gap depends on those.
