# OneTrust (united-states) - competitor to C1111: California DROP for data brokers

## Verdict: beatable

OneTrust is an actively developed enterprise privacy suite. It does offer a DROP deletion workflow, but it sits inside a DSAR module that costs roughly $10K/year or more, which is too much for small data brokers.

## Evidence
- OneTrust has a blog post on DROP and says DROP will be supported through its Data Subject Request Automation, with a deletion workflow for intake, fulfilment across systems and records of deletions. This is vendor marketing, so how well it works is unverified. Source: https://www.onetrust.com/blog/californias-drop-what-the-delete-act-changes-for-consent-preferences-and-data-deletion/
- Deadline context: from Aug 1, 2026, brokers must check DROP at least every 45 days and delete matching data, including inferences. Source: https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/california-s-drop-regime-will-change-6495324/ and the OneTrust blog post above.
- Review sites (via the Sprinto and Consently review articles, not read directly): G2 4.4/5 (255 reviews), Capterra 4.3/5 (55), Trustpilot 1.5/5 (24). Trustpilot complaints are about price increases, billing disputes and aggressive sales outreach. Sources: https://sprinto.com/blog/onetrust-review/ and https://consently.net/blog/onetrust-review
- Competitor-authored material says that small buyers find it too heavy and that support hours are billed separately. Sources: https://www.osano.com/articles/why-companies-are-leaving-onetrust and the Sprinto and Consently articles above. These are biased sources.
- No complaints specific to DROP or data brokers were found. No Reddit, Google Play or App Store complaints were found in 4 searches. Local-language search was not applicable (US, English).
- Momentum: layoffs of 110 staff (about 4.8%) confirmed on March 4, 2026, after 950 in 2022. Not a shutdown. It is still shipping DROP support. Sources: https://www.corporatecomplianceinsights.com/onetrust-layoffs-reaction/ and https://channelfutures.com/channel-business/onetrust-layoffs-impact-25-of-workforce-as-part-of-reorganization . I did not confirm which source covers which year. No 2026 acquisition news was found.

## Pricing
- No published prices; quote only.
- Third-party estimates: from about $10,000/year, with a reported $10K/year minimum from Q2 2026 (unverified). Median Vendr spend is about $11.6K/year, with a range of about $1.6K to $44.8K. Large deployments reach $200K-$300K+. Sources: https://www.trackingplan.com/blog/onetrust-pricing-what-drives-costs-and-how-to-evaluate-value-en and https://erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/data-privacy/onetrust/pricing
- Pricing is driven by modules and data-subject volume, not seats.

## Fit gaps
- It is a general suite (consent, data mapping, DSAR, third-party risk, AI governance), not a tool built for data brokers.
- Cost and sales-led onboarding are out of reach for small or mid-size brokers, who face a single compliance task: pull the DROP list, match, delete and report.
- Support is billed extra. This claim comes from competitor material and is unverified.
- Whether it has a native DROP matching and 45-day reporting flow, or only a generic DSAR workflow, is unverified.

## Opening
A cheap, single-purpose DROP tool (list pull, matching, deletion log, 45-day reporting) for small brokers that cannot afford a $10K+ suite. This depends on DataGrail and other cheaper tools also being weak.
