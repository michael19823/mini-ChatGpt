# SwiftComply (united-states, commercial)

Killed idea: C1104 Backflow tester multi-utility hub.
Searches used: 4 of 6 (English only; no local-language variant applies for the US).

## Verdict: strong

It is the incumbent utility-side backflow portal, adopted by many US water utilities, and it is growing by acquisition. It is a utility-paid SaaS, so it is the system testers are forced to use. A tester-side hub can only sit on top of it, not replace it.

## Evidence
- Used by many utilities for tester report submission. Longmont CO requires all testers to use it from 2 Feb 2026. Gig Harbor WA, Dallas Water Utilities and Anchorage Water & Wastewater also use it. Sources: https://longmontcolorado.gov/water/drinking-water/backflow-prevention/ , https://gigharborwa.gov/856/Tester-Resources , https://dallascityhall.com/departments/waterutilities/Documents/Announcement%20-%20DWU%20Backflow%20Process%20Change.pdf
- Momentum is strong. It acquired CloudCompli and NPDESPro (April 2025, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250429178049/en/SwiftComply-Acquires-CloudCompli-and-NPDESPro-to-Expand-Stormwater-Compliance-Capabilities). Search results also report a June 2026 ComplianceGo acquisition and more than 700 customers (https://www.stormwater.com/stormwater-bmps/compliance/article/55386706/qa-swiftcomply-ceo-on-acquiring-compliancego-and-the-future-of-stormwater-compliance-software). I did not open these pages, so treat the customer count as a search-snippet figure.
- Complaints (limited, one source). A Bothell WA project page says the Western Washington Cross Connection Professionals gave unfavorable responses. It describes the tool as "difficult and clumsy" and says the default form does not meet WA DOH requirements. Source: https://projects.bothellwa.gov/news/19-swift-comply-gets-poor-reviews . This is a municipal staff note, not tester reviews. I did not verify its date.
- Capterra has a listing (https://www.capterra.com/p/201104/SwiftComply/) but the snippets show minimal reviews. No app-store, G2, Trustpilot or Reddit complaints were found.

## Pricing
- Utility-side SaaS pricing is not published (unverified). A Greenfield IN service order exists (https://www.greenfield.in.gov/phocadownload/BOW/Jun-25-2024/swiftcomply%20service%20order%20for%20greenfield%20in-1.pdf) but I did not read its figures.
- Tester fees vary by utility. Longmont charges $10 per passing test submitted, Gig Harbor $5 per passing test, and Victorville CA charges nothing. Sources are the Longmont, Gig Harbor and Victorville (https://www.victorvilleca.gov/government/city-departments/water/cross-connection/backflow-testers-information) pages.
- A multi-utility tester therefore pays per-submission fees and keeps separate accounts per utility (the per-utility accounts are inferred, not confirmed).

## Fit gaps (for a tester-side multi-utility hub)
- Built for utilities and regulators, not testers. It has no evident unified tester dashboard across utilities (unverified).
- Forms are set by each utility, and the Bothell note suggests they may not match state requirements.
- Per-submission fees fall on testers, which a hub could aggregate or avoid.
- Utilities not on SwiftComply use other portals, so a hub would have to integrate with several systems.

## Opening
A tester-side layer that enters a job once and files it across SwiftComply and other utility portals is possible, but it depends on portal access that the utility-side incumbent controls.
