# VEPO CrossConnex (united-states) - competitor check

Killed idea: C1104 Backflow tester multi-utility hub.

## Verdict: strong

Water utilities mandate it as their submission channel and it is already free to testers, so a neutral tester-side hub has little to attach to unless it submits into VEPO.

## Evidence
- Vepo LLC says it has managed cross-connection data for cities, utilities and water companies since 2010 (https://vepollc.com/about_us.aspx).
- Utilities require test reports to be filed through it. Examples: York, SC (https://www.yorksc.gov/utilities/page/backflow-prevention), Yorktown NY (https://yorktownny.gov/node/44553), Hershey/Hummelstown area (https://hswa-pa.org/commercial/).
- Portland Water District (Maine) moves to VEPO from March 1, 2026 (https://www.pwd.org/?p=10215). This shows it is still winning utilities, so momentum is active.
- Features per search summaries: iOS app (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vepo-crossconnex/id1378882147), Android app, and a web portal. Testers can submit and schedule tests, manage gauges, and manage certifications and licenses. Utilities get assembly tracking and retest renewal letters.
- Complaints: none found. Searches for Reddit, forum and Google Play complaints returned nothing relevant. The App Store and Google Play ratings were not retrieved, so they are unverified. No G2, Capterra or Trustpilot pages were found.

## Pricing
- Tester app is described as free to download.
- Filing fee is reported at about $15 per test report, paid by the tester in most jurisdictions. It ranges $15-$20 by district, according to a search summary. The exact figure is unverified, as the primary utility page was not opened.
- No published utility-side pricing was found (unverified).

## Fit gaps
- Each utility is a separate VEPO tenant or program, so a tester working across many utilities uses one app but sees only each utility's own rules. This is inferred, not verified.
- Not every utility uses VEPO. Others use different systems or PDF/email submission (for example Dallas has its own page, https://dallascityhall.com/departments/waterutilities/Pages/Backflow-Test-Reports.aspx). A tester serving mixed jurisdictions may still juggle channels. Unverified how many utilities use VEPO.
- Tester-business tools were not seen in the results: invoicing, customer scheduling, route planning, quoting, and repair follow-up. Unverified whether VEPO offers them.

## Opening
Only a tester-side back-office layer (invoicing, scheduling, customer reminders, filing into VEPO and other utilities' portals) is plausible, and it depends on VEPO's tolerance. Replacing VEPO's utility-facing role is not.
