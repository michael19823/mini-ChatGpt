# C0408 — Hong Kong: 468 continuous-contract tracking and MPF-offsetting split calculation

## Verdict and score
**Still closed: 3.3/10.** Both halves are covered.
- **468 tracking.** Info-Tech now markets exactly this. It tracks each employee's rolling 4-week
  hours by linking time-attendance to payroll, alerts managers *before* the 68-hour threshold, and
  provides "Rule 468 reports" and employee self-check in the app. It has a dedicated SME page.
  Workstem, another local HRMS, markets 468 early warning, rolling-hour capture and 12-month
  average-daily-wage calculation, including for hospitality.
- **MPF-offsetting split.** The Labour Department's free **EasyCal** calculates severance and long
  service payments before and after the 1 May 2025 abolition, including the government subsidy,
  and publishes calculation-statement templates. The offsetting split also shrinks every year,
  because it applies only to pre-transition service.

## What changed versus the Sonnet evidence
- Sonnet called Info-Tech "beatable" because it found no 468 tracking. That is wrong: Info-Tech
  blog posts (p=2383, "416/468 Rule for SMEs and how Info-Tech can help") describe rolling-hour
  tracking with pre-threshold alerts.
- The EOR/payroll file had no evidence at all (every search refused). Workstem and the free
  government EasyCal were missing from the list.

## Barriers
- A free government calculator for the offsetting split.
- 468 tracking needs time-attendance data, which HRMS vendors already hold.
- The trigger is one-off: the 468 rule started 18 Jan 2026 and offsetting was abolished 1 May
  2025. Vendors have already updated.

## Buyer and price
The buyer is an HR or payroll manager at an SME with part-time and shift staff (F&B, retail,
hotels). The price anchor is Info-Tech at about HK$15 per employee per module per month
(unverified). A standalone calculator would be hard to price above that, against a free EasyCal.
