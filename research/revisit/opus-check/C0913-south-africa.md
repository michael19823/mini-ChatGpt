# C0913 · South Africa · COIDA injury-on-duty medical claims (provider side)

## Verdict and score
**Still closed. 3/10.** The pain is extreme: a backlog of more than 150,000 cases, hundreds of millions of rands owed to private providers, about 347-day average payment in the source's figures, and Netcare litigating to have CompEasy declared "dysfunctional". But the provider-side market is already dominated by a service that sells what practices actually need, which is cash.
- **CompSol** administers and factors about **405,000 COID medical accounts a year**. That is "in excess of 60% of all medical accounts submitted to the Compensation Fund".
  - It takes those accounts from about 1,810 practices (about 5,000 practitioners).
  - It pays them within 10 working days, and has pre-funded more than R4.5bn over 5 years.
  - It also runs IOD administration for 2,000+ employers.
- A software-only claim-prep and tracking tool cannot match "paid in 10 days". The bottleneck is the Fund's adjudication and payment, which software cannot fix, as the original rejection said.
- **Regulatory overhang.** Clause 43 of the COID Amendment Bill would void cessions of medical invoices to third parties. SAMA warned that would be "disastrous", and the Fund withdrew related regulations in 2021 after pushback. If cession were banned, CompSol's model would break, and practices would need admin help without factoring. That is the only scenario that reopens the idea. Its current status is unverified and has been contested for years.

## What changed versus the Sonnet evidence
- Sonnet called intermediaries "beatable" because they are "not software". That misreads the market. CompSol already handles most of the provider-side volume, and its product is factoring plus administration, which a cheaper tool cannot undercut on the dimension buyers care about.
- Sonnet correctly classed CompEasy as the rail, not a competitor.
- New: Fund statements in 2026 parliamentary replies claim improved turnaround and throughput. Netcare's High Court case shows large providers fight through litigation, not tooling.
- Practice-management systems (GoodX, Datamax Panacea, switching houses MediSwitch and Healthbridge) handle medical-aid switching. I found no evidence of dedicated COID tracking in them (unverified either way).

## Barriers
- The Fund's own payment performance is the binding constraint.
- MSP and system-user registration on CompEasy, plus gazetted COID tariffs and ICD-10 rules.
- Legal uncertainty over cession and third-party rules (Amendment Act status unverified).

## Buyer and price
- **Buyer.** Practice manager at an occupational-health, orthopaedic or GP practice near industrial employers.
- **Price anchor.** CompSol's factoring discount is not published (unverified). The alternative is "insist on cash", which many providers already do.
