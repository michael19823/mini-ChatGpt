# C1063 — Turkey: CBAM/SKDM carbon software (cheap exporter-SME tier)

## Verdict and score
**Still closed: 4.0/10.** Sonnet was right that most of the eight names in the original list are
phantoms. Omnimatic, CimpactPro, Akkaş Karbon, Climeteo and Enexion were not findable,
QuickCarbon serves EU importers, and ATP GreenX is a carbon-credit marketplace. But the real
Turkish field is not empty, and the economics of the "cheap SME tier" have worsened.
- **Carbongate** is a Turkish-language CBAM SaaS that automates SKDM processes and declaration
  preparation, with a dedicated industry/production offering. It is the most visible local
  vendor, and Sonnet's own files kept pointing to it.
- **CarbonEmit** (Turkey, founded 2024) offers CBAM, ISO 14064-1, ISO 14067 and water-footprint
  modules.
- **PwC's Carbon Border Manager** is listed on SAP, and global CBAM SaaS vendors and Big-4 or
  verifier consultancies serve the large steel and cement exporters.
- The **EU's 2026 simplification** exempts importers under 50 t/year: about 90% of importers,
  mostly SMEs, while 99% of emissions remain covered. So the many small EU buyers of Turkish SMEs
  no longer ask for data. The remaining demand comes from large importers, whose suppliers are
  mostly larger Turkish mills already working with consultants.
- **Default values** are now country- and product-specific Commission Excel files and **need no
  verification**. The markup rises from 10% in 2026 to 20% in 2027 and 30% in 2028. For a small
  exporter, "use defaults" is the zero-cost answer. Actual values need an accredited verifier,
  and software can't supply that.
- Export associations (İMMİB, UİB, AKİB) and the Ministry of Trade run SKDM information and
  support programmes.

## What changed versus the Sonnet evidence
- The phantom competitor list is confirmed. The real local rivals are Carbongate and CarbonEmit
  (both flagged but not checked by Sonnet) plus the PwC/SAP module.
- Sonnet's open "self-serve, low-priced tool for SME exporters" ignores the 50 t threshold and the
  no-verification default-value route. Together these remove most SME willingness to pay. The
  rising markup (to 30% by 2028) gives a reason to move to actual data, but the cost of that is
  verification, not software.

## Barriers
- Verification by accredited bodies is required for actual values, and that cost dominates.
- The 50 t de minimis removes small EU buyers.
- The EU's free communication template and default values.
- Large exporters are served by Big-4 and enterprise tools.

## Buyer and price
The buyer is the export or quality manager at a mid-size Turkish downstream steel or aluminium
products maker (fasteners, pipes, profiles) selling to 50-t-plus EU importers. Price anchors are
unpublished (Carbongate and CarbonEmit quote-based), against a free default-value route and
verifier fees (unverified amounts).
